# Concept profile: ع-ق-ل (`Eql`) — to bind / to reason

> **Source:** Parallel-agent deep-read, deep "research" investigation, 2026-04-26.
> **Data:** `data/concepts/Eql.json` (49 occurrences, 1 lemma, 30 surahs, 30 Meccan / 19 Medinan)
> **Critical morphological feature:** ZERO nouns. 100% verbs. The Quran has no nominal `ʿaql` (the intellect).

## The distinctive role of Eql: a verb-only root

The data confirms it categorically. ع-ق-ل occurs **49 times across 49 ayahs in 30 surahs**, and every single occurrence is a **verb** (`pos_distribution: {"V": 49}`). There is exactly **one lemma**: `Eaqala` (عَقَلَ). The Quran has no `ʿaql` (العقل, "the intellect" as noun), no `ʿāqil` ("rational person"), no `maʿqūl` ("the intelligible"). The morphological surface is even narrower than that — the 49 tokens collapse into just **five inflected forms**:

| Form | Translation | Count |
|---|---|---|
| تَعْقِلُونَ (taʿqilūn) | "you (pl.) reason" | 24 |
| يَعْقِلُونَ (yaʿqilūn) | "they reason" | 22 |
| عَقَلُوهُ (ʿaqalūhu) | "they comprehended it" | 1 (2:75) |
| يَعْقِلُهَا (yaʿqiluhā) | "he reasons it" | 1 (29:43) |
| نَعْقِلُ (naʿqil) | "we (would have) reasoned" | 1 (67:10) |

So 46 of 49 tokens are imperfect plurals — almost the entire root is the second- and third-person plural verb. Compare this to `Hkm` (حكم, wisdom): **13 lemmas**, **210 tokens**, with adjectives (85, mostly the divine epithet *Ḥakīm*) and nouns (75, *ḥukm*/*ḥikma*) outweighing verbs (50). Hikma is a thing one *has* and that God *is*; ʿaql is only ever something one *does* — or, more often, fails to do. **This is a structural theological fact, not an accident of vocabulary.**

The root's literal sense — to bind a camel's leg with a tether (ʿiqāl) — informs the verbal action: ʿaql is the act of *tying inferences together*, binding signs to conclusions. The Quran preserves this dynamic, kinetic sense and refuses to reify it into a static faculty.

## The "do you not reason?" rhetorical challenge

The signature formula `afalā taʿqilūn / yaʿqilūn` (أَفَلَا تَعْقِلُونَ — "then will you not reason?") appears **14 times** out of 49 (≈29%). When you add the closely related `lā yaʿqilūn` ("they do not reason," **12 occurrences**) and `lā taʿqilūn` patterns, the **explicitly negated/indictment uses total ~25 of 49 (≈51%)**. The rhetorical pattern is consistent: a sign or moral teaching is laid out, and the audience is challenged with a clipped, almost exasperated `afalā taʿqilūn`. Examples include 2:44 (Children of Israel preaching what they do not practice), 21:67 (Abraham to the idolaters), 23:80 (life, death, alternation of night and day), 36:68 (the reversal of aging), 37:138 (the ruins of Lot's people).

## Subject patterns — who reasons, who fails

Quantifying tone:
- **Negative/indictment (you/they do not reason, will you not reason):** ~25
- **Conditional/aspirational ("that you might reason," "perhaps you will reason"):** ~8 (e.g. 2:73, 2:242, 12:2, 43:3, 57:17)
- **The "people who reason" descriptive formula (positive):** **8** (see below)
- **Other:** the remarkable 67:10, the only first-person occurrence — the *damned in hellfire* confessing "if only we had listened or reasoned" (`law kunnā nasmaʿu aw naʿqilu`).

God, prophets, and angels never `yaʿqil` in the Quran. Reasoning is exclusively a **human** activity, and the Quran's tone toward it is overwhelmingly an indictment of human failure.

## The "li-qawmin yaʿqilūn" formula

The descriptive positive formula **`li-qawmin yaʿqilūn`** ("[signs] for a people who reason") occurs **exactly 8 times**: 2:164, 13:4, 16:12, 16:67, 29:35, 30:24, 30:28, 45:5. This precisely matches the `li-qawmin yatafakkarūn` count (8) the team found for fkr — a striking parallel. There is also a **negative qawm** version, `qawmun lā yaʿqilūn` ("a people who do not reason"), at 5:58 and 59:14 — a direct mirror image used as a reproach. So the formula's polarity is itself contested: the Quran can describe a community by either its reasoning or its failure of it.

Notably, every positive `qawmin yaʿqilūn` follows a catalogue of **natural signs** (āyāt): heavens/earth, alternation of night and day, rain reviving dead earth, fruits, lightning, ships at sea. This is the inferential mode in action — binding observed phenomena to their Creator.

## Reason vs Wisdom (Eql vs Hkm)

This contrast is the most theologically loaded finding. Compare the morphology:

| | Eql (ع-ق-ل) | Hkm (ح-ك-م) |
|---|---|---|
| Lemmas | 1 | 13 |
| POS profile | 100% verb | 40% ADJ, 36% N, 24% V |
| Divine attribute? | **Never** | **Yes** — *al-Ḥakīm* (97×) |
| Subject | only humans | God, prophets, judges, humans |
| Tone | indictment / failure | praise / endowment |

`Ḥikma` is something God *gives* (e.g. to David, Luqman, Muhammad); `ʿaql` is never given, never possessed, never a divine attribute. The Quran's epistemology, then, is asymmetric: **wisdom descends as a gift, reason is demanded as an act**. The closest the Quran comes to praising reasoners is the descriptive `qawmin yaʿqilūn` — and even that is matched by `qawmun lā yaʿqilūn`.

## Illustrative ayahs (translator panel)

**2:44** (Medinan, الَ-بَقَرَة) — the founding rebuke
- Saheeh: "Do you order righteousness of the people and forget yourselves while you recite the Scripture? Then will you not reason?"
- Pickthall: "…Have ye then no sense?"
- Khattab: "Do you preach righteousness and fail to practice it…? Do you not understand?"
- Arberry: "Do you not understand?"

**2:164** — the *qawmin yaʿqilūn* formula in full bloom
- Saheeh: "…signs for a people who use reason."
- Pickthall: "…signs for people who have sense."
- Khattab: "…signs for people of understanding."
- Arberry: "…signs for a people having understanding."

**8:22** — the bestial indictment
- Saheeh: "Indeed, the worst of living creatures in the sight of Allah are the deaf and dumb who do not use reason."
- Pickthall: "…the deaf, the dumb, who have no sense."
- Khattab: "…the ˹wilfully˺ deaf and dumb, who do not understand."
- Arberry: "…those that are deaf and dumb and do not understand."

**21:67** — Abraham's exasperation at the idolaters
- Saheeh: "Uff to you and to what you worship instead of Allah. Then will you not use reason?"
- Pickthall: "Fie on you… Have ye then no sense?"

**29:43** — the singular `yaʿqiluhā`, where reasoning is gated by knowledge
- Saheeh: "And these examples We present to the people, but none will understand them except those of knowledge (al-ʿālimūn)."
- Pickthall: "…none will grasp their meaning save the wise."

**59:14** — the negative qawm formula
- Saheeh: "…their hearts are diverse. That is because they are a people who do not reason."

**67:10** — the only first-person form, from the damned
- Saheeh: "And they will say, 'If only we had been listening or reasoning, we would not be among the companions of the Blaze.'"
- Khattab: "If only we had listened and reasoned…"

## Translator divergence

Translation is unusually stable: Saheeh and Khattab favor "reason / understand," Arberry uniformly chooses "understand," Pickthall uses the (now archaic) "have sense / no sense." The only revealing divergence is **29:43**, where Pickthall renders `al-ʿālimūn` as "the wise" — quietly conflating reasoning, knowledge, and wisdom that the Arabic carefully keeps distinct. Khattab's "people of understanding" for the *qawmin yaʿqilūn* formula slightly softens the action-oriented verb into a quasi-noun, leaning toward the very nominalization the Arabic resists.

## Semantic field (top co-occurring roots)

The companions of `Eql` are revealing:
- **Alh** (إله, "god/deity") — 19 ayahs (38.8%): reasoning is almost always linked to the question of true vs false deities
- **Ayy** (آية, "sign") — 13 ayahs (26.5%): reason's object is the sign
- **qwm** (قوم, "people") — 11 ayahs: the *qawmin yaʿqilūn* formula
- **Hyy** (حيي, "life"), **mwt** (موت, "death") — 10/7: reviving dead earth as the prime sign-cluster
- **smE** (سمع, "hearing") — 6: paired in 67:10 and 8:22 — reason and hearing are the two failed channels
- **ArD/smw** (earth/heavens), **lyl** (night), **nzl** (sending down), **mwh** (water): the natural-signs catalogue
- **kvr** (كثر, "most"): "most of them do not reason"
- **kfr** is *not* in the top 25 — disbelief is implied through *Alh* and through the negation framing rather than named

## What surprised me

Three things stand out:

1. **The morphology is the message.** A root with 49 occurrences and exactly *one* lemma in *one* tense pattern (almost entirely 2pl/3pl imperfect) is unprecedented for a major theological term. The Quran appears to actively refuse the nominalization that later Islamic philosophy (the falāsifa, the Muʿtazila) would build entire epistemic systems on. ʿAql in the Quran cannot be *reified*; it can only be *enacted* — or accusingly noted in its absence.

2. **The damned remember reason, not faith.** In 67:10, hellfire's inhabitants do not say "if only we had believed" — they say "if only we had *listened or reasoned*" (`law kunnā nasmaʿu aw naʿqil`). Reasoning is here positioned as the prior epistemic act whose failure makes disbelief possible. The single first-person form in the entire root is a posthumous confession.

3. **The asymmetry with Ḥikma.** The Quran's God *is* Wise (Ḥakīm, 97 times), *gives* wisdom, and *judges* (ḥakama, 45 times). The Quran's God never reasons and never gives reasoning — He *demands* it, indicts its absence, and offers signs to it. Ḥikma is vertical and gifted; ʿaql is horizontal and required. This is a profound theological geometry hidden in the morphology.

## One-sentence summary

`Eql` is the Quran's verb of inferential binding — a strictly human, almost exclusively plural, and overwhelmingly negated act of tying signs to their meaning, which the Quran demands as a duty but pointedly refuses to dignify with a noun.
