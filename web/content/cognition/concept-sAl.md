# Concept profile: س-أ-ل (`sAl`) — to ask / inquire / question / request

> **Source:** Parallel-agent deep-read, deep "research" investigation, 2026-04-26.
> **Data:** `data/concepts/sAl.json` (129 occurrences, 6 lemmas, 118 ayahs, 47 surahs, 89 Meccan / 40 Medinan)
> **POS:** 115 verbs, 14 nouns. Top surah: Al-Baqara (17). Lemmas: `sa>ala` (سَأَلَ, ask — 106), `yatasaA^'al` (يَتَسَا^ءَلُ, ask each other — 9), `saA^il` (سَا^ئِل, asker/beggar — 7), `mas'uwl` (مَسْؤُول, one questioned/responsible — 5), `su'l` (سُؤْل) and `su'aAl` (سُؤَال, request — 1 each).

## The distinctive role of sAl

sAl is the only root in the Quran's cognitive constellation that is **inherently dyadic**. fkr (think), nZr (look), *kr (remember), Eql (reason), lbb (have intellect), bSr (perceive), Hkm (judge), and Elm (know) all describe what a single mind does — solo cognition or knowledge-as-state. sAl describes **knowledge transfer between two parties**: an inquirer with an epistemic gap, an addressee assumed to have epistemic surplus. Even in its passive form (سُئِلَ, "be asked") and its reciprocal form (تَسَا^ءَلَ, "ask each other"), the root never collapses into a private mental act. This is why the Quran has so many cognition roots — they cover orthogonal axes. sAl owns the **interpersonal axis**: the moment cognition becomes social, transactional, and on the record. **The single appearance of `jwb` (answer) co-occurring with sAl across all 118 ayahs is itself revealing — the Quran does not narrate "answers" as a separate action.** The pattern is "they ask … say (qul)." Revelation answers; it does not "reply."

## The "they ask you about X" formula (yas>aluwnaka Ean)

**17 of 129 occurrences** carry the `yas>aluwnaka` / `wa-yas>aluwnaka` / `yas>aluka` shape — the formula by which the early community's questions enter the canonical text. Saheeh's bracketed `[O Muhammad]` makes it explicit; Pickthall renders "they ask thee", Arberry the archaic "they will question thee." The topics-asked log:

| Ayah | Topic asked about |
|------|---|
| 2:189 | the new moons (calendar) |
| 2:215 | what to spend (charity) |
| 2:217 | fighting in the sacred month |
| 2:219 (×2) | wine & gambling; what to spend |
| 2:220 | orphans |
| 2:222 | menstruation |
| 4:153 | (People of the Book) demanding a book from the sky |
| 5:4 | what foods are lawful |
| 7:187, 79:42, 33:63 | the Hour (when?) |
| 8:1 | spoils of war (al-anfal) |
| 17:85 | the spirit / soul (al-ruh) |
| 18:83 | Dhul-Qarnayn |
| 20:105 | the mountains (eschatological fate) |

Almost every formula occurrence is **Medinan** except the eschatological/cosmological ones (Hour, soul, mountains, Dhul-Qarnayn — Meccan). This bifurcation is itself a finding: in Mecca the community asks about *cosmology and the unseen*; in Medina they ask about *law and ethics*. The formula is the Quran's recorded research-questions log — ask any historian "what was the early Muslim community confused about?" and these 14 topics are the answer.

## Sense distribution

| Sense | Count |
|---|---|
| Inquiry (asking-for-information, all forms) | ~80 |
| Eschatological interrogation (passive `yus'al` / `tus'aluwna` / `latus'alunna`) | 15 |
| Prophets' refrain "I ask no reward" (`as>alukum ajran`) | 11 |
| Asking-each-other (`tasaA^'al`) | 9 |
| Beggar / petitioner (`saA^il`) | 7 |
| Held-accountable (`mas'uwl`) | 5 |
| Request-as-noun (`su'l` / `su'aAl`) | 2 |

The 11 occurrences of أَسْـَلُكُمْ — "I do not ask you any reward" — are spoken by Noah (11:29, 11:51), Hud, Salih, Lot, Shu'ayb (the five-fold refrain across 26:109/127/145/164/180), and Muhammad himself (6:90, 25:57, 38:86, 42:23). Here sAl crosses from **information** into **economic demand** — the prophets reject patronage. This is the same semantic stretch that produces `saA^il` (the beggar): an asker is one with a deficit, whether of knowledge or wealth.

## Subject–addressee patterns

- **Community → Muhammad** (the formula): 17 — the central social-cognitive event of the Quran.
- **Muhammad → others**, imperative: 12+ ("ask the Children of Israel" 2:211, 17:101; "ask the people of the reminder" 16:43, 21:7; "ask the city" 12:82; "ask those We sent before you" 43:45; "ask them which is responsible" 68:40). Muhammad is also commanded to *do research*.
- **Allah → the questioned**: passive constructions (15) — humans asked by God on Judgment Day.
- **Disbelievers → Muhammad** (mocking): asking when the Hour comes (51:12, 79:42), demanding the punishment (70:1).
- **Prophet → Allah** (rare, individual): Noah asks about his son and is rebuked (11:46–47); Moses is told "do not ask me" by Khidr (18:70, 18:76); Solomon makes a request (38:35, conceptually adjacent).
- **Disciples → prophet within narrative**: Joseph's brothers asked to "ask the city" (12:82); Sleepers of the Cave ask each other (18:19).

## Five illustrative ayahs

**1. Asking-Muhammad, legal (2:222 — menstruation):**
- Saheeh: "And they ask you about menstruation. Say, 'It is harm…'"
- Pickthall: "They question thee… concerning menstruation. Say: It is an illness…"
- Khattab: "They ask you ˹O Prophet˺ about menstruation. Say, 'Beware of its harm!'"
- Arberry: "They will question thee concerning the monthly course."

**2. Asking-Muhammad, metaphysical (17:85 — the spirit):**
- Saheeh: "And they ask you, [O Muhammad], about the soul. Say, 'The soul is of the affair of my Lord.'"
- Pickthall: "They are asking thee concerning the Spirit."
- Arberry: "They will question thee concerning the Spirit."

**3. Eschatological interrogation (102:8):**
- Saheeh: "Then you will surely be asked that Day about pleasure."
- Khattab: "you will definitely be questioned about ˹your worldly˺ pleasures."
- Arberry: "you shall be questioned that day concerning true bliss."

**4. The beggar (93:10):**
- Saheeh: "And as for the petitioner, do not repel [him]."
- Pickthall: "Therefor the beggar drive not away."
- Khattab: "nor repulse the beggar."
- Arberry: "as for the beggar, scold him not."

**5. Asking-each-other (4:1):**
- Saheeh: "fear Allah, through whom you ask one another, and the wombs."
- Khattab: "be mindful of Allah—in Whose Name you appeal to one another."
- Arberry: "fear God by whom you **demand** one of another."

**6. Translator-divergent (70:1, `sa'ala saA^ilun`):**
- Saheeh: "A **supplicant asked** for a punishment bound to happen."
- Pickthall: "A **questioner questioned** concerning the doom about to fall."
- Khattab: "A **challenger has demanded** a punishment bound to come."
- Arberry: "A **questioner asked** of a chastisement about to fall."
A four-way split — supplicant/questioner/challenger — across `ask/question/demand`. The verb sa'ala here straddles inquiry, request, and defiance; the translators each pick a different point on that arc. Same divergence at 2:61 where the Israelites' food-request is "asked" (Saheeh, Khattab) vs "demand" (Pickthall, Arberry).

## Semantic field

Top co-occurring roots in sAl ayahs: **qwl** (40, "say" — the canonical answering verb), **Alh** (37, "god/deity"), **Elm** (27, "know" — sAl as the inverse of knowing), **kwn** (27), **rbb** (20, "Lord"), **Ajr** (19, "reward" — driven by the prophets' refrain), **Aty** (19), **qbl** (13, "before"), **Amn** (11), **ywm** (10, "Day" — eschatological), **smw** (10, "heaven"), **rsl** (10, "messenger"), **ArD** (10). Notably absent: **jwb** ("answer") co-occurs only **once**. The paired verb is `qul` ("say"), not `ajaba`.

## What surprised me

1. **`jwb` is essentially absent from sAl ayahs (1/118).** The Quran does not pair "ask" with "answer." Revelation's response-act is `qul` — direct speech, not dialogic reply. This is structurally important: sAl questions don't get *replies*, they get *Sayings*.
2. **The topics-asked list reads like a community FAQ.** Calendar, war ethics, intoxicants, gambling, orphan custody, menstrual law, dietary law, war-spoils — and three metaphysical fixations: the Hour, the soul, the eschatological fate of mountains. This is a real window into early-Medinan and late-Meccan concerns: pragmatic legal questions in Medina, cosmological anxieties in Mecca.
3. **The beggar (saA^il) and the inquirer share one word.** A semantic identity that flattens information-poverty and material-poverty into the same status: someone with a deficit, owed a response. The same root that means "research question" also means "outstretched palm."
4. **The five-fold prophetic refrain in surah 26 (`la> as>alukum Ealayhi min ajr`) is sAl in its negative form** — five different prophets denying that they ask anything. Cognition-talk turns into economics-talk: the question of *what the messenger is asking from you* is part of the messenger's authentication.
5. **Nine `tasaA^al` occurrences are mostly eschatological** — people asking each other on the Day, in Heaven (52:25, 74:40), or in confused silence (23:101, 28:66). Mutual inquiry is a feature of the Hereafter as much as of the world.

## One-sentence summary

**sAl is the Quran's vocabulary for cognition-as-relation** — the root that records *who asked whom about what* — and it spans a single arc from research-question to outstretched palm to the interrogation of Judgment Day, all governed by the principle that every question, in this world or the next, is on the record and addressed to someone who must respond.
