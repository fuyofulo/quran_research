# Concept profile: ن-ظ-ر (`nZr`) — to look / observe / consider / wait

> **Source:** Parallel-agent deep-read, deep "research" investigation, 2026-04-26.
> **Data:** `data/concepts/nZr.json` (129 occurrences, 10 lemmas, 48 surahs, 99 Meccan / 30 Medinan)
> **POS:** 107 V (83%) / 22 N — overwhelmingly verbal, the most-commanded cognitive root.

## The distinctive role of nZr

Among the ~14 cognitive roots, nZr is the **active verbal mode of attention**. The morphology proves it: of 129 occurrences, **107 are verbs (83%)** versus only 22 nouns. Compare bSr (`ب-ص-ر`, 148 occurrences): **95 N + 20 ADJ + only 33 V**. The grammatical asymmetry is the cognitive asymmetry — bSr is the *capacity* (sight, insight, faculty) one possesses; nZr is the *act* one performs. nZr is what a researcher *does* with the faculty bSr names.

The root is also overwhelmingly *commanded*. Form I `n~aZara` accounts for 87 occurrences; of these, **43 are imperative** (`unZur`, `fa-nZur`, `wa-nZur`, `fa-l-yanZur`, `wa-l-tanZur`). Roughly **one in three** Quranic uses of nZr is the divine voice ordering "Look!" — making nZr the Quran's signature verb for the rhetorical command to *direct attention*.

**Eschatological-waiting senses combined = 44 of 129 (34%)** — almost exactly equal in weight to the imperative-look. nZr is genuinely bimodal: a third commands attention; a third names the act of awaiting (often hostilely framed: "what are they waiting for, except…?").

## The "look-at-the-signs" empiricist formula

Of the 43 imperatives, the most striking pattern is **`unẓur kayfa…`** ("look how…") — the imperative + interrogative `kayfa`. The root `kyf` co-occurs with nZr in **36 ayahs (31% of nZr ayahs)** — by far the highest cognitive co-occurrence. This is the Quran's signature "directed-examination" formula.

Targets of the imperative break down as:
- **Historical fate (`how was the end of…`): 14 ayahs** — the Quran's "go through the earth and observe" archaeological gesture (3:137, 6:11, 27:69, 30:42…).
- **Natural phenomena: 12 ayahs** — the camel (88:17), the food (80:24), what man was created from (86:5), rain and ripening fruit (6:99), the dead earth revived (30:50), the kingdom of heavens and earth (7:185), how creation began (29:20), the bones being reassembled (2:259).
- **Self-examination: 1 ayah** — 59:18 *"let every soul look to what it has put forth for tomorrow"*.
- **Moses asking to see God: 2 imperatives in 7:143**.
- **Rhetorical/comparative ("see how they invent / strike comparisons"): ~14**.

The empiricist gesture is unmistakable: the Quran says *look at the camel, look at your food, look at the rain, look at the bones*. Co-occurrence confirms this — `Ayy` (signs) appears in 11 nZr-ayahs, `ArD` (earth) in 20, `smw` (heavens) in 10, `syr` (travel/move-through) in 12.

## The eschatological-waiting pattern

The waiting sense is *morphologically layered*:
- **Form VIII `intaẓara`** (8 verbs + 6 `muntaẓirūn`) — pure waiting, often reciprocal: *"wait — they too are waiting"* (32:30, 11:122).
- **Form IV passive `yunẓaru`** (12) and **`munẓarūn`** (6) — *"be granted respite/reprieve"* — the cosmic clock formula. Iblīs's request `fa-anẓirnī ilā yawmi yub`athūn` (15:36) is the archetype.
- **Form I `yanẓurūna illā…`** (11) — the rhetorical-hostile *"What are they waiting for except [the Hour, the angels, the Lord]?"* (2:210, 6:158, 16:33, 43:66, 47:18). This is where the look/wait polysemy is sharpest.

`ywm` (day) co-occurs in 13 ayahs; `Aty` (to come) in 11. The semantic field of nZr-as-waiting is uniformly **eschatological-temporal**: what is awaited is always the Hour, the Lord, the angels, the decree.

## The eye-of-God exception

I found **four** ayahs where the looker is divine or the looking-direction is upward to God:

1. **75:23** — `ilā rabbihā nāẓirah` ("Looking at their Lord") — the literal beatific vision, faces of the saved on Resurrection Day. All four translators agree: Saheeh "Looking at," Pickthall "Looking toward," Khattab "looking at," Arberry "gazing upon."
2. **3:77** — `lā yukallimuhumu llāhu wa-lā yanẓuru ilayhim` ("Allah will not… look at them on the Day of Resurrection") — the negative inverse: divine non-regard as punishment.
3. **7:143** — Moses begging `arinī anẓur ilayka` ("show me, that I may look at You") — denied; the imperative `unẓur ilā l-jabal` ("look at the mountain") is offered as substitute.
4. **10:14** — `li-nanẓura kayfa taʿmalūn` ("that We may observe how you act") — divine `kayfa`-observation, the exact mirror of the formula God commands humans to use.

These four ayahs are theologically charged precisely because nZr is normally a *human* act. When it has a divine subject, it is always paired with eschatological judgment.

## Translator divergence

The cleanest split is **Arberry vs. everyone else** on Form I `yanẓurūna illā…`. In **6 of 11** Form-I-wait ayahs, Arberry alone preserves the literal *"look for"* while Saheeh/Pickthall/Khattab modernize to *"await/wait"* (2:210, 6:158, 7:53, 16:33, 43:66, 47:18). Arberry's choice keeps visible the etymological bridge: in Arabic to wait is to *look ahead at* the expected. The other three sacrifice the gesture for English idiom. This is a real interpretive cost: the empiricist formula `unẓur` and the eschatological formula `yanẓurūn` are the *same verb*, and three of the four English translators hide the connection.

## Semantic field (top co-occurring roots)

`kwn` (be) 41 · `qwl` (say) 40 · **`kyf` (how) 36** · `Alh` (god) 30 · `Eqb` (end/aftermath) 21 · **`ArD` (earth) 20** · `Amn` (believe) 17 · `qbl` (before) 17 · **`ywm` (day) 13** · `rbb` (lord) 13 · **`syr` (travel) 12** · `rAy` (see) 11 · `Aty` (come) 11 · **`Ayy` (signs) 11** · **`smw` (heavens) 10** · `E*b` (punish) 9 · `k*b` (deny) 9 · `bEv` (resurrect) 8.

The triple **`kyf` + `syr` + `Eqb`** (how + travel + end) is the archaeological-empirical cluster. The triple **`Ayy` + `smw` + `ArD`** is the natural-signs cluster. The triple **`ywm` + `Aty` + `bEv`** is the eschatological-waiting cluster. nZr sits at the intersection of all three.

## nZr vs bSr — the cognitive distinction

The pos_distribution is decisive:

| Root | V | N | ADJ | Total | Verbal share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **nZr** | 107 | 22 | 0 | 129 | **83%** |
| **bSr** | 33 | 95 | 20 | 148 | 22% |

bSr is the **noun of capacity**: `baṣīr` (51) "Seer/perceptive" is overwhelmingly a divine name, and `baṣar` (48) is the eye-as-faculty. nZr is the **verb of act**: `naẓara`/`yanẓur`/`unẓur` is what one *does* with that faculty. Together they form a complete cognitive economy: bSr names what God-and-humans *have*; nZr names what humans are *commanded to do* with it. The thesis "bSr = noun-of-capacity, nZr = verb-of-observation" is borne out cleanly in the data.

## What surprised me

1. **The waiting sense is huge.** I expected nZr to be ~80% looking. The data shows ~34% waiting/respite — a near-even bimodality. Polysemy isn't peripheral; it is structural.
2. **`Eqb` (end/aftermath) is the 5th-highest co-occurrence (21 ayahs).** The Quran's signature "go look at how-was-the-end of past nations" formula is more prevalent than I anticipated. nZr is as much an *archaeological* verb as an empirical one — a directive to read the ruins.
3. **One single ayah is the entire beatific-vision proof-text** (75:23, two words: `ilā rabbihā nāẓirah`). A theological tradition of immense weight rests on a hapax-style fem. active participle.
4. **Iblīs's respite (`anẓirnī`) and the believer's "wait!" (`unẓurnā`) are the same verb in different voices** — including the hypocrites' desperate `unẓurūnā` at 57:13. The morphology of waiting binds Satan, hypocrites, and prophets alike.
5. **Meccan dominance (99 vs 30)** — at 77% Meccan, nZr is more strongly tied to the early empirical/eschatological phase of preaching than to the legal Medinan corpus.

## One-sentence summary

**nZr is the Quran's commanded act of attention — the imperative-driven verbal mode in which a researcher is told to direct the gaze at signs, ruins, and the encroaching Hour, and which collapses the difference between *looking-at* the world and *looking-for* the End into a single root.**
