# Concept profile: ق-ر-أ (`qrA`) — to read / recite

> **Source:** Parallel-agent deep-read, deep "research" investigation, 2026-04-26.
> **Data:** `data/concepts/qrA.json` (88 occurrences across 79 ayahs in 42 surahs. 78 Meccan, 10 Medinan. 4 lemmas.)
> **Critical:** The Quran's name for itself, **al-Qur'an** ("The Recitation"), is from this root.

## The distinctive role of qrA — the self-referential root

**qrA is the only cognitive root in the Quran that names the Quran.** Where roots like `Eql`, `fkr`, `*kr` describe *what to do with* revelation, qrA describes the revelation itself: it is `al-Qur'an` — literally "The Recitation." The scripture's identity is built from the verb of the cognitive act it demands. To name a book "The Reading" is to embed the prescribed cognitive mode into the title. **Of 88 occurrences, 70 (79.5%) are the noun `quro'aAn`** — and 68 of those are tagged PN (proper noun: the Quran itself). Only 2 noun uses are common-noun "a recitation" (75:17–18, where God describes assembling Muhammad's `qur'aanahu` = "his recitation").

## The 96:1 imperative — the headline of the project

The first word ever revealed to Muhammad is `ٱقْرَأْ` (iqra), the imperative of qara>a. Surah 96 (Al-Alaq) opens:

- **96:1** — "Read / Recite in the name of your Lord who created"
- **96:2** — "Created man from a clinging clot"
- **96:3** — "Read / Recite, and your Lord is the Most Generous"
- **96:4** — "Who taught by the pen"
- **96:5** — "Taught man what he knew not"

Two extraordinary features. First, **what is commanded is the act, not the object** — `iqra` is given without a direct grammatical object. The translators visibly struggle: Saheeh and Arberry choose "Recite"; Pickthall and Khattab choose "Read." The Arabic does not specify what to read. The bare imperative makes the command *to perform the cognitive act* the foundational divine instruction. Second, **the surrounding frame is pedagogy of cognition**: pen, teaching, knowledge previously absent. The first revelation is not theology, not law, not prophecy — it is an order to read, paired with a description of how God taught man to know.

Of the 16 verb occurrences, **6 are imperatives** (96:1, 96:3, 17:14, 69:19, 73:20 ×2). Imperatives constitute over a third of the verb form — qrA in verbal mode is overwhelmingly *commanded*.

## Sense distribution

- **70/88 (79.5%)** — noun `qur'aan`: the Recitation as a thing
- **16/88 (18.2%)** — verb "read/recite"
- **1/88** — causative "make-recite" (87:6: "We shall make you recite, and you will not forget")
- **1/88** — `quru'` (menstrual cycles, 2:228) — the lexical outlier; here the root carries an old Arabic sense of "gathering/collecting at intervals" that is etymologically related but semantically isolated

The root is **massively front-loaded toward self-reference**: 4 out of every 5 occurrences are the Quran naming itself.

## The Quran's self-description

Where `qur'aan` appears as PN, it carries a recurring vocabulary of attributes. The descriptive frame is remarkably stable:
- **Easy** for remembrance — 54:17, 22, 32, 40 (the refrain of Al-Qamar)
- **Arranged distinctly** in measured order (`rattalnaahu tartila`) — 25:32; cf. 73:4 "recite the Qur'an in measured recitation"
- **Noble** (`kareem`) — 56:77; **glorious** (`majeed`) — 50:1, 85:21; **wise** (`hakeem`) — 36:2
- **Arabic** — 12:2, 20:113, 39:28, 41:3, 42:7, 43:3 (six times — the linguistic identity is doctrinally fixed)
- **Healing and mercy** — 17:82
- **Unmatchable**: jinn and humans together cannot produce its like — 17:88; if revealed on a mountain, the mountain would split in awe — 59:21
- **Sworn-by**: divine oaths use it as object (50:1, 36:2, 38:1, 85:21) — the Quran swears *by itself*

## Recitation as practice

qrA verbs concentrate around the act of liturgical recitation:
- **17:78** — `qur'aan al-fajr` ("the dawn recitation/prayer") is "witnessed" — recitation tied to the salat schedule
- **73:4, 73:20** — Recite measuredly at night vigil; recite "what is easy of it" — the only place qrA verb and `qur'aan` noun appear together in the same ayah twice
- **7:204** — "When the Qur'an is recited, listen and be silent" — communal liturgical etiquette
- **16:98** — Before reciting, "seek refuge from Satan" — the still-practiced `ta'awwudh`
- **17:45** — Reciting itself creates a metaphysical barrier between the Prophet and disbelievers
- **17:106** — "A Qur'an We have separated [in stages] that you may recite it to people at intervals" — *the verse explicitly grounds the noun in the verb*

## Translator divergence — read vs. recite

Across the verb forms, **Pickthall consistently chooses "read"; Saheeh and Arberry consistently choose "recite"; Khattab oscillates** ("read" at 96:1, 17:14, 69:19; "recite" at 7:204, 73:4, 17:106). The single Arabic verb encompasses both silent reading and oral articulation, and English forces the translator to commit. Pickthall most strikingly translates the **noun** itself non-conventionally three times: "a Lecture in Arabic" (12:2, 41:3, 13:31) — preserving the original Arabic sense that `qur'aan` is a *common noun* meaning "thing-recited" before it is a proper noun. Khattab does the same once at 13:31 ("a recitation"). Saheeh's footnote-style "[any] qur'an" at 13:31 is also revealing — they recognize the common-noun sense is in play.

This divergence is not stylistic — it tracks a real exegetical question: *Is `qur'aan` here referring to The Quran, or to the abstract category "a recitation"?*

## qrA vs. tly — two roots for "recite"

`tly` (تلو, "to follow / recite in sequence") shares only 3 ayahs with qrA. This is structurally important: the two roots almost don't overlap. The cognitive distinction is subtle but real:

- **`tly`** foregrounds **sequence and following** — reciting *as one item follows another*. When the Quran says people "recite the verses of God to them" it usually uses `tly`. The metaphor is *trailing* — to read by tracking what comes next.
- **`qrA`** foregrounds **the act itself** — *gathering* the words into voice or sight. The root etymologically relates to "collecting" (witness `quru'` for menstrual cycles — gathered intervals; and 75:17 where God says "upon Us is its `jam'` (gathering) and its `qur'aan` (recitation)").

This is why the **scripture itself is `qur'aan` and not `tilaawah`**: the name foregrounds the gathering-into-voice, the act of producing the recitation, not the linear following of it. `tly` describes what the *reader* does ayah by ayah; `qrA` describes what the *Reciter* (Gabriel, the Prophet, God Himself at 75:17–18) *makes happen* in the act of articulation.

## What surprised me

1. **75:17–18 — God recites.** The most theologically arresting use: God says the act of recitation is *His* (`'alaynaa qur'aanahu`), not the Prophet's. The Quran is grammatically God's recitation, not Muhammad's. This grounds the noun `al-Qur'an` in a divine verb.
2. **17:14 and 69:19 — eschatological reading.** On Judgment Day, every soul is commanded `iqra' kitaabaka` ("Read your book") — the same imperative as 96:1. Life begins with "Read!" addressed to the Prophet, and ends with "Read!" addressed to every soul. **The first divine command and the final divine command share a verb.**
3. **13:31 — "if any qur'an could move mountains..."** The Quran imagines itself as a *category* — "a recitation" — and asserts that even if such a magical recitation existed, it would still be *this one*. The noun briefly de-capitalizes to admit its own rhetorical context.
4. **The first revelation has no object.** `iqra` stands alone. Translators must guess. Centuries of Islamic exegesis have read this as *the act itself being the point*.
5. **`quru'` at 2:228** — the menstrual-cycles outlier — preserves the root's archaic "gathering at intervals" sense, which then quietly underwrites the theology of revelation-in-stages (17:106, 25:32: the Quran is gathered to the Prophet *at intervals*).

## One-sentence summary

**qrA is the Quran's self-naming root: a verb of gathering-into-voice that became a proper noun, with the bare imperative `iqra` as both the first word ever revealed and the command spoken to every soul on Judgment Day — making the entire scripture a 70-fold echo of a single divine instruction to perform the cognitive act of reading.**
