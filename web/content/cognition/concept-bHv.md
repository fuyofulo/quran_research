# Concept profile: ب-ح-ث (`bHv`) — to scratch / dig / search / "research"

> **Source:** Initial analysis from `notes/concept-research.md` (the first pass at the cognitive constellation), 2026-04-26.
> **Data:** `data/concepts/bHv.json` (1 occurrence, 1 lemma, 1 surah, 1 Medinan)
> **CRITICAL:** This is the modern Arabic word for academic "research" (baḥth). It appears EXACTLY ONCE in the entire Quran.

## The headline finding

**The modern Arabic word for "research" — بحث (b-ḥ-th) — appears exactly once in the entire Quran.**

That single occurrence is at **5:31 (Al-Maaida)**, the verse about Cain and Abel. After Cain murders his brother Abel, Allah sends a crow that **يَبۡحَثُ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ** ("scratches/digs/searches in the earth") to teach Cain how to bury his brother's body.

## The single occurrence — full context

**Surah:** 5 (Al-Maaida, Medinan)
**Ayah:** 5:31
**Word:** يَبۡحَثُ (yabḥathu)
**Form:** Form I imperfect, 3rd singular masculine
**Lemma:** يَبۡحَثُ (yaboHavu)
**POS:** V (verb)

**Full ayah (Arabic):**
> فَبَعَثَ ٱللَّهُ غُرَابࣰ ا يَبۡحَثُ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ لِيُرِيَهُۥ كَيۡفَ يُوَٰرِي سَوۡءَةَ أَخِيهِۚ قَالَ يَٰوَيۡلَتَىٰٓ أَعَجَزۡتُ أَنۡ أَكُونَ مِثۡلَ هَٰذَا ٱلۡغُرَابِ فَأُوَٰرِيَ سَوۡءَةَ أَخِيۖ فَأَصۡبَحَ مِنَ ٱلنَّٰدِمِينَ

## The translator panel — exposing the etymology

The translation of يَبْحَثُ across the four sources reveals what the verb originally meant:

| Source | Rendering of يَبْحَثُ |
|---|---|
| Literal word-by-word (quran.com) | "it (was) scratching" |
| Saheeh International | "searching" |
| Pickthall | "scratching up" |
| Khattab | "digging" |
| Arberry | "scratching into" |

**The original Arabic verb means: a bird clawing the soil to uncover or hide what lies beneath.**

## Full ayah translations

**Saheeh:** "Then Allah sent a crow searching in the ground to show him how to hide the disgrace of his brother. He said, 'O woe to me! Have I failed to be like this crow and hide the body of my brother?' And he became of the regretful."

**Pickthall:** "Then Allah sent a raven scratching up the ground, to show him how to hide his brother's naked corpse. He said: Woe unto me! Am I not able to be as this raven and so hide my brother's naked corpse? And he became repentant."

**Khattab:** "Then Allah sent a crow digging ˹a grave˺ in the ground ˹for a dead crow˺, in order to show him how to bury the corpse of his brother. He cried, 'Alas! Have I ˹even˺ failed to be like this crow and bury the corpse of my brother?' So he became regretful"

**Arberry:** "Then God sent forth a raven, scratching into the earth, to show him how he might conceal the vile body of his brother. He said, 'Woe is me! Am I unable to be as this raven, and so conceal my brother's vile body?' And he became one of the remorseful."

## The etymological discovery

The metaphorical sense — "to investigate, to research" — is downstream of this physical image. **The modern academic activity we call "research" descends from a Quranic image of a crow teaching a murderer how to bury his crime.**

This single verse already tells us something foundational about the Quran's epistemic landscape: the Quran does **not have a noun-concept for "research"** as a settled academic activity. What it has instead is a **verbal vocabulary of cognitive acts**, each describing a different mode of inquiry. bHv is a hapax — a one-time occurrence — and its etymology pulls back from cognition to a literal, physical, animal act of clawing earth.

## Co-occurring roots (in this single ayah)

Because there's only 1 ayah, the co-roots all have share=1.00:
- راي (rAy, see) — "to show him"
- ارض (ArD, earth) — what the crow scratches
- ندم (ndm, regret) — Cain became regretful
- مثل (mvl, like) — "like this crow"
- سوا (swA, evil/disgrace) — the disgrace/corpse of his brother
- كيف (kyf, how) — "how to hide"
- وري (wry, conceal) — to hide/conceal
- بعث (bEv, send) — Allah sent
- اخو (Axw, brother) — his brother
- قول (qwl, say) — "He said"

The semantic field is: **showing, earth, regret, similarity, evil, how, concealing, sending, brother, speech.** This is a uniquely narrative cluster — the verb of "research" in the Quran appears only inside a story, never as an abstract category.

## What this single occurrence teaches

1. **The Quran does not have a word for "research" as such.** What modern Arabic uses for academic research (`baḥth`) appears exactly once, in a narrative about a bird.
2. **The metaphorical sense is downstream.** "Research" descends from "scratching earth to uncover or hide." The cognitive act of investigation is etymologically tied to the physical act of clawing soil.
3. **The Quran's cognitive vocabulary is not noun-categorical but verb-procedural.** It describes operations (looking, asking, finding, remembering, reflecting), not disciplines.
4. **The sole bHv use is itself a teaching moment.** Allah sends a creature to show a human how to do something. The first "research act" in the Quran is a divine pedagogy delivered through nature.

## One-sentence summary

**bHv is the Quranic root for the physical act of a bird clawing earth to uncover or hide what lies beneath — appearing exactly once, in the verse where Allah sends a crow to teach Cain how to bury his murdered brother — and its single occurrence is the structural fact that the Quran has no settled noun-category for "research" as an academic activity, only a verbal vocabulary of cognitive operations distributed across at least 14 distinct roots.**
