# Concept profile: و-ج-د (`wjd`) — to find / encounter / experience

> **Source:** Parallel-agent deep-read, deep "research" investigation, 2026-04-26.
> **Data:** `data/concepts/wjd.json` (107 occurrences across only 2 lemmas, 32 surahs, 59 Meccan / 48 Medinan)
> **Notable:** unusually morphologically narrow — only 2 lemmas for 107 occurrences.

## The distinctive role of wjd

The data confirms a striking morphological narrowness: **107 occurrences across only 2 lemmas — `wajada` (verb, 106×) and `wujd` (noun, 1× at 65:6 meaning "means/capacity")**. There is no Form II causative, no Form IV, no derived noun "discovery," no agentive participle "finder," no abstract noun "finding." Within the verb, just **41 distinct surface forms** — and the top form alone, **وَجَدْنَا ("we found"), accounts for 13 occurrences** (12% of the entire root). For comparison, most cognitive roots in the Quran fan out across 4–8 lemmas.

This concentration is itself a clue. The Quran does not theorize "discovery" as a faculty or a method (the way it nominalizes ʿilm as "knowledge" or fikr as "thought"). wjd is almost purely **eventive** — it names the *act* of encountering, never the *capacity* to encounter. The cognitive achievement is the simple punctual realization "X is the case." It is the verb of the Latin *invenire* — "to come upon" — not of "research." There is no methodology embedded in it. You don't *try* to wajada something; you simply do, or you don't.

The conjugational profile reinforces this: 33 second-person ("you will find"), 27 third-person, 14 first-person plural (heavy with the وَجَدْنَا taqlid formula), 6 first-person singular, 1 passive (12:75, "in whose bag it is found"). The verb lives in direct address and reportage of experience — it is fundamentally **testimonial**.

## The "we found our fathers" anti-formula (وَجَدْنَا آبَاءَنَا)

This is the analytical jackpot. Filtering for the religious-taqlid usage (excluding the literal cases like 12:65 "found their merchandise" and 12:94 "I find Joseph's smell"), there are exactly **9 occurrences** of the `wajadnā ābāʾanā` formula — all critiques of inherited belief, all (8 of 9) **Meccan**:

| Ayah | Surah | Form | Critique |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5:104 | Al-Maaida (Med) | وَجَدْنَا | "Sufficient for us is what we found our fathers upon" |
| 7:28 | Al-A'raaf (Mec) | وَجَدْنَا | "We found our fathers doing it [immorality]" |
| 10:78 | Yunus (Mec) | وَجَدْنَا | "Turn us from what we found our fathers upon?" |
| 21:53 | Al-Anbiyaa (Mec) | وَجَدْنَا | "We found our fathers worshippers of them" (Abraham's challenge) |
| 26:74 | Ash-Shuʿaraa (Mec) | وَجَدْنَا | "We found our fathers doing thus" |
| 31:21 | Luqman (Mec) | وَجَدْنَا | "We will follow what we found our fathers upon" |
| 43:22 | Az-Zukhruf (Mec) | وَجَدْنَا | "We found our fathers upon a religion (umma)" |
| 43:23 | Az-Zukhruf (Mec) | وَجَدْنَا | "Affluent of every city said: we found our fathers upon a religion" |
| 43:24 | Az-Zukhruf (Mec) | وَجَدتُّمْ | The warner's reply: "Even if I bring better than what you found your fathers upon?" |

This is a sustained, formulaic Quranic argument against **taqlid as epistemic warrant**. Note that the Quran does not deny the *truth-value* of the claim "we found our fathers doing X" — that finding is real. What it denies is its **probative force**. 5:104 makes this explicit: "Even though their fathers knew nothing (لَا يَعْلَمُونَ شَيْئًا) and were not guided?" The chain of finding is sound; the source is not. The epistemic verb is invariant — what shifts is *whose finding* counts.

The co-occurrence data corroborates this: **`Abw` (father) shares 14 ayahs with wjd (14% of all wjd ayahs)** — the 5th most-co-occurring root. For a root where 9 ayahs are the taqlid formula, that 14 is close to a ceiling.

## The "found Allah" sense

Only **2 occurrences** explicitly say "find Allah" with a divine attribute, both in Surah an-Nisaa, both Medinan, both about repentance:

- **4:64** — "...they would have found Allah Accepting of repentance, Merciful" (لَوَجَدُوا اللَّهَ تَوَّابًا رَّحِيمًا)
- **4:110** — "...will find Allah Forgiving, Merciful" (يَجِدِ اللَّهَ غَفُورًا رَّحِيمًا)

This is a small but theologically heavy use. Allah is positioned as something *encountered* — not deduced or inferred but **met** — at the precise moment of istighfar. Wjd here functions as the **existential result of an action**: do X, you will find Allah to be Y. It collapses the distance between predication and experience.

A related "found by Allah" sense appears in the 93:6–8 trio about the Prophet's biography: "Did He not **find you** (وَجَدَكَ) an orphan… find you lost (ḍāllan)… find you poor?" Here Allah is the subject of finding — the only place in the Qurʾan where Allah Himself is the one doing the wajada-encounter. This is the inverse of the 4:64/4:110 pattern.

## Translator divergence

wjd's "find" is remarkably stable across translators — Saheeh, Pickthall, and Arberry preserve "find/found" almost mechanically. The systematic outlier is **Khattab**, who *idiomizes* in the legal-means contexts. Where the Arabic literally says "if he does not find" (لَمْ يَجِدْ) for slave-manumission, fasting, food (5:89, 4:92, 58:4, 9:91, 58:12, 9:79), Khattab regularly substitutes "if they cannot afford" / "lacking the means" / "if he is unable." This obscures the conceptual unity: in Arabic these are all the *same verb of encounter* as the metaphysical "find Allah Forgiving." The verb's range — from "find a slave to free" to "find God merciful" — is one of the most semantically interesting features of the root, and English idiom flattens it. **Pickthall** also drops "find" in the Joseph narrative (12:65, 12:94) using "discovered"/"conscious of" — a hint that translators reach for "find" for cognitive/legal cases but freer verbs for sensory ones.

The noun **wujd** at 65:6 ("according to your means/wealth") is not rendered as anything related to "finding" by any translator — yet etymologically it is *exactly* "what you have found / what is at hand to you." This is the only nominal trace of the root and it has been entirely absorbed into the legal idiom of "means."

## The epistemological hypothesis — does it hold?

**Hypothesis:** wjd is empiricist (encounter as evidence) but the Quran self-critiques inherited encounter, validating only first-hand encounter with reality.

**Verdict: largely confirmed, with refinement.** The data shows wjd doing two opposite epistemic jobs:

- **Validating encounter** when the encounterer is positioned correctly: the believers will *find* the promise of the Lord true (7:44); the believer who repents will *find* Allah Forgiving (4:110); Khidr is *found* by Moses on a real journey (18:65); the People of the Book *find* the prophet's description in their own scripture (7:157, the Quran's own appeal to first-hand textual evidence). In each case, the finder has done the work of going.

- **Invalidating encounter** when the chain of transmission is the only basis: 9 times the Quran quotes opponents saying "we found our fathers..." and answers either "your fathers knew nothing" (5:104), "even if Satan invites them?" (31:21), or "even if I bring better?" (43:24). The verb is the same; the rebuke is methodological.

The deepest evidence is **5:104's pairing of وَجَدْنَا with لَا يَعْلَمُونَ** — the Quran is explicitly distinguishing finding-as-inheritance from knowledge. Tradition transmits the *form* of finding without the *substance* (ʿilm, hudā). The Quran is making a sophisticated point: **encounter is the right epistemic verb, but it must be encounter with reality — including the reality of revelation — and not encounter with one's parents' practice.**

## What surprised me

1. **The morphological starvation.** Just one verb-lemma plus one rare noun for 107 occurrences is extraordinary — the Quran refuses to nominalize "discovery." Compare ʿilm (knowledge), which nominalizes everywhere. Finding stays a *verb-act* — never a *thing*.

2. **The "found you" trio about the Prophet (93:6–8)** is conceptually huge: Allah does the wajada-ing of Muhammad — orphan, lost, poor. This is the Quran inverting its own usual subject-position. In *every* other use, humans find or fail to find. Here the divine encounters the human.

3. **8 of 9 taqlid ayahs are Meccan.** This is overwhelmingly a Meccan-period polemic. The argument against inherited religion is concentrated in the period when the Prophet was *himself* breaking from inherited practice. After Hijra, the formula nearly disappears (only 5:104 in Medinan al-Maaida).

4. **Khattab's translation systematically erases the verb** in legal contexts ("lacking the means" instead of "do not find"), masking the conceptual unity of wjd from "finding water for wudu" to "finding Allah Forgiving."

5. **The noun وُجْد at 65:6** ("according to your means") preserves an etymological fossil: "means" = "what you have found at hand." Arabic still encodes the empiricist intuition that resources are *encountered*, not constructed.

## One-sentence summary

**wjd is the Quran's verb of first-hand encounter — the cognitive moment of finding-X-to-be-the-case — and the Quran simultaneously deploys it as its strongest epistemic tool (one finds Allah Forgiving, finds the Lord's promise true, finds the prophet attested in scripture) and as its most precisely targeted critique, demolishing the 9-fold "we found our fathers" defense of inherited religion by insisting that encounter is valid evidence only when it is encounter with reality, not with someone else's prior encounter.**
