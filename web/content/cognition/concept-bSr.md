# Concept profile: ب-ص-ر (`bSr`) — sight / insight / inner vision

> **Source:** Parallel-agent deep-read, deep "research" investigation, 2026-04-26.
> **Data:** `data/concepts/bSr.json` (148 occurrences, 139 ayahs, 62 surahs, 10 lemmas, 109 Meccan / 39 Medinan)
> **POS:** 95 N / 33 V / 20 ADJ — heavily nominal/adjectival.

## The distinctive role of bSr

bSr is the Quran's great **dual-meaning root**: the same triliteral simultaneously names a body organ (`baSar` = eye/eyesight, 48x), a divine attribute (`baSiyr` = the All-Seeing, 51x), and a metaphysical faculty (`baSiyrap` = insight/discernment, 7x). Where most cognition roots in the Quran specialize (`Eql` = ratiocination, `fqh` = comprehension, `Hkm` = wisdom), bSr deliberately *refuses* to specialize. The same word covers the optic nerve and the eye of the heart. This is exactly the raḥm/raḥma pattern (womb / mercy) the project flagged: a single Arabic etymon refusing the physical/spiritual split that English requires translators to enforce on every rendering.

## The blind-seeing inversion pattern

The "blind-seeing" contrast (often paired with "deaf-hearing") is a signature Quranic figure. Among 148 bSr occurrences, **13 ayahs explicitly contain "blind"** in translation, and the root `Emy` (blindness) co-occurs in **11 ayahs** (7.9% of all bSr ayahs — striking for a 148-occurrence root). The signature formula is the rhetorical question: *"Are the blind and the seeing equal?"* — appearing at **11:24, 13:16, 35:19, 40:58** (and modulated at 5:71, 6:50, 6:104, 22:46). Notably the triad blind/deaf/dumb (`Emy`/`Smm`/`bkm`) does NOT appear in any bSr ayah in this dataset — that triad clusters around `smE` instead. bSr's binary is consistently **two-term** (blind ↔ seeing), occasionally extended to a **four-term lattice** at 11:24: blind+deaf vs seeing+hearing. The contrast is rarely about sensory disability per se — it is always a moral or epistemic verdict (35:19 → 35:22: "the dead and the living are not equal").

## Divine attribute al-Basir

Of 51 `baSiyr` occurrences, **~43 are the divine attribute** (Allah is Seeing / He is the All-Seeing); only 4 describe a creature's sight (Joseph 12:93, 12:96; the resurrected blasphemer 20:125; created humanity 76:2) and 4 belong to the blind-seeing contrast formula. The pairing with `samīʿ` (All-Hearing, root `smE`) is the dominant divine collocation: **`smE` co-occurs with bSr in 37 ayahs (26.6%)** — by far the highest cognitive co-occurrence. The phrase **"He is the Hearing, the Seeing" (huwa al-samīʿ al-baSīr)** is a fixed liturgical doublet (4:134, 17:1, 22:61, 22:75, 31:28, 40:20, 42:11, 17:1, 58:1...). Theologically loaded peak: **42:11** — *"There is nothing like unto Him; and He is the Hearing, the Seeing"* — the Quran's strongest tanzīh statement immediately attaches its negation of likeness to two sensory-perceptual divine names. God is unlike anything *and* sees/hears: the apophatic and the perceptual are bound together.

## The "open eyes, closed hearts" structural figure

This is bSr's signature rhetorical inversion. The triplet **`qulūb` (heart) + `samʿ` (hearing) + `abṣār` (sight)** appears as a unit in roughly **14 ayahs** (qlb co-occurs in 14 bSr-ayahs = 10.1%). Two structural variants:

1. **Faculties present, disabled:** 7:179 *"They have hearts with which they do not understand, eyes with which they do not see, ears with which they do not hear"*; 2:7 (sealed hearts, sealed hearing, veiled vision); 6:46; 16:108; 45:23; 46:26.
2. **Locus-shift (the radical inversion):** 22:46 *"It is not the eyes that are blinded, but blinded are the hearts within the breasts"* — the Quran explicitly relocates blindness from the optic organ to the chest cavity. **This is the key verse for the project's thesis that bSr is a metaphysical, not optical, capacity.**

The eschatological mirror: **50:22** — *"We have removed your cover, so your sight today is sharp (baṣaruka al-yawma ḥadīd)"* — physical sight at resurrection becomes hyperacute precisely because it is finally identified with insight.

## Translator divergence

The classic split is on **`baṣīrah`** (insight, 7x). Saheeh and Khattab favor "insight/enlightenment"; Pickthall and Arberry pivot toward "proofs / sure knowledge / clear testimony / examples" — i.e. the *evidentiary* register over the *perceptual* register. **75:14** is the most extreme case: *baṣīratun* about the human soul gets rendered as "witness" (Saheeh, Pickthall), "testify" (Khattab), and "clear proof" (Arberry) — *none* render it as "insight," even though that is the lemma's standard meaning. Arabic lets *baṣīrah* mean the act of seeing, the thing seen, and the proof that compels seeing, all at once; English forces a choice. Even more: at 7:201 `mubṣirūn` ("they see clearly") is translated by Saheeh as **"they have insight"** — the verb of physical sight gets a metaphysical English noun.

## Semantic field (top co-occurring roots)

- **`smE` (hear) — 37 ayahs (26.6%)** — overwhelmingly dominant; the auditory-visual sensorium pair
- **`qlb` (heart) — 14 (10.1%)** — the eyes/hearts inversion field
- **`Elm` (know) — 13 (9.4%)** — ties seeing to knowledge
- **`rAy` (see/think) — 13 (9.4%)** — the *other* root for visual perception
- **`Ayy` (signs) — 12 (8.6%)** — what one is supposed to see
- **`Emy` (blind) — 11 (7.9%)** — the negation
- **`*kr` (remember) — 11 (7.9%)** — seeing → remembering chain
- minimal overlap with `Eql` (1), `fqh` (1), `Hkm` (2), `nZr` (only 4)

## bSr vs nZr

The data confirms the proposed distinction sharply. **nZr is overwhelmingly verbal (107 of 129 = 83%), bSr overwhelmingly nominal (95 of 148 = 64%)**. nZr is the *act* of looking — directional, voluntary, often imperative ("look at how We…"). bSr is a *capacity, an organ, an attribute, a content*. Their co-occurrence is striking: **only 4 ayahs** out of the entire corpus pair them — they are near-disjoint despite being "the two seeing roots." nZr never gets mapped onto God's essential names; there is no *al-Nāẓir*. bSr produces a divine name (al-Baṣīr); nZr produces eschatological waiting (`yantaẓir`). nZr is "where you point your gaze"; bSr is "what kind of perceiver you are."

## What surprised me

1. **The Form IV `mubṣir`/`mubṣirah` reversal.** I expected the causative ("makes one see"), but in 6 of 7 instances it describes **the daytime** ("the day, *making see*" — 10:67, 17:12, 27:86, 40:61) or *visible* signs (17:59, 27:13). The grammar is causative ("sight-giving"); the application is environmental, not pedagogical. The day is itself an epistemic agent.
2. **`baṣīrah` is 7 occurrences but carries enormous theological weight** — including 12:108, where the prophetic mission itself is defined as *daʿwah ʿalā baṣīrah* ("calling on insight") — and 75:14, where the *self* becomes a *baṣīrah* against itself on Judgment Day. The faculty becomes the verdict.
3. **Almost no overlap with `Eql` (reason) or `fqh` (comprehension)** — only 1 ayah each. bSr is *not* part of the discursive cognition cluster. It belongs to a different cognitive mode entirely: direct apprehension, not inference.
4. **6:103** ("vision does not grasp Him; He grasps vision") is a stunning paradox: God is the All-Seeing, yet is precisely the one thing eyesight cannot reach. The root that names His perception negates ours of Him.
5. **Form IV imperatives `abṣir!` (5 occurrences)** are nearly all addressed to the Prophet about the unbelievers' future fate (37:175, 37:179, 19:38, 68:5) — "wait and see." Sight here is eschatological vindication, not present cognition.

## One-sentence summary

**bSr is the Quran's claim that perception itself is theological: the same Arabic faculty names the eye, names the All-Seeing God, and names the inner organ that the unbeliever still possesses but no longer uses — making blindness, in the Quran's vocabulary, never a defect of the eye but a condition of the heart.**
