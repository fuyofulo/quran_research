# Cluster 96 — Dunya vs. Ākhirah

> **Size:** 19 roots | **Source:** Parallel-agent analysis, 2026-04-26
> **Top members:** اخر · حيي · دنو · متع · زين · وذر · غرر · بقي
> **Full data:** `data/full-quran-graph/clusters.json` key "96"

## Cluster name
**الدنيا والآخرة — Dunya vs. Ākhirah: The Deceiving World and the Lasting Hereafter**

## Semantic territory

This cluster is the Qur'an's signature ethical-eschatological dyad: the contrast between *al-ḥayāt al-dunyā* (the lower/nearer life) and *al-ākhirah* (the last, the Hereafter). Almost every member operates inside one of three slots in that contrast: (a) naming the two abodes (اخر, دنو, حيي, بقي), (b) describing the *seductive surface* of dunyā that lures people away from ākhirah (متع, زين, زخرف, غرر, لعب, لهو, خوض, برج), or (c) marking the moral *act of leaving/being left* (وذر, حبس, زحزح). Even the outliers fit: نوق (the she-camel of Thamūd) and قطف (low-hanging fruit) anchor the two endpoints — worldly sign rejected, paradisal reward enjoyed — and رسخ ("firmly rooted in knowledge") names those who are *not* deceived. The cluster is essentially the Qur'an's vocabulary for time-horizon morality.

## Sub-themes

1. **The two abodes named (اخر / دنو / حيي / بقي).** الآخرة and الحياة الدنيا almost always co-occur (Hyy↔dnw: 66 ayahs; Axr↔dnw: 57). بقي ("what remains/abiding") supplies the contrastive predicate — *al-ākhirah khayr wa-abqā* (87:17, 28:60).
2. **The seductive surface of dunyā (زين, زخرف, متع, برج, غرر).** Beautification (زُيِّنَ للناس), gold-plating (زخرف), short-term enjoyment (متاع), conspicuous display (تبرّج), and outright delusion (الغرور). 3:14 and 57:20 are the locus classicus — both packed with this cluster's vocabulary.
3. **Distraction and play (لعب, لهو, خوض).** The rhetorical formula *"the worldly life is nothing but play and amusement"* (6:32, 29:64, 47:36, 57:20) literally is لعب + لهو; خوض ("plunging in idle talk") joins it as the cognitive form of the same vice.
4. **Reprieve, abandonment, deliverance (وذر, حبس, زحزح).** The dunyā is a temporary respite — God *leaves* the deceived to their delusion (ذرني والمكذبين, 73:11), *holds back* punishment briefly (11:8), and the saved are *drawn away* from the Fire (3:185, the famous زُحْزِحَ verse that ties the whole cluster together).
5. **Edge imagery of the two ends (نوق, قطف, رسخ, برج).** Concrete sign-imagery: the Thamūd she-camel (worldly test rejected), low-hanging paradisal fruit (قُطُوفُهَا دَانِيَة, 69:23 — note دنو reused for *nearness* of reward), the zodiacal towers above (برج), and those *firmly rooted* whose hearts cannot be deceived.

## Top 7 structurally important roots

1. **اخر (Axr)** — eschatological pole; co-occurs with Alh (123 ayahs!) — the iman-bi-Allah-wa-l-yawm-il-akhir formula anchors the cluster.
2. **دنو (dnw)** — worldly pole and its semantic twin "nearness" — the explicit antonym slot.
3. **حيي (Hyy)** — supplies *al-ḥayāt* to both poles; structural connector between the two abodes.
4. **متع (mtE)** — defines the dunyā ontologically: *matāʿ qalīl* / *matāʿ al-ghurūr* — short-lived enjoyment.
5. **زين (zyn)** — primary verb of seduction (zuyyina lahum); links to Satan, women/wealth, and the cosmos's adornment.
6. **لعب (lEb) + لهو (lhw)** — together form the canonical dunyā-formula; inseparable rhetorical pair.
7. **غرر (grr)** — names the moral mechanism (delusion) by which dunyā subverts ākhirah; the ethical hinge.

## Surprises

- **دنو does double duty.** It supplies the negative ("worldly, lower") *and* the positive paradisal nearness (قطوفها دانية, 69:23). The same root names both the trap and one of the rewards — a striking semantic reversal the cluster captures.
- **وذر (yadhar) is structurally huge here (45 occurrences, all imperative-leave/forsake).** It is not a "world" word at all in isolation, but the cluster reveals it as the ethical *action verb* paired with the dyad: God leaves the deluded; humans are told to leave the idols/dunyā.
- **برج** appears for *both* cosmic towers (zodiac, 85:1, 15:16) and women's display (33:33). The cluster surfaces this internal pun: adornment of heavens vs. adornment of self.
- **نوق (the she-camel)** showing up looks anomalous, but co-occurs with وذر — every Thamūd narrative says *"leave (ذروا) the she-camel of Allah"* — a worldly *sign* given and rejected, the dunyā-test in narrative form.
- **زحزح** occurs only twice, but one is 3:185, the densest single verse for the whole cluster (death, qiyāmah, fire, paradise, dunyā, matāʿ al-ghurūr). It functions as a cluster-anchor verse despite its rarity.

## Suggested research directions

1. **The rhetorical formula مَا الْحَيَاةُ الدُّنْيَا إِلَّا لَعِبٌ وَلَهْوٌ.** Map every variant (6:32, 29:64, 47:36, 57:20, etc.) and quantify which subset of cluster-96 vocabulary appears in each. Is there a Meccan→Medinan progression in the formula's expansion?
2. **The زُيِّنَ-passive construction.** Who is the implied agent across the corpus — Satan, God, "their deeds," dunyā itself? Cross-reference زين with Iblīs/shayṭān roots to see whether dunyā-seduction is theologically external or internal.
3. **دنو's semantic split.** Build a contrastive concordance of *adnā/dunyā/dāniyah* showing the bifurcation between "worldly" (negative) and "near/low-hanging" (positive paradisal). When does the Qur'an exploit the pun?
4. **Ākhirah-without-dunyā vs. paired occurrences.** اخر appears 250 times but only co-occurs with دنو in 57 ayahs. What does the *unpaired* اخر discourse look like? Does it cluster with judgment/resurrection vocabulary (likely a different cluster) rather than this seduction-cluster?

## One-sentence summary

Cluster 96 is the Qur'an's lexicon of *time-horizon morality* — the dyad of fleeting, beautified, deluding dunyā against the lasting ākhirah, plus the verbs of seduction (زين، متع، غرر), distraction (لعب، لهو، خوض), and the moral act of leaving one for the other (وذر، زحزح).
