# Cluster 19 — Household Law and the Lexicon of Lineage

> **Size:** 40 roots | **Source:** Parallel-agent analysis, 2026-04-26
> **Top members:** ذكر · مثل · ولد · دين · اخو · قرب · زوج · رجل
> **Full data:** `data/full-quran-graph/clusters.json` key "19"

## Cluster name
**Household Law and the Lexicon of Lineage** — kinship, marriage, inheritance, and the legal arithmetic of the family

## Semantic territory

This is the cluster where the Quran legislates the family. It clusters together (a) the kinship ontology — وَلَد، أَخ، زَوْج، اِمْرَأَة، رَجُل، أَنْثَىٰ، ذَكَر، يَتِيم، قُرْبَىٰ — (b) the legal machinery applied to it — وَرَّثَ، تَرَكَ، وَصِيَّة، فَرَضَ، عَدْل، قِسْط، دَيْن — and (c) the numerical apparatus of estate division — نِصْف، ثُلُث، رُبُع، خُمُس، سُدُس، وَاحِد، اثْنَان، حَظّ. The verbs ضَرَبَ ("set forth a parable / strike"), مَثَل ("the like of" — load-bearing in 4:11 "للذكر مثل حظ الأنثيين"), and حَضَرَ ("when death approaches") sit in the same legal ayahs and so are pulled in by raw co-occurrence. Surah-level footprint is exactly what you'd predict: al-Baqara (70), an-Nisāʾ (49), āl ʿImrān (37), al-Māʾida (33). The cluster is strikingly *Madinan-juristic* in flavor.

## Sub-themes

1. **Kin terms and household composition** — وَلَد، أَخ، زَوْج، اِمْرَأَة، رَجُل، أَنْثَىٰ، ذَكَر، يَتِيم. The dual sense of ذكر is real but the cluster placement is overwhelmingly carried by the *male* noun: 17 of 18 *akar ayahs sit inside kinship contexts (Q 3:36, 3:195, 4:11, 4:124, 4:176, 16:97, 42:49–50, 49:13, 53:45, 75:39, 92:3).
2. **Marriage, divorce, the waiting term** — زَوْج، اِمْرَأَة، عِشْرَة، رَبَصَ (the ʿidda waiting), شَهْر (months counted), ثَلَاث (three quroo'), فَرَضَ (the *farīḍa* mahr/decree).
3. **Inheritance and bequests** — وَرَّثَ، تَرَكَ، وَصَّىٰ، حَظّ، نِصْف، ثُلُث، رُبُع، خُمُس، سُدُس، وَاحِد، مَثَل ("the like of two females' share"), دَيْن (in its *debt* sense — Q 4:11–12 explicitly subordinates inheritance to "after a bequest he has bequeathed or a debt").
4. **Justice and equity within the family** — عَدْل، قِسْط (the famous "أَلَّا تُقْسِطُوا فِي الْيَتَامَىٰ" frame), the ethical posture toward parents (Aff = "uff" in 17:23), and toward orphans.
5. **Witnessing and presentation at moments of household transition** — حَضَرَ ("when death is present" in 2:133, 2:180, the bequest moment), شَهْر (sacred months bracketing pilgrimage and ʿidda), جَهَّزَ (Yusuf furnishing his brothers — kin provisioning), فَوْر (the boiling-over of the tannūr that triggers Noah's family embarkation).

## Top 7 structurally important roots

1. **ذكر *kr** (292) — the highest-frequency root in the cluster; bridges "remember/mention" and "male," and 17/18 male-noun uses anchor the inheritance/kinship axis.
2. **مثل mvl** (169) — pivotal because *mithl* is the unit of inheritance arithmetic ("mithl ḥaẓẓ al-unthayayn") AND the parable connector via ضَرَبَ مَثَلًا.
3. **ولد wld** (102) — children, parents, offspring; the genealogical hub.
4. **دين dyn** (101) — straddles "religion" (dīn) and "debt" (dayn); its inclusion is partly driven by Q 2:282 (the longest ayah, the *mudāyana* contract) and the recurring "min baʿdi waṣiyyatin yūṣī bihā aw dayn."
5. **اخو Axw** (96) — siblings; the *kalāla* inheritance case (4:12, 4:176) hinges on it.
6. **قرب qrb** (96) — the *aqrabūn / dhū al-qurbā* (kindred) layer of inheritance and ethical obligation.
7. **زوج zwj** (81) — the spousal axis of marriage, divorce, and the surviving-spouse share.

## Surprises

- **دين (dyn)** lives here, not in a "religion" cluster. The *dayn* sense (debt, Q 2:282, 4:11–12) dominates its co-occurrence behavior even though *dīn* outnumbers *dayn* by 92 to 5.
- **رِجْل/رَجُل (rjl)** is split: the cluster harvests it via the *rijāl* (men, plural) sense; the "feet" sense (15 occurrences) is a hitchhiker.
- **مَثَل (mvl)** is here in its arithmetic-comparative sense ("the like of"), not its parabolic sense, even though parables are the more famous use.
- **ضَرَبَ (Drb)** lands here despite its broad semantic range — pulled in by *ḍaraba mathalan* and likely also Q 4:34.
- **ذكر as "male"** is much more load-bearing than its raw lemma count (18) suggests: nearly every single *akar usage is in a kinship/inheritance ayah.
- **Tiny Joseph-cycle roots (jhz, fwr, gbr)** were absorbed because the Yusuf narrative is a kinship narrative (brothers furnishing, the Noah/Lot wife who "remained behind").
- **كَاهِن (khn, soothsayer)** is the strangest member — it appears in only two ayahs (52:29, 69:42) which happen to share vocabulary with *tadhkira* and *rajul* contexts.
- **قَلَائِد (qld)** sneaks in via the sacrificial-garlands-and-sacred-months legal frame of Q 5:2/5:97.

## Suggested research directions

1. **Jurisprudential ayah graph.** Restrict the corpus to this cluster's union of ayahs and re-run community detection — does the inheritance arithmetic (نِصْف، ثُلُث، رُبُع، سُدُس، حَظّ) split cleanly from the marriage-and-ʿidda arithmetic (شَهْر، رَبَصَ، ثَلَاث)? Surah 4 vs Surah 2 likely separate.
2. **The dīn/dayn collision.** Test whether the cluster co-occurrence specifically tracks *dayn* ayahs (5 of them) or whether *dīn* ayahs are also pulled by their proximity to family-law contexts (apostasy of women, "lā ikrāha fī al-dīn" near kinship verses). A lemma-level rebuild of the graph would resolve this.
3. **The dual-sense behavior of ذكر.** Build a contextual classifier that distinguishes *dhakar* (male) from *dhikr* (remembrance) at the ayah level using the cluster as ground truth, then quantify how many "remember" ayahs nonetheless invoke kinship lexicon (e.g., "remember your brother / your forefathers").
4. **Madinan signature.** This cluster looks Madinan-juristic by surah footprint (2, 4, 5 dominate). Compare cluster-ayah revelation chronology against a Meccan/Madinan label set — does this cluster have the highest Madinan share of any cluster in the graph?

## One-sentence summary

Cluster #19 is the Quran's family-law lexicon — the kin terms, the marriage and divorce machinery, and the fractional arithmetic of inheritance — held together by the dual word ذكر "male/remember" and the connector مثل "the like of."
