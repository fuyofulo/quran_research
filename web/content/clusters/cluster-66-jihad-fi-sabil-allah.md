# Cluster 66 — Jihād fī Sabīl Allāh

> **Size:** 17 roots | **Source:** Parallel-agent analysis, 2026-04-26
> **Top members:** سبل · قتل · نفق · مول · صدد · جهد · هجر · قعد
> **Full data:** `data/full-quran-graph/clusters.json` key "66"
> **Cross-reference:** Confirms the cognition investigation finding that jhd in the Quran is exclusively physical struggle, ZERO cognitive uses. See `notes/cognition/research-negative-space.md`.

## Cluster name
**Jihād fī Sabīl Allāh — Striving, Fighting, and Spending in God's Path** (with the shadow theme: those who *bend, bar, sit out, or are held back* from it)

## Semantic territory

This is the Quran's complete operational vocabulary for armed and economic struggle on behalf of the new Medinan community. It pairs three movements — *go forth* (نفر، هجر), *fight* (قتل، جهد), *spend* (نفق، مول) — along the named axis of *sabīl Allāh* (سبل، 176×, the cluster's keystone). Around this positive core sits an unusually rich negative-space vocabulary: *hindering* (صدد), *bending crooked* (عوج), *sitting back* (قعد، ضجع), *being prevented* (حصر), *averting* (درا), *lying in ambush* (رصد), *causing harm* (اذي) — and the imperative counter-move: *urging* the reluctant to fight (حرض). The Medinan signature is overwhelming: of the ten less-frequent roots I sampled, nine occur first in Medinan surahs, and the lone Meccan outlier (فجج) is a topographic word meaning "mountain pass / highway" — preserving the literal sense of *path*.

## Sub-themes

1. **The Path itself (سبل، عوج، فجج، صدد).** *Sabīl Allāh* is treated almost as physical terrain — a road that can be straight (no عوج, "crookedness") or barred (صدّ عن سبيل الله). فجج, though Meccan and topographic, gets pulled into this cluster because the Quran's metaphor of "path" rests on the literal road network.

2. **The act of fighting (قتل، جهد، حرض، رصد، درا).** قتل is the bare verb of killing/fighting; جهد marks total exertion (and the data confirms: physical, never cognitive); حرض is the prophet's task of *urging* the believers into battle (4:84, 8:65); رصد is the tactical vocabulary of ambush stations (9:5, 9:107); درا is the rhetorical "avert death from yourselves if you can" (3:168).

3. **Wealth as weapon (نفق، مول).** The Medinan synthesis insists striving is *bi-amwālihim wa-anfusihim* — "with their wealth and their selves." نفق ("spending") and مول ("wealth") cluster here not because they are economic terms in general, but because in this Medinan grammar they are operationally fused with قتل and جهد.

4. **Mobilization vs. sitting out (نفر، هجر، قعد، ضجع، حصر).** The cluster contains a built-in moral binary: those who *march out* (نفر) and *emigrate* (هجر) versus those who *sit at home* (قعد) on their *beds* (مضاجع — ضجع, 4:34's marital "beds" co-located morphologically with 3:154's "places where they would have lain dead"). حصر sits ambiguously between "legitimately prevented" (2:196 ill or hindered pilgrim) and "restrained for God's cause" (2:273 the muhājirūn-poor).

5. **Persecution as legitimating cause (صدد، اذي).** The cluster encodes the Quran's casus belli logic: enemy *barring* from the sacred mosque (صدّ) and *harming* believers (اذي) is what reframes fighting as defensive/just (2:217 is the locus classicus, where qtl, sbl, Sdd, and kfr all co-occur).

## Top 7 structurally important roots

1. **سبل (sbl, 176)** — the cluster's gravitational center; the directional preposition that makes all other actions *count* as religious.
2. **قتل (qtl, 170)** — the bare verb of fight/kill; co-edges with almost every other member.
3. **نفق (nfq, 111)** — proves wealth-spending is structurally inseparable from fighting in the Medinan worldview.
4. **مول (mwl, 86)** — the substance that نفق acts on; bridges this cluster to economic/inheritance vocabulary elsewhere.
5. **صدد (Sdd, 81)** — the antagonist's verb; without it the cluster would lack its justifying foil.
6. **جهد (jhd, 41)** — the abstract noun the cluster eventually crystallizes into ("jihād").
7. **هجر (hjr, 31)** — the founding act (the Hijra) that creates the muhājirūn category to which this whole vocabulary first applies.

## Surprises

- **ضجع (DjE, only 3×) lands here at all.** Two of its three uses are 4:34's marital "beds" — yet it co-clusters with قتل/قعد because of 3:154's "places where they would have lain (dead)" at Uhud. The algorithm picked up a martial reading of an apparently domestic root.
- **درا (drA, 5×).** Outside this cluster you would expect "avert/repel" to land in a juridical or evidentiary cluster (it does mean "avert" the *ḥadd* punishment in later fiqh) — but in the Quran itself it lives entirely inside battle discourse (3:168, 24:8 the liʿān aside).
- **فجج (fjj)** — a Meccan topographic word for "mountain passes" gets absorbed into a Medinan war cluster, suggesting the *sabīl* metaphor reaches back to retroactively recolor literal road vocabulary.
- **حرض confirms the Prophet has a distinct verb for "rallying troops"** — separate from the general دعو ("calling"). It only appears twice (4:84, 8:65) and both are explicit battlefield exhortations.
- **The cluster has no Hereafter-reward vocabulary** (no جنة، شهد، أجر as core members despite obvious thematic adjacency). The reward layer apparently lives in a *neighboring* cluster, and Louvain correctly separates the *operational* from the *eschatological* register.

## Suggested research directions

1. **Map the "negative-space" subcluster (Sdd, Ewj, qEd, DjE, HSr, drA) as a sub-graph.** These roots are *defined relationally* to the positive jihad vocabulary; quantifying their internal modularity would test whether the Quran has a coherent "anti-jihad" lexicon or just scattered antonyms.
2. **Compute the Meccan/Medinan ratio per root in this cluster vs. the whole corpus.** Hypothesis: this cluster will show the strongest Medinan skew of any in the graph — quantifying it would validate the tradition's chronological ordering from purely lexical evidence.
3. **Trace the bi-amwāl-wa-anfus formula.** Co-occurrence of nfq+mwl+nfs (self) within single ayat: where does the triplet appear, and does نفس (self) actually live in this cluster or in a neighbor — a test of whether "wealth and selves" is a fixed Medinan formula or a cluster-internal pattern.
4. **Compare this cluster's edges to the *hidāya/ḍalāl* (guidance/straying) cluster.** The road metaphor (sbl, Ewj, fjj) bridges both. Identifying the *boundary roots* that bridge "fighting on the path" with "guidance along the path" would expose how the Quran fuses ethical and martial road-imagery.

## One-sentence summary

Cluster #66 is the Quran's complete Medinan grammar of *jihād fī sabīl Allāh* — a tightly coupled lexicon where going-forth, fighting, and spending wealth are inseparable, and which carries its own internal antagonist vocabulary of barring, bending, sitting out, and being held back.
