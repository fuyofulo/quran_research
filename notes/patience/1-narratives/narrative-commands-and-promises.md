# Commands and promises: what comes with "be patient"

## Headline finding

**Patience is almost never commanded bare. Of the 20 commands to the Prophet, 18 carry an act or a reassurance in the same ayah (13 an act, 11 a reassurance, 6 both); only 70:5 and 74:7 are a single clause, and 70:6-7 supplies a reassurance next door.** The reassurance is about God's nearness and the truth of His promise, never a dated outcome. The promises to the patient are mostly worldly and collective: God's company, help, inheritance, leadership. The individual's are final: the chamber, the garden, reward "without account". **No promise of a specific worldly outcome to an individual, and no duration, exists anywhere.** The only inner-state promise is *that you may be satisfied* (20:130).

[interpretive] The commands are rarely "endure"; they are "do this *meanwhile*". 38:17 sets a person beside the command (David), 68:48 a warning (Jonah).

## 2. The community

Data-grounded.

- **2:45, 2:153:** *seek help through patience and prayer*. Patience is an instrument for asking help (7:128 Moses: "seek help through God and be patient"). 2:153 follows with *God is with the patient*.
- **2:155-157:** a test (fear, hunger, loss), then *give good news to the patient*, then their act: when disaster strikes they say *innā lillāhi wa innā ilayhi rājiʿūn*. The sentence is the speech, not silence. Then blessings, mercy, guidance.
- **3:186, 3:200:** patience with fear of God ("of the matters of resolve"); *iṣbirū wa ṣābirū wa rābiṭū wa ttaqū*, endure, outlast, stay at the post, be mindful.
- **Fighting:** 3:146-147 they "did not weaken"; their words were "forgive our sins, plant our feet". 8:45-46 stand firm, remember God much, obey, do not dispute, *be patient*; 8:65-66 twenty overcome two hundred. Discipline: obedience, unity, remembrance.
- **16:126:** retaliate in kind, *but if you are patient it is better for the patient*. Patience is the option beside lawful retaliation.
- **90:17, 103:3:** *tawāṣaw bi-l-ṣabr*, advise one another to patience, together with truth (103) and compassion (90), after freeing a slave and feeding the hungry.

**What the community does, not only bears:** seeks help; says a sentence; fears God; holds a post; stays united; remembers God; forgives; feeds; advises one another. Patience is communal in 103:3 and 90:17: it is *passed between people*. [interpretive] It is not carried alone.

## 4. Time

Data-grounded.

- ***ḥattā*** occurs in 5 ṣ-b-r ayahs: 7:87 and 10:109 (*until God judges*), 6:34 (*until Our victory came*), 49:5 (*until you came out to them*, the believers' impatience at the door), 47:31 (*until We make evident* the patient). In each, the end is **an act of God or of another person, not a time**.
- **40:77** refuses the question: *whether We show you some of what We promise or take you in death, to Us they return.* The patient may die before the thing arrives.
- **46:35** reverses the sense: on the Day they will feel they stayed *an hour of a day* (*sāʿatan min nahār*). The only measure in the corpus' ṣ-b-r ayahs is the retrospective shrinking of the wait.
- **Script over all 93 ṣ-b-r ayahs:** no year (*sana, ʿām*) and no duration word attached to patience. Time roots present: *ḥīn* (2:177, 25:42, 52:48), *sāʿa* and *labitha* (46:35), *yawm*.

> **[6:34]** وَلَقَدۡ كُذِّبَتۡ رُسُلࣱ مِّن قَبۡلِكَ فَصَبَرُواْ عَلَىٰ مَا كُذِّبُواْ وَأُوذُواْ حَتَّىٰٓ أَتَىٰهُمۡ نَصۡرُنَاۚ وَلَا مُبَدِّلَ لِكَلِمَٰتِ ٱللَّهِۚ وَلَقَدۡ جَآءَكَ مِن نَّبَإِيْ ٱلۡمُرۡسَلِينَ
> *Saheeh:* And certainly were messengers denied before you, but they were patient over [the effects of] denial, and they were harmed until Our victory came to them. And none can alter the words of Allah. And there has certainly come to you some information about the [previous] messengers.

[interpretive] In place of a deadline the text gives God's nearness.

## 5. Negative space

Data-grounded unless marked.

- **A command with no reason or promise in reach:** only 74:7 comes close, and its reason is *for your Lord*. 70:5 has none in the ayah, but 70:7 next door. 38:17, 50:39, 68:48, 73:10, 76:24 have an act and no promise to the Prophet; 73:11 and 68:49-50 do the reassuring (*leave Me with the deniers*; Jonah rescued).
- **A command not to feel:** yes, two. 16:127 (*do not grieve ... do not be in distress*) and 30:60 (*let them not unsettle you*). Both are coupled with a reason (*your patience is through God*; *the promise is true*). Neither says "do not feel pain". 16:127 is the only ṣ-b-r ayah with grief. 12:84 and 21:83 show patient people grieve and cry out: neighbours, not the same ayah.
- **A promise of a specific worldly outcome to an individual for patience:** none. Job (21:84, 38:42-43) is healed, but after his call, and 38:44 *found him patient* comes after the healing in the text. 12:90 (Joseph) gives the reward of doers of good, without a deliverance. The Prophet's own: *the outcome is for the mindful* (11:49), *you may be satisfied* (20:130), *best of judges* (10:109).

## For the practice guide

- The Quran's own order is *be patient, and then do something*: praise at dawn and dusk, pray, ask forgiveness, remember David, avoid graciously.
- Lines to hold: *you are in Our eyes* (52:48); *your patience is only through God* (16:127).
- The Quran never says how long; 40:77 allows that the wait may outlast a life.
- Patience is shared: *advise one another to patience* (103:3, 90:17).
- No individual is promised a particular worldly result; the personal reward (the chamber, the garden, no account) is final.
