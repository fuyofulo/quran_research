# What patience is and is not (Phase 2, cross-cutting)

## Headline finding

**In the Quran, patience means staying turned toward God while a hard thing lasts, and acting meanwhile. It is not freedom from grief, and it is not silence.** The verb "to complain" occurs twice, both times to God (12:86, 58:1). All 8 "do not grieve" commands to the Prophet concern the deniers, and God first says "We know it grieves you" (6:33). Patience excludes despair of God, panic, haste and flight in anger. Passive patience appears only among the damned. Where patience means holding back, what it holds back is permitted. "Patience from sin" has no instance.

Data-grounded unless marked [interpretive].

## 1. Grief and complaint

**Grief** shares an ayah with ṣ-b-r once (16:127). Jacob's *ṣabr jamīl* (12:83) and whitened eyes (12:84) are neighbours. **14 ayahs forbid grief** (a grief verb after *lā*):
- The 8 to the Prophet each name what he grieves over: *them*, *their speech*, *his unbelief*, *those who hasten into unbelief* (3:176, 5:41, 10:65, 15:88, 16:127, 27:70, 31:23, 36:76). None concerns a loss of his own.
- The other 6 give a reason in the same ayah (3:139, 9:40, 19:24, 28:7, 29:33, 41:30).
- Only his sons tell Jacob to stop (12:85).

> **[6:33]** قَدۡ نَعۡلَمُ إِنَّهُۥ لَيَحۡزُنُكَ ٱلَّذِي يَقُولُونَۖ فَإِنَّهُمۡ لَا يُكَذِّبُونَكَ وَلَٰكِنَّ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ بِـَٔايَٰتِ ٱللَّهِ يَجۡحَدُونَ
> Saheeh: We know that you, [O Muhammad], are saddened by what they say. And indeed, they do not call you untruthful, but it is the verses of Allah that the wrongdoers reject.

6:34 then points to the patient messengers. [interpretive] Two griefs: Jacob's for a loss, recorded without rebuke; the Prophet's for the deniers' refusal, acknowledged, given a reason, and lifted. Neither is called a failure of patience.

**Complaint.** Root `$kw` has 3 tokens; *mishkāt* (24:35) is a homonym. The two verbs are *innamā ashkū baththī wa-ḥuznī ilā llāh*, "I complain of my anguish and grief only to God" (12:86), and *tashtakī ilā llāh* (58:1). God answers both, by knowing and by hearing. Job's two cries go *to his Lord* (21:83, 38:41); Jacob first *turned away from them* (12:84). The text never forbids complaint, nor uses the verb for complaint to people. In 58:1 the woman argues with the Prophet *and* complains to God, and *God hears the exchange of you both*. [interpretive] The line is the address, not the voice.

**Excluded:**
- **Despair *of God's relief or mercy*** (12:87, 15:56, 29:23, 39:53), or as the reflex when harm touches (11:9, 17:83, 30:36, 41:49); 11:11 makes *the patient* the exception. Exhaustion is not blamed: at 12:110 the messengers *istayʾasa* (object unnamed), and help came.
- **Panic.** *Jazūʿ* (70:20) never stands in a praised ayah.
- **Acting on anger.** Jonah *went off angry* (21:87) and *fled* (37:140), set against *be patient for your Lord's judgment* (68:48).
- **Haste** (46:35, 49:5).

## 2. Knowledge

18:68 is the only ayah naming a condition for patience: *khubr*, knowledge that takes the matter in. In the 9 *ʿilm* pairings God is the knower in 5 (3:142, 4:25, 8:66, 12:83, 47:31); in 3:142 and 47:31 the test exists *so that God makes evident* the patient. Jacob says *I know from God what you do not know* (12:86) and repeats it at the reunion (12:96).

**Ruling.** The patient are never told the end (40:77). [interpretive] Patience is tied to knowing *who* knows, not what will happen: Moses, without reasons, fails (18:68-78); Jacob, with knowledge *from God*, holds. What steadies it is knowledge of God and of past outcomes: Noah's story, then *be patient* (11:49); stories that *make your heart firm* (11:120).

## 3. Time

No duration word occurs in the 93 ayahs. *Ḥattā* occurs 5 times: 4 end on an act of God (6:34, 7:87, 10:109, 47:31), and 49:5 ends on the Prophet coming out. 40:77 says *whether We show you some of it or take you in death*: the wait may outlast the person.

> **[70:6-7]** إِنَّهُمۡ يَرَوۡنَهُۥ بَعِيدࣰ ا وَنَرَىٰهُ قَرِيبࣰ ا
> Saheeh: Indeed, they see it [as] distant, But We see it [as] near.

In 2:214 (no ṣ-b-r; the same *baʾsāʾ* and *ḍarrāʾ* as 2:177) the messenger himself asks *when is God's help?*, and is answered *near*, not rebuked. The only measure is retrospective: *an hour of a day* (46:35).

**The only clock in the patience ayahs is worship's.** Five name hours by root (3:17, 18:28, 20:130, 40:55, 50:39), and 52:48 adds *when you rise*; every one is an hour of prayer or praise. [interpretive] The wait is long from inside and near from God's side: never dated, filled with praise, its end God's.

## 4. Action

- **Battle.** The fighters are the patient (3:146). 8:45-46: stand firm, remember, obey, do not dispute, *be patient*. 2:249-250: they cross, ask, and fight.
- ***Ribāṭ*.** 3:200 stacks *iṣbirū, ṣābirū, rābiṭū*: endure, outlast, hold the post.
- **Restraint.** Three ayahs say being patient *is better* (4:25, 16:126, 49:5). Each holds back something the passage permits or forgives: lawful retaliation, a lawful marriage, calling at the door. 42:41 allows retaliation *without blame*; 42:43 joins patience *with forgiving*. *Yadraʾūna bi-l-ḥasanati s-sayyiʾa*, "they avert evil with good", occurs only twice, both in patience ayahs (13:22, 28:54), and 41:34-35 command it.
- **Searching.** Jacob: *go and search* (12:87). The barest command, *for your Lord be patient* (74:7), closes six commands to act (74:2-7).

**Ever passive?** Only among the damned (14:21, 41:24, 52:16: *be patient or not, it is the same*). [interpretive] Patience that does nothing and changes nothing belongs to the damned.

## 5. Thanks

*Ṣabbārin shakūr* ×4: the days of God, torment and rescue (14:5-6); ships running by God's favour (31:31); Sabaʾ's gardens ruined (34:19); the wind stilled (42:33). Each setting turns between gift and loss; three have *kufr* within two ayahs (14:7, 31:32, 34:17). 31:32 mirrors it: *every khattārin kafūr*, "treacherous ingrate", same patterns, opposite sense. [interpretive] The *ṣabbār shakūr* reads the signs in both turns: patient when the wind stops, thankful when the ship runs.

**Read (a).** The gratitude study found that first-person thanks asks to be *enabled* (27:19, 46:15) and never shares a verse with seeking forgiveness. Patience matches the first (asked for, 2:250, 7:126; never claimed by anyone praised, §11) and differs on the second (*istighfār* at 3:17, 40:55). God bears *Shakūr* as a Name, no ṣ-b-r word.

## 6. Worship

*Seek help through patience and prayer* (2:45, 2:153); 2:153 ends *God is with the patient*, not "with those who pray". All four glorify-commands (20:130, 40:55, 50:39, 52:48) carry hours (§3). *Laʿallaka tarḍā* (20:130), the only *r-ḍ-w* in the 93 ayahs, closes the list of hours of praise: the one inner state promised comes at the end of praise. Form VIII *iṣṭabir* is for worship (19:65), prayer (20:132) and watching a trial (54:27). [interpretive] Worship needs patience, and carries it.

## 7. Turning back

By script, `Awb` shares an ayah with ṣ-b-r only at **38:17 and 38:44**, both in Ṣād; `nwb` and `twb` never; `rjʿ` only at 40:77, the deniers' return.
- 38:17: *be patient… remember Our servant David… he was awwāb*.
- *Niʿma l-ʿabd innahu awwāb* occurs exactly twice: Solomon, given abundance (38:30), and Job, given harm (38:44). Only Job is also *ṣābir*.
- *To Him we return* (2:156) is the patient's speech, one ayah after 2:155.

[interpretive] The co-occurrence is thin; the relation is direction. Patience faces God (13:22, 74:7); turning back renews that facing, in abundance or harm.

## 8. Given, not made

God is the subject of 0 of 103 tokens; patience is commanded 29 times, always to a human. Only 16:127 names a source: *wa-mā ṣabruka illā bi-llāh*, "your patience is only through God".
- *Pour* occurs only as a request (2:250, 7:126); the text never reports God pouring it.
- What 28:80 and 41:35 grant (*mā yulaqqāhā illā*) is not patience: *-hā* is feminine, *ṣabr* masculine (*ṣabrun jamīl*, 12:18). In 41:35 it is 41:34's *allatī hiya aḥsan*; in 28:80 it is unnamed. The patient receive.

[interpretive] You do it, and it is only through God.

## 9. The three kinds of patience

Read (b), line 164, gives *"Three sub-senses: patience under affliction, patience from sin, patience in obedience"* as the Quran's own, uncited.

**Ruling.** Affliction is well supported (2:155-156, 2:177, 3:186, 22:35, 31:17). Obedience rests on three ayahs (19:65, 20:132, 18:28). **From sin: no instance.**
- No token takes *ʿan* (0 of 103).
- 12:23-34 has no ṣ-b-r. Joseph refuses by refuge (12:23), his Lord's proof (12:24), *istaʿṣama* (12:32) and prayer; God turns the evil away (*ṣ-r-f*, 12:24, 33, 34). *Aṣbu* (12:33) is root *ṣ-b-w*, "incline", a lookalike.
- 12:90 *man yattaqi wa-yaṣbir* is general. The word that keeps from sin is *taqwā*, paired with patience in 10 ayahs. 18:28 and 76:24 set a prohibition beside the command, in its own clause.
- 4:25, the nearest candidate: translators render *ʿanat* "sin", but the root's other four uses mean hardship (2:220, 3:118, 9:128, 49:7), and what is refrained from is a permitted marriage.

It misses the text's own kinds: over people's words, for God's judgment, over what one cannot take in (18:68), in battle.

**Cross-investigation correction (CORRECTED).** In `notes/success/concept-success-deep.md` line 164, replace "Three sub-senses: patience under affliction, patience from sin, patience in obedience." with:

> "The Quran does not divide patience into kinds; the scheme (under affliction, from sin, in obedience) is tradition. Affliction is well attested (2:155-157, 2:177, 31:17) and obedience appears in three ayahs (19:65, 20:132, 18:28). 'From sin' has no instance: no ṣ-b-r token takes *ʿan*, Joseph's refusal (12:23-34) uses refuge, not ṣ-b-r, and where patience is restraint it holds back something permitted (4:25, 16:126, 42:43, 49:5)."

Also mark "the inner stance that keeps prayer meaningful" [interpretive]: 2:45 and 2:153 say only *seek help through patience and prayer*.

## 10. What patience is NOT

1. **Not silence:** *to Him we return* (2:156); Job calls (21:83); Jacob complains to God (12:86).
2. **Not the absence of grief:** Jacob's eyes whiten (12:84); *We know it grieves you* (6:33).
3. **Not passivity:** it fights, holds a post, forgives, repels with good, searches.
4. **Not a virtue in itself:** 7 misdirected ayahs; the deniers were "patient" over their gods (25:42).
5. **Not self-made:** *only through God* (16:127).
6. **Not timed:** the wait may outlast a life (40:77).
7. **Not a worldly guarantee for one person:** worldly promises go to groups.
8. **Not restraint from sin:** that is *taqwā*.
9. **Not despair of God, panic, haste or angry flight.**
10. **Not a self-description:** no one praised says "I was patient".

## For the practice guide

- Grief is not a failure of patience: Jacob grieved until his eyes whitened; "We know it grieves you" (6:33).
- Complain, to God: "I complain of my grief only to God" (12:86).
- Patience refuses despair *of God*, panic and acting in anger, not feeling.
- No date: "They see it far, We see it near" (70:6-7). The hours are filled with praise.
- Patience keeps doing: searching, praying, forgiving, holding the post.
- It is given: "your patience is only through God" (16:127). Ask: "pour patience upon us" (2:250).
