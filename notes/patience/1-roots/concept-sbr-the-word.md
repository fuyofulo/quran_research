# Ṣ-b-r, the word: forms, objects, neighbours

## Headline finding

**Patience in the Quran is a verb first (62 of 103 tokens; 29 are commands), and it is a verb of staying: it is followed by *ʿalā* "over/on", *li-* "for", *ḥattā* "until", *maʿa* "with", never by *ʿan* "away from" (0 of 103).** Its object is what separates praise from blame: seven ayahs record patience the text does not praise (a food, gods, the Fire). The nearest words each part from it at one point: panic and despair never share its ayah, haste does once, and swallowed grief is described but never called patience. No ayah defines it, and none tells anyone to stop complaining. It is also *given*: God is never its subject, yet it is poured and granted.

## 1. Lemmas and forms (data-grounded)

| Lemma | Tokens | Note |
|---|---|---|
| *ṣabara* | 58 | verb, Form I |
| *ṣābir* / *ṣābira* / *ṣābirāt* | 22 | participle: the persons (Job, 38:44, is *ṣābir*) |
| *ṣabr* | 15 | noun |
| *ṣabbār* | 4 | intensive |
| *iṣṭabara* | 3 | Form VIII |
| *ṣābirū* | 1 | Form III |

**Why verbs dominate.** 62 verbs = 29 imperatives + 22 perfects ("for what they endured", "the messengers were patient", 6:34) + 11 imperfects (conditions and denials: "if you are patient", "you cannot be patient"). The text commands and reports patience far more than it describes a trait. The noun *ṣabr* (15) is rarely owned: it is sought-help-by (2:45, 2:153), poured (2:250, 7:126), advised (90:17, 103:3), called *jamīl* (12:18, 12:83, 70:5), or *not manageable* (18:67, 72, 75, 78, 82, *istaṭāʿa ṣabran*, five times). Only 16:127 says "your *ṣabr*".

- **Form III, *ṣābirū* (3:200).** Its only token, stacked with Form I *iṣbirū* and *rābiṭū* (see §4). Plural, to the believers. Translators read it as "vie/outdo in patience" (Arberry, Pickthall). [interpretive: the Form III pattern is reciprocal or contesting; the text names no rival.]
- **Form VIII, *iṣṭabir* (19:65, 20:132, 54:27).** 3 of 3 are imperatives to one person, and all are Meccan: in worship, in prayer, and watching Ṣāliḥ's people's trial. The object is a practice in 19:65 and 20:132; in 54:27 it is "watch them and *iṣṭabir*". [interpretive: sustained effort, an extra step beyond *iṣbir*.]
- **Intensive *ṣabbār*** is always *li-kulli ṣabbārin shakūr* (14:5, 31:31, 34:19, 42:33), and always about those who read signs. It is a disposition, never said of a named person. Patience is listed before thanks every time.
- ***Ṣabr jamīl*.** Three tokens, none with a verb; each stands beside someone else's wrong or taunt: the brothers' lie (12:18, 12:83), the mockers of 70:1-2. *Jamīl* also modifies leaving (73:10), pardon (15:85) and release (33:28, 33:49): seven ayahs, all about conduct toward others.

**Is "holding/binding" visible?** Not as holding *back*. No token is followed by *ʿan*. What the text shows is staying *on* or *with*: "never endure *one food*" (2:61), "we stayed with our gods" (25:42), "be patient over your gods" (38:6), "how patient they are for the Fire" (2:175). Only 18:28 gives the verb a direct object, and there it is the self:

> وَٱصۡبِرۡ نَفۡسَكَ مَعَ ٱلَّذِينَ يَدۡعُونَ رَبَّهُم بِٱلۡغَدَوٰةِ وَٱلۡعَشِيِّ يُرِيدُونَ وَجۡهَهُۥۖ وَلَا تَعۡدُ عَيۡنَاكَ عَنۡهُمۡ تُرِيدُ زِينَةَ ٱلۡحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنۡيَاۖ وَلَا تُطِعۡ مَنۡ أَغۡفَلۡنَا قَلۡبَهُۥ عَن ذِكۡرِنَا وَٱتَّبَعَ هَوَىٰهُ وَكَانَ أَمۡرُهُۥ فُرُطࣰ ا

*Iṣbir nafsaka maʿa*: "hold yourself with" those who call on their Lord. [interpretive: the picture is of keeping a self in place, not of clamping it.] I use no dictionary here.

## 2. What patience takes (data-grounded)

Beyond the 80 or so tokens with no object:

- ***ʿalā*, "over" (14).** Ten are praised and four are not (2:61, 2:175, 25:42, 38:6). Kinds, in the text's words: **what they say** (20:130, 38:17, 50:39, 73:10); **what befalls you** (*mā aṣābaka/aṣābahum*, 31:17, 22:35); **being denied and harmed** (6:34, 14:12: *mā ādhaytumūnā*); **prayer** (20:132); **what you have not encompassed in *khubr*** (18:68). Mostly other people's speech and conduct, then fate, then worship, then not understanding. Few name a pain; most name a person's words or deeds.
- ***li-*, "for" (5 with 74:7).** The Lord's *ḥukm* three times (52:48, 68:48, 76:24), His worship (19:65), "for your Lord" (74:7).
- ***ḥattā*, "until" (3).** Until God judges (7:87, 10:109); until you come out to them (49:5). With *li-*, five of eight time-or-reason phrases end in a *ḥukm*.
- ***maʿa*, "with" (18:28).** The company is the callers on their Lord; and God is *maʿa* the patient (2:153, 2:249, 8:46, 8:66). [interpretive: staying with them is staying within His company.]
- ***Fī*, "in" (2:177):** *fī l-baʾsāʾi wa-l-ḍarrāʾi wa-ḥīna l-baʾs*: hardship, harm, "the time of battle". Here patience is one clause among faith, charity, prayer and keeping oaths.

## 3. Misdirected patience (data-grounded, then interpretive)

The seven: food (2:61), the Fire (2:175), Hell's indifference (14:21), gods (25:42, 38:6), the Fire again (41:24), "it is the same for you" (52:16). In each, patience is staying power attached to the wrong thing, or staying power that changes nothing. **What makes it praiseworthy, in the text's words:** it is *ibtighāʾa wajhi rabbihim* (13:22) and *li-rabbika* (74:7).

> وَٱلَّذِينَ صَبَرُواْ ٱبۡتِغَآءَ وَجۡهِ رَبِّهِمۡ وَأَقَامُواْ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَأَنفَقُواْ مِمَّا رَزَقۡنَٰهُمۡ سِرࣰّ ا وَعَلَانِيَةࣰ وَيَدۡرَءُونَ بِٱلۡحَسَنَةِ ٱلسَّيِّئَةَ أُوْلَٰٓئِكَ لَهُمۡ عُقۡبَى ٱلدَّارِ

[interpretive] Patience is not weighed by how much it costs but by where it faces. This undercuts any definition of patience as endurance alone. A reading that "patience is the virtue" stalls on 25:42, where the deniers use the same verb as the prophets.

## 4. The neighbours, and where each parts from patience

Shared ayahs with ṣ-b-r, by script: *yaʾs* 0, *qunūṭ* 0, *halūʿ* 0, *ḥilm* 0, *jazaʿ* 1, *ʿajal* 1, *wahn* 1, *thabāt* 1, *ribāṭ* 1, *kaẓm* 1, *ḥuzn* 1.

- **Panic.** *Jazūʿ* is the human condition when harm touches (70:19-21: *halūʿ, jazūʿ, manūʿ*). It meets patience once, in Hell, as a pair that cancels: *ajazʿnā am ṣabarnā* (14:21). Patience-or-panic matters only if the object is right.
- **Haste.** *Insān* is *ʿajūl* (17:11) and made of *ʿajal* (21:37). The command in 46:35 is *iṣbir … wa-lā tastaʿjil lahum*; 20:114 and 75:16 tell the Prophet not to hurry the *Qurʾān* itself. Haste is wanting the outcome now. [interpretive: the "until" phrases are its opposite.]
- **Weakening.** 3:146 defines the patient by three negations: *mā wahanū, mā ḍaʿufū, mā istakānū* (did not weaken, did not become feeble, did not submit), then "God loves the *ṣābirīn*". 3:139 pairs *lā tahinū* with *lā taḥzanū* but has no *ṣbr* word. In 8:46 loss of nerve (*fa-tafshalū*) is what patience prevents. Patience here includes fighting: the fighters of 3:146 are the patient.
- **Standing firm.** *Thabāt* is the feet; patience is poured on. *Afrigh ʿalaynā ṣabran wa-thabbit aqdāmanā* (2:250). In 8:45 *fa-thbutū* comes first, and patience is commanded in 8:46. [interpretive: firmness is what is seen; patience, what is supplied.]
- ***Ribāṭ*.** *Rābiṭū* (3:200) is a post held. Elsewhere the root is "binding": God binds hearts (8:11, 18:14, 28:10, three of five tokens). The binding sense lives there, with God as subject.

> يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ ٱصۡبِرُواْ وَصَابِرُواْ وَرَابِطُواْ وَٱتَّقُواْ ٱللَّهَ لَعَلَّكُمۡ تُفۡلِحُونَ

- **Swallowing (*kaẓm*), 6 tokens.** *Kāẓimīna l-ghayẓ* is praised (3:134, among the loved *muḥsinīn*). Jacob is *kaẓīm* in 12:84, described, not praised, and he then speaks: *ashkū baththī wa-ḥuznī ilā llāh* (12:86). Twice it marks a father hiding shame (16:58, 43:17); in 40:18 hearts at the throat; in 68:48 it is Jonah, the figure not to resemble. So **both praised and blamed**, by what is swallowed. It is never labelled *ṣabr*.

> وَتَوَلَّىٰ عَنۡهُمۡ وَقَالَ يَٰٓأَسَفَىٰ عَلَىٰ يُوسُفَ وَٱبۡيَضَّتۡ عَيۡنَاهُ مِنَ ٱلۡحُزۡنِ فَهُوَ كَظِيمࣱ

- **Despair.** *Yaʾs* and *qunūṭ* (19 tokens in 16 ayahs) never touch patience. But 11:9-11 are one unit: the man tasting mercy then losing it is *laya'ūs kafūr* (11:9); tasting favour after hardship he is *farih fakhūr* (11:10); then **11:11, *illā lladhīna ṣabarū*.** Patience is the exception to both despair and exultation.

> وَلَئِنۡ أَذَقۡنَا ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ مِنَّا رَحۡمَةࣰ ثُمَّ نَزَعۡنَٰهَا مِنۡهُ إِنَّهُۥ لَيَـُٔوسࣱ كَفُورࣱ
> إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ صَبَرُواْ وَعَمِلُواْ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ أُوْلَٰٓئِكَ لَهُم مَّغۡفِرَةࣱ وَأَجۡرࣱ كَبِيرࣱ

103:2-3 repeats the shape: man in loss, *illā* those who believed, did good, and advised patience; 70:22 gives a different exception (prayer). Jacob stands at the point: *ṣabr jamīl* (12:83), tears and complaint (12:84-86), then "do not despair" (12:87). Adjacent, not co-occurring.

## 5. Pairings (data-grounded, same ayah)

Faith 13, taqwā 10, forgiveness 9, knowledge 9, reward 8, prayer 7, judgment 6, truth 5, mercy 5, reliance 3, thanks 4. **Most frequent: faith (13),** which only says patience belongs to believers' lists; taqwā (10) is next, usually as a condition ("if you are patient and fear God", 3:120, 3:186, 12:90). **Most telling [interpretive]:** *tawāṣaw* (90:17, 103:3): the only places patience is passed between people, paired with truth and with mercy. Next, knowledge: in five of nine ayahs it is God who knows (3:142, 4:25, 8:66, 12:83, 47:31), 11:49 and 18:68 turn on what the human did not know. Reliance: all three are *ṣabarū wa-ʿalā rabbihim yatawakkalūn* (14:12, 16:42, 29:59): trust comes after patience. Prayer: *istaʿīnū bi-ṣ-ṣabr wa-ṣ-ṣalāh* (2:45, 2:153) is help *through* the pair.

## 6. Negative space (data-grounded)

- **God is never the subject** of any of the 103 tokens, and *al-Ṣabūr* is not a Quranic name. But patience is *given*: *afrigh ʿalaynā* (2:250, 7:126, requests); *mā yulaqqāhā illā* (passive, 28:80, 41:35); "your *ṣabr* is only through God" (16:127).
- **No definition.** The 93 ayahs hold objects, formulas and persons, not "ṣabr is …".
- **Three kinds (affliction, from sin, in obedience).** *Affliction*: strongly supported (2:155, 2:177, 3:186, 22:35, 31:17). *Obedience*: supported by 19:65, 20:132 and 18:28, but those are three ayahs, not a category. *From sin*: **absent**; there is no *ṣabr ʿan*, and 12:90 only pairs *yattaqi wa-yaṣbir* without naming a temptation. The scheme is a later classification [tradition]. The text adds kinds the scheme lacks: restraint from retaliation (16:126, 42:43), waiting (*ḥattā*), staying with people (18:28), and not understanding (18:68).
- **Stop grieving or complaining?** No command to stop complaining anywhere. The only *shakā* verb said by a prophet is Jacob's *ashkū* (12:86), after *ṣabr jamīl*. Job *nādā* (21:83) is complaint in form, and 38:44 calls him *ṣābir*. "Do not grieve" appears in 14 ayahs by script, mostly to the Prophet about the deniers; with a *ṣbr* word only in 16:127: it covers *them*, not his own affliction. Panic is condemned (70:19-21); crying out is not.

## 7. Translator divergence on the hotspots (data-grounded, then interpretive)

- **42:43** *min ʿazmi l-umūr*: "matters requiring determination" / "a resolve to aspire to" / "the steadfast heart of things" / "true constancy". English must decide whose *ʿazm* it is.
- **3:200:** three verbs, one English register. Arberry keeps "vie"; Pickthall turns *rābiṭū* to "be ready"; Khattab "stand on guard".
- **41:35** *wa-mā yulaqqāhā*: the passive "none is granted it" (Saheeh) loses what *-hā* refers to, the response of 41:34.
- **18:68:** *taṣbiru* is "have patience / be patient / bear with / bear patiently"; the same Arabic becomes either endurance or tolerance.
- **13:24:** *bimā ṣabartum* is "for what you endured" (price) / "your perseverance" / "because ye persevered".
- **68:48:** *makẓūm* is "distressed", "total distress", Arberry's "choking inwardly", and Pickthall's **"in despair"**, an import: the Arabic is never *yaʾs*.

## For the practice guide

- Patience in the Quran is mostly *done* and told to someone, and it stays *on*, *with*, *for*, *until*; it is never "away from".
- It is judged by where it faces: "seeking their Lord's face" (13:22), "for your Lord" (74:7).
- It is not despair and not panic. 11:11: "except those who were patient". Jacob weeps and complains to God and is still *ṣabr jamīl*.
- It is given, not manufactured: *pour upon us patience* (2:250, 7:126); "your patience is only through God" (16:127).
- It is passed on: *tawāṣaw bi-ṣ-ṣabr* (103:3). No ayah tells anyone to stop crying out.
