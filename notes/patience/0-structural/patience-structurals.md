# Patience (ṣabr): Phase-0 structurals (orchestrator)

> Census: `scripts/build_patience_census.py` → `data/structural/patience-census.json`.
> Verses: `scripts/build_patience_versepack.py` → `data/structural/patience-versepack.json` (285 verses, 7 groups).

## Headline

**The root ṣ-b-r occurs 103 times in 93 ayahs across 45 surahs. 62 of the 103 are verbs: patience is mostly something done, not something owned.** 29 tokens are commands (*iṣbir*, *iṣbirū*, *iṣṭabir*). **20 of the 28 commanded ayahs are spoken to the Prophet, 19 of them in Meccan surahs.** The others go to the believers, to Luqmān's son, to Ṣāliḥ, to two prophets' peoples, and twice to people the text does not praise. **No ṣ-b-r verb ever has God as its subject**, and *al-Ṣabūr* is not a Quranic name. **Despair (*yaʾs*, *qunūṭ*) never shares an ayah with patience, and grief shares one only once (16:127).** The densest passage is Moses with the man of knowledge (18:60-82): 7 tokens in 23 verses, on patience with what you cannot yet understand (18:68).

- **By mood:** nouns and participles 41; imperatives 29 (22 singular, 7 plural); perfect 22; imperfect 11.
- **By revelation:** Meccan 66 ayahs, Medinan 27. That is 71% Meccan, close to the corpus share of 74%.
- **Densest surahs:** al-Baqara 9, Āl ʿImrān 8, al-Kahf 8, al-Naḥl 7, al-Anfāl 5.

## 2. Who is told to be patient (data-grounded; addressee hand-annotated in the census)

- **The Prophet: 20 ayahs.** The command almost always carries a second clause. The census lists each:
  - **"over what they say"** (20:130, 38:17, 50:39, 73:10);
  - **"for your Lord's judgment"** (52:48, 68:48, 76:24);
  - **"for your Lord"** (74:7);
  - **"God's promise is true"** (30:60, 40:55, 40:77);
  - **"until God judges"** (10:109);
  - **"as the messengers of resolve were patient"** (46:35);
  - **"your patience is only through God"** (16:127);
  - **"beautiful patience"** (70:5).
  
  Four are joined to **glorify with praise** in the same ayah (20:130, 40:55, 50:39, 52:48). One tells him **not to be like the man of the fish** (68:48).
- **Others:**
  - the believers (3:200, 8:46);
  - Moses to his people (7:128);
  - Shuʿayb to his people (7:87);
  - Luqmān to his son (31:17);
  - Ṣāliḥ (54:27).
- **Misdirected patience.** The text records patience it does not praise, in 7 ayahs:
  - Israel: "we will never be patient with one food" (2:61);
  - "how patient they are for the Fire!" (2:175);
  - the weak in Hell: "whether we panic or are patient, there is no escape" (14:21);
  - the deniers: "had we not been patient over our gods" (25:42), and "be patient over your gods" (38:6);
  - "if they are patient, the Fire is their home" (41:24);
  - "be patient or not, it is the same for you" (52:16).

  **Patience is not praised in itself. Its object and direction decide.** Compare *ibtighāʾa wajhi rabbihim*, "seeking their Lord's face" (13:22), and *wa li-rabbika fa-ṣbir*, "for your Lord be patient" (74:7).

## 3. What the word takes (data-grounded, from the word that follows)

- **ʿalā, "over" (14):** over what they say, over what befalls you (31:17, 22:35), over being denied (6:34), over the harm you do us (14:12), over prayer (20:132), over what you do not encompass in knowledge (18:68).
- **li-, "for" (4):** your Lord's judgment, His worship.
- **ḥattā, "until" (3):** until God judges (7:87, 10:109); until you come out to them (49:5).
- **maʿa, "with" (1):** "keep yourself patient *with* those who call on their Lord, morning and evening" (18:28).

## 8. Negative space to test (data-grounded where stated)

- **God is never the subject of ṣ-b-r.** *Al-Ṣabūr* comes from the hadith name lists, not the Quran.
- **No ayah defines patience.** It is shown in people and commanded with objects.
- **The common scheme of three kinds of patience** (in affliction, from sin, in obedience) is a later classification. A cross-investigation claim to test: the success study states it as the Quran's own. 19:65 and 20:132 (*iṣṭabir* in worship and prayer) are the nearest textual support.
- **Patience never shares an ayah with despair**, and shares one with grief only once.
- **"Patience is half of faith" and "patience is at the first blow" are hadith**, not the Quran.

   - Joseph (12:90);
   - Moses and the man of knowledge (18:60-82);
   - Ishmael (37:102), with Ishmael, Idrīs and Dhū l-Kifl as "all among the patient" (21:85);
   - the magicians (7:126); Ṭālūt's few (2:249-250);
   - the messengers in general (6:34, 14:12, 46:35);
   - Luqmān (31:17);
   - Jonah as the counter-image (68:48).

   What did each one do, say and feel? Did any of them stop feeling?

   - the 20 commands to the Prophet and what follows each;
   - what the community is asked;
   - what is promised (God's company, love, good news, reward without account, twice, the chamber, the angels' greeting);
   - the time words (until, a while).
