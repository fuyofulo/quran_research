# Patience in the Quran: a guide for a long hardship

This guide comes from a close reading of every verse in the Quran that uses the word for patience, *ṣabr*, and the stories around those verses. It is written for someone going through something hard that has lasted a long time, who wants to know what the Quran itself means by patience.

The Quran is the only source here. Verse numbers are in brackets. The Arabic is copied exactly from the Quran text. Many well-known sayings about patience come from hadith or from later scholars. Where a saying is not in the Quran, the guide says so. That does not make it wrong. It only means it is not in the Quran itself. Where the guide gives its own understanding, it says "A reading."

## What patience is

In the Quran, patience is almost always something you *do*, not something you have. The Quran speaks of patience "over" what hurts, patience "for" your Lord, patience "until" God acts, and once, "keep yourself patient *with* those who call on their Lord, morning and evening" (18:28). Not once, in the whole Quran, is it patience "away from" anything. A reading: it is less like holding something in, and more like staying. Staying turned toward God while the hard thing goes on, and going on living and doing good while it lasts. The people who have it still grieve. They still cry out to God, and still ask Him for things. And it is not something you have to make on your own: "your patience is only through God" (16:127).

## What patience is not

- **It is not silence.** The patient say, "We belong to God, and to Him we are returning" (2:156). Job called out to God, twice. Jacob said, "I complain of my grief to God" (12:86).
- **It is not being without grief.** Jacob grieved until his eyes turned white (12:84). God said to the Prophet, "We know that what they say grieves you" (6:33).
- **It is not doing nothing.** Patient people in the Quran search, pray, work, forgive and hold their ground. The only patience that changes nothing is in Hell: "be patient or not, it is the same for you" (52:16).
- **It is not something you make by yourself.** "Your patience is only through God" (16:127). In the whole Quran, God is never the one who is "being patient". Patience is something people ask Him to pour on them.
- **It has no clock.** No verse about patience gives a number of days, months or years.
- **It is not a promise of a particular outcome.** No single person is promised a particular result in this life for being patient. What *is* promised comes further down.

Two more, briefly. Patience is not good in itself. People in the Quran were "patient" in clinging to their idols too (25:42). What makes it good is where it faces: "seeking their Lord's face" (13:22). And patience does not mean never complaining. That idea is common, but it is not in the Quran.

## The people to remember

**Jacob.** When his sons came home with a lie about Joseph, and his shirt with false blood on it, Jacob did not accept their story. He said, "Beautiful patience. And God is the One whose help is sought" (12:18). Later, when another son was kept from him, he said "beautiful patience" again, and "perhaps God will bring them all back to me" (12:83). Then he turned away, said "Oh, my sorrow for Joseph", and his eyes went white from grief (12:84). He said, "I complain of my anguish and my grief only to God, and I know from God what you do not know" (12:86). And then, "Go and search, and do not despair of relief from God" (12:87). The Quran does not say he wept. It says his eyes whitened from grief.

**Job.** He called to his Lord: "Harm has touched me, and You are the most merciful of all who show mercy" (21:83). Another time he said, "Satan has touched me with hardship and pain" (38:41). He did not ask for anything in so many words. He told God what hurt, and who God is. God answered him, took the harm away, and gave him back his family and as many again (21:84). Then God said, "We found him patient. What an excellent servant. He was one who kept turning back [to God]" (38:44).

**Moses and the man of knowledge.** Moses asked to travel with a man God had taught. The man said, "You will not be able to be patient with me. And how could you be patient with something you do not understand?" (18:67-68). Moses said, "You will find me patient, God willing" (18:69). He could not keep it. Three times he spoke up at things that looked wrong. At the end, the man explained each one, and each had a reason Moses could not see at the time (18:78-82). A reading: patience is hardest where we cannot see the reason. Moses' failure is told plainly, and he was still given the reasons.

**The magicians.** Pharaoh's magicians came to believe in God. Pharaoh threatened to cut off their hands and feet and crucify them. They said, "To our Lord we are returning", and "Our Lord, pour patience upon us, and let us die as people who have given themselves to You" (7:125-126).

**Ṭālūt's few.** Before a battle against a far larger army, some of those with Ṭālūt said, "We have no strength today against Goliath." Others answered, "How many a small group has overcome a large one, by God's permission. And God is with the patient" (2:249). Then, as they went out to face the army, they prayed: "Our Lord, pour patience upon us, and make our feet firm" (2:250). There was fear. They asked, and they went forward.

**The messengers.** Messengers before the Prophet were rejected and hurt, "and they were patient … until Our help came to them" (6:34). To the people hurting them they said, "Why should we not rely on God, when He has guided us on our ways? We will be patient over the hurt you do us" (14:12). There came a time when even they reached a point the Quran calls despair, without saying despair of what, and that is when help came (12:110).

**Luqmān's advice.** A wise father tells his son, "Keep up the prayer, call people to what is right and away from what is wrong, and be patient over whatever happens to you. That takes real resolve" (31:17). Patience is the fourth thing on a list of things to do.

**Jonah.** God tells the Prophet, "Be patient for your Lord's judgment, and do not be like the man of the fish" (68:48). Jonah had gone off in anger and run away (21:87, 37:140). In the darkness he called out, "There is no god but You. Glory be to You. I was one of the wrongdoers" (21:87). God answered him with the same words He used for Job, "So We answered him", and saved him from his distress, "and that is how We save the believers" (21:88). Then God chose him and made him one of the righteous (68:50). A reading: Jonah's mistake was not his distress, and not his cry. It was leaving in anger.

## The Quran's own words, for times of patience

"Who" is the Quran's own setting. "When" is our suggestion.

### 1. When the hard news first comes

> فَصَبۡرࣱ جَمِيلࣱۖ وَٱللَّهُ ٱلۡمُسۡتَعَانُ عَلَىٰ مَا تَصِفُونَ

*Fa-ṣabrun jamīl, wa-llāhu l-mustaʿānu ʿalā mā taṣifūn.*
**Meaning:** Beautiful patience. And God is the One whose help is sought against what you describe.
**Who:** Jacob, when his sons brought Joseph's shirt with false blood on it (12:18).
**When:** When you hear something you cannot accept, or a story that does not ring true. The word *jamīl*, "beautiful", appears only seven times in the whole Quran. Three are for patience. The others are for a pardon, for parting kindly, and for leaving hurtful people gracefully (15:85, 33:28, 33:49, 73:10).

### 2. When the grief is heavy

> إِنَّمَآ أَشۡكُواْ بَثِّي وَحُزۡنِيٓ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ وَأَعۡلَمُ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ مَا لَا تَعۡلَمُونَ

*Innamā ashkū baththī wa-ḥuznī ilā llāh, wa-aʿlamu mina llāhi mā lā taʿlamūn.*
**Meaning:** I complain of my anguish and my grief only to God, and I know from God what you do not know.
**Who:** Jacob, after his eyes had whitened from grief, when his sons said he would waste away (12:85-86).
**When:** When the sadness will not lift, and the people around you want you to stop feeling it.

### 3. When you feel you have nothing left

> رَبَّنَآ أَفۡرِغۡ عَلَيۡنَا صَبۡرࣰ ا وَثَبِّتۡ أَقۡدَامَنَا

*Rabbanā afrigh ʿalaynā ṣabran wa-thabbit aqdāmanā.*
**Meaning:** Our Lord, pour patience upon us, and make our feet firm.
**Who:** Ṭālūt's few, about to face a far larger army (2:250). The verse goes on, "and help us against the disbelieving people". Pharaoh's magicians began with the same words, "Our Lord, pour patience upon us" (7:126). These are the only two times anyone in the Quran asks for patience, and both were facing death.
**When:** When you are afraid, or tired, and do not have it in you.

### 4. When loss comes

> إِنَّا لِلَّهِ وَإِنَّآ إِلَيۡهِ رَٰجِعُونَ

*Innā lillāhi wa-innā ilayhi rājiʿūn.*
**Meaning:** We belong to God, and to Him we are returning.
**Who:** The patient, "who, when disaster strikes them, say" these words (2:156). The verse before names the tests: fear, hunger, and loss of wealth, lives and fruits (2:155). The verse after says, "Upon them are blessings from their Lord, and mercy" (2:157).
**When:** At the moment of a loss, large or small.

### 5. When all you can do is name the pain

> أَنِّي مَسَّنِيَ ٱلضُّرُّ وَأَنتَ أَرۡحَمُ ٱلرَّٰحِمِينَ

*Annī massaniya ḍ-ḍurru wa-anta arḥamu r-rāḥimīn.*
**Meaning:** Harm has touched me, and You are the most merciful of all who show mercy.
**Who:** Job, calling to his Lord (21:83). There is nothing else in his prayer. God answered (21:84).
**When:** When you are too worn out to find words of your own.

### 6. When other people are the cause

> وَمَا لَنَآ أَلَّا نَتَوَكَّلَ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ وَقَدۡ هَدَىٰنَا سُبُلَنَاۚ وَلَنَصۡبِرَنَّ عَلَىٰ مَآ ءَاذَيۡتُمُونَاۚ

*Wa-mā lanā allā natawakkala ʿala llāhi wa-qad hadānā subulanā, wa-la-naṣbiranna ʿalā mā ādhaytumūnā.*
**Meaning:** Why should we not rely on God, when He has guided us on our ways? We will be patient over the hurt you do us.
**Who:** The messengers, to the people who rejected and harmed them (14:12).
**When:** When the hardship comes through what someone else has done.

### 7. A line to hear, not to say

> وَٱصۡبِرۡ لِحُكۡمِ رَبِّكَ فَإِنَّكَ بِأَعۡيُنِنَاۖ

*Wa-ṣbir li-ḥukmi rabbika fa-innaka bi-aʿyuninā.*
**Meaning:** Be patient for your Lord's judgment, for you are in Our eyes.
**Who:** God, to the Prophet (52:48). The verse goes on, "and glorify your Lord with praise when you rise".
**A reading:** This was said to the Prophet, not to everyone. But it shows how the Quran speaks when it says "be patient". It almost never says it alone. Of the twenty times it tells the Prophet to be patient, eighteen come with something to do or something to hold on to. Here, the thing to hold on to is being seen.

## What to do with the days

This is not a checklist. It is what the Quran itself puts beside patience, so that the days are not empty.

- **Pray.** "Seek help through patience and prayer" (2:45, 2:153). Luqmān's first piece of advice was prayer (31:17).
- **Praise God at the edges of the day.** "Be patient over what they say, and glorify your Lord with praise before sunrise and before sunset, and in the hours of the night and at the ends of the day, so that you may be content" (20:130). Every time of day named in the patience verses is a time of prayer, praise or asking forgiveness. And "so that you may be content" is the only promise of an inner feeling anywhere in those verses.
- **Keep doing what is yours to do.** The command "for your Lord, be patient" (74:7) comes at the end of a short list: rise, warn, glorify your Lord, keep your clothes clean, keep away from what is foul, and do not give in order to get more (74:2-6).
- **Search.** Jacob did not only wait. He told his sons, "Go and search for Joseph and his brother, and do not despair of relief from God. Only those who disbelieve despair of relief from God" (12:87).
- **Stay close to people who call on their Lord.** "Keep yourself patient with those who call on their Lord morning and evening, wanting His face, and do not let your eyes look past them" (18:28).
- **Encourage one another.** People are in loss, "except those who believe, do good, and urge one another to the truth, and urge one another to patience" (103:2-3). Another verse joins it with "urge one another to compassion" (90:17). In the Quran, patience is passed between people. It is not carried alone.
- **Lean on God.** The patient are "those who are patient, and rely on their Lord" (16:42, 29:59).
- **Answer harm with good, where you can.** "Repel evil with what is better", and then, "no one is given this except the patient" (41:34-35). Even this is described as something given.

## What is promised, honestly

**For now.** "God is with the patient." The Quran says this four times (2:153, 2:249, 8:46, 8:66). It also says "God loves the patient" (3:146). It tells the Prophet to "give good news to the patient", who will have blessings from their Lord, mercy and guidance (2:155-157). The one promise of an inner feeling is "so that you may be content" (20:130), said to the Prophet at the end of a list of times of praise.

**For communities, in this life.** Help in battle, protection from plots, land, and leaders from among them (3:125, 3:120, 7:137, 32:24). These are promised to groups, not to one person.

**For the end.** "The patient will be given their reward without measure" (39:10). Some are given their reward twice (28:54). Paradise is "the high chamber, for what they endured", where they are met "with greeting and peace" (25:75). Angels come in to them from every gate, saying, "Peace be upon you, for what you endured" (13:23-24).

**What is not promised.** No single person is promised a particular result in this life for being patient. No verse says how long. Job was healed, but the Quran tells it as God's answer to his call, not as a payment for patience. God says to the Prophet himself, "Whether We show you some of what We have promised them, or take you to Us first" (40:77). The wait may be longer than a life.

A reading: God's company is the only promise that does not depend on anything changing first. It holds while the hardship lasts.

## Hard questions

**"How long?"**
The Quran never says. Not one verse on patience gives a number. Every "until" in those verses ends on something God does, "until God judges" (10:109), "until Our help came" (6:34). None ends on a date.

And the Quran does not scold the question. Believers were once so shaken that even their messenger asked, "When is God's help?" The answer was not a rebuke. It was, "God's help is near" (2:214). When the Prophet is told "beautiful patience" (70:5), the next lines say, "They see it as far away, and We see it as near" (70:6-7). In that passage, "it" is the punishment and the Day that someone had asked about (70:1-4).

When the Quran looks back on a life from the end, the life is short. On the Last Day, people asked how many years they stayed on earth answer, "a day, or part of a day" (23:112-113). Those words come right after God rewards the patient "for what they endured" (23:111).

**"Is my grief a failure of patience?"**
No. In the Quran, nobody who is patient stops feeling. Jacob named his patience beautiful, and then his eyes turned white from grief (12:83-84). God told the Prophet, "We know it grieves you" (6:33). When the Quran tells the Prophet "do not grieve", it is always about the people rejecting him. When it says "do not grieve" to others, it comes with a reason or a gift in the same breath: to Moses' mother, "We will return him to you" (28:7); to Maryam, "your Lord has placed a stream beneath you" (19:24); to the believers after a defeat, "you will be the uppermost, if you are believers" (3:139). It is comfort, not a rebuke. No verse tells anyone that grieving is a failure of patience.

**"Am I allowed to complain?"**
Yes, to God. The word "complain" appears only twice in the whole Quran, and both times the complaint goes to God. Jacob said, "I complain of my grief only to God" (12:86). A woman "complained to God" about her husband, and a whole chapter opens with, "God has heard" her (58:1). Job told God plainly what hurt (21:83). No verse tells anyone to stop complaining.

What the Quran does warn against is something else: giving up on God (12:87), panic (70:19-20), running off in anger (21:87), and rushing (46:35). The idea that patience means never complaining is common, but it is not in the Quran.

**"What if I run out of patience?"**
You would not be the first. Moses promised to be patient, "God willing", and three times he could not keep it. The man still explained everything to him (18:78-82). There came a time when even the messengers despaired, and that is when help came (12:110). Jonah ran off in anger. When he called out from the darkness, God answered him, and later chose him (21:87-88, 68:50).

Patience was never yours to make alone: "your patience is only through God" (16:127). So ask for it: "Our Lord, pour patience upon us" (2:250). And turn back. The word God used for Job, *awwāb*, means one who keeps coming back (38:44). A reading: the patient person in the Quran is not someone who never falls. It is someone who keeps turning back to God.

## To close

- Patience is staying turned toward God while the hard thing lasts.
- Grief is not failure. Jacob named his patience beautiful, and still his eyes turned white.
- Tell God what hurts. That is what the patient did.
- There is no date. There is prayer, praise and work to fill the days.
- You do not have to make patience yourself. Ask Him to pour it.
- God is with the patient now, before anything changes.
