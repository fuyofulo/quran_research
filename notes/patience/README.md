# Patience (ṣabr): investigation index

## What this investigation is

A thematic study of what the Quran itself calls patience: who is patient in it, what they do and feel, what they are told, what they are promised, and what patience is *not*. It started from reading about Job: "We found him patient … one who turns back" (38:44). The Arabic text is ground truth. Hadith, tafsīr and later ethics are outside the evidence base and are named as tradition where they come up. The study ran in four phases: a structural census (Phase 0), three Phase-1 files, one Phase-2 cross-cut, then this synthesis and a plain-language practice guide (Phase 4).

## Headline

**Patience in the Quran is a verb of staying, not of holding back.** 62 of 103 tokens are verbs, none takes *ʿan* "away from", and the only direct object is the self, kept *with* those who call on their Lord (18:28). It is weighed by direction, not cost. The patient grieve and complain, to God (12:84-86, 21:83), and they act: search, pray, praise, fight, forgive. No duration is named and no "until" ends on a date. God is never its subject, yet it is only through Him (16:127). Worldly promises go to groups; the individual's reward is final.

## Key findings

1. **A verb of staying.** 62 of 103 tokens are verbs and 29 are commands. The verb takes *ʿalā* "over" 14 times, *li-* "for" 5, *ḥattā* "until", *maʿa* "with", and *ʿan* "away from" 0 times. The only direct object is the self (18:28).
2. **Weighed by direction.** Seven ayahs record patience the text does not praise (2:61, 2:175, 14:21, 25:42, 38:6, 41:24, 52:16). What makes it good is "seeking their Lord's face" (13:22) and "for your Lord" (74:7).
3. **Nobody patient stops feeling.** Jacob grieves until his eyes whiten and complains to God (12:84-86). Job names his harm (21:83, 38:41). The verb "to complain" occurs twice, both times to God (12:86, 58:1). No ayah forbids complaint. All 8 "do not grieve" commands to the Prophet concern the deniers, and God first says "We know it grieves you" (6:33).
4. **What patience excludes:** despair of God, panic, acting on anger, haste. Despair never shares an ayah with patience, and 11:9-11 makes "the patient" the exception to both despair and exultation.
5. **Commands come with something.** 18 of 20 commands to the Prophet carry an act or a reassurance in the same ayah, most often praise at named hours.
6. **Knowledge.** 18:68 is the only ayah naming a condition for patience: *khubr*. [interpretive] Patience rests on knowing *who* knows (12:86), not what will happen.
7. **Time.** There is no duration word in any of the 93 ayahs, and every "until" ends on an act, four of five on God's. The wait may outlast a life (40:77). The only counts of time nearby (23:112-114, 46:35, 70:4-7) are seen from the end or from God's side. The only clock is worship's.
8. **Active, never passive except in Hell.** Patience fights (3:146), holds a post (3:200), forgives (42:43), repels evil with good (41:34-35) and searches (12:87). Where it restrains, what it holds back is permitted (4:25, 16:126, 49:5). "Patience from sin" has no instance; the three-kinds scheme is tradition.
9. **Given, not made.** God is the subject of 0 of 103 tokens, and *al-Ṣabūr* is not a Quranic name. "Your patience is only through God" (16:127). "Pour patience upon us" occurs only as a request (2:250, 7:126).
10. **Promises.** Worldly outcomes are promised to groups. The individual's promises are final (39:10, 25:75, 76:12). *Bi-mā ṣabarū* (×6) gives a reason, not a duration. The only inner state promised is "that you may be satisfied" (20:130).
11. **No praised person claims past patience.** There is no singular first-person ṣ-b-r verb. The verdicts are God's: "We found him patient" (38:44), "all among the patient" (21:85).
12. **Thanks and turning back.** *Ṣabbārin shakūr* occurs 4 times, always in a setting that turns between gift and loss. *Niʿma l-ʿabd innahu awwāb* is said of Solomon in abundance (38:30) and of Job in harm (38:44), and only Job is also called patient.

## Corrections log

### Phase 0 (orchestrator), corrected in later phases

1. **28:27 speaker.** Phase 0 (structurals formula table, BRIEF) named the speaker of "you will find me, God willing, among the righteous" "Shuʿayb". The speaker is the **unnamed** old man of Midian, and the ayah has no ṣ-b-r. So the frame occurs with patience twice only (18:69, 37:102). *Found by the patient-ones agent.*
2. **37:102 son.** Phase 0 named the son "Ishmael". He is **unnamed** in sura 37. Ishmael is named "among the patient" at 21:85, so the identification is an inference. *Found by the patient-ones agent.*
3. **37:101 *ḥalīm*** (an addition). The son is called forbearing one ayah before "among the patient". *Ḥilm* and *ṣabr* are neighbours there, but never in one ayah. *Patient-ones agent.*
4. **Jacob "weeping"** (structurals §9). The text never says Jacob wept. Sūrat Yūsuf's only *b-k-y* token is the brothers' feigned weeping (12:16). Jacob has grief, whitened eyes, *kaẓīm*, and complaint to God (12:84-86). *Phase 2.*

### Phase 1, corrected by Phase 2 (§11 of the Phase-2 file)

5. **`concept-sbr-the-word.md` §1:** "*Ṣabr jamīl* … none with a verb." This is wrong for 70:5, *fa-ṣbir ṣabran jamīlā* (a command with its own noun as object).
6. **Jacob's "tears":** in `concept-sbr-the-word.md` §4 and its "For the practice guide", and `narrative-the-patient-ones.md` §1 and its "For the practice guide". There are no tears in the text (see 4).
7. **`concept-sbr-the-word.md` headline and §6:** patience "granted" through *mā yulaqqāhā* (28:80, 41:35). What is granted is something else, *to* the patient: *-hā* is feminine and *ṣabr* is masculine. In 41:35 it is 41:34's response "with what is better"; in 28:80 it is unnamed.
8. **`narrative-the-patient-ones.md` §8:** "Nobody calls themselves patient in the past tense." Corrected to: no one the text praises claims past patience. The 4 first-person ṣ-b-r verbs are all plural; the two in the past (14:21, 25:42) are said by the damned and the deniers. There is no singular form.

### New in Phase 4 (precisions; sibling files left unchanged)

14. **46:35, "an hour of a day."** The ones who feel they stayed only an hour are the deniers, on the Day. The commands file's "the retrospective shrinking of the wait" is a reading, not the text's claim about the patient.
15. **28:80 in the commands file's promise table**, "None is granted [the reward]". The bracket cannot be the grammatical referent: *thawāb* is masculine and *-hā* feminine. The referent is unnamed, which agrees with Phase 2 §8.
16. **Phase 2 §1, "Only his sons tell Jacob to stop (12:85)."** 12:85 is an oath-protest ("by God, you will not cease remembering Joseph until you waste away or die"), not a command to stop.
17. **`concept-sbr-the-word.md` §5,** "Reliance: all three are *ṣabarū wa-ʿalā rabbihim yatawakkalūn* (14:12, 16:42, 29:59)". The exact phrase occurs only at 16:42 and 29:59. 14:12 has its own wording (*la-naṣbiranna … wa-ʿalā llāhi fa-l-yatawakkali l-mutawakkilūn*).
18. **Time words next door** (an addition). "No duration word in the 93 ayahs" holds by script. But year-roots stand next door twice: 23:112 ("how many years did you stay on earth?", answered "a day or part of a day") and 70:4 ("a Day whose measure is fifty thousand years"). Neither measures the wait.

**Orchestrator check of the Phase-4 files**

- The practice guide and synthesis said the Quran "never tells anyone to stop grieving over their own loss". This is false: "do not grieve" is said over one's own loss to Moses' mother (28:7), Maryam (19:24) and the believers after a defeat (3:139); 3:153 and 57:23 give the same purpose. Corrected in both files to: each time it comes with a reason or a gift, as comfort, and no verse calls grief a failure of patience. The claim about the Prophet (all of his "do not grieve" commands concern the deniers) stands.

## Open questions

1. **28:80:** what does the feminine *-hā* refer to?
2. **38:44:** why does "We found him patient" come after Job's healing in the text's order?
3. **3:200:** *ṣābirū*, "outlast": outlast whom? The text names no rival.
4. ***Kaẓm*** is praised (3:134), described (12:84) and warned against (68:48). Is the difference what is swallowed, or where it goes next?
5. **12:110:** what did the messengers despair of, if despair of God's relief belongs to disbelievers (12:87)?
6. **70:6:** is "it" the punishment of 70:1 or the Day of fifty thousand years (70:4)?
7. ***Ḥilm* and *ṣabr*:** next door at 37:101-102 but never together. How are they related?
8. ***Ṣabbār shakūr*:** one person in two turns, or two dispositions joined?
9. **39:10:** what does "God's earth is spacious" do beside "reward without account"?
10. **74:7:** is "for your Lord, be patient" the definition the text otherwise withholds?
