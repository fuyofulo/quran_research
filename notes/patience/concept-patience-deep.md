# Patience (ṣabr) in the Quran: synthesis

## Headline

**In the Quran, patience is a verb of staying, not of holding back.** 62 of the root's 103 tokens are verbs and 29 are commands. None is followed by *ʿan*, "away from", and the only direct object is the self, kept *with* those who call on their Lord (18:28). **Patience is weighed by where it faces:** the deniers are "patient" over their gods (25:42). **Nobody patient stops feeling.** Jacob grieves until his eyes whiten and complains, to God (12:84-86); Job names his harm (21:83). Of 20 commands to the Prophet, 18 come with something to do or a reassurance. **No duration is named anywhere, and no "until" ends on a date.** God is never the subject of the verb, yet "your patience is only through God" (16:127). Worldly promises go to communities; the individual's reward is final. Passive patience appears only among the damned. "Patience from sin" has no instance.

## 1. The word and its forms

Data-grounded unless marked.

The root ṣ-b-r occurs **103 times in 93 ayahs across 45 surahs**: 66 Meccan ayahs and 27 Medinan (71%, close to the corpus share of 74%).

| Lemma | Tokens | Note |
|---|---|---|
| *ṣabara* (Form I verb) | 58 | to be patient, to stay |
| *ṣābir* / *ṣābira* / *ṣābirāt* | 20 / 1 / 1 | "the patient", a company (8:66), patient women (33:35) |
| *ṣabr* (noun) | 15 | patience |
| *ṣabbār* (intensive) | 4 | always *li-kulli ṣabbārin shakūr* |
| *iṣṭabara* (Form VIII) | 3 | all commands: in worship (19:65), in prayer (20:132), watching a trial (54:27) |
| *ṣābirū* (Form III) | 1 | 3:200 only, "outlast", beside *iṣbirū* and *rābiṭū* |

The 62 verbs are 29 commands, 22 perfects ("for what they endured", "the messengers were patient") and 11 imperfects (conditions and denials: "if you are patient", "you will never be able to be patient"). The text commands and reports patience far more than it describes a trait.

**What the verb takes.**
- ***ʿalā*, "over", 14 times** (10 praised, 4 not). The text's own kinds: what they say (20:130, 38:17, 50:39, 73:10); what befalls you (31:17, 22:35); being denied and harmed (6:34, 14:12); prayer (20:132); what one has not taken in with knowledge (18:68). Mostly other people's words and deeds, then what befalls, then worship, then not understanding.
- ***li-*, "for", 5:** your Lord's judgment (52:48, 68:48, 76:24), His worship (19:65), "for your Lord" (74:7).
- ***ḥattā*, "until", directly after the verb 3 times:** until God judges (7:87, 10:109), until you come out to them (49:5).
- ***fī*, "in", once:** hardship, harm and "the time of battle" (2:177).
- ***maʿa*, "with", once,** and with the only direct object in all 103 tokens, the self:

> **[18:28, first clause]** وَٱصۡبِرۡ نَفۡسَكَ مَعَ ٱلَّذِينَ يَدۡعُونَ رَبَّهُم بِٱلۡغَدَوٰةِ وَٱلۡعَشِيِّ يُرِيدُونَ وَجۡهَهُۥۖ
> Saheeh: "And keep yourself patient [by being] with those who call upon their Lord in the morning and the evening, seeking His countenance."

- ***ʿan*, "away from": 0 of 103.**

[interpretive] The picture is a self kept in place among people who face God, not a self clamped shut. "Holding back" is not visible in the grammar. "Staying on, with, for, until" is.

**The noun is rarely owned.** *Ṣabr* is sought help through (2:45, 2:153), poured (2:250, 7:126), advised (90:17, 103:3), called *jamīl* (12:18, 12:83, 70:5), and five times said to be beyond Moses (*lan tastaṭīʿa … ṣabrā*, 18:67, 72, 75; *mā lam tastaṭiʿ ʿalayhi ṣabrā*, 18:78, 82). Only 16:127 says "your patience", and it says it is only through God.

***Ṣabr jamīl*, beautiful patience,** occurs three times. Twice it is verbless, *fa-ṣabrun jamīl* (12:18, 12:83, Jacob). Once it has its verb, *fa-ṣbir ṣabran jamīlā* (70:5, the Prophet). *Jamīl* occurs **7 times in the whole Quran**: these three, a pardon (15:85), a release from marriage (33:28, 33:49), and a leaving of the mockers (73:10). [interpretive] "Beautiful" names ways of dealing with people at a point of strain: patience, pardon, parting, distance.

**No ayah defines patience.** It is commanded, reported, and found in people.

**Translator divergence.** Patience verses translate less steadily than the corpus (median 0.649 against 0.635). The hotspots are where meaning resists: 42:43 (97th percentile, *min ʿazmi l-umūr*), 3:200 (three verbs, one English register), 41:35 (*wa-mā yulaqqāhā*), 18:68 (*khubrā*), 68:48 (*makẓūm*).

## 2. Who is told, and what comes with the command

Data-grounded unless marked.

**29 command tokens in 28 ayahs. 20 ayahs are spoken to the Prophet, 19 of them in Meccan surahs.** The rest go to the believers (3:200, 8:46), from Moses to his people (7:128), from Shuʿayb to his (7:87), from Luqmān to his son (31:17), to Ṣāliḥ (54:27), and twice in settings the text does not praise: the chiefs telling one another "be patient over your gods" (38:6), and the damned told "be patient or not" (52:16).

**What comes with the 20.** An act in the same ayah: 13. A reassurance: 11. Both: 6 (10:109, 20:130, 20:132, 40:55, 46:35, 52:48). Neither: 2 (70:5, 74:7), and 70:6-7 supply a reassurance next door.

- **Acts:** glorify with praise (20:130, 40:55, 50:39, 52:48); ask forgiveness (40:55); remember David, *awwāb* (38:17); leave them with a beautiful leaving (73:10); stay with those who call on their Lord (18:28); worship (19:65); enjoin prayer on your family (20:132); follow the revelation (10:109); do not obey the sinner (76:24); do not hasten (46:35); do not be like the man of the fish (68:48).
- **Reassurances:** God's promise is true (30:60, 40:55, 40:77); *fa-innaka bi-aʿyuninā*, "you are in Our eyes" (52:48); He is the best of judges (10:109); the outcome is for the mindful (11:49); God does not lose the reward of those who do good (11:115); "your patience is only through God" (16:127); *laʿallaka tarḍā*, "that you may be satisfied" (20:130); "We provide for you" (20:132).
- **Commands not to be moved:** two only. 16:127 (do not grieve over them, do not be in distress at their plotting) and 30:60 (let them not unsettle you). Each carries its reason. Neither says "do not feel".

**The community** is told to seek help through patience and prayer (2:45, 2:153); is tested with "fear, hunger, and loss of wealth, lives and fruits" and answers with a sentence, *innā lillāhi wa-innā ilayhi rājiʿūn* (2:155-156); joins patience to fear of God (3:186, 3:200); keeps battle discipline (8:45-46); chooses restraint over lawful retaliation (16:126); and is described as *tawāṣaw bi-ṣ-ṣabr*, "advising one another to patience" (90:17, 103:3). These two are the only places patience passes between people.

[interpretive] The commands rarely mean "endure". They mean "do this meanwhile" and "here is why you can".

## 3. The patient people

Data-grounded unless marked.

**Jacob (12:16-18, 12:83-87, 12:96).** Both times he first refuses his sons' account, *bal sawwalat lakum anfusukum amrā*, "rather, your souls have enticed you to something", and then says *fa-ṣabrun jamīl*. In 12:18 the same breath seeks help, *wa-llāhu l-mustaʿān*; in 12:83 it hopes, "perhaps God will bring them all to me". Next door, with no ṣ-b-r, he turns away from them, cries "my sorrow for Joseph", his eyes whiten from grief, and he is *kaẓīm* (12:84). His sons protest that he will not stop remembering Joseph until he wastes away or dies (12:85). He complains of his anguish and grief to God (12:86), then sends them to search and forbids despair of God's relief (12:87). **The text never says Jacob wept** (CORRECTED; the sura's only *b-k-y* token is the brothers' feigned weeping, 12:16). When his sight returns he repeats, "Did I not tell you I know from God what you do not know?" (12:96). [interpretive] Beautiful patience is not agreement with the story. It is what is left standing after refusing a false one.

**Job (21:83-84; 38:41-44).** Two calls, both *nādā rabbahu*: "harm has touched me, and You are the most merciful of the merciful" (21:83), and "Satan has touched me with hardship and torment" (38:41). Neither has a request or a command in it. God answers (*fa-stajabnā lahu*), removes the harm, gives back his family "and the like of them with them" (21:84, 38:43), and a spring to bathe in and drink (38:42). Then: "We found him patient" (38:44). In the text's order the verdict follows the healing. Job is not in 21:85's list of "the patient"; his patience is named in Ṣād only.

**Joseph (12:90).** The one ṣ-b-r token on him is general: *man yattaqi wa-yaṣbir*, "whoever is mindful and patient", God does not lose the reward of those who do good. His own patience is never narrated with the word.

**Moses and the man of knowledge (18:60-82).** The densest passage: 7 tokens in 23 ayahs. "You will never be able to have patience with me" (18:67, 72, 75). Moses: "You will find me, God willing, patient, and I will not disobey you" (18:69). He breaks it at the ship, the boy and the wall. The man parts from him (18:78) but tells him every reason, and ends: "I did not do it of my own accord" (18:82).

**The unnamed son (37:101-107).** Called *ḥalīm*, forbearing (37:101), one ayah before he says "you will find me, God willing, among the patient" (37:102). Abraham asks, "see what you think"; the son answers, "do what you are commanded". Both submit (37:103), and a ransom follows (37:107). Nothing is said of his feeling. **The son is unnamed in the sura** (CORRECTED). Ishmael is named "among the patient" at 21:85, with Idrīs and Dhū l-Kifl, so the identification is an inference. **The third "you will find me, God willing" (28:27) is spoken by an unnamed old man of Midian** (CORRECTED; not named Shuʿayb in the text) and says "among the righteous", with no ṣ-b-r. The frame occurs with patience twice.

**Pharaoh's magicians (7:120-126).** Threatened with cut hands and feet and crucifixion (7:124), they say "to our Lord we are returning" (7:125) and *rabbanā afrigh ʿalaynā ṣabran wa-tawaffanā muslimīn* (7:126).

**Ṭālūt's few (2:249-250).** Most drink from the river; a few do not. Those who crossed say, "We have no power today against Goliath and his soldiers." Those certain of meeting God answer, "How many a small company has overcome a large one by God's leave. And God is with the patient" (2:249). Then: *rabbanā afrigh ʿalaynā ṣabran wa-thabbit aqdāmanā* (2:250). Fear is in the text, and so is the crossing.

**The messengers.** Denied and harmed, "they were patient … until Our help came to them" (6:34). Their words to those who harmed them: "Why should we not rely on God, when He has guided us on our ways? We will be patient over the harm you do us" (14:12). The Prophet is told to be patient "as the messengers of resolve were" (46:35). At 12:110 they reach *istayʾasa*, with the object unnamed, and help comes.

**Luqmān to his son (31:17).** Keep up prayer, enjoin right, forbid wrong, "and be patient over what befalls you; that is among the matters of resolve". Patience is the fourth of four commands, listed with deeds.

**Jonah, the image not to copy (68:48-50; 21:87-88; 37:139-146).** "Be patient for your Lord's judgment, and do not be like the man of the fish when he called out, *makẓūm*" (68:48). Before the call he went off angry (21:87), fled to the laden ship (37:140), and was blameworthy (37:142). The call has no complaint: "there is no god but You, glory be to You, I was among the wrongdoers" (21:87). He is answered with Job's verb, *fa-stajabnā lahu* (21:88; 21:84), "and so We save the believers", and then chosen and made one of the righteous (68:50). [interpretive] Neither the call nor the swallowed distress is his fault (Jacob is *kaẓīm*, 12:84). The anger and the leaving are, set against "for your Lord's judgment".

**Across all of them.**
- **No one stops feeling.** Grief to whitened eyes; "harm has touched me"; "no power for us today"; messengers harmed. No ayah says the grief or the fear ended before the relief came.
- **No one stops asking.** Only two groups ask *for* patience, and both stand before death (2:250, 7:126).
- **No one praised claims past patience.** Patience is spoken only in the future or without a verb: "you will find me", "we will be patient", "beautiful patience". There is no singular first-person ṣ-b-r verb at all. The four first-person verbs are plural, and the two in the past (14:21, 25:42) belong to the damned and the deniers (CORRECTED). The past-tense verdict is God's: "We found him patient" (38:44), "all among the patient" (21:85).
- **All of them do something:** search, stamp the ground, submit, cross and fight, pray and enjoin, stay.

## 4. Grief and complaint

Data-grounded unless marked.

**Grief** (*ḥuzn*, 42 tokens) shares an ayah with ṣ-b-r only once, 16:127, and the grief forbidden there is over the deniers. **14 ayahs forbid grief.** The 8 addressed to the Prophet each name what he grieves over: *them*, *their speech*, *his unbelief*, *those who hasten into unbelief* (3:176, 5:41, 10:65, 15:88, 16:127, 27:70, 31:23, 36:76). None concerns a loss of his own. The other 6 give a reason in the same ayah, for example "do not grieve; God is with us" (9:40; also 3:139, 19:24, 28:7, 29:33, 41:30). Before any of this God says He knows: *qad naʿlamu innahu la-yaḥzunuka lladhī yaqūlūn*, "We know that what they say grieves you" (6:33). The next ayah points to the patient messengers (6:34). His sons are the only ones in the text who remonstrate with Jacob's grief (12:85), and their words are a protest, not a command.

**Complaint.** The verb "to complain" (*sh-k-w*) occurs twice, both times toward God. The root's third token, *mishkāt* (24:35), is a lamp niche.

> **[12:86]** قَالَ إِنَّمَآ أَشۡكُواْ بَثِّي وَحُزۡنِيٓ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ وَأَعۡلَمُ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ مَا لَا تَعۡلَمُونَ
> Saheeh: He said, "I only complain of my suffering and my grief to Allah, and I know from Allah that which you do not know.

The other is 58:1: a woman argues with the Prophet about her husband and *tashtakī ilā llāh*, "complains to God", and "God hears the exchange between you both". Job's two calls go to his Lord. Jacob turns away from his sons before he speaks of grief (12:84). **No ayah forbids complaint, and none uses the verb for complaint to people.** [interpretive] The line the text draws is the address, not the voice.

**Swallowing (*kaẓm*).** Jacob is *kaẓīm* (12:84), described, not praised; Jonah is *makẓūm* (68:48), the image not to copy; "those who swallow anger" are praised (3:134). It is never itself called patience. Pickthall renders *makẓūm* "in despair" (68:48, 90th percentile divergence), an import: the Arabic is not *yaʾs*.

**What patience excludes, by the text:**
- **Despair of God's relief or mercy** (12:87, 15:56, 29:23, 39:53), or despair as the reflex when good is withdrawn (11:9, 17:83, 30:36, 41:49). *Yaʾs* (13 tokens) and *qunūṭ* (6) never share an ayah with patience. 11:9-11 is one unit: the one who loses mercy is "despairing, ungrateful", the one who regains favour is "exultant, boastful", **"except those who are patient and do good"** (11:11).
- **Panic.** The human is *halūʿ*, *jazūʿ* when harm touches (70:19-20), and the exception there is those who pray (70:22). Panic and patience share one ayah, in Hell, where they cancel: "whether we panic or are patient, there is no escape for us" (14:21).
- **Acting on anger:** Jonah (21:87, 37:140), set against "for your Lord's judgment" (68:48).
- **Haste:** "do not seek to hasten for them" (46:35); the callers at the door should have waited "until you came out to them" (49:5).

**Exhaustion is not blamed.** At 12:110 the messengers *istayʾasa* and "thought they had been denied", and then help came. At 2:214 (no ṣ-b-r) the believers are "shaken" until the messenger himself asks "when is God's help?", and the answer is not a rebuke: "God's help is near".

[interpretive] Two griefs are in view. Jacob's is for a loss, recorded without rebuke. The Prophet's is for his people's refusal, acknowledged, given a reason, and lifted. Neither is called a failure of patience.

## 5. Knowledge and not knowing

Data-grounded unless marked.

> **[18:68]** وَكَيۡفَ تَصۡبِرُ عَلَىٰ مَا لَمۡ تُحِطۡ بِهِۦ خُبۡرࣰ ا
> Saheeh: And how can you have patience for what you do not encompass in knowledge?"

**18:68 is the only ayah that names a condition for patience:** *khubr*, knowledge that takes the matter in. Moses' promise, "God willing" (18:69), does not secure it. What he protests is what looks like wrong (a holed ship, a killed boy, unpaid work on a wall), and each act has a reason that was invisible at the time (18:79-82).

**Of the 9 ṣ-b-r ayahs paired with *ʿilm*, God is the knower in 5** (3:142, 4:25, 8:66, 12:83, 47:31). In 3:142 and 47:31 the test exists so that God "makes evident" the patient. Jacob holds through 12:84-87 with "I know from God what you do not know" (12:86), and says it again when his sight returns (12:96). The Prophet is told Noah's story, "you did not know it, nor your people", then "be patient" (11:49). The messengers' stories "make your heart firm" (11:120).

**The patient are not told the end:** "whether We show you some of what We promise them or take you in death" (40:77).

[interpretive] Patience is tied to knowing *who* knows, not to knowing what will happen. Moses, without reasons, fails. Jacob, with no information but with knowledge "from God", holds. What steadies patience in the text is knowledge of God and of how earlier stories ended.

## 6. Time: no duration anywhere

Data-grounded unless marked.

**No duration word is attached to patience in any of the 93 ayahs.** The roots for year, month and age (*s-n-w*, *ʿ-w-m*, *sh-h-r*, *d-h-r*, *ḥ-q-b*) occur in none of them. *Ḥattā* occurs in 5: four end on an act of God (6:34 "until Our help came"; 7:87 and 10:109 "until God judges"; 47:31 "until We make evident"), and 49:5 ends on the Prophet coming out to his callers. **None ends on a date.**

> **[40:77]** فَٱصۡبِرۡ إِنَّ وَعۡدَ ٱللَّهِ حَقࣱّۚ فَإِمَّا نُرِيَنَّكَ بَعۡضَ ٱلَّذِي نَعِدُهُمۡ أَوۡ نَتَوَفَّيَنَّكَ فَإِلَيۡنَا يُرۡجَعُونَ
> Saheeh: So be patient, [O Muhammad]; indeed, the promise of Allah is truth. And whether We show you some of what We have promised them or We take you in death, it is to Us they will be returned.

The wait may outlast the one who waits.

**The only counts of time near patience are seen from God's side or from the end, and none measures the wait** (new in this synthesis; by script, year-roots stand next door to a ṣ-b-r ayah only at 23:112 and 70:4). Two of them shrink a lifetime; the third gives a Day's measure and then calls it near.
- **23:111-114.** God rewards those who were mocked "for what they endured" (23:111). Next door He asks the damned, "How long did you stay on earth, in number of years?" They answer, "a day or part of a day" (23:112-113), and He says, "you stayed only a little, if you had only known" (23:114).
- **46:35.** On the Day they see what they were promised, *they*, the deniers for whom the Prophet is told not to hasten, will feel they "had not stayed except an hour of a day". The span that shrinks is the deniers' stay, which is the same span the Prophet waits through. (The commands file called this "the retrospective shrinking of the wait"; that is a reading.)
- **70:4-7.** The one large number beside a patience command is "a Day whose measure is fifty thousand years" (70:4). Then *fa-ṣbir ṣabran jamīlā* (70:5), and "they see it far, and We see it near" (70:6-7). "It" is masculine and may be the punishment of 70:1 or the Day of 70:4 [interpretive which].

**The only clock in the patience ayahs is worship's.** Every hour of the day they name is an hour of prayer, praise or asking forgiveness: before dawn (3:17), morning and evening (18:28, 40:55), before sunrise and sunset, hours of the night and the ends of the day (20:130, 50:39), "when you rise" (52:48). The ayahs that follow add night prostration and praise (50:40, 52:49, 76:25-26). The other *ḥīn* phrases are moments, not hours: "the time of battle" (2:177), "when they see the punishment" (25:42).

[interpretive] The wait is long from inside and short from God's side and from the end. It is never dated, it is filled with praise, and its end is God's act.

## 7. Action and restraint

Data-grounded unless marked.

**Patience fights.** The fighters beside the prophets "did not weaken, did not grow feeble, did not yield; and God loves the patient" (3:146). Their only words were "forgive us, make our feet firm, help us" (3:147). 8:45-46: stand firm, remember God much, obey, do not quarrel and lose heart, be patient; God is with the patient. A hundred patient overcome two hundred (8:66). Ṭālūt's few cross the river and fight (2:249-251). Standing firm is the feet; patience is what is poured (2:250).

**Patience holds a post.** *Iṣbirū wa-ṣābirū wa-rābiṭū wa-ttaqū llāh* (3:200): be patient, outlast, hold the post, be mindful of God. [interpretive] Form III suggests outlasting someone; the text names no rival.

**Patience restrains, but what it restrains is permitted.** Three ayahs say patience "is better": a lawful marriage held off (4:25), lawful retaliation forgone (16:126), calling at the door (49:5). 42:40-41 allow retaliation "with no blame on them"; 42:43 then says "whoever is patient and forgives, that is among the matters of resolve". "They avert evil with good" occurs only twice, both in patience ayahs (13:22, 28:54). 41:34 commands it, and 41:35 says only the patient are given it.

**Patience searches and works.** Jacob: "go and search" (12:87). Luqmān: pray, enjoin, forbid, be patient (31:17). The barest command, *wa-li-rabbika fa-ṣbir* (74:7), closes six commands to act: rise and warn, glorify your Lord, purify your clothes, shun filth, do not give to gain more (74:2-6).

**Is patience ever passive?** Only among the damned: "whether we panic or are patient" (14:21); "if they are patient, the Fire is their home" (41:24); "be patient or not, it is the same for you" (52:16). [interpretive] Patience that does nothing and changes nothing is a picture of Hell.

**Is there "patience from sin"? No instance.**
- No ṣ-b-r token takes *ʿan*.
- Joseph's refusal (12:23-34) has no ṣ-b-r. He seeks refuge (12:23), sees his Lord's proof (12:24), holds himself back (*istaʿṣama*, 12:32) and prays, and God turns the plot away (*ṣ-r-f*, 12:24, 33, 34). *Aṣbu* in 12:33 is root *ṣ-b-w*, "to incline", a lookalike.
- 4:25's *ʿanat*, which translators render "sin", means hardship in the root's other four uses (2:220, 3:118, 9:128, 49:7), and what is held off is a permitted marriage.
- The word that keeps from sin is *taqwā*, paired with patience in 10 ayahs (for example 3:120, 3:186, 12:90).

The scheme of three kinds (under affliction, from sin, in obedience) is tradition. Affliction is well attested (2:155-157, 2:177, 3:186, 22:35, 31:17). Obedience rests on three ayahs (19:65, 20:132, 18:28). Sin rests on none. The text's own kinds are other: over people's words, for God's judgment, over what one cannot take in, in battle, and in restraint from what is allowed.

## 8. With thanks, with prayer, with turning back

Data-grounded unless marked.

**Thanks.** *Inna fī dhālika la-āyātin li-kulli ṣabbārin shakūr*, "in that are signs for everyone patient and thankful", occurs 4 times (14:5, 31:31, 34:19, 42:33), with patience always first. Each setting turns between gift and loss: the days of God, torment and rescue (14:5-6); ships running by His favour (31:31); Sabaʾ's gardens ruined (34:19); the wind stilled at sea (42:33). Three have ingratitude within two ayahs (14:7, 31:32, 34:17), and 31:32 mirrors the pair: *kullu khattārin kafūr*, "every treacherous ingrate", the same intensive patterns in the opposite sense. God bears *Shakūr* as a name; no ṣ-b-r word is His. [interpretive] The *ṣabbār shakūr* is one person reading both turns of the same sign: patient when the wind stops, thankful when the ship runs.

**Prayer.** "Seek help through patience and prayer" (2:45, 2:153), and 2:153 ends "God is with the patient", not "with those who pray". Prayer shares 7 ayahs with patience (2:45, 2:153, 2:177, 13:22, 20:132, 22:35, 31:17). Form VIII *iṣṭabir* is for worship (19:65) and prayer (20:132). The one inner state promised in the 93 ayahs, "that you may be satisfied" (20:130), closes a list of hours of praise. [interpretive] Worship needs patience, and it also carries it.

**Reliance.** *Alladhīna ṣabarū wa-ʿalā rabbihim yatawakkalūn*, "those who are patient and rely on their Lord", occurs twice (16:42, 29:59). The messengers' 14:12 says the same in its own words: "why should we not rely on God … we will be patient". In all three, patience comes before reliance.

**Turning back.** By script, *awwāb* shares an ayah with ṣ-b-r only twice, both in Ṣād: 38:17 (be patient … remember David, *awwāb*) and 38:44 (Job).

> **[38:44, last clause]** إِنَّا وَجَدۡنَٰهُ صَابِرࣰ اۚ نِّعۡمَ ٱلۡعَبۡدُ إِنَّهُۥٓ أَوَّابࣱ‏
> Saheeh: "Indeed, We found him patient, an excellent servant. Indeed, he was one repeatedly turning back [to Allah]."

*Niʿma l-ʿabd innahu awwāb* occurs exactly twice: of Solomon, given abundance (38:30), and of Job, given harm (38:44). Only Job is also *ṣābir*. "To Him we are returning" (2:156) is what the patient say. [interpretive] The overlap is thin; the link is direction. Patience faces God (13:22, 74:7), and turning back renews the facing, in abundance or in harm.

## 9. Given, not made

Data-grounded unless marked.

**God is the subject of none of the 103 tokens.** *Al-Ṣabūr* is not a Quranic name; it comes from the hadith name lists. Patience is commanded 29 times, always to people. One ayah names its source:

> **[16:127]** وَٱصۡبِرۡ وَمَا صَبۡرُكَ إِلَّا بِٱللَّهِۚ وَلَا تَحۡزَنۡ عَلَيۡهِمۡ وَلَا تَكُ فِي ضَيۡقࣲ مِّمَّا يَمۡكُرُونَ
> Saheeh: And be patient, [O Muhammad], and your patience is not but through Allah. And do not grieve over them and do not be in distress over what they conspire.

**"Pour patience upon us" occurs only as a request** (2:250, 7:126); the text never reports God pouring it. **What 28:80 and 41:35 grant is not patience** (CORRECTED). In *wa-mā yulaqqāhā illā ṣ-ṣābirūn*, *-hā* is feminine and *ṣabr* is masculine (*ṣabrun jamīl*, 12:18). In 41:35 it is 41:34's response "with what is better". In 28:80 it is unnamed, beside "God's reward is better". The patient receive something; they are not handed patience.

[interpretive] You do it, and it is only through God. The text holds both and does not explain how.

- **Worldly outcomes are promised to groups** (communities, messengers, Israel). None names an individual. Job is healed, but as an answer to his call, and "We found him patient" comes after the healing.
- **The individual's promises are final:** the Chamber, the garden, reward without account. 39:10 is the only "without account" about the patient; the other six are about provision.
- **The reward formula *bi-mā ṣabarū*, "for what they endured", occurs 6 times** (7:137, 13:24, 23:111, 25:75, 28:54, 76:12). It gives a reason, not a duration.
- **The one inner state promised** is "that you may be satisfied" (20:130), to the Prophet.
- **God's company (*maʿa*) is the promise in the present tense.** [interpretive] It is the only promise that needs nothing to change in order to be kept.
- 39:10 sets "God's earth is spacious" beside the reward without account. [open]

## 11. Misdirected patience

Data-grounded, then interpretive.

**Seven ayahs record patience the text does not praise:** Israel "will never be patient with one food" (2:61); "how patient they are for the Fire" (2:175); "whether we panic or are patient" (14:21); "had we not been patient over our gods" (25:42); "be patient over your gods" (38:6); "if they are patient, the Fire is their home" (41:24); "be patient or not" (52:16).

What makes patience praiseworthy, in the text's words, is its face: *ibtighāʾa wajhi rabbihim*, "seeking their Lord's face" (13:22); *wa-li-rabbika fa-ṣbir*, "for your Lord, be patient" (74:7); *yurīdūna wajhahu*, "wanting His face" (18:28).

[interpretive] Patience is not weighed by how much it costs but by where it faces. Any definition of patience as endurance alone stalls on 25:42, where the deniers use the prophets' verb for clinging to their gods.

## 12. What patience is not

1. **Not silence.** The patient say "to Him we are returning" (2:156); Job calls out (21:83, 38:41); Jacob complains to God (12:86).
2. **Not the absence of grief.** Jacob's eyes whiten (12:84); "We know that what they say grieves you" (6:33).
3. **Not passivity.** It fights (3:146), holds a post (3:200), forgives (42:43), repels evil with good (41:34-35), searches (12:87).
4. **Not a virtue in itself.** Seven misdirected ayahs; the deniers are "patient" over their gods (25:42).
5. **Not self-made.** "Your patience is only through God" (16:127).
6. **Not timed.** No duration; the wait may outlast a life (40:77).
7. **Not a worldly guarantee for one person.** Worldly promises go to groups.
8. **Not restraint from sin.** That is *taqwā*'s work; "patience from sin" has no instance.
9. **Not despair of God, panic, haste or angry flight** (12:87, 70:20, 46:35, 21:87).
10. **Not a self-description.** No one the text praises says "I was patient".

## 13. Negative space

Data-grounded.

- No ayah defines patience.
- God is never the subject; *al-Ṣabūr* is not a Quranic name.
- No *ṣabr ʿan*; no "patience from sin".
- No duration and no date; no "until" ends on a time.
- No command to stop complaining. *(CORRECTED by the orchestrator: an earlier draft added "and no command to stop grieving over one's own loss". "Do not grieve" is said over one's own loss to Moses' mother (28:7), Maryam (19:24) and the believers after Uḥud (3:139; cf. 3:153, 57:23). Each time it comes with a reason or a gift, as comfort. No verse calls grief a failure of patience.)*
- No mention of Jacob weeping.
- Despair (*yaʾs*, *qunūṭ*), restlessness (*halūʿ*) and forbearance (*ḥilm*) never share an ayah with patience. *Ḥilm* stands next door once (37:101-102).
- No singular first-person ṣ-b-r verb; no praised person claims past patience.
- No individual is promised a specific worldly result for patience.
- Joseph's own patience is never narrated with the word; Job is called patient only in Ṣād.
- No ṣ-b-r ayah is among the corpus' near-duplicate refrains.
- **The common sayings are not in the text:** "patience is half of faith" and "patience is at the first blow" (hadith); the three kinds (tradition); "patience means not complaining" (contradicted by 12:86 and 58:1).

## 14. Open questions

1. **28:80.** What does the feminine *-hā* refer to, if not the masculine "reward"?
2. **38:44.** Why does "We found him patient" come after Job's healing in the text's order? A verdict on the whole, or on the call?
3. **3:200.** *Ṣābirū*, "outlast": outlast whom? The text names no rival.
4. ***Kaẓm*** is praised (3:134), described (12:84) and warned against (68:48). Is the difference what is swallowed, or where it goes next?
5. **12:110.** What did the messengers despair of, if despair of God's relief belongs to the disbelievers (12:87)?
6. **70:6.** Is "it" the punishment of 70:1 or the Day of fifty thousand years (70:4)?
7. ***Ḥilm* and *ṣabr*** stand next door (37:101-102) but never together. How are they related? Untested.
8. ***Ṣabbār shakūr*:** one person in two turns, or two dispositions joined?
9. **39:10.** What does "God's earth is spacious" do beside "reward without account"?
10. **74:7.** Is the barest command, "for your Lord, be patient", the definition the text otherwise withholds?
