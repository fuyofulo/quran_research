# Istighfār in the Quran: synthesis

## Headline finding

The Quran names the act with one verb (`istaghfara`, 40×) and shows it being done in another: a
person speaking **to** God, `rabbi / rabbanā ighfir`. *I seek forgiveness* (`astaghfiru`) occurs
only 3×, and each time it is a promise made for someone else. Istighfār is a **spoken request**. Its
one stated condition falls on the asker: he does not knowingly persist (3:135). **Its object is sin;
its occasion need not be a known sin.** Setting right belongs to tawba, which follows it. The text
fixes no count and no commanded hour, gives believers no fixed formula, and requires no feeling. The
limit it sets on asking is time, not the size of the sin.

## 1. The shape of the act: named in one voice, done in another

**[D]** The root `gfr` (234 occurrences) splits the work between two verbs. **`ghafara`** (form I,
65) is the forgiving: 61 tokens are God's, 4 are human (42:37, 42:43, 45:14, 64:14). **`istaghfara`**
(form X, 40 tokens) is the *seeking*, done by humans or angels. The noun *istighfār* occurs once
(9:114), attached to an istighfār Ibrāhīm abandoned. `mustaghfirīn` (3:17) and `ghufrān` (2:285)
also occur once each.

**[D]** Who seeks, by subject: ordinary people commanded or described 16; the Prophet commanded or
addressed 12; earlier prophets and individuals 7; the Messenger as intercessor 2; angels 2; the
Prophet asked by the Bedouin 1. God is the named object in 21 of 40 tokens. The sin is named in only 5.

**[D]** `astaghfiru` occurs **3×** (12:98, 19:47, 60:4). All three are first person, future, and for
someone else, and *Allāh* is never their object. Jacob's (12:98) is never narrated as done. Ibrāhīm's
two promises for his father are withdrawn (9:114) and excepted from the model (60:4). After
normalisation, the consonants of *astaghfiru llāh* occur once, as the command `wa-staghfiri llāh` to
the Prophet (4:106). The plural `istaghfirū llāha` occurs at 2:199 and 73:20.

**[D]** The verb for the act and the quoted words share a verse **once**, the angels at 40:7.
Elsewhere the act is narrated without words (38:24, 3:135, 51:18), or the words are given as direct
speech without the verb (the petitions).

**[I]** The act is *named* in the third person and *performed* in the second. The traditional
*astaghfiru llāh* is a report ("I seek…"). The Quran's own model for oneself is a request made to Him.

## 2. The petition: skeleton and voice

**[D]** Phase 0 counted 22 quoted petitions. Strictly, **17 are `ighfir` requests** (2:286, 3:16,
3:147, 3:193, 7:151, 7:155, 14:41, 23:109, 23:118, 26:86, 28:16, 38:35, 40:7, 59:10, 60:5, 66:8,
71:28). The other five are 7:23 and 11:47 (conditional: *if You do not forgive…*), 2:285 (the noun
`ghufrānaka`), 26:82 (a hope), and 5:118 (ʿĪsā, conditional, for others).

> وَقُل رَّبِّ ٱغۡفِرۡ وَٱرۡحَمۡ وَأَنتَ خَيۡرُ ٱلرَّٰحِمِينَ
> **23:118** *And say, "My Lord, forgive and have mercy, and You are the best of the merciful."*
> The only petition commanded with `qul`. Six Arabic words after `wa-qul`, with neither "me" nor "us".

- **[D] Address.** `rabbi` 7, `rabbanā` 12, none 3.
- **[D] Voice.** Every singular *forgive me* is spoken by a prophet. All 8 petitions by non-prophet
  humans are plural (`ighfir lanā`). This is not a bar on the singular. 4:110 narrates one person
  asking; non-prophets pray `rabbi … lī` elsewhere (46:15, 66:11); 23:118 has no pronoun at all.
  **[I]** The text gives the act to the individual and the words to the group.
- **[D] Ground with `fa-` (*so*)**, in 6: faith (`āmannā`, 3:16, 3:193, 23:109), confession (28:16),
  an attribute of God (7:155, 40:7).
- **[D] Order.** Forgiveness comes before mercy in 7 of the 7 petitions that ask both. `ighfir` is
  the first request in 13 of 17. 2:286 climbs: pardon → forgive → mercy.
- **[D] Confession is rare.** Explicit self-wrong appears in 2 (7:23, 28:16). Sin is named as a noun
  in 4 more (`dhunūb` 3:16, 3:147, 3:193; `khaṭīʾa` 26:82). **15 of 22 confess nothing.** Yūnus's cry
  (21:87) is tawḥīd, tasbīḥ and confession, with no `gfr` word.
- **[D] Closings.** `khayr al-rāḥimīn` (23:109, 23:118), `arḥam al-rāḥimīn` (7:151), `khayr
  al-ghāfirīn` (7:155), `raʾūf raḥīm` (59:10), `al-Wahhāb` (38:35), `al-ʿAzīz al-Ḥakīm` (5:118, 40:8,
  60:5), `qadīr` (66:8). **`Ghafūr` never closes a forgiveness petition.** **[I]** The closing name
  often mirrors the companion request: `al-Wahhāb` after *grant me*, `qadīr` after *perfect our light*.
- **[D] Long forms** (40:7–9, 2:286, 3:193–194, 7:155) add what the forgiveness is *for*: shelter from
  the Fire and from disgrace, burdens lifted, firm feet, victory, dying with the righteous.
- **[D] Shortest.** `ghufrānaka rabbanā` (2:285) is two words. 23:118 is six, tying 28:16 and 26:86.
  23:109 is 23:118's twin, with `āmannā` and `lanā`, spoken by servants who were mocked for it (23:110).

> رَبِّ إِنِّي ظَلَمۡتُ نَفۡسِي فَٱغۡفِرۡ لِي فَغَفَرَ لَهُۥٓۚ
> **28:16** *"My Lord, indeed I have wronged myself, so forgive me," and He forgave him.* The only
> petition answered in its own verse.

**Skeleton [I reading of D]:** address → ground (`fa-`) → `ighfir` → mercy and other needs → a
closing name. The cleanest instance is 23:109.

## 3. Two stress tests

### 3a. A spoken act, or a change of conduct?

**[D] For "spoken."** The verb's object is God. The fruit hangs on the request in grammar of
certainty: jussive answers (11:3, 11:52, 71:11) and the conditional of 4:110 (*whoever does evil or
wrongs himself, then seeks God's forgiveness, will find God Forgiving, Merciful*). Only 27:46 hedges
(`laʿalla`). The repair verbs `aṣlaḥa` (8 verses) and `ʿamila ṣāliḥan` (5) follow tawba in 13
verses. A script over every istighfār ayah finds them attached to istighfār in **none**. 8:33: God
*would not punish them while they seek forgiveness*, said of people who obstruct the Sacred Mosque
(8:34).

**[D] For "change."** 3:135 writes the stopping into the istighfār sentence, and 3:136 calls the
reward *the wage of the doers*. 71:7 is its mirror: the root ṣ-r-r as a verb occurs only 4×, and
there Noah's people `aṣarrū wa-stakbarū`. 48:11 exposes a request spoken *with their tongues, not
in their hearts*. Of the 15 `istaghfir-` commands issued by God or a prophet, **13 carry a second
imperative to the same hearer in the same verse** (`tūbū` ×4, `istaqīmū`, `sabbiḥ` ×2, `iʿlam`,
`aqīmū…`, `afīḍū`, `uʿfu`, `adhan`, `bāyiʿ`).

> وَٱلَّذِينَ إِذَا فَعَلُواْ فَٰحِشَةً أَوۡ ظَلَمُوٓاْ أَنفُسَهُمۡ ذَكَرُواْ ٱللَّهَ فَٱسۡتَغۡفَرُواْ لِذُنُوبِهِمۡ وَمَن يَغۡفِرُ ٱلذُّنُوبَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ وَلَمۡ يُصِرُّواْ عَلَىٰ مَا فَعَلُواْ وَهُمۡ يَعۡلَمُونَ
> **3:135** *And those who, when they commit an immorality or wrong themselves, remember Allah and
> seek forgiveness for their sins (and who can forgive sins except Allah?) and do not persist in what
> they have done while they know.*

**Verdict [I reading of D]: a verbal request, with a condition on the one asking.** There are three
layers. (1) **The words** are the act. (2) **The stance** is that he is not *knowingly* persisting.
`lam yuṣirrū … wa-hum yaʿlamūn` describes a state; it is not a vow. (3) **The repair** belongs to
tawba. Istighfār needs (1) and (2). Every failure the text records is a failure at (2), or of heart
against tongue: persisting (71:7), arrogance (63:5, 71:7), a tongue without a heart (48:11). The
line runs between the words and the person's stance, not between the words and his record.

### 3b. For sins, or not waiting for one?

**[D]** Whenever the object is named, it is sin: `li-dhunūbihim` 3:135, `li-dhanbika` 40:55 and 47:19,
`dhunūbanā` 3:16, 3:147, 3:193, 12:97. Where a wrong has happened, the response is quick: 7 of the 8
occasioned prophetic cases ask in the same or the next verse. **But** of 15 prophetic units, **7
follow no stated event** (71:28, 14:41, 26:82, 4:106, 40:55, 47:19, 110:3). **None of the 8 believers'
petitions follows a narrated wrong**: they are standing prayers. The pre-dawn verses describe a habit.

**Verdict: the object is sin; the occasion need not be a known sin.** Three of the 7 unoccasioned
units still name a sin (26:82, 40:55, 47:19). **[I]** The text treats sin as present whether or not
one has been found: istighfār is *for* sin, not only *after* it. **[D]** The text never says that a
good deed itself needs forgiving.

## 4. Istighfār, tawba, setting right

> وَأَنِ ٱسۡتَغۡفِرُواْ رَبَّكُمۡ ثُمَّ تُوبُوٓاْ إِلَيۡهِ يُمَتِّعۡكُم مَّتَٰعًا حَسَنًا إِلَىٰٓ أَجَلࣲ مُّسَمࣰّى وَيُؤۡتِ كُلَّ ذِي فَضۡلࣲ فَضۡلَهُۥۖ
> **11:3** *Seek forgiveness of your Lord, then repent to Him; He will let you enjoy a good provision
> for a specified term and give every doer of favor his favor.*

- **[D]** Sūrat Hūd uses the formula `istaghfirū rabbakum thumma tūbū ilayhi` 4 times out of 4
  (11:3, 11:52, 11:61, 11:90). `thumma` makes it a sequence. 5:74 reverses the order (*turn to God
  and seek His forgiveness*). Nine verses hold both roots.
- **[D] Direction.** With a human subject, `tāba` takes `ilā` (*turn to*) in 12 verses. `tāba ʿalā`
  (*turn toward*, relenting) occurs in **22 verses, always with God as subject** (recounted here; the
  Phase-1 files said 16 and 19). Istighfār has no such movement. Its object is God Himself.
- **[D] The third act.** `aṣlaḥa` (2:160, 3:89, 4:16, 4:146, 5:39, 6:54, 16:119, 24:5) and `ʿamila
  ṣāliḥan` (19:60, 20:82, 25:70, 25:71, 28:67) attach to tawba, never to istighfār. Conduct verbs do
  travel with istighfār, though: 41:6 joins it to `istaqīmū ilayhi`.
- **[I]** Where the text names the setting-right, it mirrors the sin. Concealers make clear (2:159–160).
  Hypocrites hold fast and become sincere (4:145–146). Usurers keep only the principal (2:279).
- **[D] Other effects on a sin.** God forgives it (`ghafara`), removes it (`kaffara ʿan`, tied to
  charity, taqwā and avoiding major sins), replaces it with good (25:70, after tawba, faith and good
  work), and good deeds cancel it (11:114).

**[I]** Istighfār is a request put into words. Tawba is a change of direction. Setting right is the
repair. The text keeps the three distinct and puts them in sequence.

## 5. When

- **[D] Right after a wrong.** 3:135: `idhā … dhakarū … fa-staghfarū`. Adam and Moses ask in the
  next verse (7:22–23, 28:15–16). *Soon* (`min qarīb`, 4:17) is said of tawba.
- **[D] The last part of the night** (3:17, 51:17–18). Both verses describe the praised, in the plural
  and in the habitual. Neither is a command, and neither is addressed to the Prophet alone.
- **[D] Commanded at occasions:** after the outpouring from ʿArafāt (2:199), at the close of the
  night-prayer passage (73:20), after the victory (110:3, to the Prophet).
- **[D] Not tied to an hour by command.** 40:55's *evening and morning* attach to `sabbiḥ`, not to
  the istighfār. No ayah ties istighfār to the end of ṣalāt. What follows the prostrations in 50:40
  is tasbīḥ.
- **[D, hypothesis narrowed]** Phase 0's "istighfār follows worship or success" holds as *position*:
  5 of the 6 timed occurrences fall at an ending. It fails as a *reason*. Each ending names a lack
  nearby (2:198 *you were before that among the astray*; 73:20 *He knew you would not be able to do
  it*; the `muḥsinīn` of 51:16–18 still ask). The success cases name a `dhanb` (40:55, 47:19, 48:2).
  48:2 runs the other way: the victory is given *so that* God forgives.

## 6. With what

- **[D] Remembrance first.** 3:135 has `dhakarū llāha fa-staghfarū`, and 2:198–199 has *remember
  God … then depart … and seek forgiveness*. A statement of tawḥīd precedes istighfār or shares its
  verse in 7 ayahs (47:19: *know that there is no god but God, and seek forgiveness*). This is not a
  rule: 17 of the 22 petitions have no glorification, praise or remembrance in the three verses before.
- **[D] Glorify with praise, then ask:** 4× (the angels at 40:7 and 42:5; commanded at 40:55 and 110:3).
- **[D] Body and vow.** David asked, bowed and turned (38:24). Moses made his vow (28:17) *after* he
  was forgiven, not as its price. Outside the prophets, the magicians made one too (20:72).

## 7. For whom

- **[D] Circles.** Yourself; parents (14:41, 71:28); a brother (7:151); *whoever enters my house a
  believer*, then all believing men and women (71:28); those who preceded you in faith (59:10). The
  angels ask for the believers (40:7–9) and for those on earth (42:5).
- **[D] Self first.** 5 of the 7 imperative petitions that reach others include the speaker. 26:86
  does so at passage level (26:82), and the angels never do. This does **not** hold for the Prophet's
  commanded istighfār for others (3:159, 24:62, 60:12 have no self-clause).
- **[D] Barred and void.** For associators, even kin, the bar comes once it *has become clear*
  (`tabayyana`) that they are of the Fire (9:113–114). For the hypocrites it is void (9:80, 63:6). It
  is not barred for believers who sin (4:64, 12:98, 47:19). 14:41 and 26:86 stand unretracted, and
  the text does not reconcile them with 9:114.
- **[D] Asking another to ask.** In 4:64 they come *and seek God's forgiveness themselves*, and the
  Messenger seeks it for them. In 12:97 the sons confess first. 48:11 is exposed as tongue-only. An
  outcome of intercessory istighfār is stated once, and only conditionally (4:64). **[I]** Asking
  another is never shown replacing one's own asking. It is shown as care, not as a mechanism.

## 8. What follows

- **[D]** The promises are stated as certain. The one hedge is 27:46.
- **[D] Worldly fruit** (enjoyment, rain, strength, wealth, sons, gardens) is promised in **3** of the
  8 plural commands (11:3, 11:52, 71:10–12). All 3 were addressed to peoples refusing a messenger. The
  two plural commands to believers (2:199, 73:20) close only on `ghafūr raḥīm`. The believers'
  reward is *forgiveness from their Lord and gardens* (3:136).
- **[D] Grant recorded** in 3 of 15 prophetic units (28:16, 38:25; Adam via `tāba ʿalayhi`, 2:37),
  and to the Prophet as God's pledge (48:2). `Ghafūr raḥīm` closes **71** ayahs. 10 concern seeking
  forgiveness, the act or its answer; 30 follow repentance or a concession; only 5 stand beside
  punishment. **[I]** The text records the asking more than the reply, and in place of the reply it
  gives the Name.

## 9. What blocks it, and the limit

- **[D] Stated blocks:** arrogance (63:5, 71:7), persisting (71:7), a tongue without a heart (48:11),
  waiting to see the punishment first (18:55). For others: shirk once its end is clear (9:113), and
  `fisq` (9:80, 63:6). **Not stated as blocks:** the size of the sin, ignorance of the right words, shame.
- **[D] Size.** 39:53 says *God forgives all sins* and *do not despair*, and 39:54 adds *turn … before
  the punishment comes*. 25:68–70 excepts whoever turns, believes and does good from a list that opens
  with *invoking another god*, and promises **replacement** of their bad deeds by good.
- **[D] The stated limit.** *God does not forgive that partners be associated with Him* (4:48, 4:116).
  Neither verse contains a `twb` or `nwb` word. Every verse that joins association or disbelief with
  turning or ceasing gives forgiveness (5:73–74, 2:54, 7:152–153, 4:153, 8:38). Time limits tawba
  (4:18: not when death has come, not for one who dies a disbeliever). **This investigation does not
  reconcile 4:48 with these verses.** It reports them side by side. **[I]** The limit the text sets on
  *asking* is time, not size.

## 11. The ḥiṭṭa: the one formula given to a people

> وَٱدۡخُلُواْ ٱلۡبَابَ سُجَّدࣰا وَقُولُواْ حِطَّةࣱ نَّغۡفِرۡ لَكُمۡ خَطَٰيَٰكُمۡۚ وَسَنَزِيدُ ٱلۡمُحۡسِنِينَ
> **2:58** *…and enter the gate bowing humbly and say, "ḥiṭṭatun" [unburdening]. We will forgive your
> errors, and We will increase the doers of good.*

**[D]** The root ḤṬṬ occurs twice (2:58, 7:161), both times as this word. Among all `gfr` ayahs,
only two "say" commands introduce a petition: `qūlū ḥiṭṭatun` (to a community, with a promise) and
`qul rabbi ighfir wa-rḥam` (23:118, to the Prophet, with no promise). The ḥiṭṭa was bound to a gate
and a posture. The two tellings differ in order, verb and plural, but **`ḥiṭṭatun` is identical in
both**. Those who wronged changed it, and were punished (2:59, 7:162). The text never says what they
said instead. **[I]** The one formula ever given is a single noun asking for a load to be set down.
It confesses nothing and names no attribute. Al-Baqara frames it: the word is changed (2:59), Israel
says *we hear and disobey* (2:93), and the surah closes *we hear and obey, ghufrānaka rabbanā*
(2:285). For this community the Quran fixes the act and gives models, not a liturgy.

## 12. People forgiving people

> وَلۡيَعۡفُواْ وَلۡيَصۡفَحُوٓاْۗ أَلَا تُحِبُّونَ أَن يَغۡفِرَ ٱللَّهُ لَكُمۡۚ وَٱللَّهُ غَفُورࣱ رَّحِيمٌ
> **24:22** *…and let them pardon and overlook. Would you not like that Allah should forgive you? And
> Allah is Forgiving and Merciful.*

**[D]** Humans forgive with `ʿafā` (11 verbs) far more than with `ghafara` (4). Of 14
forgiving-others verses, **2 tie human pardon explicitly to God's forgiveness**. 24:22 does it as a
question, addressed to people of means who had sworn off helping kin and the needy. 64:14 does it as a
condition, about spouses and children: *pardon, overlook, forgive → God is Forgiving, Merciful*.
Joseph forgives and invokes God's forgiveness in one breath (12:92). 3:159 gives an order: *pardon
them, seek forgiveness for them, consult them*. 59:10 joins istighfār for the predecessors to *no
rancour in our hearts*. Pardon is paired with reconciliation (42:40) and with caution (64:14). **[I]**
24:22 is framed as a question, not a contract.

## 13. Corrections the investigation made to itself

1. **`ghafūr raḥīm` 56 → 71** (`tawwāb raḥīm` 7 → 9, `ʿafuww ghafūr` 3 → 4). The names agent found
   15 `kāna ghafūran raḥīman` that Phase 0 missed. The cause was a **data bug**: `quran.json` writes
   open-fathatan tanwīn with a space before the final alif (2,054 tokens, 1,533 ayahs), so exact-string
   formula searches silently missed every such word. The bug was fixed in both census scripts and added
   to `notes/agent-context.md`.
2. Mercy is requested in **7** petitions, not 10 (28:16 is the narrator's names, 40:7 the ground,
   59:10 the closing).
3. `Zlm` self-confession is **2**, not 3 (71:28's `ẓālimīn` are the people Noah wants destroyed).
4. "22 petitions" contains **17 `ighfir` requests**.
5. The speakers of 3:147 are the `ribbiyyūn` who fought with prophets, not prophets.
6. The "endings after worship/success" hypothesis was **narrowed** to *istighfār does not wait for a
   known wrong* (§5).
7. 40:55's time phrase attaches to `sabbiḥ`.
8. Worldly promises: **3** of the six commands to non-believers, not 6 (the commands file's headline;
   its own table was right).
9. Prophetic units **16 → 15** (48:1–2 is a grant). Unoccasioned units 8 → 7.
10. Phase-2 corrections: "tawba's fruits hedged, istighfār's flat" is not a contrast (4 of 60 vs 1).
    41:6 carries a conduct verb with istighfār. "`Ghafūr`/`Tawwāb` never close a human petition" holds
    for forgiveness petitions only (2:128, 14:36). "Self always first" holds for petitions, not for the
    Prophet's commanded istighfār. The ḥiṭṭa is not *the* formula (23:118 is a second). 28:17 is the
    only vow *among prophets* (20:72 is another). The tahajjud synthesis's "only devotion pinned to an
    hour" holds only outside ṣalāt.
11. **This synthesis:** 23:118 is six words, not five, and 2:285's `ghufrānaka rabbanā` is shorter.
    `tāba ʿalā` occurs in 22 verses, not 16 or 19. 7:23 is a dual, Adam and his wife together.

## Open questions

1. How does 4:48/4:116 fit beside 5:73–74, 25:68–70, 39:53 and 8:38? This was not reconciled here.
2. 8:33 credits istighfār to obstructers; 48:11 voids a tongue-only request. The text does not
   reconcile them.
3. **Relapse.** `wa-hum yaʿlamūn` limits the condition to *knowing* persistence, and `idhā` with the
   perfect frames the cycle as one that recurs. The text says nothing more direct.
4. Is the believers' "us" a model or an accident of who is quoted? 4:110 shows one person asking alone.
5. Why do the timed istighfārs cluster at endings? The text gives no reason.
6. Ibrāhīm's 14:41 and 26:86 against his withdrawal in 9:114.
7. Why does `ghafūr`, the commonest name in the narrator's replies, never close a forgiveness petition?
8. The highest-divergence verses in the field are 3:17 (0.756), 51:18 (0.701) and 8:33 (0.688).
   No divergence-panel pass was run on them.

## For practice

- Speak **to** Him: `rabbi ighfir lī`, or with others `rabbanā ighfir lanā`. The shortest commanded
  form is 23:118. *Astaghfiru llāh* is permitted and answers the command, but the model is address.
- When you know the wrong: remember God, ask at once, and do not knowingly carry on (3:135). When you
  don't know of one, ask anyway: before dawn, and at the close of a prayer, a pilgrimage or a success.
- If you give a reason, join it with *so*. Ask forgiveness first, then mercy, then what it is for.
  Close by naming Him the best of those who forgive or show mercy.
- Follow it by turning (`tūbū`), and set right what the wrong broke. Repair belongs to the turning.
- Carry others in after yourself: parents, household, the believers, those who came before. Ask for a
  heart without rancour (59:10). Pardon the person who wronged you (24:22).
- No number, no hour, no tears are required. No sin is too large to ask about while there is time.
