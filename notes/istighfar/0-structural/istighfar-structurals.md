# Structural pre-cut — istighfār (seeking forgiveness)

> Deterministic layer. Counted, not interpreted, unless flagged **[interpretive]**.
> `scripts/build_istighfar_census.py` → `data/structural/istighfar-census.json`;
> `scripts/build_istighfar_versepack.py` → `data/structural/istighfar-versepack.json` (195 verses, 11 groups).

## Headline finding

The Quran names the act `istighfār` (form X, *to seek forgiveness*, 40×) but **never once puts the
popular first-person formula in a believer's mouth for himself.** `astaghfiru` (*I seek forgiveness*)
occurs **exactly 3 times — 12:98, 19:47, 60:4 — and all three are a promise to seek forgiveness *for
someone else*** (Jacob for his sons; Ibrāhīm, twice, for his father), all in the future (`sawfa`,
`sa-`, `la-…nna`). Whenever the Quran quotes a person seeking forgiveness *for himself*, he speaks
**to** God, in the imperative: **`rabbi ighfir lī`** — *my Lord, forgive me* (17 imperatives, plus
4 conditional / nominal forms: 22 quoted petitions). The act is named in the third person; it is
performed in the second.

The noun *istighfār* — the name of the practice — occurs once in the Quran, and it is attached to a
seeking-of-forgiveness that was **abandoned** (9:114: *when it became clear to him that he was an
enemy of God, he disassociated himself*).

## 2. The imperative audit — who is told, who asks

**`istaghfir-` imperatives (19):**

| Person | Count | Refs | To whom |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2MP (*you all*) | 8 | 2:199, 11:3, 11:52, 11:61, 11:90, 41:6, 71:10, 73:20 | a community |
| 2MS (*you*, one) | 10 | 3:159, 4:106, 9:80, 12:97, 24:62, 40:55, 47:19, 48:11, 60:12, 110:3 | the Prophet ×8; Jacob (12:97, by his sons); the Prophet (48:11, by the Bedouin) |
| 2FS | 1 | 12:29 | the governor's wife: *seek forgiveness for your sin* |

- **5 of the 8 plural commands are in prophets' sermons to their peoples**: 11:3 (Muhammad), 11:52
  (Hūd), 11:61 (Ṣāliḥ), 11:90 (Shuʿayb), 71:10 (Noah). **Four of them are in Sūrat Hūd**, and those
  four share one formula: **`istaghfirū rabbakum thumma tūbū ilayhi`** — *seek your Lord's forgiveness,
  then turn to Him* (4/4). Istighfār and tawba are distinct acts, in that order.
- **The Prophet is commanded istighfār 8 times: 4 for himself** (4:106, 40:55, 47:19, 110:3) **and 4
  for others** (3:159, 24:62, 60:12; 9:80 for the hypocrites — futile).

**`ighfir` — the direct petition to God (17 imperatives, 2MS addressed to God):** 2:286, 3:16, 3:147,
3:193, 7:151, 7:155, 14:41, 23:109, 23:118, 26:86, 28:16, 38:35, 40:7, 59:10, 60:5, 66:8, 71:28. Plus
**7:23** and **11:47** (conditional: *if You do not forgive us / me…*), **2:285** (`ghufrānaka`),
**26:82** (Ibrāhīm: *I hope He will forgive me my error on the Day*), **5:118** (ʿĪsā, for others).

Speakers [orchestrator reading, for the agents to verify]: prophets (Adam & Eve, Noah, Ibrāhīm,
Moses, Sulaymān, ʿĪsā; the Prophet commanded at 23:118), believers' groups (2:286, 3:16, 3:147,
3:193, 23:109, 59:10, 66:8), angels (40:7).

## 4. Root lift — what is over-represented in the 59 core ayahs

`*nb` sin ×23 · `xTA` error ×21 · **`twb` ×15 (10 ayahs)** · `rHm` ×7.7 (23 ayahs) · **`Hmd` praise ×6.9**
· `ftn` ×5.5 · **`sbH` glorify ×4.8** · `Abw` father ×4.1 · `Axw` brother ×4.0 · `wld` parent ×4.0.

- **Glorify + praise + seek forgiveness is a fixed triad, 4×**: 40:7 and 42:5 (the angels:
  `yusabbiḥūna bi-ḥamdi rabbihim … wa-yastaghfirūna`), 40:55 and 110:3 (commanded to the Prophet:
  `sabbiḥ bi-ḥamdi rabbika … wa-staghfir`). Every `sbH` hit in the core is also a `Hmd` hit.
- **Kin**: istighfār for a father, brother, parents or sons — 9:114, 12:97-98, 19:47, 26:86, 60:4,
  7:151, 14:41, 71:28 (8 ayahs). (`Axw` at 11:61 is the prophet-as-brother formula; `wld` at 60:12 is
  about children, not prayer — both excluded.)

Core is **30 Meccan / 29 Medinan** — balanced, unlike most devotional vocabulary.

## 5. When [interpretive hypothesis — test it]

Time-anchored istighfār: **3:17, 51:18** (`bi-l-asḥār`, the last part of the night — and 51:17 says
they had slept little of the night before it); **2:199** (after pouring out from ʿArafāt, inside the
Ḥajj); **73:20** (the last command of the night-prayer surah, after reciting and establishing prayer);
**110:3** (after the victory and the crowds entering the religion); **40:55** (morning and evening).

**[interpretive]** Most of these are **endings** — the end of the night, the end of the standing at
ʿArafa, the end of a night of prayer, the end of a mission — and several **follow worship or
success, not sin**. If that holds, the Quran teaches istighfār as the closing act of *good* deeds as
well as the remedy for bad ones. Build this case, then try to break it.

## For practice

- When the Quran shows someone asking forgiveness for himself, he talks **to** God: *my Lord, forgive
  me*. The words are direct, short, and addressed.
- Seeking forgiveness and turning back are two acts, named separately, in that order (11:3, 11:52,
  11:61, 11:90).
- Four times, seeking forgiveness comes right after glorifying God with His praise.
- Several of the Quran's times for it are endings — the last part of the night, after ʿArafa, after
  the night prayer, after victory.
