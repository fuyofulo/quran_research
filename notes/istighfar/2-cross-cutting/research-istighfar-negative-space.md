# Negative space: what the Quran does not say about seeking forgiveness

## Headline finding

The Quran commands istighfār but almost never fixes its words, count or hour. *Astaghfiru* appears only as a promise made for someone else (12:98, 19:47, 60:4). The consonants of *astaghfiru llāh* occur once, as the command to the Prophet (4:106). The one formula given to a community is a single noun, *ḥiṭṭatun* (*unburdening*, 2:58, 7:161). It was tied to a gate and a posture, and changing it was punished (2:59, 7:162). The only number is seventy, and it measures futility (9:80). Of the four fiqh conditions of tawba, the text states ceasing. Resolve and restitution appear only in particular cases; regret is never required.

## 1. The formula *astaghfiru llāh*

**Traditional practice:** say *astaghfiru llāh* ("I seek God's forgiveness"), often and on its own.

**Text [D]:** `istaghfara` has 40 verbs (19 imperative, 16 imperfect, 5 perfect). Three are first-person singular: 12:98, 19:47, 60:4.
- All three are future and all three are for someone else (`la-kum`, `la-ka`).
- In none of them is *Allāh* the object.
- Two of the three are repudiated (9:114, 60:4).

After normalization, the consonantal string *ʾstghfr ʾllh* occurs **once: 4:106, `wa-staghfiri llāh`**, the imperative to the Prophet. The plural `istaghfirū llāha` occurs at 2:199 and 73:20. Every quoted self-directed petition is spoken **to** God, `rabbi/rabbanā ighfir` (17 imperatives).

**Verdict: permitted, not stated.** [I] The formula is the first-person answer to the Quran's own command, and nothing in the text resists it. But no one in the text says it about himself. When the text shows the act being done, the speaker addresses God directly.

## 2. The one formula a people was told to say

> وَإِذۡ قُلۡنَا ٱدۡخُلُواْ هَٰذِهِ ٱلۡقَرۡيَةَ فَكُلُواْ مِنۡهَا حَيۡثُ شِئۡتُمۡ رَغَدࣰا وَٱدۡخُلُواْ ٱلۡبَابَ سُجَّدࣰا وَقُولُواْ حِطَّةࣱ نَّغۡفِرۡ لَكُمۡ خَطَٰيَٰكُمۡۚ وَسَنَزِيدُ ٱلۡمُحۡسِنِينَ — **2:58** *Enter this city and eat from it wherever you will, in abundance; enter the gate bowing humbly and say "ḥiṭṭatun". We will forgive your errors, and We will increase the doers of good.*

> فَبَدَّلَ ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُواْ قَوۡلًا غَيۡرَ ٱلَّذِي قِيلَ لَهُمۡ فَأَنزَلۡنَا عَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُواْ رِجۡزࣰا مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ بِمَا كَانُواْ يَفۡسُقُونَ — **2:59** *But those who wronged changed it to a saying other than what had been said to them, so We sent down upon them a punishment from the sky for their defiance.*

**Data [D].**
- **Uniqueness.** The root ḤṬṬ occurs **twice in the Quran**, both times as this word.
- **Only two "say this petition" formulas.** I searched every ayah containing `gfr` for a "say" imperative. Only two introduce a petition: `qūlū ḥiṭṭatun` (2MP, 2:58, 7:161) and `qul rabbi ighfir wa-rḥam` (2MS to the Prophet, 23:118, with no promise attached). Every other `qul` introduces a proclamation.
- **What comes with the word.** A place, a posture (`sujjadan`), a promise (`naghfir lakum khaṭāyākum`), and a surplus for the doers of good.
- **The two tellings differ** in the order (gate then word, or word then gate), in the plural of *errors* (`khaṭāyā` / `khaṭīʾāt`) and in the verb (`udkhulū` / `uskunū`). **`ḥiṭṭatun` is identical in both.**
- **The alteration.** 7:162 says it was those who wronged *of them*. It gives the cause as `yaẓlimūn`, where 2:59 has `yafsuqūn`. **The text never says what they said instead.**

**Implications [I].**
1. The only time God tells a community "say this and We will forgive you", the formula is **one noun**. It asks for a load to be put down. It does not confess, and it names no attribute of God. Its nearest relative is the believers' `ghufrānaka` (2:285).
2. It was bound to a threshold and a posture, and the text never extends it to anyone else.
3. What was punished was **replacing a given wording with one's own**. The retellings vary everything around the word except the word itself. The speakers were not Arabic-speaking (14:4). So what was fixed was **what they were told to ask for**, not a string of letters.
4. Al-Baqara frames it. The word is changed (2:59). Israel says *samiʿnā wa-ʿaṣaynā* (2:93). The surah ends with *samiʿnā wa-aṭaʿnā, ghufrānaka rabbanā* (2:285).
5. For this community, the Quran fixes the **act** (`istaghfirū`) and gives **models**. It gives no liturgy with a promise attached.

## 3. Numbers and repetition

**Traditional practice:** a set count (100 a day; 70).

**Text [D]:** Across the 38 ayahs containing an istighfār word, the only number is **9:80**. The *third of the night* in 73:20 measures the prayer, not istighfār.

> ٱسۡتَغۡفِرۡ لَهُمۡ أَوۡ لَا تَسۡتَغۡفِرۡ لَهُمۡ إِن تَسۡتَغۡفِرۡ لَهُمۡ سَبۡعِينَ مَرَّةࣰ فَلَن يَغۡفِرَ ٱللَّهُ لَهُمۡ — **9:80** *Ask forgiveness for them or do not. If you should ask forgiveness for them seventy times, never will God forgive them.*

The seventy is conditional, addressed to the Prophet, and about asking **for others**. It measures **futility**: no count moves forgiveness for those who *disbelieved in God and His Messenger*. (*Sabʿīn* occurs 3×: 7:155, seventy men; 9:80; 69:32.)

**Contrast [D].**
- Remembrance is quantified with *kathīran* in **10 ayahs**: 3:41, 8:45, 20:33, 20:34, 22:40, 26:227, 33:21, 33:35, 33:41, 62:10.
- Istighfār is quantified in **0**. The *kathīran* at 38:24 means *many partners*.
- A string search without the tanwīn fix finds only 1 of the dhikr hits.
- The text marks istighfār's frequency by **aspect** instead: the habitual imperfect (51:18 `hum yastaghfirūn`; 8:33; angels 40:7, 42:5) and the participle (3:17).

**Verdict: silent.** [I] Istighfār is a habit and an identity, not a tally. Counting is permitted, not stated.

## 4. Fixed times

**Text [D]:** In the 38 istighfār ayahs, a clock-time word appears in four:
- **3:17 and 51:18** (`al-asḥār`, the pre-dawn). Both **describe** the God-fearing; neither commands.
- **40:55** (evening and morning). The phrase attaches to `sabbiḥ`, not clearly to the istighfār.
- **73:20** (the night). The time words belong to the prayer.

**Commanded** istighfār is tied to **occasions**: after the outpouring from ʿArafāt (2:199), after the night-prayer concession (73:20), and after the victory (110:3, to the Prophet). **Described** occasions: right after a wrong (3:135 `dhakarū llāha fa-staghfarū`), and *soon* for tawba (4:17).

**After prayer:** no ayah ties istighfār to the end of ṣalāt. What comes *after the prostrations* in 50:40 is **tasbīḥ** (2MS).

The tahajjud synthesis calls asḥār-istighfār the only devotional act pinned to a named hour. That needs a small correction: ṣalāt has named hours too (24:58, 17:78), so the claim holds only for devotions other than prayer.

## 5. The fiqh conditions of tawba against the text's own

> وَٱلَّذِينَ إِذَا فَعَلُواْ فَٰحِشَةً أَوۡ ظَلَمُوٓاْ أَنفُسَهُمۡ ذَكَرُواْ ٱللَّهَ فَٱسۡتَغۡفَرُواْ لِذُنُوبِهِمۡ وَمَن يَغۡفِرُ ٱلذُّنُوبَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ وَلَمۡ يُصِرُّواْ عَلَىٰ مَا فَعَلُواْ وَهُمۡ يَعۡلَمُونَ — **3:135** *Those who, when they commit an indecency or wrong themselves, remember God and seek forgiveness for their sins (and who forgives sins except God?), and do not persist in what they did while they know.*

|---|---|---|
| **Ceasing** | 3:135 `lam yuṣirrū`. `intahā` with forgiveness in 3 verses: 2:192, 2:275, 8:38 (`in yantahū yughfar lahum mā qad salaf`). 4:18 bars tawba for whoever persists until death | **stated** |
| **Regret** | Never commanded (regret synthesis: `ndm`/`ḥasra` have no verb, and nothing tells anyone to regret). The 7 `ndm` ayahs contain no tawba or istighfār. A felt state is described once (9:118). At 7:149, Saheeh's "regret" renders *it fell into their hands* | **points elsewhere**: the text commands *return* |
| **Resolve** | Two vows: Moses 28:17 (after forgiveness) and the magicians 20:72 (before their hope of it, 20:73). These are the only `lan` + first-person vows within 2 ayahs of `gfr`/`twb`, apart from a refusal at 2:55. *Returning* is threatened right after the past is pardoned (2:275, 5:95, 8:38). 24:17: *God admonishes you never to return to its like* | **implied** |
| **Restitution** | `tāba` + `aṣlaḥa` in **8 verses** (2:160, 3:89, 4:16, 4:146, 5:39, 6:54, 16:119, 24:5). 46:15 is excluded because its `aṣliḥ` is a petition for offspring. The object is unstated in 6 of the 8. **The Quran's own conditions, in its words [D]:**
- `dhakarū llāha` and `lam yuṣirrū` (3:135)
- `tābū ilā`
- `aṣlaḥū`
- `āmanū wa-ʿamilū ṣāliḥan` (25:70, 20:82)
- `bayyanū` (2:160)
- `iʿtaṣamū … akhlaṣū` (4:146)
- `min qarīb` (4:17), before death or before the punishment (4:18, 39:54)

[I] Where the text names the setting-right, it **mirrors the sin**:
- concealers make clear (2:159 → 2:160)
- hypocrites hold fast and become sincere (4:145 → 4:146)
- usurers keep only the principal (2:279)

## 6. Emotion, tears, intermediaries

**Traditional practice:** weep and feel contrition.

**Text [D]:**
- Weeping occurs in 9 ayahs (`bky` 7, `dmʿ` 2). **None is over the weeper's own sin.**
- The praised tears are at revelation (5:83, 17:109, 19:58) or at having nothing to give (9:92).
- The one weeping beside a sin is **staged to hide it** (12:16, Joseph's brothers).
- 3:135 names an act of mind (`dhakarū llāha`), not an emotion. `awwāh` (9:114, 11:75) is a trait praised in Ibrāhīm, not a requirement.
- What is tested is sincerity: *they say with their tongues what is not in their hearts* (48:11).

**Verdict: silent** on a required feeling. The text **points to** heart and tongue agreeing.

**Traditional practice:** an intermediary helps.

**Text [D]:**
- *Who forgives sins except God?* (3:135).
- 4:110: whoever seeks God's forgiveness *will find God Forgiving, Merciful*, with no one between.
- 4:64 is counterfactual, and **their own istighfār comes first**: *they came, sought God's forgiveness, and the Messenger sought it for them*.
- The Messenger's istighfār is void for those who rejected (9:80, 63:6).

**Verdict: permitted, not required, never a substitute.**

## 7. "My sin is too big"

> إِلَّا مَن تَابَ وَءَامَنَ وَعَمِلَ عَمَلࣰا صَٰلِحࣰا فَأُوْلَٰٓئِكَ يُبَدِّلُ ٱللَّهُ سَيِّـَٔاتِهِمۡ حَسَنَٰتࣲۗ وَكَانَ ٱللَّهُ غَفُورࣰا رَّحِيمࣰا — **25:70** *Except whoever repents, believes and does righteous work: for them God will replace their evil deeds with good. God is ever Forgiving, Merciful.*

**What sits next to what [D]. I do not reconcile these.**
- **25:68–70.** The list excepted begins with *invoking another god*, then killing, then fornication. The exception requires `tāba`, `āmana` and `ʿamila ṣāliḥan`, and the result is **replacement**.
- **39:53.** *Forgives all sins* sits beside *do not despair*. **39:54** follows: *turn back and submit before the punishment comes*. It is a turn with a deadline, and no sins are excluded.
- **3:135.** The first case named is `fāḥisha`.
- **4:48 / 4:116.** *God does not forgive association.* **Neither verse contains a `twb` or `nwb` word.** Its neighbours are People of the Book and self-purifiers (4:47, 4:49), and opposing the Messenger and idols (4:115, 4:117).
- **Every verse that joins association or disbelief with tawba or ceasing gives forgiveness:** 5:73–74 (*third of three … will they not turn to God and seek His forgiveness?*), the calf (2:54, 7:152–153, 4:153 *We pardoned that*), and 8:38.
- **Time limits tawba:** death, or dying a disbeliever (4:18).

**Verdict: the text points elsewhere.** It never measures a sin as too large to ask about. It names despair of mercy as the trait of *the astray* (15:56).

## 8. Other absences

- **Hiding sin from people.** No petition asks for it: `str` and `khfy` occur in 0 petitions. Where petitions fear disgrace, it is disgrace **on the Day** (3:194, 26:87). 4:108, inside the istighfār passage, **censures** those who *hide from people but cannot hide from God*. **Silent.**
- **Itemising sins.** Believers' petitions name sin only generically (`dhunūb`, `sayyiʾāt`, `isrāf`, forgetting and erring). Only **11:47** (Noah) names his act inside the petition. 28:16 and 7:23 confess *self-wrong*, and the act itself is told in the narrative. **Silent.**
- **Confessing to a person.** This is never commanded. It happens only toward **those wronged**: Joseph's brothers to Joseph (12:91) and to their father (12:97). Joseph answers, *God will forgive you* (12:92).
- **Rain, wealth, children.** These are **stated, to unbelieving peoples** (11:3, 11:52, 71:10–12). The two plural commands to believers (2:199, 73:20) promise only God's names, and the practitioners' reward is *forgiveness and gardens* (3:136).

## Phase-1 claims corrected

1. **The headline of `narrative-commanded-and-its-fruits.md`** says "only those six carry worldly promises". **Three of the six do** (11:3, 11:52, 71:10–12). 11:61 and 11:90 close on divine names, and 41:6 on *woe to the associators*. The file's own §3 table is correct.
2. **The ḥiṭṭa is not "the" prescribed formula.** It is the only one given to a **community** with a promise. 23:118 is a second, given to the Prophet with `qul`.
3. **`narrative-prophets-seeking-forgiveness.md` Q3** calls 28:17 the only behavioural commitment. That holds among prophets. Outside them, the magicians' vow (20:72) is a second.

## For practice

- Say *astaghfiru llāh* freely: it answers *istaghfirū llāh*. For your own sins, also speak **to** Him: *rabbi ighfir lī*.
- Don't aim at a number. The text praises people who **keep** asking (51:18). Make it a habit, not a tally.
- The pre-dawn is the one hour the text praises. Otherwise ask at endings, and **right after a wrong** (3:135).
- Measure yourself by the text's conditions, not your tears: remember God, ask, stop persisting, turn, and **set right what the sin broke**. Disclose what you hid, and pay what you owe (2:160, 2:279).
- No one has to stand between you and God. Asking someone to pray for you comes **after** asking yourself (4:64).
- No sin is too big to ask about. The text's limit is the deadline, not the size of the sin (4:18, 39:53–54, 25:70).
