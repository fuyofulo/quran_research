# Narrative: seeking forgiveness for others, where it is void, and people forgiving people

## Headline finding

The Quran shows istighfār for others in three layers: the Prophet for believers (mostly unasked, commanded), prophets for kin (always inside a petition that also names themselves), and angels and later believers for all believers. It also has one hard limit. Istighfār for the associators is barred even for kin (9:113), and for the hypocrites it is void (9:80, 63:6). The bar falls on the *seeker*, and is triggered when the beneficiary's state "becomes clear" to him. When a person asks another to seek forgiveness for him, the text calls it genuine only when he has already confessed and turned to God himself (4:64, 12:97). Human pardon links to God's forgiveness explicitly in only 2 of 14 verses (24:22, 64:14).

## 1. The map: who seeks for whom [data-grounded]

| Seeker | Beneficiary | Type | Asked by beneficiary? | Outcome stated |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prophet | believers, after pardon + before consulting (3:159) | community | no, commanded | none stated |

| Jacob | his sons (12:98) | kin | yes (12:97) | none narrated; God named *the Forgiving, the Merciful* |
| Noah | parents, whoever enters his house a believer, all believers (71:28) | kin + community | no | none stated |
| Ibrāhīm | parents + believers (14:41) | kin + community | no | none stated |
| Moses | brother Aaron (7:151) | kin | no | none stated |
| Angels | those who believed (40:7-9) | all believers | no | content: forgive "those who repented and followed Your way", admit to Eden |
| Angels | "those on earth" (42:5) | all on earth | no | "God is the Forgiving, the Merciful" |
| Later believers | themselves + earlier believers (59:10) | community | no | none; paired with *no resentment in our hearts* |

**Counts.** 14 rows. The beneficiary asked in 1 (12:97), invited in 1 (63:5), conditional in 1 (4:64). Elsewhere it is unasked. An outcome is stated in exactly one case, and it is conditional (4:64). In every other row the only "outcome" is a closing divine name or nothing. The text gives no narrated fulfilment even for Jacob: `sawfa astaghfiru` is the last word on it. **[interpretive]** Intercessory istighfār in the Quran is an act of care, not a mechanism with promised results.

**Self first.** In the petitions, nobody seeks for others without including himself. 7:151, 14:41, 71:28, 59:10 and 47:19 all put self before others (`ighfir lī wa-li…`, `li-dhanbika wa-lil-muʾminīn`). 26:86 looks like the exception, but the same speaker places himself at 26:82. The angels (40:7) are the only seekers with no self-clause. Phase 0's "several prophets seek for others" is true; the text adds that the seeker is never merely a donor.

**Addressee check.** The 2MS commands for others (3:159, 24:62, 60:12, 47:19) are to the Prophet alone. The transferable form is the petition (59:10, 71:28, 14:41) **[interpretive]**.

## 2. Who asks to be prayed for [data-grounded, then interpretive]

- **12:97** the sons: first *confession* (`innā kunnā khāṭiʾīn`), then the request. Jacob's answer is `sawfa`: he defers, and says only that God is the Forgiving, the Merciful.
- **4:64** the pattern: they wronged themselves, *came*, **sought forgiveness of God themselves**, *and* the Messenger sought it for them. Their own istighfār comes first and is named.
- **48:11** the Bedouin who stayed behind: an excuse (`shaghalatnā amwālunā wa-ahlūnā`), then `fa-staghfir lanā`. The text exposes it: *they say with their tongues what is not in their hearts.* The Prophet's reply turns to God's sole control of harm and benefit.
- **63:5** *Come, the Messenger of God will seek forgiveness for you*: they turn their heads and are `mustakbirūn` (arrogant). The invitation is refused.

**What separates genuine from empty [interpretive, from these four]:** a request is genuine when (a) it comes with an admission of the wrong, (b) the asker is himself turning to God, and (c) heart and tongue agree. It is empty when it carries an excuse, a mouth-only formula, or arrogance. Asking is *never* shown as a substitute for one's own istighfār.

## 3. Where it is forbidden or void [data-grounded]

| Verse | What it says | Trigger | Limit is on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9:113 | not for the Prophet and the believers to seek forgiveness for **al-mushrikīn**, *even if kin* | `min baʿdi mā tabayyana lahum annahum aṣḥābu l-jaḥīm`: after it became clear to *them* | seeker, triggered by beneficiary's state |
| 9:114 | Ibrāhīm's istighfār was only `ʿan mawʿidatin`; when it became clear (`tabayyana lahu`) that his father was an enemy of God, `tabarraʾa` | same | seeker withdraws |
| 9:80 | "seek forgiveness for them or do not": seventy times, *never* will God forgive | `kafarū bi-llāhi wa-rasūlih`; God does not guide the `fāsiqīn` | the *outcome*; the act is neither commanded nor prohibited here |
| 63:6 | `sawāʾun ʿalayhim`: same for them whether you seek or not | `fāsiqīn` | beneficiary |
| 60:4 | Ibrāhīm is a model *except* his saying `la-astaghfiranna laka`, with `mā amliku laka mina llāhi min shayʾ` | the people persist in what they worship; enmity "until you believe" | the seeker: do not imitate this part |

**Reading it exactly.** Three different things are in play. 9:113 is a *prohibition* on the believers. 9:80 and 63:6 say the istighfār is *ineffective* (and 9:80 leaves the Prophet free to do it or not). 60:4 marks Ibrāhīm's promise as *not a model to follow*. The common trigger is **clarity about the beneficiary's end**: the word is `tabayyana`, and it is used in both 9:113 and 9:114. It is neither a ban on istighfār for kin as such nor a ban on istighfār for sinners: 4:64, 12:98 and 47:19 show it for believers who sin. The limit falls on the seeker's knowledge, and it ends at shirk and open hostility to God and His Messenger.

**What the text does not say.** It does not say *when* a living person's state is "clear". 9:113's `tabayyana` makes it a recognised condition, not a rule of thumb. 9:114 says Ibrāhīm withdrew, but 14:41 (parents) and 26:86 (father) remain unretracted in the text, and the Quran does not reconcile them. **[interpretive]** 9:114 praises him in the same verse (`awwāhun ḥalīm`); the withdrawal is not framed as a fault.

## 4. People forgiving people [data-grounded]

14 verses in the pack group (24:22, 42:37, 42:40, 42:43, 45:14, 64:14, 2:109, 2:237, 3:134, 5:13, 7:199, 15:85, 43:89, 2:263), plus 3:159 and 12:92.

**Verbs.** `ʿafā` (pardon) in 8 of the 14: 24:22, 42:40, 64:14, 2:109, 2:237, 3:134, 5:13, 7:199. `ṣafaḥa` (overlook) in 6: 24:22, 64:14, 2:109, 5:13, 15:85, 43:89. `ghafara` with a human subject in 4: 42:37, 42:43, 45:14, 64:14.

**Linked explicitly to God's forgiveness: 2 of 14.**
- **24:22** *question*: `a-lā tuḥibbūna an yaghfira llāhu lakum`, right after `li-yaʿfū wa-li-yaṣfaḥū`.
- **64:14** *condition*: `in taʿfū wa-taṣfaḥū wa-taghfirū fa-inna llāha ghafūrun raḥīm`. This is about spouses and children called `ʿaduww`. The three verbs rise in degree: pardon, overlook, forgive.
- A third, **12:92**, is narrative: Joseph says `lā tathrība ʿalaykum… yaghfiru llāhu lakum`. He forgives and invokes God's forgiveness in one breath (juxtaposition).

**Linked to God's love, not forgiveness: 3:134, 5:13** (`yuḥibbu l-muḥsinīn`). **Linked to God's reward:** 42:40 (`fa-ajruhu ʿalā llāh`), 45:14. **Linked to taqwā:** 2:237. **No link:** 42:37, 42:43, 7:199, 15:85, 43:89, 2:109 (until God brings His command), 2:263.

**3:159: the sequence.** `fa-ʿfu ʿanhum wa-staghfir lahum wa-shāwirhum`: pardon *them* (ʿan), seek forgiveness *for* them (li), consult them, then `fa-idhā ʿazamta fa-tawakkal`. **[interpretive]** The order puts your own grievance down first, then raises their need before God, then trusts them with counsel. The text states the order and not its reason.

**Limits on pardon:** 42:40 (`wa-aṣlaḥa`: pardon with reconciliation) and 64:14 (`fa-ḥdharūhum`: caution alongside pardon).

## 5. Kin [data-grounded, verified]

Phase 0 said 8 ayahs; **verified**, with one clarification. Seeking or promising forgiveness for kin appears in 8 ayahs: 19:47, 26:86, 60:4, 9:114 (Ibrāhīm, father: 4); 14:41 (Ibrāhīm, parents); 71:28 (Noah, parents); 7:151 (Moses, brother); 12:98 (Jacob, sons). 12:97 is a *request* to Jacob, so it is a ninth ayah in the story but not an act of seeking. No ayah shows anyone seeking forgiveness for a spouse or a child, except Jacob for his sons at their request.

**Ibrāhīm's father:** a promise after the stoning threat (19:47); a petition grounded in the father's error, not merit (26:86); withdrawn (9:114); excepted from the model (60:4).

**Verse panel, the limit and the model:**

> **9:113** مَا كَانَ لِلنَّبِيِّ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓاْ أَن يَسۡتَغۡفِرُواْ لِلۡمُشۡرِكِينَ وَلَوۡ كَانُوٓاْ أُوْلِي قُرۡبَىٰ مِنۢ بَعۡدِ مَا تَبَيَّنَ لَهُمۡ أَنَّهُمۡ أَصۡحَٰبُ ٱلۡجَحِيمِ
> It is not for the Prophet and those who have believed to ask forgiveness for the polytheists, even if they were relatives, after it has become clear to them that they are companions of Hellfire.

> **4:64** …وَلَوۡ أَنَّهُمۡ إِذ ظَّلَمُوٓاْ أَنفُسَهُمۡ جَآءُوكَ فَٱسۡتَغۡفَرُواْ ٱللَّهَ وَٱسۡتَغۡفَرَ لَهُمُ ٱلرَّسُولُ لَوَجَدُواْ ٱللَّهَ تَوَّابࣰ ا رَّحِيمࣰ ا
> …if, when they wronged themselves, they had come to you and asked forgiveness of Allah and the Messenger had asked forgiveness for them, they would have found Allah Accepting of repentance and Merciful.

> **71:28** رَّبِّ ٱغۡفِرۡ لِي وَلِوَٰلِدَيَّ وَلِمَن دَخَلَ بَيۡتِيَ مُؤۡمِنࣰ ا وَلِلۡمُؤۡمِنِينَ وَٱلۡمُؤۡمِنَٰتِۖ
> My Lord, forgive me and my parents and whoever enters my house a believer and the believing men and believing women.

> **24:22** …وَلۡيَعۡفُواْ وَلۡيَصۡفَحُوٓاْۗ أَلَا تُحِبُّونَ أَن يَغۡفِرَ ٱللَّهُ لَكُمۡۚ وَٱللَّهُ غَفُورࣱ رَّحِيمٌ
> …and let them pardon and overlook. Would you not like that Allah should forgive you? And Allah is Forgiving and Merciful.

> **3:159** …فَٱعۡفُ عَنۡهُمۡ وَٱسۡتَغۡفِرۡ لَهُمۡ وَشَاوِرۡهُمۡ فِي ٱلۡأَمۡرِ…
> …so pardon them and ask forgiveness for them and consult them in the matter…

**Concentric order (71:28) [interpretive].** Noah moves outward: self, parents, the household guest who believes, then all believers. The sphere is defined by *faith*, not blood. Compare 40:8, where the angels ask that righteous fathers, spouses and offspring join the believers in the Garden. In both, kin come into the prayer *with* faith. Where kin are not believers (the associator father), the same prayer reaches its limit at 9:113.

**Corrected / clarified Phase-0 claims.** (1) Kin = 8 ayahs confirmed, but only 4 of the 8 are Ibrāhīm-for-his-father, and one of them is a withdrawal (9:114) and one an excepted model (60:4). (2) The Phase-0 line that "many prophets seek forgiveness for others" should read: they *ask God* in the imperative; the first-person `astaghfiru` appears only in promises (12:98, 19:47, 60:4).

## For practice

1. Put others into your own petition, but never *instead* of yourself: the Quran's model is `rabbi ighfir lī wa-li-wālidayya wa-lil-muʾminīn` (71:28, 14:41, 7:151). Self first, then parents, then household, then believers.
2. Pray for the believers who came before you, and ask God to remove resentment from your heart toward them (59:10). The two requests are one verse.
3. To ask someone to pray for you, do what 4:64 and 12:97 show: admit the wrong and seek God yourself first. Do not ask as an excuse (48:11).
4. For a parent or relative who dies or persists in shirk, the limit is real (9:113-114, 60:4). The text bars it once the matter is clear; it is not a reason to stop for believing kin who sin.
5. When someone has wronged you, 3:159 gives the order: pardon, then seek forgiveness for them, then consult. 24:22 and 64:14 put God's forgiveness of you next to your pardon.
6. Forgive at the moment of anger (42:37); pardon with reconciliation (42:40); pardon the household while still being cautious (64:14).
