# Narrative — the prophets seeking forgiveness for themselves

## Headline finding

Sixteen prophetic self-directed seeking-of-forgiveness units were tested (11 narrated prophets, 5 the Prophet). **Eight follow a stated event; eight follow no stated wrong** (three narrated, all five of the Prophet's). Where an event precedes, the prophet is **fast**: 7 of 8 petition in the same or next verse. Accompaniments are concrete: confession (`ẓalamtu nafsī`), bowing and turning (David), a vow (Moses 28:17), tasbīḥ (Yūnus). God's forgiveness is stated as **granted in only 4 units**. Phase 0's "istighfār closes good deeds" **holds as timing, not as stated purpose**: the Prophet's victory-istighfārs still name a `dhanb`.

## Panels

**7:23** قَالَا رَبَّنَا ظَلَمۡنَآ أَنفُسَنَا وَإِن لَّمۡ تَغۡفِرۡ لَنَا وَتَرۡحَمۡنَا لَنَكُونَنَّ مِنَ ٱلۡخَٰسِرِينَ — *"Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves, and if You do not forgive us and have mercy upon us, we will surely be among the losers."*

**28:16-17** قَالَ رَبِّ إِنِّي ظَلَمۡتُ نَفۡسِي فَٱغۡفِرۡ لِي فَغَفَرَ لَهُۥٓ … رَبِّ بِمَآ أَنۡعَمۡتَ عَلَيَّ فَلَنۡ أَكُونَ ظَهِيرࣰا لِّلۡمُجۡرِمِينَ — *"My Lord, I have wronged myself, so forgive me," and He forgave him… "for the favor You bestowed upon me, I will never be an assistant to the criminals."*

**21:87-88** فَنَادَىٰ فِي ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ أَن لَّآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّآ أَنتَ سُبۡحَٰنَكَ إِنِّي كُنتُ مِنَ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ فَٱسۡتَجَبۡنَا لَهُۥ — *he called out within the darknesses… "Indeed, I have been of the wrongdoers." So We responded to him.*

## Q1. Sin-occasioned or not — build, break, verdict

**Count (data-grounded).** Event-named: Adam, Noah 11:47, Moses ×3, David, Sulaymān, Yūnus = 8 of 11 narrated units; seven follow the petitioner's *own* act, and 7:155 follows the people's wrong and a divine seizure. No wrong named: Noah 71:28, Ibrāhīm 14:41, 26:82 (all three are sustained supplications, not crisis scenes). Prophet: 0 of 5 name a wrong.

**Case for "istighfār also closes good and success" (interpretive).** (a) 110:1-3 and 48:1-2 tie it to the victory itself. (b) 40:55 sits inside patience and 47:19 inside knowing tawḥīd, with no failure in view. (c) Ibrāhīm's 14:41 comes after praising God for his sons (14:39), asking to be a prayer-establisher (14:40); 26:82 sits among blessings listed in 26:78-81. (d) Noah's 71:28 closes the surah of a lifetime of calling.

**Attack.** (1) The text never says istighfār is *for* the success. 40:55, 47:19 and 48:2 name the object: **`dhanb`**, "your sin", and 48:2 covers sin "preceding and following". 110:3 gives its ground as God's being `tawwāb`, not the Prophet's achievement. (2) 48:2's grammar runs the other way: the conquest is given *so that* God forgives (`li-yaghfira`). Victory is the channel of forgiveness, not its object. (3) In the 11 narrated units, **no** istighfār follows a good act alone; the 3 unoccasioned ones are standing supplications, in which forgiveness is one item among many. (4) Timing for the Prophet is ambiguous: 40:55 is mid-struggle, not an ending.

**Verdict.** The prophets' evidence supports a narrower claim: **istighfār does not wait for a known wrong.** A prophet who has just won, and one who is simply persevering, is told to seek forgiveness. What the text does not say is that victory or worship is itself the thing needing forgiveness. Phase 0's "closes good deeds" is **correct about position and unsupported about reason**. [Interpretive: what stays in view is the human gap before God, `dhanb` even in a prophet.]

## Q2. Speed

Same or next verse, counting from the event or the rebuke: Adam (7:22→23), Noah (11:46→47), Moses 28:15→16, 7:150→151, 7:155 (same), David 38:24 (same), Yūnus 21:87 (same) = **7 of 8**. The exception, Sulaymān, has `thumma anāba` (38:34), then the petition (38:35). **Caveat:** verse adjacency is narrative order, not a clock. The text gives no duration for any gap. David's `ẓanna` (he realised) leads directly to `fa-staghfara`; the realisation is the trigger, not the dispute. In 28:16 and 7:151 the petition has no `fa-` connector, just `qāla`; only 21:87 (`fa-nādā`) and 38:24 (`fa-staghfara`) use it.

Moses' 28:17 is the only post-forgiveness behavioural commitment. It is not a condition (the forgiveness is already in 28:16); it follows it. **[Data-grounded]** It is spoken to God, not about God.

## Q4. Is forgiveness stated as granted?

Counted over the 16 units: **granted, in the same passage: Moses 28:16, David 38:25, Prophet 48:2** (3). **Granted elsewhere in the text for the same event: Adam, `fa-tāba ʿalayhi` 2:37 and 20:122** (1; note 20:122 says "turned to him", not `ghafara`). Total 4 of 16. **Answered without a forgiveness verb:** Yūnus (`istajabnā`, `najjaynā`), Sulaymān (kingdom given, `zulfā wa-ḥusna maʾāb` 38:40), Moses 7:155 (mercy). **Not stated:** Noah ×2, Ibrāhīm ×2, Moses 7:151, Prophet 4:106, 40:55, 47:19, 110:3. So the Quran seldom records the reply; it records the asking. [Interpretive: its pattern pairs the *attribute* (`ghafūr raḥīm` 28:16; `tawwāb` 110:3; `ghafūr raḥīm` 4:106) with the act, which tells the reader whom they are addressing.]

**Correction to Phase 0 §2 "speakers" reading:** 5:118 is ʿĪsā for others (excluded here); 7:23 is a dual (Adam and wife), not Adam alone.

## Q5. Iblīs, the contrast

At 7:12-16 Iblīs is asked, and answers with ranking (`anā khayrun minhu`, 7:12). He asks for respite (7:14), not forgiveness, and in 7:16 shifts the cause onto God: **`fa-bi-mā aghwaytanī`**, "because You have put me in error". The prophets' sentence is `ẓalamnā anfusanā` (7:23): the fault is in the self. [Data-grounded: neither Iblīs nor the unrepentant request forgiveness in the narrated passage. Interpretive: the difference is *whose act it is* in the first clause.]

## For practice

- Say who did it: `ẓalamtu nafsī`, "I wronged myself", is the opening clause in 3 of the best-attested cases (7:23, 28:16, 21:87). Own the act before asking.
- Ask at once. Seven of eight event cases are in the same or next verse; Sulaymān shows turning first, then asking.
- You don't need a named wrong: Ibrāhīm, Noah and the Prophet seek forgiveness in the middle of devotion, patience, and success. Don't wait for a trigger.
- Let the body and conduct join the words: bow (38:24), turn (`anāba`), and, if the wrong was against someone, state what you will not do again (28:17).
- Add `tasbīḥ` and praise when no wrong is in front of you (40:55, 110:3). Call out from the dark place (21:87).
- Expect the text's silence on the reply, then trust the name you called: `al-ghafūr al-raḥīm`, `tawwāb`. The Quran shows the asking more than the receiving.
