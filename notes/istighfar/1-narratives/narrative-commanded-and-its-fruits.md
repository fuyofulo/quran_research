# Istighfār commanded to communities, its fruits, its times, its refusals

## Headline finding

Six of the eight plural commands to seek forgiveness are spoken to people who do not yet believe (Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Shuʿayb, Noah, the Prophet to Quraysh). Only those six carry worldly promises (a good enjoyment, rain, strength, wealth, sons, gardens). The two addressed to believers (2:199, 73:20) promise nothing but God's names. Istighfār is *asking* God (He is its direct object); tawba is *turning* (`tāba ilā`, 12 of 12 with a human subject; `tāba ʿalā` is always God). A "third act" (`aṣlaḥa`, `ʿamila ṣāliḥan`) attaches to tawba in 13+ verses, never to istighfār. Five of six timed istighfārs are endings; the text gives no reason.

**Audience (counted):** 6 of 8 address non-believers or a people in denial (11:3, 11:52, 11:61, 11:90, 41:6, 71:10); 5 of the 8 are prophets' sermons. Two address a believing body. **No command is addressed to a believer with a worldly reward attached.**

**Third-person counterpart:** 4:64 — if the hypocrites *had come, asked God's forgiveness, and the Messenger asked it for them*, they would have found God `tawwāban raḥīman`.

## 2. Istighfār and tawba: what each asks of the person

**The Hūd formula is `istaghfirū rabbakum thumma tūbū ilayhi`, 4/4** (11:3, 52, 61, 90). `thumma` makes it a sequence, not a synonym pair.

**Data-grounded, from the morphology** (63 `tāba` verbs): with a **human** subject, `tāba` takes `ilā` — 12 verses (2:54, 5:74, 7:143, 11:3, 11:52, 11:61, 11:90, 24:31, 25:71, 46:15, 66:4, 66:8). `tāba` with `ʿalā` — 19 verses (2:37, 2:54, 2:128, 2:160, 2:187, 3:128, 4:17, 4:26, 4:27, 5:39, 5:71, 9:102, 9:106, 9:117, 9:118, 20:122, 33:24, 58:13, 73:20) — is **always God as subject**. Tawba is therefore a pair of movements: you turn **to** Him; He turns **upon** you. Istighfār has no such movement. Its object is God Himself (`istaghfirū rabbakum`, `Allāha`, `-hu`), with the sin as a `li-` phrase when named (3:135, 40:55). **[Interpretive]**: istighfār is a request put into words; tawba is a change of direction. Hūd asks first for the request, then the turn.

**Order is not fixed:** 5:74 reverses it: `yatūbūna ilā Allāhi wa-yastaghfirūnahu` (turn, then ask). Hūd (4×) has istighfār first. Nine verses hold both roots (4:64, 5:74, 11 ×4, 40:7, 73:20, 110:3). **[Interpretive]**: in Hūd the hearers have not yet turned, so asking is the first accessible act; in 5:74 the hearers hold a specific false creed, so turning from it comes first. The text states neither reason.

**A third act after tawba.** Counted by script and reading, tawba is followed or joined by a further stated act in 13 verses: `aṣlaḥa` in 8 (2:160, 3:89, 4:16, 4:146, 5:39, 6:54, 16:119, 24:5) and `ʿamila ṣāliḥan` in 5 (19:60, 20:82, 25:70, 25:71, 28:67). 20:82 adds a fourth step: `tāba wa-āmana wa-ʿamila ṣāliḥan thumma ihtadā`, which God forgives (`ghaffār`). 6:54 puts it as `ʿamila sūʾan bi-jahālatin thumma tāba min baʿdihi wa-aṣlaḥa`. **No istighfār verse carries `aṣlaḥa` or `ʿamila ṣāliḥan`.** The nearest equivalent is the negative clause in 3:135. The Hūd verses end at tawba; nothing is added.

## 3. The fruits

| Ref | Stated result | World | Grammar |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11:3 | good enjoyment to a fixed term; every one of grace his grace | this | jussive answer, certain |
| 11:52 | rain, strength added to strength | this | jussive answer, certain |
| 71:11-12 | rain, wealth, sons, gardens, rivers | this | jussive answer, certain |
| 8:33 | not punished `wa-hum yastaghfirūn` | this | negated `kāna`, categorical |
| 27:46 | `laʿallakum turḥamūn` | open | **the only `laʿalla`** |
| 4:110 | `yajidi Allāha ghafūran raḥīman` | open | jussive conditional, certain |
| 4:64 | `lawajadū Allāha tawwāban raḥīman` | open | counterfactual (they did not come) |
| 3:136 | forgiveness from their Lord, gardens, rivers | hereafter | nominal, certain |
| 11:61, 11:90 | `qarīb mujīb`, `raḥīm wadūd` | God's character | names |

Counts: **this-world** material fruit 4 verses (11:3, 11:52, 71:11-12, 8:33); **hereafter** explicit 1 (3:136; 3:133 and 3:15/51:15 place gardens beside the practitioners); **hoped** phrasing 1 (27:46); **certain** phrasing for all others. **Contrast:** the fruits of *tawba* are hedged elsewhere with `ʿasā` or `laʿalla` (9:102, 24:31, 28:67, 66:8), while istighfār's promises are flat.

**Whom the material promises were for (data-grounded):** 11:3 (Quraysh), 11:52 (ʿĀd), 71 (Noah's people), 8:33 (the Meccans), 27:46 (Thamūd). Every one was spoken to a people refusing a messenger. **[Interpretive]**: a believer today cannot read "rain, wealth, sons" as a guaranteed return on his own istighfār; they are what *those* peoples were told. What transfers is the structure: a people who ask are not destroyed (8:33), and asking is the door the prophets keep pointing to. The believers' pages (2:199, 73:20, 3:136) speak of God's being forgiving and merciful, a forgiveness-and-gardens reward, not rain.

## 4. The practitioners

**3:15-17.** The list sits under the garden of 3:15: `al-ṣābirīn, al-ṣādiqīn, al-qānitīn, al-munfiqīn, al-mustaghfirīn bi-l-asḥār`. Four qualities, then istighfār *last*. Just before (3:16) these same people say `rabbanā innanā āmannā fa-ghfir lanā dhunūbanā`. Istighfār is the final item and is **in the pre-dawn**.

**51:15-19**, gardens for the `muḥsinīn` (51:16):

> كَانُواْ قَلِيلࣰا مِّنَ ٱلَّيۡلِ مَا يَهۡجَعُونَ ۝ وَبِٱلۡأَسۡحَارِ هُمۡ يَسۡتَغۡفِرُونَ
> *They used to sleep but little of the night, and in the hours before dawn they would ask forgiveness.* (Saheeh)

After it: 51:19, a right in their wealth for the petitioner and the deprived. Before: sleeping little. The pronoun `hum` fronted before `yastaghfirūn` is emphatic. They are called *muḥsinīn* and still seek forgiveness.

**3:133-136.** 3:133 `sāriʿū ilā maghfiratin min rabbikum`; 3:134 spending, restraining anger, pardoning people; then 3:135:

> وَٱلَّذِينَ إِذَا فَعَلُواْ فَٰحِشَةً أَوۡ ظَلَمُوٓاْ أَنفُسَهُمۡ ذَكَرُواْ ٱللَّهَ فَٱسۡتَغۡفَرُواْ لِذُنُوبِهِمۡ وَمَن يَغۡفِرُ ٱلذُّنُوبَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ وَلَمۡ يُصِرُّواْ عَلَىٰ مَا فَعَلُواْ وَهُمۡ يَعۡلَمُونَ
> *Those who, when they commit an immorality or wrong themselves, remember God and seek forgiveness for their sins (and who can forgive sins except God?) and do not persist in what they did while they know.* (Saheeh)

**The "how", step by step (data-grounded):**
1. **Trigger** — `idhā` + perfect: *whenever* they do `fāḥisha` or wrong themselves. The sin is expected, not hypothetical.
2. **`dhakarū Allāha`** — remembered God. No `fa-`: remembrance is the first reflex.
3. **`fa-staghfarū li-dhunūbihim`** — `fa-` joins it straight to the remembrance; the object is the sins in the plural (specific, not general).
4. **The parenthesis** `wa-man yaghfiru al-dhunūba illā Allāh` — grounds the act: the one asked is the only one who can.
5. **`wa-lam yuṣirrū ʿalā mā faʿalū wa-hum yaʿlamūn`** — jussive after `lam`, then a circumstantial clause. Non-persistence is a **state**, not a promise.

Ṣ-r-r is the corpus's word for persisting (4 verbs: 3:135, 45:8, 56:46, 71:7). **71:7 is its mirror:** Noah's people `aṣarrū wa-stakbarū`; 3:135's people `lam yuṣirrū`. Persistence is the exact antonym of the 3:135 sequence. Reward: 3:136 `jazāʾuhum maghfiratun min rabbihim`, and gardens.

## 5. When: the "endings" hypothesis, tested

**For (data-grounded):** 3:17 and 51:18 end the night; 2:199 follows the pouring out from ʿArafāt; 73:20 is the last command of the night-prayer passage; 110:3 comes after victory and crowds entering; in 3:17 it ends a list of five qualities. **5 of the 6 time-anchored occurrences** (the sixth is 40:55) follow worship or success.

**Attempt to break:**
- **3:135, 4:64, 4:110, 5:74** are sin-occasioned. Only 6 of the 18 istighfār occurrences treated here are time-anchored; 4 are sin-occasioned believers' or hypocrites' cases (3:135, 4:64, 4:110, 5:74); the rest speak to unbelief (11 ×4, 41:6, 71:10, 27:46, 8:33). So *endings* is a second pattern, not the main one.
- **40:55** is `bi-l-ʿashiyyi wa-l-ibkār`, "evening and morning". Grammatically the time phrase sits against `sabbiḥ`, the nearest verb; istighfār is not clearly timed. It is also *for your sin* (`li-dhanbika`).
- **The endings are not sin-free.** 2:198 says "you were among the astray before"; 73:20 follows a concession because they could not keep the night; 3:16 and 51:16-18 show the *muḥsinīn* asking too. In each ending, a lack is acknowledged nearby.

**Verdict:** the endings pattern holds as description (5/6 timed), but it is **not** an alternative to the sin pattern. The text pairs istighfār with *completion* and with *wrong*, and in the described righteous, the two coincide: they are good-doers who close their deeds by asking (3:17, 51:16-18). The text gives no explicit reason. **[Interpretive]**: the Quran does not treat istighfār as only the cure for a wrong; it treats it as what a good deed ends with.

## 6. Refusals: what blocks it, as the text states it

- **63:5** — told "come, the Messenger will ask forgiveness for you", `lawwaw ruʾūsahum`, and you see them turning away, `wa-hum mustakbirūn`. The stated block: **arrogance** (`istikbār`). 63:6 adds that the verdict is closed for them.
- **71:7** — `jaʿalū aṣābiʿahum fī ādhānihim, wa-staghshaw thiyābahum, wa-aṣarrū wa-stakbarū istikbāran`. Four acts: not hearing, not seeing, **persisting**, **arrogance**. Noah's invitation was *so that You may forgive them* (`li-taghfira lahum`).
- **18:55** — what stopped people from believing and asking their Lord's forgiveness was *only* that the precedent of the earlier peoples should befall them or the punishment should come before their eyes. The text names no cause beyond waiting to see it. 41:5 adds hearts "in coverings".

Counted: **istikbār in 2 of 3** (63:5, 71:7); **persisting in 1** (71:7); **wanting to see punishment first in 1** (18:55). Not stated as blocks: ignorance of the words, the size of the sin, or shame.

## For practice

- Take the verb's object seriously: you *ask God*; name the sin when you have one (`li-dhunūbihim`, 3:135), and the parenthesis there is the reason to ask: no one else forgives.
- Use 3:135's order: remember God, then ask, then stop. The last clause is a state to maintain, not a one-time promise.
- Seeking forgiveness and turning are two acts. Ask, then turn (Hūd) or turn, then ask (5:74); the Quran uses both orders.
- If you want the remedy to be complete, the third act belongs to tawba: set right (`aṣlaḥa`) and do good (`ʿamila ṣāliḥan`).
- Close good deeds with it: the end of the night (3:17, 51:18), the end of a night prayer (73:20), after the pilgrims' departure (2:199), after success (110:3).
- Do not read "rain, wealth, sons" as a payment for your own istighfār; those were said to peoples refusing a messenger. What is plainly offered to a believer is forgiveness, mercy, and gardens (3:136).
- What blocks it, by the text, is arrogance and persistence. Neither is about the size of the sin.
