# Asking God's forgiveness: what the Quran teaches

This guide comes from reading every verse in the Quran about seeking forgiveness. The numbers in
brackets let you check every line: [3:135] means surah 3, verse 135. Where something is our own
suggestion and not what the Quran says, we tell you.

## What istighfār is, in three sentences

Istighfār is asking God, directly, to forgive you. The Quran names the act, and whenever it shows
someone doing it for himself, that person speaks straight **to** God: "My Lord, forgive me", "Our
Lord, forgive us". And you do not have to know your sin first. Prophets and believers ask before
dawn, in calm times and after success, not only after a fall.

---

## The Quran's own words

Here are twelve prayers the Quran quotes, copied exactly from the Arabic. The Latin spelling
only guides the sound: ā, ī and ū are long vowels, ʿ is the letter ʿayn, and ' is a short catch in
the throat. **The "when you might use it" lines are our suggestions.**

### A short one for every day

**1. The one God told the Prophet to say [23:118]**

> وَقُل رَّبِّ ٱغۡفِرۡ وَٱرۡحَمۡ وَأَنتَ خَيۡرُ ٱلرَّٰحِمِينَ

*Sound:* Wa qul: Rabbi-ghfir warham, wa anta khayru r-rāhimīn.
*Meaning:* "And say: My Lord, forgive and have mercy. You are the best of those who show mercy."
*Who and when:* God tells the Prophet to say it, in the last verse of the surah *The Believers*.
Earlier, almost the same words ("Our Lord, we have believed, so forgive us…") were the prayer of
God's servants whom others mocked [23:109–110].
*Notice:* there is no "me" and no "us". It simply asks.
*When you might use it:* any time. It is the everyday short form, for when you only have a breath.

**2. The end of al-Baqara [2:285–286, short excerpts]**

> سَمِعۡنَا وَأَطَعۡنَاۖ غُفۡرَانَكَ رَبَّنَا وَإِلَيۡكَ ٱلۡمَصِيرُ
> رَبَّنَا لَا تُؤَاخِذۡنَآ إِن نَّسِينَآ أَوۡ أَخۡطَأۡنَاۚ … وَٱعۡفُ عَنَّا وَٱغۡفِرۡ لَنَا وَٱرۡحَمۡنَآۚ أَنتَ مَوۡلَىٰنَا

*Sound:* Samiʿnā wa ataʿnā, ghufrānaka rabbanā wa ilayka l-masīr. … Rabbanā lā tu'ākhidhnā in
nasīnā aw akhta'nā. … Wa-ʿfu ʿannā, wa-ghfir lanā, wa-rhamnā, anta mawlānā.
*Meaning:* "We hear and we obey. Your forgiveness, our Lord, and to You is the return. … Our Lord,
do not blame us if we forget or make a mistake. … Pardon us, forgive us, have mercy on us. You are
our Protector."
*Who and when:* the Messenger and the believers, at the close of the Quran's longest surah.
*Notice:* "ghufrānaka rabbanā" (two words) is the Quran's shortest request for forgiveness. And the
asking climbs: pardon, then forgive, then mercy.
*When you might use it:* at the end of the day, when what weighs on you is forgetting and mistakes
more than chosen wrongs.

### Right after a wrong you know you did

**3. Adam and Eve [7:23]**

> رَبَّنَا ظَلَمۡنَآ أَنفُسَنَا وَإِن لَّمۡ تَغۡفِرۡ لَنَا وَتَرۡحَمۡنَا لَنَكُونَنَّ مِنَ ٱلۡخَٰسِرِينَ

*Sound:* Rabbanā zalamnā anfusanā, wa in lam taghfir lanā wa tarhamnā, lanakūnanna mina l-khāsirīn.
*Meaning:* "Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves. If You do not forgive us and have mercy on us, we
will surely be among the losers."
*Who and when:* Adam and his wife together, in the very next verse after they ate from the tree
[7:22]. God turned to Adam [2:37].
*Notice:* "we wronged *ourselves*". They blame no one else.
*When you might use it:* after a wrong, especially one done together with someone. Say it together.

**4. Moses [28:16]**

> رَبِّ إِنِّي ظَلَمۡتُ نَفۡسِي فَٱغۡفِرۡ لِي

*Sound:* Rabbi innī zalamtu nafsī, fa-ghfir lī.
*Meaning:* "My Lord, I have wronged myself, so forgive me."
*Who and when:* Moses, right after he struck a man and the man died [28:15]. The same verse goes on:
"and He forgave him." Only then did Moses promise: "I will never be a helper to the criminals"
[28:17]. The promise came *after* the forgiveness. It was not the price of it.
*When you might use it:* when you know exactly what you did. Own it in one line, then ask.

### When you feel far away

**5. Yūnus in the dark [21:87]**

> لَّآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّآ أَنتَ سُبۡحَٰنَكَ إِنِّي كُنتُ مِنَ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ

*Sound:* Lā ilāha illā anta, subhānaka, innī kuntu mina z-zālimīn.
*Meaning:* "There is no god but You. Glory be to You. I have been one of the wrongdoers."
*Who and when:* Yūnus, who had gone off in anger, calling out "in the darknesses". God says: "So We
answered him and saved him from distress, and so We save the believers" [21:88].
*Note:* it does not contain the word "forgive". It is a call: God's oneness, His glory, owning your
wrong. We include it as the Quran's clearest picture of turning back from a dark place and being
answered.
*When you might use it:* when you feel trapped, low, or far from God, and can't find other words.

### On an ordinary day, standing on your faith

**6. The God-conscious [3:16]**

> رَبَّنَآ إِنَّنَآ ءَامَنَّا فَٱغۡفِرۡ لَنَا ذُنُوبَنَا وَقِنَا عَذَابَ ٱلنَّارِ

*Sound:* Rabbanā innanā āmannā, fa-ghfir lanā dhunūbanā wa qinā ʿadhāba n-nār.
*Meaning:* "Our Lord, we have believed, so forgive us our sins and protect us from the punishment of
the Fire."
*Who:* the people promised gardens [3:15]. The next verse describes them: patient, truthful,
devoted, generous, "and asking forgiveness before dawn" [3:17].
*Notice:* the reason they give is "we have believed", not a particular sin.
*When you might use it:* in the last part of the night if you are awake, or on any ordinary day.

**7. People of understanding [3:193, second half]**

> رَبَّنَا فَٱغۡفِرۡ لَنَا ذُنُوبَنَا وَكَفِّرۡ عَنَّا سَيِّـَٔاتِنَا وَتَوَفَّنَا مَعَ ٱلۡأَبۡرَارِ

*Sound:* Rabbanā fa-ghfir lanā dhunūbanā wa kaffir ʿannā sayyi'ātinā wa tawaffanā maʿa l-abrār.
*Meaning:* "Our Lord, so forgive us our sins, wipe away our bad deeds, and let us die among the
righteous." The verse begins: "we heard someone calling to faith… and we believed."
*Who:* people who remember God standing, sitting and lying down, and think about the creation of the
heavens and the earth [3:190–191].
*When you might use it:* when you think about how you want your life to end.

### When life is hard

**8. The steadfast [3:147]**

> رَبَّنَا ٱغۡفِرۡ لَنَا ذُنُوبَنَا وَإِسۡرَافَنَا فِيٓ أَمۡرِنَا وَثَبِّتۡ أَقۡدَامَنَا وَٱنصُرۡنَا عَلَى ٱلۡقَوۡمِ ٱلۡكَٰفِرِينَ

*Sound:* Rabbana-ghfir lanā dhunūbanā wa isrāfanā fī amrinā, wa thabbit aqdāmanā, wa-nsurnā ʿala
l-qawmi l-kāfirīn.
*Meaning:* "Our Lord, forgive us our sins and our going too far in our affairs. Make our feet firm,
and help us against the people who deny."
*Who:* the many godly people who fought alongside prophets and did not weaken or give in. "Their
words were nothing but this" [3:146–147]. They were believers, not prophets.
*When you might use it:* under pressure, when you have overdone things and still need to keep going.

### For your family

**9. Ibrāhīm [14:41]**

> رَبَّنَا ٱغۡفِرۡ لِي وَلِوَٰلِدَيَّ وَلِلۡمُؤۡمِنِينَ يَوۡمَ يَقُومُ ٱلۡحِسَابُ

*Sound:* Rabbana-ghfir lī wa li-wālidayya wa lil-mu'minīna yawma yaqūmu l-hisāb.
*Meaning:* "Our Lord, forgive me, my parents and the believers, on the Day the reckoning is set up."
*Who and when:* Ibrāhīm, at the end of a long prayer, after thanking God for sons given in old age [14:39].
*When you might use it:* for yourself and your parents, each day.

**10. Noah [71:28, first part]**

> رَّبِّ ٱغۡفِرۡ لِي وَلِوَٰلِدَيَّ وَلِمَن دَخَلَ بَيۡتِيَ مُؤۡمِنࣰا وَلِلۡمُؤۡمِنِينَ وَٱلۡمُؤۡمِنَٰتِۖ

*Sound:* Rabbi-ghfir lī wa li-wālidayya wa li-man dakhala baytiya mu'minan wa lil-mu'minīna
wal-mu'mināt.
*Meaning:* "My Lord, forgive me, my parents, whoever enters my house as a believer, and the believing
men and women."
*Who and when:* Noah, in the last verse of his surah, after a lifetime of calling his people.
*Notice:* the circles widen: me, my parents, my home, all believers. (Moses does the same: "My Lord,
forgive me and my brother" [7:151].)
*When you might use it:* for your household.

### For the community

**11. Those who came after [59:10]**

> رَبَّنَا ٱغۡفِرۡ لَنَا وَلِإِخۡوَٰنِنَا ٱلَّذِينَ سَبَقُونَا بِٱلۡإِيمَٰنِ وَلَا تَجۡعَلۡ فِي قُلُوبِنَا غِلࣰّا لِّلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ رَبَّنَآ إِنَّكَ رَءُوفࣱ رَّحِيمٌ

*Sound:* Rabbana-ghfir lanā wa li-ikhwāninā lladhīna sabaqūnā bil-īmān, wa lā tajʿal fī qulūbinā
ghillan lilladhīna āmanū, rabbanā innaka ra'ūfun rahīm.
*Meaning:* "Our Lord, forgive us and our brothers who came before us in faith, and put no bitterness
in our hearts toward those who believe. Our Lord, You are Kind and Merciful."
*Who:* the believers who came after the first generation [59:8–10].
*Notice:* in the same breath, they ask God to clean their hearts of bitterness.
*When you might use it:* for those before you, and when you feel resentment toward other believers.

### Looking ahead to the Last Day

**12. The believers with their light [66:8]**

> رَبَّنَآ أَتۡمِمۡ لَنَا نُورَنَا وَٱغۡفِرۡ لَنَآۖ إِنَّكَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيۡءࣲ قَدِيرࣱ

*Sound:* Rabbanā atmim lanā nūranā wa-ghfir lanā, innaka ʿalā kulli shay'in qadīr.
*Meaning:* "Our Lord, complete our light for us and forgive us. You have power over all things."
*Who and when:* the believers on the Last Day, their light running ahead of them.
*When you might use it:* when you think about the end, or when your light feels weak.

## How the Quran shows it being done

**The clearest "how" is [3:135].** It describes people who, when they do something shameful or wrong
themselves:
1. **remember God**,
2. **ask forgiveness for their sins** (and the verse stops to ask: *who forgives sins except God?*),
3. **and do not knowingly keep doing what they did.**

The third step describes how they are, not an oath never to slip. The verse begins "*when* they
do…": it does not imagine people who never fall. Their reward is "forgiveness from their Lord, and
gardens" [3:136].

**Praise Him first, then ask.** After the victory, the Prophet is told:

> فَسَبِّحۡ بِحَمۡدِ رَبِّكَ وَٱسۡتَغۡفِرۡهُۚ إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ تَوَّابَۢا

*Fa-sabbih bi-hamdi rabbika wa-staghfirh, innahū kāna tawwābā.* "So glorify your Lord with His praise
and ask His forgiveness. He is always ready to accept those who turn back." The same pairing appears
four times [40:7, 42:5, 40:55, 110:3].

**Speak to Him, and give your reason with "so".** "We have believed, *so* forgive us" [3:16].
"I have wronged myself, *so* forgive me" [28:16].

**Forgiveness first, then mercy, then the rest.** In every prayer that asks for both, forgiveness
comes first. The longer prayers then ask for what the forgiveness is *for*: firm feet, protection from
the Fire, a good death [3:147, 3:16, 3:193]. Many close by naming who He is: "You are the best of
those who show mercy" [23:118], "the best of those who forgive" [7:155].

**Yourself first, then others.** "Forgive me, and my parents, and the believers" [14:41, 71:28, 7:151].

**"Us" more than "me".** Every prayer for forgiveness the Quran quotes from ordinary believers speaks
as "us". "Forgive me" is quoted only from prophets. That is a pattern, not a rule: the Quran also
speaks of one person asking alone [4:110].

**Then turn back, and set right what you can.** Four times in Sūrat Hūd, a prophet tells his people:
"Ask your Lord's forgiveness, *then* turn back to Him" [11:3, 11:52, 11:61, 11:90]. Asking and turning
back (tawba) are two steps. The Quran ties *setting things right* to the turning back: "whoever does
wrong out of ignorance, then turns back after it and puts things right…" [6:54; also 2:160, 24:5].
The repair matches the wrong: those who hid the truth make it clear [2:159–160]; those who took
interest keep only what they lent [2:279].

## When

- **Right away after a wrong.** Adam and Moses ask in the very next verse [7:22–23, 28:15–16].
- **The last part of the night.** "They used to sleep only a little of the night, and before dawn they
  would ask forgiveness" [51:17–18; also 3:17]. The Quran praises this as the habit of the
  God-conscious. It does not command it.
- **At the end of good things.** After the pilgrims leave ʿArafāt [2:199]. At the close of the passage
  on night prayer [73:20]. After victory [110:3]. One caution: the Quran never says the good deed
  itself needs forgiving. Near each one it mentions a shortfall: "before this you were astray" [2:198],
  "He knew you would not be able to keep it up" [73:20].
- **Without waiting for a known sin.** Ibrāhīm, Noah and the Prophet ask in calm times and in success
  [14:41, 71:28, 47:19, 110:3].

## What it does not require

- **A number.** The only number beside seeking forgiveness is "seventy times": even seventy would not
  help the hypocrites [9:80]. The Quran says to remember God "much" in ten places but never counts
  istighfār. It calls it a habit: "they would ask forgiveness" [51:18]. Counting (a hundred a day, say)
  is a traditional practice from the hadith. The Quran neither asks for it nor forbids it.
- **A fixed time.** Only the pre-dawn is praised [3:17, 51:18]. Saying it after each prayer is a
  cherished traditional practice. The Quran itself ties *glorifying* God, not istighfār, to the end of
  prayer [50:40].
- **A set formula.** "Astaghfirullāh" ("I seek God's forgiveness") is good to say. It answers God's
  command, "seek God's forgiveness" [4:106, 73:20]. But "I will seek forgiveness" appears only three
  times, and each time it is a promise to ask for *someone else* [12:98, 19:47, 60:4]. The Quran's
  own model is to speak to Him: "My Lord, forgive me". The one fixed word God ever gave a people
  ("ḥiṭṭa", *unburden us*) was tied to a gate and to bowing [2:58]. Those who swapped it for their own words were punished [2:59]. For us, the Quran gives the command and many examples,
  not a script.
- **Tears or a feeling.** Tears in the Quran come from hearing God's words [5:83, 17:109, 19:58] or
  from having nothing to give [9:92]. They never come over one's own sins. The one time tears sit next
  to a sin, they are fake [12:16]. What the Quran checks is that tongue and heart agree [48:11]. (An
  earlier study here found the Quran never commands regret. It commands turning back.)
- **Listing every sin.** The believers say "our sins" and "our going too far" [3:16, 3:147]. General
  words are enough.
- **A middleman.** "Who forgives sins except God?" [3:135; also 4:110]. Asking a good person to pray
  for you is fine, but in the Quran that comes *after* you ask yourself [4:64, 12:97].
- **Confessing to people.** Never commanded. People in the Quran confess only to those they wronged
  [12:91, 12:97].
- **A small enough sin.** "My servants who have gone too far against yourselves: do not despair of
  God's mercy. God forgives all sins" [39:53]. The next verse adds: turn back "before the punishment
  comes" [39:54]. In 25:68–70 the list begins with calling on another god, then killing, then adultery,
  and then: "except whoever turns back, believes and does good. God will change their bad deeds into
  good."

  **The one limit, stated honestly.** The Quran says: "God does not forgive that partners be set up
  with Him" [4:48, 4:116]. The same Book also promises forgiveness to people who had worshipped others
  and then turned back [5:73–74, 25:68–70]. We put these side by side and do not try to settle them.
  What the Quran says plainly about turning back is that it has a **deadline**. It is not accepted
  once death has arrived, or from one who dies denying [4:18]. Turning back *soon* is what it praises
  [4:17]. The size of your sin is not what closes the door. Time is.

## What blocks it

- **Arrogance.** "Come, the Messenger will ask forgiveness for you", and they turn their heads away,
  full of pride [63:5].
- **Knowingly carrying on.** Noah's people put their fingers in their ears, wrapped themselves in
  their clothes, persisted, and grew arrogant [71:7]: the exact opposite of 3:135.
- **Words without heart.** "They say with their tongues what is not in their hearts" [48:11].
- **Waiting to see the punishment first** [18:55].
- **For someone else:** once it has become clear that a person is an enemy of God [9:113–114]. This
  does not bar praying for believing relatives who sin [12:98, 47:19].

## Forgiving others

> وَلۡيَعۡفُواْ وَلۡيَصۡفَحُوٓاْۗ أَلَا تُحِبُّونَ أَن يَغۡفِرَ ٱللَّهُ لَكُمۡۚ

"Let them pardon and overlook. *Do you not love that God should forgive you?*" [24:22]. It was said to
people of means who had sworn to stop helping relatives and the needy. The Quran asks a question; it
does not strike a deal.

- Families: "If you pardon, overlook and forgive, God is Forgiving, Merciful" [64:14].
- The Prophet is told: pardon them, ask forgiveness for them, consult them, in that order [3:159].
- The good "forgive when they are angry" [42:37].
- Joseph, to the brothers who threw him in the well: "No blame on you today. God will forgive you"
  [12:92].

## A simple daily practice

*This is our suggestion, built only from what is above. It is not a rule from the Quran.*

1. **Before dawn, if you wake:** a few quiet minutes with prayer 6 [3:16] or prayer 1 [23:118], the habit
   the Quran praises [51:18].
2. **When you slip:** stop where you are. Remember God, and say prayer 4 [28:16] or prayer 3 [7:23].
   Then don't knowingly carry on. If someone was hurt, put it right that day.
3. **After a prayer or a good deed:** glorify and praise Him in your own words, then ask: "Rabbi-ghfir
   lī" (the shape of 110:3).
4. **Once a day, widen the circle:** prayer 10 [71:28] or prayer 9 [14:41] for yourself, your parents
   and your home, then prayer 11 [59:10] for the believers before you.
5. **Before sleep:** the end of al-Baqara, prayer 2 [2:285–286].
6. **Once a week, ask yourself one question:** "Is there something I am knowingly carrying on with?"
   That is the one condition the Quran puts on the person asking [3:135].
7. **Forgive one person**, and remember the question in 24:22.
