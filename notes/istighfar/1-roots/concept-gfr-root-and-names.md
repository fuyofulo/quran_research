# gfr: form X vs form I, the three `astaghfiru`, and the divine names

## Headline finding

The Quran splits forgiveness into two verbs. God does the forgiving (`ghafara`: 61 of 65 are divine; 4 are human). Humans do the seeking (`istaghfara`, 40), and its commonest grammatical shape is a command plus a reason: *seek your Lord's forgiveness, for He is Forgiving, Merciful*. Words are quoted where the verb "seek forgiveness" is absent, and the verb is narrated where words are absent. The only joint case is the angels (40:7). `astaghfiru` (12:98, 19:47, 60:4) is confirmed as a future promise for someone else. Two of the three are repudiated (9:114, 60:4). Phase 0 undercounted the names: `ghafūr raḥīm` is 71 ayahs, not 56.

**Object (the one asked).** Unstated: 19. `Allāh` by name: 9. `rabb` + possessive: 8 (`rabbakum` 4, `rabbī` 2, `rabbahum` 1, `rabbahu` 1). Bare suffix `-hu`: 4. `rabb` (8) and `Allāh` (9) are nearly equal; `rabb` mostly sits in prophets' sermons or speech (11:3/52/90, 71:10, 12:98, 19:47, 38:24).

**Beneficiary (`li-`)**: none, i.e. self-reflexive: 18. A person: 17 (`lahum` 8, `lakum` 2, `laka` 2, `lanā` 2, and four others). A sin: 4 (`li-dhunūbihim` 3:135, `li-dhanbiki` 12:29, `li-dhanbika` 40:55, `dhunūbanā` 12:97). Sin and people together: 1 (47:19). Only 5 of 40 name the sin.

**The typical istighfār sentence** is an imperative, the One asked, optionally `li-` plus beneficiary, then a ground clause. 13 ayahs carry a divine name as the reason (2:199, 4:64, 4:106, 4:110, 5:74, 11:90, 12:98, 24:62, 42:5, 60:12, 71:10, 73:20, 110:3). That covers 8 of the 19 `istaghfir-` imperatives (2:199, 4:106, 11:90, 24:62, 60:12, 71:10, 73:20, 110:3).

## 2. The three `astaghfiru`

> قَالَ سَوْفَ أَسْتَغْفِرُ لَكُمْ رَبِّيٓ ۖ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلْغَفُورُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ — **12:98** "I will ask forgiveness for you from my Lord. Indeed, it is He who is the Forgiving, the Merciful." (Saheeh)

Verified, with all three in the first person, future, and for another: 12:98 (`sawfa`, Jacob for his sons), 19:47 (`sa-`, Ibrāhīm for his father), 60:4 (`la-…-anna`, Ibrāhīm for his father). Nothing in the Arabic makes them present or self-directed.

Implications:
- **Two are repudiated by the text.** 9:114 says Ibrāhīm's istighfār for his father was only "because of a promise" and was dropped "when it became clear to him that he was an enemy of God." 60:4 lists it as the one exception to the imitable pattern in Ibrāhīm ("except the saying of Ibrāhīm to his father"). **[interpretive]** The only first-person "I seek forgiveness" the Quran quotes is therefore either deferred (Jacob) or marked as not to be copied.
- **Jacob's is never narrated as done.** His sons ask `istaghfir lanā dhunūbanā` (12:97) and he promises (12:98). Nothing later in the surah reports the act. Phase 0's "all future" holds; the act itself is outside the narrative.

**Counter-check: narrated act versus quoted words.**
- *Act narrated, words quoted:* only 40:7 (angels, then `rabbanā wasiʿta kulla shayʾin raḥmatan wa-ʿilman fa-ghfir…`).
- *Act narrated, words not quoted:* 38:24 (David, the only real past event in the 40; 38:25 `fa-ghafarnā lahu`), 3:135, 4:64, 4:110, 5:74, 8:33, 18:55, 42:5, 51:18, plus 3:17 (`mustaghfirīn`).
- *Words quoted, no `istaghfara` within +-4 ayahs:* all other petitions, introduced by `qāla`, `yaqūlūna` or `qul` (2:285, 3:16, 3:147, 7:23, 7:151, 7:155, 11:47, 23:109, 23:118, 28:16, 38:35, 59:10, 66:8) or as bare speech (3:193, 5:118, 14:41, 26:82, 26:86, 71:28, 2:286). 60:5 is near 60:4 but follows quoted speech, not the promise.
- *Act deferred:* 12:97-98. *Words withheld:* 2:37 (Adam "received words", then `tāba ʿalayhi`; 7:23 supplies them).

**[interpretive]** The noun for the practice and its quoted wording almost never share a verse. The act is named in the third person; the words are given in direct speech.

## 3. Human forgiving (`ghafara`)

Of 65 `ghafara` tokens, 4 have human subjects: 42:37 (when angered, `hum yaghfirūn`), 42:43 (`ṣabara wa-ghafara`), 45:14 (`qul … yaghfirū`), 64:14 (`taʿfū wa-taṣfaḥū wa-taghfirū`). 61 are divine. Humans forgive more often with `ʿafā` (11 verbs: 2:109, 2:178, 2:237 x3, 3:159, 4:149, 5:13, 24:22, 42:40, 64:14) than `ghafara` (4). 64:14 stacks `taʿfū wa-taṣfaḥū wa-taghfirū`.

Only 5 of 71 sit beside punishment; 30 of 71 follow repentance or a concession.

**Other pairings** (counts by script, ayah classification by hand):
- `tawwāb raḥīm` **9, not 7**: 2:37, 2:54, 2:128, 2:160, 4:16, 4:64, 9:104, 9:118, 49:12. Phase 0 missed 4:16 and 4:64. All nine sit in a tawba scene; 2:128 is the human petition `tub ʿalaynā`.
- `ʿafuww ghafūr` **4**: 4:43 (tayammum concession), 4:99 (those unable to emigrate), 22:60 (retaliation), 58:2 (ẓihār). Phase 0 missed 4:99. Each attaches to a legal accommodation or a wrong.
- `ghafūr ḥalīm` 4 (2:225 oaths, 2:235 secret promises, 3:155 flight with `ʿafā`, 5:101 questions) plus reversed `ḥalīm ghafūr` 17:44, 35:41. Three of four concern speech (oaths, promises, questions); 3:155 is battle.
- `ghafūr shakūr` 3: 35:30, 35:34, 42:23. All three follow a statement about reward being increased for good deeds. So `shakūr` goes with **deeds**, as proposed.
- `ʿazīz` + `ghafūr/ghaffār` 5: 35:28, 38:66, 39:5, 40:42, 67:2. None is attached to a named sin.
- `al-ghafūr al-wadūd` 85:14 follows 85:10 (those who tortured believers and did not repent).
- `wāsiʿ al-maghfira` 53:32 stands right after "those who avoid major sins and immoralities, except `al-lamam`".
- 40:3 puts forgiver, acceptor of tawba and severe-in-punishment in one verse; `dhū maghfira` 13:6 and 41:43 are each followed by punishment.

`ghaffār` carries a four-part condition in 20:82 and follows `istaghfirū` in 71:10.

## 5. The limits God states

> إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يَغْفِرُ أَن يُشْرَكَ بِهِۦ وَيَغْفِرُ مَا دُونَ ذَٰلِكَ لِمَن يَشَآءُ — **4:48** "Indeed, Allah does not forgive association with Him, but He forgives what is less than that for whom He wills." 4:48 ends: *whoever associates … has fabricated a tremendous sin*. **4:116** is word-for-word the same and ends *has gone far astray*.

> قُلْ يَٰعِبَادِىَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَسْرَفُوا۟ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ لَا تَقْنَطُوا۟ مِن رَّحْمَةِ ٱللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَغْفِرُ ٱلذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًا — **39:53** "Say, 'O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of the mercy of Allah. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins. Indeed, it is He who is the Forgiving, the Merciful.'"

- What the text places beside **4:48/4:116**: a negated `yaghfiru` with `an yushraka bihi` as its object, then the wider forgiveness is *what is less than that, for whom He wills*. The same `li-man yashāʾ` formula occurs at 2:284, 3:129, 5:18, 48:14.
- What sits beside **39:53**: `ʿibādī`, `asrafū ʿalā anfusihim`, `lā taqnaṭū`, `jamīʿan`, and the name pair. The next verses are **39:54** "turn to your Lord and submit to Him before the punishment comes upon you; then you will not be helped" and 39:55 "follow the best of what was revealed … before the punishment comes." So the text's condition beside 39:53 is a command to `anībū` and `aslimū` with a deadline, `min qabli an yaʾtiyakum al-ʿadhāb`. It is not a list of sins excluded.
- Neither passage mentions the other. One is third-person with a negated verb; the other addresses servants with `jamīʿan`. I do not reconcile them.

## 6. Adjacent vocabulary, and what each does to the sin

| Term | Count and subject | What it does to the sin | Notes |

**Phase 0 claims corrected**: `ghafūr raḥīm` 56 to 71 (56 nominative plus 15 `kāna …an`); `tawwāb raḥīm` 7 to 9; `ʿafuww ghafūr` 3 to 4. The claim that all three `astaghfiru` are "all future, all for others" stands. Not corrected but sharpened: two are expressly repudiated in the Quran.

## For practice

- Seek forgiveness as an addressed sentence: *the verb* + *your Lord* + (*for my sin*), then the reason: He is Forgiving and Merciful. That is the shape 8 of 19 plain commands take.
- Quoted words and the verb for the act are separate in the text. When the Quran gives you words, they are direct speech to God (`rabbi ighfir lī`), not "I seek forgiveness."
- "I will seek forgiveness for you" is the one first-person form, and the two cases about an unbeliever are not a model (9:114, 60:4). Seek it with God, not as a pledge made to someone.
- Pair istighfār with turning back, and expect `ʿalā` from God: He turns *toward* you when you turn *to* Him (`tāba ilā` 12 vs `tāba ʿalā` 16).
- Forgiveness is not the only effect: `kaffara`, `yubaddil` and `yudhhibna` are separate, tied to deeds (`ṣāliḥ`, taqwā, charity).
- When you must pardon people, the text uses `ʿafā` far more than `ghafara`, and twice ends the command with the divine pair (24:22, 64:14).
