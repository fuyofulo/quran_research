# The 22 quoted petitions, anatomised

## Headline finding

The Quran quotes 22 people or groups asking forgiveness, and the words have a recognisable shape. **Individuals address God as `rabbi`, groups as `rabbanā`** (7 vs 12; 3 have no address). **Nobody outside the prophets ever says "forgive me"**: all 8 non-prophet human petitions are plural (`ighfir lanā`). **Confession is rare (2 of 22: `ẓalamnā anfusanā` 7:23, `ẓalamtu nafsī` 28:16).** What recurs instead is a *ground clause joined by `fa-`* (*we have believed, so forgive us*), forgiveness asked first and mercy second, and a closing divine name that mirrors the request (`khayr al-rāḥimīn`, `khayr al-ghāfirīn`). The minimal form is 23:118, five words.

## 2. Counts (script-checked)

- **Address:** `rabbi` 7 (7:151, 7:155, 11:47, 23:118, 28:16, 38:35, 71:28); `rabbanā` 12; none 3 (5:118, 26:82, 26:86). 7+12+3=22.
- **Confession:** explicit self-wrong 2 (7:23, 28:16). Sin named as a noun 4 more (`dhunūb` 3:16, 3:147, 3:193; `khaṭīʾa` 26:82). 2:286 only hypothesises error. **15 of 22 contain no confession.** `kuntu min al-ẓālimīn` (21:87, Yūnus) has *no* `ighfir` at all.
- **Ground:** faith `āmannā` 3 (3:16, 3:193, 23:109); obedience 1 (2:285); divine attribute 2 (40:7, 7:155); trust-and-return 1 (60:4 before 60:5). 12 of 22 give none.
- **Forgiveness first.** In the 7 petitions asking mercy too (2:286, 7:23, 7:151, 7:155, 11:47, 23:109, 23:118), forgiveness precedes mercy 7/7. In 2:286 it is a rising ladder: `ʿafw` → `ghafr` → `raḥma`.
- **Others:** 7 reach beyond the praying group (5:118, 7:151, 14:41, 26:86, 40:7, 59:10, 71:28).
- **Closing:** a divine name or superlative 10; `mawlānā` 1; a clause 2; none 9.

## 3. Order of elements

Modal skeleton **[interpretive reading of the data]**: **address → ground (`fa-`) → `ighfir` → mercy or companion asks → name**. Cleanest realisation, 23:109: *rabbanā, āmannā, fa-ghfir lanā, wa-rḥamnā, wa-anta khayru l-rāḥimīn*.

- `ighfir` is the *first* request in 13 of 17 imperative petitions. Exceptions: 2:286 (burden first), 60:5 (not-a-trial first), 66:8 (light first), 26:86 (fourth in Ibrāhīm's run).
- The six petitions with a ground clause (3:16, 3:193, 7:155, 23:109, 28:16, 40:7) all join it to `ighfir` with **`fa-`** (*so*). No other petition has a ground.
- **Exceptions:** 7:23 and 28:16 put confession *before* the request; 5:118, 7:23, 11:47 are conditional; 26:82 is hope; 2:285 puts the address after the request.

```
23:118  وَقُل رَّبِّ ٱغۡفِرۡ وَٱرۡحَمۡ وَأَنتَ خَيۡرُ ٱلرَّٰحِمِينَ
        "My Lord, forgive and have mercy, and You are the best of the merciful." (Saheeh)
28:16   قَالَ رَبِّ إِنِّي ظَلَمۡتُ نَفۡسِي فَٱغۡفِرۡ لِي فَغَفَرَ لَهُۥٓ
        "My Lord, indeed I have wronged myself, so forgive me," and He forgave him.
```

## 4. Shortest and fullest

- **Minimal:** 23:118 (5 words, no pronoun at all: neither "me" nor "us"), 28:16 (6), 26:86 (6). 28:16 is the only one with a recorded outcome in its own verse: *so He forgave him*.
- **Fullest:** 40:7–9 (about 42 words), 2:286 (37), 3:193–194 (34), 7:155 (31). What the long form adds is **not more forgiveness language**. It adds (a) protection from consequences (*the Fire*, *disgrace*, *evil*), (b) burdens lifted (2:286), (c) steadfastness and victory (2:286, 3:147), (d) the end (*die with the righteous*, *Gardens*). The full form treats forgiveness as the first item in a list of what a servant needs.
- **Bookend:** 23:109 and 23:118 are nearly the same sentence. The Day recounts the believers' words; the surah's last verse commands the Prophet to say them without `āmannā` or `lanā`. The ground and the plural are what the commanded form leaves out. **[interpretive]**

## 5. Singular vs plural

| | `lī` (singular) | `lanā` / `ighfir… lanā` |
|---|---|---|
| Prophets | 7:151, 11:47, 14:41, 26:82, 28:16, 38:35, 71:28 (7) | 7:23, 7:155, 60:5 (3) |
| Non-prophet humans | **0** | **8** (2:285, 2:286, 3:16, 3:147, 3:193, 23:109, 59:10, 66:8) |

**Data-grounded:** every "forgive me" in the Quran's quoted petitions is a prophet's. The believers' form is *forgive us*. Of the singular petitions, only 11:47, 28:16, 38:35 and 26:82 ask for the speaker alone; the rest carry parents, a brother or the believers with them. Ibrāhīm switches from `rabbi` (14:40) to `rabbanā` at the very point of asking forgiveness (14:41), keeping `lī`. **Do not over-read:** the Quran does not forbid "forgive me" to others, and 23:118 commands a bare `ighfir`.

## 6. Setting

19 petitions are spoken in this life. **Spoken in the hereafter:** 5:118 (ʿĪsā answering) and 66:8 (believers with their light). **Spoken here, about the Day:** 23:109 (recounted on the Day), 14:41, 26:82, 26:87, 3:194. Angels (40:7) ask continually for the believers. Prophet-specific vs general: only 23:118 is a command, and it is to the Prophet by `qul`.

## 7. Divine names in the closing (negative space)

`Ghafūr`, `Tawwāb` and `ʿAfuww` **never close a human petition**. Humans close with `khayr al-ghāfirīn` (7:155), `khayr`/`arḥam al-rāḥimīn` (7:151, 23:109, 23:118), `raʾūf raḥīm` (59:10), `al-Wahhāb`, `al-ʿAzīz al-Ḥakīm` (5:118, 40:8, 60:5), `Qadīr` (66:8). `Ghafūr raḥīm` appears only in the *narrator's* reply (28:16). Notably 5:118 closes on `ʿAzīz Ḥakīm` where one expects `Ghafūr`. The closing name often matches the other request: `khayr al-rāḥimīn` after mercy, `al-Wahhāb` after `hab lī`, `Qadīr` after *perfect our light*. **[interpretive]**

## Phase-0 claims corrected

1. "Mercy is asked in 10 of 22." The root `rHm` is in 10 ayahs, but mercy is **requested** in 7. 28:16 is the narrator's names; 40:7 uses mercy as ground; 59:10 uses `raḥīm` as the closing name.
2. "`Zlm` in 3 petitions (7:23, 28:16, 71:28)." 71:28 `al-ẓālimīn` are the people Nūḥ wants destroyed, not a confession. Confession by `ẓlm` is 2.
3. "22 petitions." Only 17 are imperatives; 5:118 (conditional, concerns others), 26:82 (hope, not addressed) and 2:285 (a noun) are not requests in the ordinary sense. 26:82 and 26:86 are one passage of Ibrāhīm.
4. 3:147 speakers are the `ribbiyyūn` with a prophet, i.e. believers, not prophets.

## For practice

- **Address God directly.** Alone, `rabbi`; with others, `rabbanā`. Every quoted form begins that way or has the name inside it.
- **Say it short.** The minimal form (`rabbi ighfir wa-rḥam`) is quoted as a command (23:118). Confession is not required by the wording; two petitions have it (`ẓalamtu nafsī`, `ẓalamnā anfusanā`).
- **If you give a reason, give it with *fa-*:** *we have believed, so forgive us*; *I wronged myself, so forgive me*; *You encompass all things, so forgive*.
- **Ask forgiveness first; ask mercy next; name Him as the best of what you asked.**
- **Use "us" by default.** The believers in the text never say "forgive me"; they ask for the group, including those who came before (59:10), and the prophets add parents and believers (14:41, 71:28).
- **Ask also for what the forgiveness is for:** protection from the Fire, firm feet, light, a good end (3:16, 3:147, 3:193, 66:8).
