# The Destruction-Machinery — Cross-Cutting Synthesis

## 1. Headline finding

The Quran's failure-execution is not arbitrary divine wrath. It is a four-part architecture: (1) a grammar that distributes moral causation — humans choose conditions in passive/middle voice, God executes consequences in active voice; (2) explicit *self-binding* justice-constraints (*wa-mā kunnā* formulas) that make destruction conditional on prior warning and demonstrated wrongdoing; (3) a poetic matching of failure-domain to destruction-means (Pharaoh's sea-power → drowning; Qārūn's earth-wealth → swallowed by earth; ʿĀd's strength → wind); (4) a delayed-consequence theology of respite-then-seizure, with Iblīs as cosmic-scale exception. The historical destructions are *local previews* of the cosmic eschaton, both rendered in the same vocabulary scaled up.

---

## 2. The active-passive grammatical pattern

The grammar IS the theology. Across all six loss/destruction roots and the heart-failure cluster, voice marking distributes agency along a stable axis:

| Root | Active divine form | Passive/middle human form | Distribution | Theological work |
|------|-------------------|---------------------------|--------------|------------------|
| hlk (هلك) | *ahlaknā* (We destroyed) — causative IV, 51 occ | *halaka* (perished) — intransitive, 5 occ | 91% of verbal hlk is active-divine | Destruction *requires* divine initiation |
| xsr (خسر) | — (no active divine *yukhsir*) | *khasirū anfusahum*, *al-khāsirūn* (32×) | Agentive participle dominant | Loss is what humans become, not what God does to them |
| xyb (خيب) | — (no active divine form) | *khāba man dassāhā* (91:10) | All 5 occ are middle-voice "potential frustrated" | The soul's capacity collapses from inside |
| xtm (ختم) | *khatama Allāh* (God sealed) | (no human active *khatama qalbahu*) | Divine-only active | Sealing is divine prerogative |
| qsw (قسو) | *jaʿalnā qulūbahum qāsiya* (5:13) | *qasat qulūbuhum* (their hearts hardened, 2:74, 6:43, 57:16) | Mostly middle-voice "became hard"; divine causation implicit | Hardening reads as middle-voice consequence; God named only when emphasized |
| knn (كنن) | *jaʿalnā ʿalā qulūbihim akinna* (We placed sheaths) | (kāfir self-claim at 41:5: *qulūbunā fī akinna*) | God-as-placer dominant; one human self-attribution | Sheath is *placed* — a divine response to refusal |
| DyE (ضيع) | *lā nuḍīʿu ajra* (We do not waste) — 9× negation | *aḍāʿū al-ṣalāh* (they wasted prayer, 19:59) | God = active negation; humans = active wasters | God does not waste reward; humans waste the relational link |
| bwr (بور) | — | *qawman būran*, *makruhum yabūru* | Predicate-state of human collectives | Withering is what people become |
| bTl (بطل) | *yubṭilu ʿamalakum* (renders deeds void) | *al-bāṭilu kāna zahūqā* (17:81 — falsehood is constitutionally vanishing) | Bāṭil collapses by its own nature | Even active divine "voiding" hits an already-hollow target |

**Two macro-rules emerge:**

**Rule 1 — Active destruction = divine prerogative.** The Quran has no human verb meaning "to destroy oneself" in the *hlk* register. Humans *lose* themselves (*khasirū anfusahum*), *waste* their deeds (*aḍāʿū*), *bury* their souls (*dassāhā*) — but the verb of civilizational annihilation (*ahlaknā*) is reserved for God.

**Rule 2 — God's heart-disablement verbs follow human acts.** *Khatama*, *jaʿalnā akinna*, *zādahum maraḍā* — every divine sealing/sheathing/disease-increasing is preceded by a prior human refusal. The order is invariant: human resistance → divine confirmation. The grammar encodes a procedural justice: divine action *responds* to human action; it does not pre-empt it.

The DyE (ضيع) ratio is the cleanest single witness: 9 of 10 occurrences are *lā yuḍīʿu* (God does *not* waste reward); the one inversion is *aḍāʿū al-ṣalāh* (19:59 — humans waste the prayer that maintained the link). The grammar says God is *constitutively* the non-waster; humans alone do the wasting.

## 3. The justice-constraint architecture (the *wa-mā kunnā* formulas)

The Quran self-binds divine action to procedural fairness through a recurrent negative formula: *wa-mā kunnā* (and We were never / We do not). This is the syntax of divine self-limitation:

> وَمَا كُنَّا مُعَذِّبِينَ حَتَّىٰ نَبۡعَثَ رَسُولًا
> [17:15] "We were never punishers until We sent a messenger."
> Saheeh: "We never punish until We have sent a messenger." Khattab: "Nor would We ever punish [a people] until We have sent a messenger."

> وَمَا كُنَّا مُهۡلِكِي ٱلۡقُرَىٰٓ إِلَّا وَأَهۡلُهَا ظَٰلِمُونَ
> [28:59 cont.] "And We never destroyed cities except when their people were wrongdoers."

The constraint chain across the corpus:

| Verse | Self-binding statement | Implication |
|-------|----------------------|-------------|
| [17:15] | No punishment without prior messenger | Procedural — warning first |
| [28:59] | No city-destruction without (a) sent messenger to its mother-city + (b) wrongdoing inhabitants | Two conditions: warning + guilt |
| [6:131] | "Your Lord would not destroy cities for wrongdoing while their people were unaware" — *bi-ẓulmin wa-ahluhā ghāfilūn* | Awareness condition — no destruction-by-surprise |
| [11:117] | "Your Lord would not destroy cities unjustly while their people were reformers" — *wa-ahluhā muṣliḥūn* | Reformer-immunity — destruction excludes the constructive |
| [35:45] | "If God were to seize people for what they earned, He would not leave a creature on its surface — but He delays them to a stated term" | Non-collective-immediate — built-in mercy delay |
| [6:164], [17:15], [35:18], [39:7], [53:38] | *lā taziru wāziratun wizra ukhrā* — no soul bears another's burden | Individual moral accounting; no inherited guilt |

The cluster constitutes a *charter* of divine procedural fairness. The theological distinctiveness: God is REPRESENTED AS HAVING RULES — and explicitly states them. The Quran's God does not destroy:
- without warning (17:15)
- without wrongdoing (28:59)
- in unawareness (6:131)
- while reformers are present (11:117)
- by inherited or borrowed guilt (35:18)
- without delay (35:45)

The constraints are stated in *wa-mā kunnā* (perfect-tense negation) — not "We will not" but "We have *never been* of that kind." It is constitutive divine character, not contingent policy.

## 4. The matching of failure-mode to destruction-means

The destruction inverts or mirrors the failure-domain. Across the named cases:

| People / Figure | Failure-mode | Destruction-means | Mirror logic |
|-----------------|--------------|------------------|--------------|
| Nūḥ's people | Generational corruption that Nūḥ asks to be totally erased [71:26-27] | *aghraqnā* — drowning by total flood [71:25] | Total corruption → total water-erasure |
| ʿĀd | Boast: "*man ashaddu minnā quwwa?*" — who is greater than us in strength? [41:15] | *Rīḥ ṣarṣar ʿātiya* — violent screaming wind [69:6-7]; *rīḥ ʿaqīm* (sterile wind) [51:41]; reduces them to *aʿjāz nakhl khāwiya* — hollow palm-trunks [69:7] | Strength-claim → stronger physical force; "greatest" reduced to *hollow* |
| Thamūd | *Istaḥabbū al-ʿamā ʿalā al-hudā* — preferred blindness over guidance [41:17]; hamstrung the camel-sign | *Ṣayḥa wāḥida* — a single cry [54:31] reduces them to *hishīm al-muḥtaẓir* (dried chaff); *rajfa* (earthquake) [7:78] | The deaf-to-words destroyed by ONE sound |
| Madyan | Cheated weights and measures; blocked trade-routes [7:85-86] | *Ṣayḥa* + *rajfa* [11:94, 7:91]; Aṣḥāb al-Ayka: *ʿadhāb yawm al-ẓulla* (day of the cloud, 26:189) | Cheaters of *proportion* met *non-proportional* total force |
| Lūṭ's people | Sexual inversion of the natural order [7:80-81] | *Jaʿalnā ʿāliyahā sāfilahā* — We made the high its low [15:74]; cities physically inverted + *ḥijārah min sijjīl* (stones of clay) | Inverted desire → inverted geography |
| Pharaoh | "*Anā rabbukum al-aʿlā*" — I am your highest lord [79:24]; sea-empire and Nile-control (drowned Israelite sons in water, 28:7) | *Aghraqnāhu* — drowned in the sea [10:90]; corpse preserved as a *sign* [10:92] | Sea-power claim → sea-death; would-be divine made *displayed evidence* |
| Hāmān | Institutional complicity — built tower toward heaven [28:38, 40:36-37] | Drowned with Pharaoh's armies [7:136]; no separate destruction | Fate fused with the system he served |
| Qārūn | "*Innamā ūtītuhū ʿalā ʿilmin ʿindī*" — I was given this only by knowledge with me [28:78]; wealth from earth's treasures | *Khasafnā bihi wa-bi-dārihi al-arḍa* — earth swallowed him and his house [28:81] | Wealth FROM earth → consumed BY earth |
| Aṣḥāb al-Ukhdūd | Burned believers in a trench [85:4-7] | *Qutila aṣḥāb al-ukhdūd* — cursed (passive imprecation), Quran withholds explicit means but [85:10] promises *ʿadhāb al-ḥarīq* (burning-punishment) | Fire-burners get fire-fate |

[29:40] catalogues four destruction-types in one verse — *ḥāṣib* (stone-storm, Lot's people), *ṣayḥa* (the cry, Thamūd/Madyan), *khasf* (earth-swallowing, Qārūn), *ighrāq* (drowning, Pharaoh + Nūḥ's people) — each tied externally to its narrative. The four are not interchangeable: each is the *signature destruction* for a class of failure.

The Quran does not state the mirror-logic explicitly. But the parallelism across nine cases is consistent enough to constitute a structural pattern: **the means of failure becomes the means of destruction**. The destruction is *legible* as inversion of the sin — making it pedagogically transparent, not arbitrarily punitive.

## 5. The *yashāʾu* sovereignty

The justice-constraints describe God's *normal* mode but do not formally bind Him. The Quran preserves divine sovereignty via the *yashāʾu* (whom He wills) operator:

- *yuḍillu man yashāʾu wa-yahdī man yashāʾu* — He misguides whom He wills, guides whom He wills [14:4, 16:93, 35:8, 74:31]
- *yaghfiru li-man yashāʾu wa-yuʿadhdhibu man yashāʾu* — He forgives whom He wills, punishes whom He wills [2:284, 5:18, 5:40]
- *yarḥamu man yashāʾu* [29:21]

The theological architecture: rules + sovereign discretion. The constraints describe what God *does*, not what God *must* do. *Yashāʾu* is the discretion-operator; *wa-mā kunnā* is the regularity-operator. They are not contradictory: the regularity is the manifest pattern; the discretion is the underlying source. God *chooses* to bind Himself to procedural fairness — that is what makes the binding theologically meaningful, rather than a metaphysical limit.

The success investigation found the same dual structure: success is given to those who reach for it (*yahdī*-conditions), but ultimately *Allāh yahdī man yashāʾu*. Both ends of the system have the same architecture: rule-governed default + reserved sovereign agency.

## 6. The respite-then-seizure pattern

Failure is not immediately punished. The Quran installs a theology of *delayed consequence*:

- **Istidrāj** (drawing them on stage by stage): *sa-nastadrijuhum min ḥaythu lā yaʿlamūn* — "We will draw them on by degrees from where they do not know" [7:182, 68:44]. Worldly success can be a slow-trap.
- **Imlāʾ** (giving rein): *wa-umlī lahum inna kaydī matīn* — "I give them rein; My plan is firm" [7:183, 68:45]. Respite is part of the plan, not its absence.
- **Imhāl-then-akhdh**: *ka-ayyin min qaryatin amlaytu lahā wa-hiya ẓālimatun thumma akhadhtuhā* — "How many a city I gave respite to while it was wrongdoing, then I seized it" [22:48]. The *thumma* (then) is the seizure-marker.
- **Direct statement**: *wa-laqad amlaytu li-l-ladhīna kafarū thumma akhadhtuhum fa-kayfa kāna nakīr* — "I gave the disbelievers respite, then I seized them — how was My disapproval?" [22:44, paraphrased at 13:32].

The pattern: failure-arc completes, then seizure. This connects to the *ajal musamman* (appointed term) architecture from the time investigation — every nation has an *ajal* that comes when its failure-trajectory has run its course. Destruction is not interruption; it is *fulfillment* of the term:

> لِكُلِّ أُمَّةٍ أَجَلٌ فَإِذَا جَآءَ أَجَلُهُمۡ لَا يَسۡتَأۡخِرُونَ سَاعَةً وَلَا يَسۡتَقۡدِمُونَ
> [7:34, also 10:49, 16:61] "For every community there is a term; when their term comes, they cannot delay it an hour nor advance it."

Time itself is the medium of justice: the gap between failure and seizure is the *opportunity* for repentance. The respite is mercy. When the term arrives, it is because the opportunity is exhausted.

## 7. The Iblīs exception — cosmic respite for the cosmic adversary

Iblīs requested respite *till the Day they are raised* and was GRANTED it [7:14-15, 15:36-38, 38:79-81]. This is the maximal respite in the Quranic system — full creation-history.

Why grant the cosmic adversary such generous terms? Two convergent readings:

**(a) Test-architecture necessity.** Iblīs's continued presence is the *condition* for human moral testing. Without an active tempter, the bifurcation between Adam-trajectory (admit fault → repent → succeed: *qālā rabbanā ẓalamnā anfusanā* [7:23]) and Iblīs-trajectory (claim ontological superiority → blame the system → fall) cannot be played out across humanity. The adversary's respite IS the human test-arena.

**(b) The most extreme respite for the most extreme failure.** This is the Quran's most striking inversion: the same *delay-mechanism* that mercifully postpones human destruction is given without limit to the worst rebel. The pattern is consistent — even Iblīs receives procedural fairness. He asks; he is granted; the seizure waits until the resurrection's gathering [38:81: *ilā yawm al-waqt al-maʿlūm*].

The cosmic frame: the most extreme respite enables the most extreme test, which enables the most extreme moral differentiation. Iblīs is the unblinking demonstration that *yashāʾu*-discretion can grant respite even to the cosmically irreversible.

## 8. Heart-disablement as procedural divine response

The heart-failure cluster (qsw / xtm / qfl / mrD / glf / knn) operates by the same procedural-justice logic as the cosmic destructions:

**Stage 1** — Human resists message (covenant-breaking, taking desire as god, refusing the camel-sign, time-elapsed without engagement).
**Stage 2** — God responds with hardening / sealing / sheathing / disease-increasing.
**Stage 3** — Human now CANNOT receive the message — but the cause was the prior refusal.

The verbs and their triggers:
- *thumma qasat qulūbukum* (then your hearts hardened, 2:74) — the *thumma* marks the prior cause (witnessing the cow-miracle and still resisting)
- *jaʿalnā qulūbahum qāsiyatan* (5:13) — explicit causation: covenant-breaking → divine hardening
- *fa-zādahum Allāhu maraḍā* (2:10) — hypocrites' wavering → God increases the disease
- *jaʿalnā ʿalā qulūbihim akinna an yafqahūhu* (6:25, 17:46, 18:57) — turning away from signs → divine sheathing
- *khatama Allāh ʿalā qulūbihim* (2:7) — leadership in disbelief → divine seal

Heart-failure is the personal-scale analogue of civilizational destruction. The same procedural logic operates: divine action confirms a state the human has already chosen. The cluster is the inverse of *sharḥ al-ṣadr* (divine breast-opening for the oriented heart, success investigation): the same God responds to orientation with opening, and to refusal with closure. Both responses are procedurally just.

The Israelite *ghulf* claim ("our hearts are wrapped" [2:88, 4:155]) is the test-case: the Quran refuses to validate self-asserted disablement. The *bal* (rather/nay) refutation places agency back on the human: it is *kufr* that brought the curse, not a passive incapacity. Even claiming you cannot is treated as another active refusal.

## 9. The archaeological pedagogy

The destruction-narratives point to physical *standing evidence*. The Quran addresses an audience that can *go look*:

- *fa-tilka buyūtuhum khāwiyatan bi-mā ẓalamū* — "Those are their houses, fallen empty because of the wrong they did." [27:52]
- *fa-hal tarā lahum min bāqiya* — "Do you see any remnant of them?" [69:8]
- *fa-aṣbaḥū lā yurā illā masākinuhum* — "Morning found them with nothing visible but their dwellings." [46:25]
- *wa-innakum la-tamurrūna ʿalayhim muṣbiḥīna wa-bi-l-layl* — "You pass by them in the morning and at night" [37:137-138, of Lot's cities]
- *a-fa-lam yasīrū fī al-arḍ fa-yanẓurū kayfa kāna ʿāqibatu al-ladhīna min qablihim* — [12:109, 30:9, 35:44, 40:21, 40:82, 47:10]

The pedagogy is *geographical*. The ʿĀd ruins (al-Aḥqāf), Thamūd ruins (al-Ḥijr), Lot's overturned cities — on trade routes the Meccans traversed. The destroyed dwellings are simultaneously the destruction-record AND the present warning. [25:39] parallels: *kullan ḍarabnā lahu al-amthāla wa-kullan tabbarnā tatbīran* — parable-striking and physical destruction as paired instruments of the same pedagogy.

## 10. The cosmic-destruction endgame — failure-vocabulary scaled up

The largest destruction is reserved for the END, in the SAME vocabulary scaled to creation-level:

- [81:1-14] *idhā al-shamsu kuwwirat... al-nujūmu nkadarat... al-jibālu suyyirat* — sun rolled up, stars fall, mountains move
- [82:1-5] sky split, stars scattered
- [99:1-6] *zulzilati al-arḍu zilzālahā* — earth shaken with its own quaking
- [101:1-5] *al-qāriʿa* — the striking calamity; mountains as fluffed wool
- [69:13-16] single trumpet-blast; mountains and earth crushed in *one* blow

The vocabulary is continuous: *ṣayḥa* (Thamūd's cry) becomes the trumpet-blast; *rajfa* (Madyan's earthquake) becomes world-shaking; *aghraqnā* (Pharaoh's drowning) becomes seas set on fire (*al-biḥāru sujjirat*, 81:6). Historical destructions are *local previews*. The justice-constraints map onto the eschaton: prophets' warnings precede; wrongdoers are seized for what *they* did; no soul bears another's burden. The *machinery* — procedural-justice rules + matching destruction + delayed seizure — operates at every scale, from individual heart-sealing to civilizational annihilation to the cosmic Day.

## 11. Cross-investigation — divine agency mirror in success

The success investigation (*concept-success-deep.md*) found a precise grammatical-theological mirror:

| Axis | Success | Failure |
|------|---------|---------|
| Divine ACTIVE verb | *yahdī*, *yarḥamu*, *yatūbu ʿalā*, *zakkā*, *fataḥa*, *sharaḥa al-ṣadr*, *naṣara* | *ahlaka*, *khatama*, *aghraqa*, *khasafa*, *ʿaḏḏabnā*, *jaʿalnā ʿalā qulūbihim akinna* |
| Human PASSIVE/middle form | *ihtadā* (be guided), *al-muflihūn* (be among the prosperers), *muttaqūn* (made-to-shield), *zukkū* (be purified) | *al-khāsirūn* (be among the losers), *khāba* (be frustrated), *qasat qulūbuhum* (became hard), *halaka* (perished) |
| Source-marker | *min rabbihim* (from their Lord, 2:5) | *min ʿindi Allāh* destruction-formulas; "We destroyed" |
| Conditional gate | Human cooperation: *in tanṣurū Allāha yanṣurkum* (47:7) | Human resistance: refusal → divine confirmation |
| Sovereign override | *yahdī man yashāʾu* (whom He wills, 14:4) | *yuḍillu man yashāʾu* (same verse) |
| Delay structure | Iqtiraba al-amr — opportunity-window | Imhāl + ajal musamman — respite-window |

The structure is **mirror-symmetric**: divine ACTIVE giving + human PASSIVE receiving (success); divine ACTIVE executing + human PASSIVE experiencing (failure). Both are gated by human prior-orientation. Both reserve sovereignty in the *yashāʾu* operator. Both operate on a delay-then-fulfillment temporal architecture.

The single most concentrated mirror-pair is [91:9-10]:

> قَدۡ أَفۡلَحَ مَن زَكَّىٰهَا — He has succeeded who purifies it [the soul]
> وَقَدۡ خَابَ مَن دَسَّىٰهَا — He has failed who buries it

Same syntactic frame. *Aflaḥa* (succeeded — passive-of-divine-rescue) vs *khāba* (was frustrated — middle-voice collapse). The human's act (*zakkāhā* / *dassāhā*) is the trigger; the outcome is *received* in both directions.

## 12. Negative space

**No tragic destruction.** The Quran has no vocabulary for civilizational collapse by impersonal cosmic force, by unjust gods, or by tragic fate. Every destruction has a *named* failure-mode and a *justified* divine response.

**No destruction without messenger.** [17:15] formally excludes this. There is no *unwarned* destroyed people in the Quran's named-narratives.

**No reversal-mechanism for sealed hearts.** Heart-opening (*sharḥ al-ṣadr*) is described as divine response to orientation. Heart-unsealing is never described — the silence is load-bearing.

**No collective inherited guilt.** [35:18] excludes burden-transfer. Each individual is seized *bi-dhanbihi* (by HIS sin, 29:40).

**No competition between justice and sovereignty.** The *wa-mā kunnā* and *yashāʾu* operators sit comfortably together. The Quran is not anxious to resolve them.

**No "natural" destruction.** Even wind, earthquake, flood, and stones are divine instruments — not autonomous nature. The destruction-modes are *signs*, never blind forces.

**No female named destruction-archetype.** Pharaoh, Hāmān, Qārūn, Iblīs are named individual failures. No woman is named as such; Āsiya (Pharaoh's wife) is the named *counter*-example from inside the failed household.

## 13. Verses for direct study

1. **[28:59]** — the most explicit justice-constraint: *wa-mā kāna rabbuka muhlika al-qurā ḥattā yabʿatha fī ummihā rasūlan... wa-mā kunnā muhlikī al-qurā illā wa-ahluhā ẓālimūn*. The two-condition gate on civilizational destruction.

2. **[10:90-92]** — the Pharaoh death-bed scene + corpse-as-sign: the *āl-āna* divine interrogation, the rejection of coerced faith, and the body preserved as standing evidence.

3. **[28:78-81]** — Qārūn's *ʿilm ʿindī* claim and earth-swallowing in tight sequence: the meritocratic claim refuted by historical counter-evidence, then the destruction matching the wealth-domain.

4. **[7:182-183]** + **[22:48]** — the *istidrāj* and *imhāl-then-akhdh* respite-theology in compressed form.

5. **[91:9-10]** — the success-failure mirror pair: *aflaḥa man zakkāhā / khāba man dassāhā* — same syntax, opposite verbs, both with human-act trigger and received-outcome.

6. **[2:7-12]** — the heart-typology in 12 ayahs: muttaqūn (heart works) → kāfirūn (heart sealed) → munāfiqūn (heart diseased and increased). The compressed three-class map.

7. **[81:1-14]** — the cosmic eschaton in failure-vocabulary scaled up: *kuwwirat*, *nkadarat*, *suyyirat*, *suʿʿirat* — historical destruction-verbs at universal scale.

## 14. Open questions / rabbit holes

- **Map every named destroyed people against the *prior messenger* condition.** Verify each *kam ahlaknā* case has a named warner. Aṣḥāb al-Rass (no narrative) is the test.

- **The *ṣayḥa* / *rajfa* / *trumpet* continuity.** Trace whether Thamūd's *ṣayḥa*, Madyan's *rajfa*, and the eschatological *nafkhat al-ṣūr* are vocabulary-distinct or the same phenomenon at different scales.

- **The four destruction-modes of [29:40] as exhaustive taxonomy.** Where do Lot's *sijjīl*-stones and ʿĀd's *rīḥ ṣarṣar* map? Test whether [29:40] catalogues the full canonical typology or a sample.

- **Iblīs's eventual seizure.** Iblīs gets full respite — does the Quran describe his Day-of-Judgment fate analogously to historical destructions? Trace [38:85, 7:18, 15:43, 26:91-95].

- **Heart-disablement reversibility.** The Quran is silent on un-sealing, but God is *al-Tawwāb*. Does *tawba*-vocabulary ever apply to people previously *makhtūm*?

- **Pedagogy verification.** Map the geographical accessibility of al-Ḥijr (Thamūd), al-Aḥqāf (ʿĀd), the Dead Sea (Lot) to 7th-century Arabia.

- **The *yashāʾu* + *wa-mā kunnā* tension.** Co-occurrence analysis: where both operators appear in the same passage, how is the tension handled?
