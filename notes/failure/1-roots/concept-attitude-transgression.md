# Attitude/Transgression Cluster: kbr · Tgy · bgy · srf · fjr

## 1. Headline Finding

Five roots map a complete architecture of human moral failure. **istakbara** (Form X of kbr) is the cosmic signature verb: Iblīs's refusal to bow [2:34, 38:74], Pharaoh's self-deification [28:39], the Meccan elites' rejection of the Prophet — all use the same form. The hypothesis holds: **kbr is the root failure** (self-elevation over God or God's command); Tgy (transgression of limits), bgy (insolence/rebellion against authority), srf (excess/no moderation), and fjr (moral wickedness) are downstream expressions of the same internal movement. Crucially, [91:8] reveals that the soul was created with BOTH fujūr and taqwā already inscribed in it — the dual nature is ontological, not accidental.

## 3. kbr / istakbara — The Cosmic Refusal-to-Bow

Form X (istafʿala) = "to deem/make oneself X." **istakbara** = reflexive self-elevation. The 40 istakbara occurrences catalog every category capable of this failure: Iblīs [2:34, 38:74], Pharaoh and his armies [28:39], the Thamūd elite [7:75–76], Shuʿayb's wealthy opponents [7:88], Meccan elites [31:7, 45:8], and a general categorical threat: "Those who are too arrogant to worship Me will enter Hell" [40:60].

The structural constant: **the object of istakbara is always a legitimate command or truth** — prostration [38:74–75], the Quranic signs [7:36, 7:40], the messengers [23:46], worship [40:60]. The Form X makes explicit that arrogance is internally generated; they *made themselves* great. The greatness was never real.

Theological inversion at [59:23]: **al-Mutakabbir** (The One Who Rightfully Claims Greatness) is a Divine Name. Same morphological form — legitimate when applied to God, catastrophic when a creature applies it to itself. Pharaoh usurps al-kibriyāʾ (Majesty) at [10:78] — a term otherwise reserved for God [45:37].

## 4. The kbr/sjd Binary

kbr and sjd (prostration) co-occur in **6 ayahs** (share 0.039), all anchored at the Iblīs narrative:

> **وَإِذۡ قُلۡنَا لِلۡمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ ٱسۡجُدُواْ لِأٓدَمَ فَسَجَدُوٓاْ إِلَّآ إِبۡلِيسَ أَبَىٰ وَٱسۡتَكۡبَرَ وَكَانَ مِنَ ٱلۡكَٰفِرِينَ**
> [2:34] — "He refused and acted arrogantly, and became of the disbelievers." (Ṣaḥīḥ International)

Three verbs, causal sequence: *abā* (refused) → *istakbara* (arrogance as cause) → *kāna mina-l-kāfirīn* (disbelief as result). This establishes kbr → kfr as the structural chain. kfr is not primary; it is the end-state of istakbara.

At [38:74–75], God challenges Iblīs directly: *"Were you arrogant, or were you already among the exalted?"* — an interrogation that defines arrogance as either temporary pride or metaphysical status-claim. Iblīs answers neither; he defaults to comparative denigration: "I am better than him" [38:76]. The creation-substance argument IS the arrogance — claiming that origin-material confers superiority.

**Data-consistent interpretation:** sjd = the physical expression of recognizing creaturely status. kbr = refusal of that recognition. The binary maps the entire Quranic moral axis: orientation toward God (bowing) vs. orientation toward self (claiming superiority). All downstream failures are specifications of the second posture.

## 5. Tgy — The Pharaoh Archetype

Tgy's semantic core: **overriding a designated limit** — physical or moral. At [55:8], the same verb applied to Pharaoh ("do not ṭaghā the balance") applies to cosmic measure. The commissioning of Moses is terse:

> **ٱذۡهَبۡ إِلَىٰ فِرۡعَوۡنَ إِنَّهُۥ طَغَىٰ**
> [79:17] — "Go to Pharaoh — indeed he has transgressed." (Ṣaḥīḥ International)

Pharaoh's ṭaghā is then defined at [79:24]: *"I am your lord, the most high"* (ana rabbukum al-aʿlā) — usurping the Divine Name al-Aʿlā [87:1]. ṭaghā = **claiming divine sovereignty over persons under one's power**.

**ṭāghūt** (8 occurrences) is the institutionalized form: not just a person who transgresses but a system — false god, political idol, competing sovereignty. [2:256]: "Whoever disbelieves in ṭāghūt and believes in Allah has grasped the firmest handle." [4:76] polarizes armies: for God vs. for ṭāghūt.

The mechanism of ṭughyān is given at [96:6–7]: *"man transgresses (yaṭghā) because he sees himself self-sufficient (istaghna)."* Perceived self-sufficiency = the political expression of kbr. ṭughyān is kbr in power.

## 6. bgy — Insolence and the ibtaghā Ambiguity

bgy contains a critical semantic split. **ibtaghā** (Form VIII, 34 occurrences) = seeking/pursuing — predominantly POSITIVE. It co-occurs with Allāh (اله) at 61% share; *ibtighaʾa wajh Allāh* (seeking God's face, [2:265, 13:22]) is one of the Quran's highest spiritual expressions. The failure register belongs entirely to baghā/baghy (36 occurrences combined).

The **baghy formula** — *baghyan baynahum* (out of insolence between themselves) — appears verbatim at [2:213], [3:19], and [42:14]:

> [2:213] — "They did not differ except after clear proofs came to them — **out of insolence between themselves**."

Tripled repetition = structurally important claim. Religious schism is not caused by uncertainty; communities KNOW the truth. They split for power (baghy). Sectarianism is diagnosed as volitional, not epistemic.

At [7:33], baghy is enumerated alongside fāḥisha (immorality), ithm (sin), and shirk as one of four things truly forbidden. At [16:90], God "forbids immorality, wickedness, and baghy" — a comprehensive social prohibition. At [7:45]: *yabghūnahā ʿiwajan* (they seek to make the way deviant) — baghy as active sabotage of truth.

## 7. srf — The Moderation Command

23 occurrences, 3 lemmas, single semantic field. **isrāf = exceeding measure in any direction**. The formula:

> **وَكُلُواْ وَٱشۡرَبُواْ وَلَا تُسۡرِفُوٓاْۚ إِنَّهُۥ لَا يُحِبُّ ٱلۡمُسۡرِفِينَ**
> [7:31] — "Eat and drink, but do not be excessive. Indeed, He does not love those who commit excess." (Ṣaḥīḥ International)

*Lā yuḥibbu al-musrifīn* — a divine aesthetic claim, not merely a prohibition: the excessive are repugnant to God. The same formula recurs at [6:141]. The domain of srf is deliberately wide: food/spending [7:31, 6:141], the balanced middle (*qawāman*) at [25:67], legitimate retaliation [17:33] — *"do not exceed in killing"* (even justified vengeance has a srf-limit), and power [10:83] where Pharaoh is explicitly named among *al-musrifīn*. At [39:53], *asrafū ʿalā anfusihim* (those who exceeded against themselves) = the Quranic term for sinners seeking mercy.

**srf is the most contextually bounded failure** — unlike kbr (orientation-failure), srf can occur in single domains without full self-elevation. It is a failure of measure, not of ground-orientation.

## 8. fjr — Wickedness (Distinct from Dawn-Sense)

The dawn-sense (6 fajr occurrences) belongs to the time investigation. The wickedness register: fujjār (4 occ), fujūr (1 occ), fājir (1 occ), yafjuru (2 occ). The entire theological load rests on one verse:

> **فَأَلۡهَمَهَا فُجُورَهَا وَتَقۡوَىٰهَا**
> [91:8] — "And He inspired it [the soul] with its fujūr AND its taqwā." (Ṣaḥīḥ International)

The verb *alhamahā* (inspired) is critical: God is the agent of BOTH inscriptions. The soul receives the knowledge of wickedness AND God-consciousness from the same source. Not dualism — the surrounding verses show taqwā prevails for those who purify (zakkāhā) [91:9] and fujūr prevails for those who bury (dassāhā) [91:10] — but the DUAL INSCRIPTION is the condition for moral responsibility. You cannot violate a law you were never taught.

The eschatological stakes: fujjār "will be in hellfire" [82:14]; their record is in Sijjīn (lowest register) [83:7]. At [38:28], fujjār is the direct antonym of muttaqīn.

The physical etymology is load-bearing: fjr = to burst/gush through. fujūr = the soul **bursting through** its moral constraints, as water bursts through a dam. Same root as the miraculous springs Moses struck from the rock.

## 9. The Five-Fold Failure Architecture

**Hypothesis: kbr is the root failure; the others are downstream specifications.** Three evidential supports:

1. **Iblīs's sequence** [2:34] — istakbara first; all subsequent human failures (Tgy, bgy, srf, fjr) are his program for humanity after his fall. The root failure generates the downstream program.

2. **Pharaoh embodies all five** — taghā [79:17] + istakbara [28:39] + divine lordship claim [79:24] + musrifīn [10:83] + social bgy over his subjects. One figure instantiates the whole architecture because kbr generates the other four in sequence.

3. **The self-sufficiency mechanism** [96:6–7] — man taghā BECAUSE he sees himself self-sufficient (istaghna). Perceived independence = kbr. ṭughyān is kbr expressed politically.

```
kbr (self-elevation / refusal to bow)
│
├── fjr: soul-corruption (bursting through moral constraints — internal)
├── Tgy: political transgression (claiming divine sovereignty)
├── bgy: social rebellion (splitting communities for power)
└── srf: material excess (failing measure in any domain)
```

Positive-opposite table:

| Failure root | Positive opposite |
|---|---|
| kbr (arrogance) | tawāḍuʿ / khushūʿ (humility / lowering oneself) |
| Tgy (transgression) | mīzān (the balance — 55:8–9) |
| bgy (insolence) | ʿadl (justice / following legitimate authority) |
| srf (excess) | qawām (the balanced middle — 25:67) |
| fjr (wickedness) | taqwā (God-consciousness — 91:8) |

## 10. Negative Space

**What the Quran does NOT do:** It never uses istakbara as a Divine attribute — only mutakabbir (God's rightful greatness) vs. istakbara (creature's usurped greatness). The Form X is structurally reserved for illegitimate self-elevation; Form V and VI for the same act described descriptively. The Quran maintains the distinction between God's kibriyāʾ (Majesty, [45:37]) and the creature's kibr (pride, [40:56]) with precision. No verse conflates them.

**What the bgy data reveals:** ibtaghā (seeking) is predominantly positive in the Quran (34/96 occurrences), meaning the root is not condemned wholesale — only baghy/baghā in the sense of insolence. This complicates a simple reading; the Quran uses the same consonants for both noble pursuit and destructive rebellion.

## 11. Verses for Direct Study

1. **[2:34]** — The Iblīs sequence: abā + istakbara + kāna mina-l-kāfirīn. The compressed causal chain: refusal → arrogance → disbelief.
2. **[38:74–75]** — God's direct challenge to Iblīs: "Were you arrogant, or already among the exalted?" The rhetorical question that defines the failure.
3. **[91:7–10]** — The four-verse soul passage: created, balanced, inspired with BOTH fujūr and taqwā, then the two outcomes (zakkāhā / dassāhā). The minimal model of moral ontology.
4. **[2:213]** — The baghy formula for religious fragmentation: "they differed only baghyan baynahum." The diagnosis of sectarianism as power-seeking, not theological uncertainty.
5. **[96:6–7]** — The mechanism of ṭughyān: man transgresses because he sees himself self-sufficient (istaghna). The link between perceived independence and cosmic violation.

## 12. Rabbit Holes

- **kbr and kfr co-occurrence** — kfr (كفر) co-occurs with kbr at share 0.118 (18 ayahs). The causal chain istakbara → kāna mina-l-kāfirīn [2:34, 38:74] suggests kbr is the mechanism of kufr. Worth mapping: does every major act of kufr in the Quran have an istakbara precursor?

- **ṭāghūt as political theology** — 8 occurrences, 3 of which are in Medinan surahs [2:256–257, 4:60, 5:60]. The Medinan concentration suggests ṭāghūt-vocabulary was developed as a political-legal term for illegitimate sovereignty. Compare with the Meccan usage (3:52, 16:36, 39:17) where it seems more general. Does ṭāghūt evolve from theological to political-legal?

- **The 25:67 qawām verse** — srf's positive opposite (qawāman = the balanced middle). This verse is in the longer passage describing ʿibād al-raḥmān (servants of the Merciful). The moderation virtue is listed among the characteristics of God's closest servants. Worth cross-referencing with the JUSTICE/BALANCE investigation.

- **ibtaghā as positive seeking** — 34 of bgy's 96 occurrences are the Form VIII seeking-form, mostly positive. The formula *ibtighaʾa wajh Allāh* (seeking the face of God, 2:265, 13:22) is one of the Quran's most beautiful expressions of sincere action. The root that contains rebellion also contains the highest form of spiritual motivation. A semantic-range deep-read would clarify the morphological triggers.

- **mutakabbir as Divine Name [59:23]** — This is the only appearance of al-Mutakabbir as God's attribute. All 6 other mutakabbir occurrences refer to human arrogance (condemned). The verse [59:23] lists it in a sequence of Divine Names that culminates in "al-ʿAzīz al-Jabbār al-Mutakabbir." The theological question: what does it mean for the SAME morphological form to be the human failure and the Divine attribute? Is it that greatness (kbr) is not wrong in itself — only when creatures appropriate it?
