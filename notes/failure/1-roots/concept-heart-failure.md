# Concept profile: Heart-Failure Predicates (qsw / xtm / qfl / mrD / glf / knn)

> Six roots, 54 total occurrences, covering every Quranic mode of heart-disablement. Each is a PREDICATE-FORMULA applied directly to qalb (heart). Together they form the Quran's catalog of DISABLED HEARTS — the inversion of the orientation-trait found in the success investigation.

---

## 1. Headline finding

The Quran names six distinct modes of heart-failure: hardening (qsw), sealing (xtm), locking (qfl), disease (mrD), covered/wrapped (glf), and sheathed (knn). These are not synonyms — each does specific theological work. All six share a causal logic: human resistance comes first, divine disablement follows. God is the agent of sealing, sheathing, and hardening — but only after the human has already turned away. The Israelites' counter-claim ("our hearts are wrapped" — glf) is the single case where humans assert the disablement themselves; the Quran immediately refutes it. The cluster maps the Quran's complete anatomy of hearts that cannot receive guidance.

---

## 2. Why heart-conditions form the cluster

The success investigation found the decisive success-trait is ORIENTATION, not capability — turning the heart toward God enables God to open it further (the shraH al-sadr formula). Heart-failure is the inversion: the heart loses capacity to receive, process, and respond to the message. The cognition investigation established the heart (qalb) as the Quran's primary epistemic organ — locus of fiqh, ʿaql, and dhikr. These six roots are the Quran's vocabulary for disabling that organ.

---

## 3. qsw — Hardness as consequence (7 occ, 6 ayahs, 6 surahs)

Root statistics: 3 verbal (qasato), 3 adjectival noun (qāsiya), 1 abstract noun (qaswah). Appears in 4 Medinan, 2 Meccan surahs. Co-occurs with qlb (heart) in 100% of its ayahs — the strongest binding in the entire cluster.

The foundational verse is 2:74:

> **ثُمَّ قَسَتۡ قُلُوبُكُم مِّنۢ بَعۡدِ ذَٰلِكَ فَهِيَ كَٱلۡحِجَارَةِ أَوۡ أَشَدُّ قَسۡوَةً**
> "Then your hearts became hardened after that, being like stones or even harder." [2:74]

The word "thumma" (then, after that) is the key. Hardening is always CONSEQUENT. At 2:74 the prior event was the Israelites witnessing the miracle of the cow yet still resisting. At 5:13 the cause is covenant-breaking (naqḍ al-mīthāq) → God MADE their hearts hard ("jaʿalnā qulūbahum qāsiya"). At 6:43 the cause is failure to humble themselves (taḍarruʿ) when punishment arrived. At 57:16 the cause is simple passage of time without engagement ("fā-ṭāla ʿalayhim al-amad fa-qasat qulūbuhum" — the time-elapsed-hardening formula the time investigation flagged).

The causal chain is consistent: PRIOR FAILURE → HARDENING. The hardness metaphor at 2:74 performs an inversion: even some stones gush rivers or fall in awe of God. The hardened heart is worse than the hardest stone — stones still respond to God; the hard heart does not.

At 39:22, hardness is the explicit CONTRAST to the opened breast (shraH al-sadr): "woe to those whose hearts are hardened against the remembrance of God — those are in manifest error." This is the success-inversion formula stated directly.

At 22:53, hardened hearts (qāsiya qulūbuhum) appear ALONGSIDE diseased hearts (mrD) — the only ayah where two of our six roots co-occur. This confirms they are distinct states, not synonyms.

## 4. xtm — Divine sealing + triple-sense-disablement (8 occ, 8 ayahs, 7 surahs)

Root statistics: 5 verbal (khatama), 1 noun xātam ("seal" used for the Prophet in 33:40), 1 adjective makhṭūm ("sealed wine" in 83:25), 1 noun khitām ("seal/end" in 83:26). Appears in 6 Meccan, 2 Medinan surahs. The heart-sealing senses are 2:7, 6:46, 42:24, 45:23 — the remaining occurrences are peripheral (prophet as seal, sealed wine).

The foundational verse is 2:7:

> **خَتَمَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمۡ وَعَلَىٰ سَمۡعِهِمۡۖ وَعَلَىٰٓ أَبۡصَٰرِهِمۡ غِشَٰوَةٌ**
> "Allah has set a seal upon their hearts and upon their hearing, and over their vision is a veil." [2:7]

This is TRIPLE-SENSE-DISABLEMENT: (1) qalb (heart/cognition) sealed, (2) samʿ (hearing) sealed, (3) abṣār (sight) veiled. The three cognitive-reception channels — understanding, hearing the message, seeing the signs — are all closed simultaneously. Note that for hearing and heart the verb is khatama (sealed), but for sight a different word ghishāwa (veil/covering) is used, suggesting a distinct layer of image.

At 45:23 the pattern repeats: "He took his desire as his god, and God led him astray ʿalā ʿilm and sealed over his hearing and heart and placed a veil over his sight." The sealing follows the moral collapse (taking desire as deity). At 6:46 sealing is presented as a divine power-question: "what other god could restore your hearing and sight and un-seal your hearts?" — a taḥaddi about divine monopoly on reversal. At 42:24 the sealing is hypothetical, spoken to the Prophet: "if God willed, He could seal your heart." The conditional implies the Prophet's heart is NOT sealed, but the possibility is real if he fabricated lies — sealing is a divine prerogative that follows moral failure, not an arbitrary predetermination.

## 5. qfl — The locks (47:24) — hapax legomenon

One occurrence, one ayah, one surah (Medinan). The only form is aqfāluhā (plural construct of qifl — locks, plural noun). The word is unique in the Quran (hapax).

> **أَفَلَا يَتَدَبَّرُونَ ٱلۡقُرۡءَانَ أَمۡ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبٍ أَقۡفَالُهَآ**
> "Do they not contemplate the Quran, or are there locks upon their hearts?" [47:24]

The rhetorical form is a binary challenge: EITHER they refuse to contemplate (tadabbur — the Quran-pondering root from the cognition investigation), OR their hearts are locked. The "or" (am) structures this as mutually exclusive possibilities — but both are damning. The verse does not resolve which: it leaves the locked-heart as an open possibility, effectively asking the audience to diagnose themselves.

The co-occurrence with qrA (reading/Quran) and dbr (pondering) in the same ayah is significant: the Quran names itself as the object that locked hearts cannot receive. The locks metaphor is more emphatic than sealing — a lock (qifl) has a keyhole; a seal (khatm) does not. Locks imply a key exists. But the verse offers no key. The question-form keeps the theological verdict open, which may be more confrontational than a declarative judgment would be.

## 6. mrD — Heart-disease, the hypocrites' state (24 occ, 23 ayahs, 13 surahs)

Root statistics: 13 maraḍ (disease as noun, all heart-disease), 10 marīḍ/marḍā (physically sick person), 1 verbal mariḍtu (I fell ill — 26:80, Abraham speaking). The split is crucial: 13 heart-disease occurrences vs. 10 literal illness. The heart-disease meaning dominates numerically among the nominal occurrences (maraḍ).

The foundational verse is 2:10, immediately following the sealed-hearts of 2:7:

> **فِي قُلُوبِهِم مَّرَضٌ فَزَادَهُمُ ٱللَّهُ مَرَضًا**
> "In their hearts is disease, so Allah increased their disease." [2:10]

This is the munāfiqūn (hypocrites) verse — 2:8-12 introduces the class of people who SAY they believe but do not. The Quran's opening sequence maps three classes of hearts:
- Muttaqūn (2:1-5): the heart WORKS — they believe, pray, spend
- Kāfirūn (2:6-7): the heart is SEALED — beyond warning
- Munāfiqūn (2:8-12): the heart is DISEASED — wavering, deceptive

Disease is graduated, not binary — it can be increased ("fa-zādahum Allāh maraḍā"). Its sign is perceptual confusion: the munāfiqūn do not know they are corrupt (2:12: "they do not perceive") — they claim to be reformers while being corrupters. Disease disrupts self-perception.

At 22:53 hardened hearts (qāsiya) and diseased hearts (mrD) appear in the same ayah — the only direct co-occurrence among our six roots — confirming these are distinct failure modes. The word maraḍ never describes physical illness in the "fī qulūbihim" formula; bodily sickness uses marīḍ/marḍā and appears only in legal contexts (fasting, purification).

## 7. glf — "Our hearts are covered" — the Israelite excuse (2 occ, 2 ayahs)

Both occurrences use the same form ghulf (plural noun). Both are Medinan. Both involve the Israelites' first-person claim. The co-occurring roots include kfr (disbelief), lʿn (cursing), and Amn (faith) in both.

> **وَقَالُواْ قُلُوبُنَا غُلۡفٌ بَل لَّعَنَهُمُ ٱللَّهُ بِكُفۡرِهِمۡ**
> "They said, 'Our hearts are wrapped.' But Allah has cursed them for their disbelief." [2:88]

The grammatical structure is decisive: "qālū" (they SAID) + first-person claim + "bal" (RATHER/NO) + divine counter-statement. The Quran does not validate the claim. The bal (nay/rather) performs a refutation — the Israelites claim inability (our hearts are covered = we cannot receive your message), and the Quran rejects the excuse by redirecting to agency (their disbelief, kufr, not an involuntary covering).

At 4:155 the glf claim appears in a list of Israelite crimes: covenant-breaking → disbelief in signs → killing prophets → SAYING "our hearts are ghulf." The verbal claim is enumerated as one more active transgression, not a neutral description. The refutation at 4:155 uses a different verb: "God SEALED (ṭabaʿa) them for their disbelief" — ṭabaʿa (stamped) rather than khatama, suggesting the Quran has multiple heart-closure verbs to counter human excuse-claims. The theological point: glf is not a valid category. The Quran refuses to grant anyone immunity from responsibility by asserting their own heart is wrapped. Rejected twice, with escalating consequence (curse in 2:88, sealing in 4:155).

## 8. knn — Divine sheaths over hearts (akinna) — 4 heart-specific occurrences

Root has 4 lemmas and 12 total occurrences. Heart-relevant lemma: akinna (sheath/covering, plural) at 6:25, 17:46, 18:57, 41:5. Remaining occurrences are maknūn (concealed pearls/Tablet) and verbal forms about concealing intentions. Distribution: 11 Meccan, 1 Medinan.

The formula is nearly verbatim across 3 of 4 occurrences:

> **وَجَعَلۡنَا عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمۡ أَكِنَّةً أَن يَفۡقَهُوهُ وَفِيٓ ءَاذَانِهِمۡ وَقۡرًا**
> "We have placed over their hearts sheaths lest they understand it (yafqahūhu), and in their ears heaviness." [6:25, 17:46, 18:57]

Three features: (1) jaʿalnā (We PLACED) — explicit divine agency; (2) purpose clause "an yafqahūhu" — lest they faqiha (deeply comprehend), connecting to the cognition investigation's finding that fiqh is 75% negated in the Quran; the sheath specifically blocks deep comprehension; (3) the deafness-in-ears pairing mirrors xtm's triple-disablement.

At 41:5 the unbelievers claim the kinna themselves — "qulūbunā fī akinna mimmā tadʿūnā ilayhi" (our hearts are in sheaths from what you call us to). Same excuse-structure as glf, but applied to knn. Unlike glf, the Quran does not refute with a "bal" — the self-claim may be accurate, but the text makes clear they are responsible for their state.

Comparison with xtm: both knn and xtm involve divine placement, block fiqh, co-occur with deafness-in-ears. The imagistic difference: xtm is a completed seal (closed-off), knn is an enveloping sheath (something around, like a scabbard). Xtm = total closure; knn = barrier-wrapping. Both require divine initiative to reverse.

## 9. The six-fold heart-disablement catalog

| Root | Form | Occ | Mode | Agent | Prior Cause | Addressee |
|------|------|-----|------|-------|-------------|-----------|
| qsw | qasā / qāsiya / qaswah | 7 | Stone-hard | Human (consequence) / God (5:13) | Covenant-breaking, non-humbling, time-elapsed | Israelites, generic |
| xtm | khatama ʿalā | 5 (heart) | Sealed closed | God | Taking desire as deity, refusing message | Kāfirūn |
| qfl | aqfāl | 1 | Locked | Unstated (question form) | Refusal to contemplate | Generic audience |
| mrD | maraḍ fī qulūb | 13 | Disease (graduated) | God (increases it) | Deception/wavering | Munāfiqūn |
| glf | ghulf | 2 | Wrapped (claimed) | Human claim (Quran refutes) | Disbelief, covenant-breaking | Israelites |
| knn | akinna | 4 (heart) | Sheathed | God (places) | Turning away from signs | Generic rejecters |

The six are NOT interchangeable:
- qsw and mrD are graduated: hardening worsens, disease increases
- xtm and knn are completed placements by God: once sealed/sheathed, the person "will never be guided, ever" (18:57)
- qfl is an interrogative image, not a declarative state
- glf is a human counter-claim that the Quran refuses to validate

## 10. Divine agency in heart-failure (mirror of success-as-given)

All six formulas involve God at some point — the question is at what stage. The pattern:

1. Human ACTS (covenant-breaking, taking desire as god, turning away from signs, letting time pass)
2. God CONFIRMS the state (seals, sheathes, increases disease, hardens)

The success investigation found God opens/expands the heart to those who orient toward it (shraH al-sadr). The failure catalog inverts this: God closes/seals the heart of those who have already closed themselves. Not predestination — the prior human act is always given first. Once God acts, the closure is total ("they will never be guided, ever" — 18:57). The exception is mrD: disease is graduated and can be increased, but the munāfiqūn are still addressed and called to reform, suggesting the diseased heart retains permeability that the sealed (xtm) or sheathed (knn) heart lacks.

---

## 11. Negative space

The Quran never describes a mechanism for un-sealing or un-sheathing a heart. Heart-opening formulas (shraH, fataḥa) describe God's act toward oriented hearts — there is no procedure the sealed person can perform to reverse their state. The silence on reversal is load-bearing: these are terminal descriptions. There is also no seventh mode of heart-failure named anywhere in the corpus. The six are the complete set.

## 12. Verses for direct study

1. **2:7** — the foundational triple-disablement: khatama ʿalā qulūbihim wa ʿalā samʿihim wa ʿalā abṣārihim ghishāwa
2. **2:10** — heart-disease doubled: "fī qulūbihim maraḍ fa-zādahum Allāhu maraḍā"
3. **18:57** — the most absolute closure statement: sheathed hearts + deafened ears + "they will never be guided, ever"
4. **47:24** — the binary challenge: "do they not contemplate the Quran or are there locks upon their hearts?"
5. **57:16** — time as hardening agent: long time elapsed → hearts hardened → many are fāsiqūn

## 13. Rabbit holes

- **TbʿA (طبع) — ṭabaʿa ʿalā qulūbihim**: A seventh heart-closure root not in this cluster but appearing at 4:155 and 9:87, 9:93, 16:108, 30:59. "Ṭabaʿa" means imprinted/stamped. Compare with xtm (sealed): is ṭabaʿa the external seal and xtm the closure-act, or are these near-synonyms? The 4:155 pairing (Israelites claim glf → God ṭabaʿa them) is a site for this comparison.

- **The 2:6-12 typology**: Al-Baqara's opening section sketches all three human types (muttaqūn / kāfirūn / munāfiqūn) in 12 ayahs — the most compressed typological map in the Quran. A narrative-level reading of this sequence as the Quran's own heart-classification system would be valuable.

- **qfl as dbr-gate**: The 47:24 hapax names tadabbur (Quran-pondering) as the activity that locked hearts prevent. Since dbr is the cognition investigation's "God-human mirror locus" verb, this raises whether ALL six heart-failures block tadabbur specifically, or whether qfl is uniquely about Quran-engagement.

- **knn's positive uses**: The same root describes protected pearls in paradise (37:49, 52:24, 56:23) and the Preserved Tablet (56:78). "Concealed for preservation" in positive contexts; "concealed from reception" in negative ones. The same image-logic applies oppositely — worth mapping fully.

- **The Israelite cluster**: qsw (5:13), glf (2:88, 4:155), and xtm/ṭabaʿa (4:155) concentrate in Israelite discourse. The Israelites are the Quran's primary case study for a community that received revelation and still failed — they model the full heart-failure sequence across multiple roots.
