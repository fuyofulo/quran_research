# Sin / Wrong-doing / Corruption — Six-Root Cluster Analysis

## 1. Headline finding

The Quran possesses a precisely differentiated wrong-doing vocabulary of six roots, each occupying a distinct semantic register. The decisive theological move is **Zlm's self-harm framing**: sin is constitutively "wronging oneself" (ẓalamū anfusahum — 15 confirmed instances), not wronging God. God is explicitly absolved (10:44: "Allah does not wrong people; it is people who wrong themselves"). This makes the locus of moral damage the sinner's own soul, not a divine ledger — a framing that cross-connects directly to the success investigation's ammāra bi-l-sūʾ theology (12:53). The six roots are not synonyms; they map onto distinct domains from personal record (dhanb) to cosmic disorder (fasād fī al-arḍ).

## 3. Zlm — Etymology and Three Sub-domains

Etymologically, ẓulm carries the sense of **putting something in the wrong place** — a spatial-moral metaphor. The lexical twin is ẓulumāt (darknesses, 23 occurrences), confirming that the root moves between literal darkness and moral displacement. The word is maximally distributed: 59 surahs, 290 ayahs — the broadest of all six roots. The ZaAlim (active participle "the wrong-doer") alone accounts for 129 occurrences, making the AGENT form dominant over the abstract noun.

Three functional sub-domains emerge from corpus data:

**Sub-domain 1 — Ẓulm of others (oppression, political register).** Pharaoh's ẓulm, the ẓulm of nations against prophets, the ẓulm of rulers. The root clusters heavily with qawm (people/community, 57 ayahs together), rabb (lord/master, 56 ayahs), and ʿadhāb (punishment, 29 ayahs). The political-oppression sense saturates the narrative passages (Hud, Yunus, al-Aʿrāf lead in density).

**Sub-domain 2 — Ẓulm of self (ẓalamū anfusahum — wronging oneself).** This is the most distinctive theological formula. 15 confirmed active-self-harm instances: wronging one's own soul is how idolatry (2:54 — calf-worship), disobedience (7:23 — Adam and Eve), refusing migration (4:97), and general sin (3:135) are consistently described. See §4.

**Sub-domain 3 — Shirk as ẓulm-ʿaẓīm (the greatest wrong).** Luqmān's warning at 31:13 names associating partners with God as "truly ẓulm ʿaẓīm." This is the apex of the root: the superlative wrong is mis-placing divine sovereignty.

## 4. The Ẓālimū Anfusihim Theology: Sin as Self-Harm

The formula ẓalamū anfusahum / ẓālimī anfusihim (they wronged themselves / wrong-doers of their souls) occurs 15 times with active force [2:54, 2:231, 3:117, 3:135, 4:64, 4:97, 4:110, 7:23, 11:101, 14:45, 16:28, 27:44, 28:16, 34:19, 65:1]. Four representative patterns:

> **[7:23]** قَالَا رَبَّنَا ظَلَمۡنَآ أَنفُسَنَا وَإِن لَّمۡ تَغۡفِرۡ لَنَا وَتَرۡحَمۡنَا لَنَكُونَنَّ مِنَ ٱلۡخَٰسِرِينَ
> "They said: Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves, and if You do not forgive us and have mercy upon us, we will surely be among the losers." (Adam and Eve's confession — *Saheeh*)

> **[10:44]** إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يَظۡلِمُ ٱلنَّاسَ شَيۡـًٔا وَلَٰكِنَّ ٱلنَّاسَ أَنفُسَهُمۡ يَظۡلِمُونَ
> "Indeed, Allah does not wrong the people at all, but it is the people who are wronging themselves." (*Saheeh*)

> **[9:70]** فَمَا كَانَ ٱللَّهُ لِيَظۡلِمَهُمۡ وَلَٰكِن كَانُوٓاْ أَنفُسَهُمۡ يَظۡلِمُونَ
> "Allah would never have wronged them, but they were wronging themselves." (destroyed nations — *Saheeh*)

> **[11:101]** وَمَا ظَلَمۡنَٰهُمۡ وَلَٰكِن ظَلَمُوٓاْ أَنفُسَهُمۡ
> "We did not wrong them, rather they wronged themselves." (*Saheeh* on destroyed peoples)

The pattern is structurally consistent: **God as explicit non-actor** + **the sinner as agent of self-harm**. This theology meshes exactly with the ammāra bi-l-sūʾ (soul commanding to evil, 12:53) from the success investigation: the soul is the locus of both the commanding-impulse and the resultant damage. Sin harms the wrongdoer ontologically, not God relationally. This also explains why God's forgiveness heals the sinner's own condition — it undoes the self-inflicted displacement — rather than satisfying an offended party.

The refrain carries across contexts: individual sin (4:110), idolatry (2:54), disobedience (7:23), injustice (16:28). The scope is total, not a special-case formula.

## 5. Dhanb — The Trailing-Record Aspect

*nb (ذنب) is the most lexically constrained of the six: 100% nominal, 2 lemmas, 39 occurrences. No verbal forms exist — one cannot "dhanaba" as a verb in the Quran. The noun records the sin but cannot describe its commission.

Etymologically dhanb = tail (as in the tail of an animal). **Sin as what trails behind you** — the accumulated record, not the act itself. This aspect is confirmed by the corpus:

- "bi-dhunūbihim" (because of their sins) appears repeatedly to explain divine punishment falling on destroyed nations [3:11, 6:6, 7:100, 8:52, 8:54, 17:17, 28:78, etc.] — 11+ instances.
- **Ghufr al-dhunūb** (forgiveness of sins) is the dominant pairing: dhanb's top co-root is gfr (غفر, forgive) at 19 ayahs together — equal to Alh (Allah). This is unique among the six roots; no other sin-root has forgiveness as its primary structural partner.
- 40:3 names God: "ghāfir al-dhanb wa-qābil al-tawb" — forgiver of sin and acceptor of repentance.
- 39:53: "Indeed, Allah forgives all sins (al-dhunūba jamīʿan)."

Dhanb is thus the **record-vocabulary**: it names the trail left behind, not the act of doing wrong. Its primary speech-context is supplication (seeking forgiveness) and divine description (God as forgiver). This contrasts with Zlm (which names the agent and the act) and ithm (which names the moral quality).

The two dhanūb occurrences at 51:59 are the word's only sense of "portion/share" rather than sin, suggesting the tail-metaphor bleeds into the share-due-to-you meaning. Worth flagging.

## 6. Ithm and the ʿUdwān Pairing

Avm (إثم, ithm) is heavily Medinan (73% Medinan) — the inverse of most sin-vocabulary. This is diagnostic: ithm enters discourse when the community needs to define social boundaries, not just individual repentance.

**Lemma gradient:** The root builds an intensity scale — ithm (moral-fault, 35 occ), āthim (sinner, 3 occ), athīm (intensely sinful, 7 occ — always ADJ). The superlative athīm appears exclusively as a characterization of individuals: "sinful" as a character attribute rather than a situational description.

**The ithm + ʿudwān pairing** appears 5 times [2:85, 5:2, 5:62, 58:8, 58:9]. This is a formulaic pair, not accidental co-occurrence:

> **[5:62]** وَتَرَىٰ كَثِيرًا مِّنۡهُمۡ يُسَٰرِعُونَ فِي ٱلۡإِثۡمِ وَٱلۡعُدۡوَٰنِ
> "And you see many of them hastening into sin (ithm) and aggression (ʿudwān)." (*Saheeh*)

The pairing reveals a two-axis model: **ithm = the moral-fault dimension** (internal wrong, violation of what is lawful); **ʿudwān = the boundary-crossing-against-others dimension** (aggression, transgressing limits set for the community). They pair as two faces of the same act: the act has an inward moral quality (ithm) and an outward social dimension (ʿudwān). This is precisely the Medinan framing — community law and social transgression come into view together.

Avm's top co-root is Edw (عدو, enemy/transgress) at 9 ayahs — data-confirming the ithm+ʿudwān structural bond.

## 7. Fsq — Rebellion Within the System

Fsq = to burst out, break out from a container. The fāsiq has **broken out of** the obedience-structure — not denied it entirely (that would be kufr) but refused to stay within it. This is a distinction the Quran explicitly draws:

> **[32:18]** أَفَمَن كَانَ مُؤۡمِنًا كَمَن كَانَ فَاسِقًاۚ لَّا يَسۡتَوُۥنَ
> "Is the one who is a believer equal to the one who is rebellious? They are not equal." (*Khattab*)

The binary here is muʾmin vs. fāsiq, not muʾmin vs. kāfir. The fāsiq is the insider-who-has-broken-out, still within the conceptual framework but operationally defiant.

The paradigm case is Iblīs at [18:50]: **fa-fasaqa ʿan amri rabbihī** — "he broke out from the command of his Lord." Iblīs is not called a kāfir here; he breaks out of the command-structure. The verb form is fa-fasaqa (then he broke out) — a punctual event, not a characterization.

Fsq's distribution confirms the social-institutional register: top co-roots are qawm (community/people, 21 ayahs) and Amn (believe, 13 ayahs). The fāsiq is defined relationally, against the community and the belief-system. Also notable: kvr (many/numerous, 11 ayahs) — the fāsiq is often quantified as "most of them," a sociological observation not made for ithm or dhanb.

Meccan share is only 37% — fsq is substantially Medinan, entering discourse once the believing community exists and its internal defectors need naming.

## 8. Fsd — Cosmic/Social Corruption

Fsd is structurally unlike the other five: it is an ACTIVE/CAUSATIVE frame. The dominant lemma is mufsid (مُفْسِد, corrupter/agent of corruption, 21 occ) — a participle naming the AGENT who causes corruption, not the person who is corrupted. The second lemma, afsada (أَفْسَدُ, 15 occ verbal), confirms the causative thrust: one does not merely "sin" (fsd is not self-regarding) but CORRUPTS a system.

The co-root data is decisive: fsd's top partner is ArD (ارض, earth/land) at 31 ayahs — **no other sin-root has earth/land as its primary structural co-root**. The fasād fī al-arḍ (corruption on earth) formula pervades [2:11, 2:205, 5:32, 5:33, 7:56, 7:85, 28:77] and more. The "earth" locates corruption cosmically and politically.

> **[2:11-12]** When it is said to them, "Do not cause corruption on the earth," they say, "We are but reformers." Unquestionably, it is they who are the corrupters, but they perceive not.

> **[7:56]** And do not spread corruption in the land after it has been set in order. (Khattab)

The fsd-SlH (reform/righteousness) pairing at 9 ayahs is significant: corruption (fsd) is explicitly contrasted with iṣlāḥ (reform/setting-right). The mufsidūn are agents of DISORDER against the ordered creation. This is cosmic vocabulary, not personal-sin vocabulary. [28:77]: "Allah does not love the mufsidūn."

Fsd is 66% Meccan — the cosmic disorder frame enters early, in prophetic preaching, before the Medinan social-law apparatus.

## 9. SwA — Generic Evil

SwA is the most semantically dispersed of the six: 12 lemmas, 167 occurrences, 45 surahs. It is the Quran's **generic evil vocabulary** — the abstract sūʾ (evil, 50 occ), the plural sayyiʾāt (evil deeds, 36 occ), the verbal sāʾa (to be evil/harm, 30 occ), the singular sayyiʾa (an evil deed, 22 occ).

The sayyiʾāt/ḥasanāt pairing is the moral economy frame:

> **[6:160]** Whoever comes with a good deed (ḥasana) will have ten times the like thereof; and whoever comes with an evil deed (sayyiʾa) will not be recompensed except the like thereof. (*Saheeh*)

> **[41:34]** Not equal are the good deed (ḥasana) and the bad (sayyiʾa). Repel evil by that which is better.

The sayyiʾāt are QUANTIFIABLE moral units that enter the cosmic accounting (ḥasanāt vs sayyiʾāt). This is action-accounting vocabulary, not sin-characterization vocabulary. SwA describes what was done, not who the doer is — contrasting with fāsiq (who the person is) or mufsid (what role the person plays).

The ammāra bi-l-sūʾ formula at 12:53 crosses into the success investigation: "The soul is a persistent enjoiner of evil (sūʾ)." Here sūʾ names the generic evil that the commanding-soul impels, while the dhanb and ithm vocabulary would name specific formal categories. SwA underlies all the specific categories as their common substrate.

Top co-root: Eml (عمل, to do/act, 42 ayahs) — confirming the ACTION register of swA. Evil here is constitutively linked to doing, not to being.

## 10. The Six-Fold Gradient

| Root | Primary Domain | Key Form | First Concern | Medinan % |
|------|---------------|----------|--------------|-----------|
| Zlm (ظلم) | Wrong-placement: self, others, shirk | ẓālimūn | Who is the agent of wrong | 33% |
| dhanb (ذنب) | Personal trailing record | dhunūb | The forgiveness-seeking relation | 44% |
| ithm (اثم) | Moral-fault quality (+ boundary transgression) | ithm + ʿudwān | Social-legal boundary marking | 73% |
| fsq (فسق) | Inside-system rebellion | fāsiq | Community defection | 63% |
| fsd (فسد) | Social/cosmic corruption | mufsidūn | Structural disorder in creation | 34% |
| swA (سوا) | Generic evil-action substrate | sayyiʾāt | Moral accounting of deeds | 34% |

The gradient runs from **most specific/personal** (dhanb — your record) through **moral-fault** (ithm), **relational-defection** (fsq), **structural-corruption** (fsd), to **broadest** (Zlm — covers all three sub-domains) and **most generic** (swA — the underlying evil-substrate for any category).

The Medinan percentage reveals a clear pattern: ithm (73%) and fsq (63%) are Medinan community-governance vocabulary. Zlm, fsd, swA remain substantially Meccan — the broadest categories (wrong, corruption, evil-in-general) appear in early preaching; the specific social categories (moral-fault, community-defection) arrive once a governed community needs naming.

## 11. Negative Space

The Quran has no dedicated root for "guilt" as a subjective psychological experience — dhunūb names the record, ithm names the moral quality, but there is no root that maps onto the internal feeling-of-guilt distinct from the objective fact of wrongdoing. Similarly, ẓulm and fasād never appear in requests for purely private forgiveness — they are always socially or cosmically framed. The introspective-guilt register that dominates Western theological discourse on sin is conspicuously absent from this vocabulary.

## 12. Verses for Direct Study

1. **[10:44]** — The canonical statement of the ẓulm-al-nafs theology: God does not wrong people; they wrong themselves. The entire cluster's theological logic depends on this verse.

2. **[31:13]** — Luqmān's wisdom: shirk as ẓulm ʿaẓīm (greatest wrong). Connects sub-domain 3 of Zlm to the theological apex of the root.

3. **[12:53]** — The ammāra bi-l-sūʾ verse: "The soul is a persistent enjoiner of evil." The self-as-source-of-evil framing that bridges the success and failure investigations.

4. **[5:32-33]** — The fasād fī al-arḍ legislation: killing one soul = killing all humanity; "striving to cause fasād in the land" receives the harshest legal penalties. The cosmic weight of fsd.

5. **[3:135]** — The recovery formula: those who do faḥisha (immorality) OR wrong themselves (ẓalamū anfusahum), remember God, seek forgiveness of their dhunūb. All three registers (Zlm, dhanb, Avm context) appear in a single verse.

## 13. Rabbit Holes

- **Ẓallām negation formula.** The word Ẓallām (intensely wronging) appears only 5 times — always negated of God ("Allah is not Ẓallām to His servants"). Why use the intensive form only in negation? Is this a rhetorical move to deny even the possibility of divine wrong-doing at the extreme?

- **Dhanūb (51:59) ambiguity.** The two occurrences of *anūb in 51:59 may not be "sin" at all but the old Arabic word for "bucket/share." If so, dhanb and dhanūb are two separate lexemes sharing Buckwalter encoding. Worth checking classical lexicography.

- **Fsd + iṣlāḥ pairing.** The fsd-SlH (9 ayahs together) suggests the Quran operates with a fasād/iṣlāḥ binary: corruption vs. reform. This binary is explicit in 2:11-12 (the hypocrites call themselves muṣliḥūn while the text calls them mufsidūn). A dedicated study of the iṣlāḥ register against the fsd frame would surface the Quran's theory of social restoration.

- **Fsq and kufr boundary.** The Quran marks fāsiq (inside-broken-out) vs. kāfir (outside/denier) as distinct, but several passages apply fsq to groups who have disbelieved [2:99]. Is there a trajectory from fsq to kufr? Do the roots interact as stages?

- **The self-harm cluster's limits.** ẓalamū anfusahum appears 15 times, but the construction always involves the agent explicitly naming themselves as victim. Are there contexts where the self-harming dynamic is implicit (no anfus pronoun) but still operative? The fsq and fsd registers never invoke the self-harm frame — structural/social corruption is always harm to OTHERS/EARTH. Why does the self-harm frame attach only to Zlm and swA?
