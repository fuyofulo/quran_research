# Narrative: Maryam's mobility set — the female teleportation locus of the Quran

## 1. Headline finding

Maryam (Mary) is the most miracle-dense single human in the Quran. No other named person — not Abraham, not Moses, not Muhammad — accumulates this many distinct supernatural events around their body and their immediate space within a single passage cluster. Five surrounding miracles converge on her:

1. Provisions appearing inside her sealed prayer-chamber (3:37).
2. Angelic descent into a curtained interior — the Spirit appearing as a perfect human (19:17).
3. Self-withdrawal to a "far place" without described transit (19:22).
4. Out-of-season fresh dates dropping from a dry palm-trunk (19:25).
5. Newborn-grade infant speaking with prophet-grade discourse (19:30-33).

Plus the seal-formula at 21:91 and 66:12: God blew of His spirit INTO her — `nafakhnā fīhā min rūḥinā`. The same verb (`n-f-x`, blow) governs the Trumpet that raises the dead at the eschatological turning. Maryam's body is a node where blowing-in and life-giving are the same word. She is the Quran's concentrated FEMALE locus of supernatural mobility, food-appearance, vertical-descent, and time-displaced consciousness — and the Quran reserves the prophet-suite of vocabulary for males while assigning Maryam more miracles than any single male prophet.

The thesis: Maryam is the implicit female prophet-equivalent. The Quran does not call her `nabī` or `rasūl`, but the miracle-density around her body is the prophet-suite delivered to a non-prophet.

## 2. The Quran's Maryam corpus (passage map)

Maryam appears in the Quran almost entirely within four anchor-points:

- **3:35-50** (Medinan, Surah Āl ʿImrān) — birth-dedication, miḥrāb provisions, angel-announcement, infant-cradle prophecy. This is the THEOLOGICAL register: the angels speak in declarative formulas, Maryam questions, the conception is decreed.
- **19:16-37** (Meccan, Surah Maryam) — withdrawal, angelic visitation behind a curtain, conception, second withdrawal, palm-tree birth, return, infant defense. This is the NARRATIVE register: the events unfold in sequence with bodily detail (childbirth pangs, palm-trunk, dates, voice from beneath, gesture toward the cradle).
- **21:91** (Meccan, Surah al-Anbiyāʾ) — single-verse seal: `wa-l-latī aḥṣanat farjahā fa-nafakhnā fīhā min rūḥinā wa-jaʿalnāhā wa-bnahā āyatan li-l-ʿālamīn`. She and her son are made A SIGN.
- **66:12** (Medinan, Surah al-Taḥrīm) — single-verse seal in a list of exemplary women: `wa Maryama bnata ʿImrān al-latī aḥṣanat farjahā fa-nafakhnā **fīhi** min rūḥinā`. Note the grammatical shift from `fīhā` (into HER, 21:91) to `fīhi` (into IT — the chastity/garment, 66:12).

A fifth verse, **23:50**, completes the post-birth picture: `wa-jaʿalnā bna Maryama wa-ummahu āyatan wa-āwaynāhumā ilā rabwatin dhāti qarārin wa-maʿīn` — God SHELTERED them on a height with a stable resting-place and a flowing spring. This pulls Maryam into the Awy root (the Cave Sleepers' root). After her self-withdrawal (`intabadhat`, 19:22), God himself shelters her (`āwaynāhumā`, 23:50). The mobility verbs trade hands: she withdraws, God shelters.

What is conspicuously absent from this corpus: any later episode in Maryam's life. No marriage, no adulthood, no death. Her narrative starts at her own infancy (the dedication while still in her mother's womb, 3:35) and ends when the infant Jesus speaks (19:33) and the high-ground refuge is mentioned (23:50). The Quran's Maryam is a TEMPORALLY COMPRESSED figure — a girl-into-mother arc with no after-image. Idrīs is raised (19:57), Jesus is raised (3:55, 4:158), but Maryam simply disappears from the frame.

## 3. The miḥrāb provisions (3:37)

The verse: `fa-taqabbalahā rabbuhā bi-qabūlin ḥasanin wa-anbatahā nabātan ḥasanan wa-kaffalahā Zakariyyā · kullamā dakhala ʿalayhā Zakariyyā al-miḥrāba wajada ʿindahā rizqan · qāla yā Maryamu annā laki hādhā · qālat huwa min ʿindi llāh · inna llāha yarzuqu man yashāʾu bi-ghayri ḥisāb`.

The mechanism: Zachariah is her guardian. He enters the `miḥrāb` (prayer chamber/sanctuary alcove). Each time he enters (`kullamā dakhala` — habitual repetition), he finds provisions WITH HER (`ʿindahā`). The Arabic does not say she has gone out, has been brought food, or has prepared food. The provisions simply ARE THERE. Zachariah asks `annā laki hādhā` — "from where is this for you?" Maryam: `huwa min ʿindi llāh` — "it is from with God." She uses the same locative preposition (`ʿinda`) for the food's divine origin that he used for its location with her: `ʿindahā` (with her) ← `ʿindi llāh` (with God). The food has TRANSITED from `ʿinda llāh` to `ʿindahā` without passing through any described intermediate.

Three observations:

(a) **The miḥrāb as sealed space.** A miḥrāb in temple-architecture is an elevated alcove — a space-of-prayer that is partly enclosed. The verb `dakhala ʿalayhā al-miḥrāba` (entered upon her in the miḥrāb) implies a threshold Zachariah must cross. Maryam is INSIDE; he comes IN. The food appears INSIDE without an external delivery agent. This is the same structural type as the angelic visitation that follows in 3:42 (and explicitly in 19:17 behind the `ḥijāb`): a thing arrives in a sealed female interior without breaking the seal. Pickthall makes this explicit on 19:16 (translating `makānan sharqiyyan` as "a chamber looking East" — see §12), reading the place as architectural; the other three translators keep it as bare "place." Pickthall's intuition tracks the miḥrāb's interior valence.

(b) **`bi-ghayri ḥisāb` — without account.** The provisions ESCAPE accountability. `ḥisāb` is the Quran's word for the eschatological reckoning, the calculation of deeds, the cycle-line bridge between countables and uncountables. Maryam's provisions are off-the-books. They are NOT zero-sum food taken from another mouth; they are EXTRA in the closed system. Compare with 22:2 (`taḍaʿu kullu ḥāmla ḥamlahā`) — pregnancy and apocalypse share `ḥaml`. Maryam's provisions and divine reckoning share `ḥisāb` (here negated). Both miracles deliberately suspend the conservation-laws the Quran usually presupposes.

(c) **A girl receives the food-miracle.** This is striking. Outside narrative-history (Manna for the Israelites, Jesus's heavenly table at 5:114), the Quran has very few clear miraculous-provision events. Maryam — a young girl in religious seclusion — is granted one. The Quran does not justify the choice. The food-miracle is not earned by an act we are shown; it is granted to her in `kullamā` repetition (every time, repeatedly). She is the only individual in the Quran for whom miraculous provision is recurrent and habitual, not one-off.

Translation divergence on 3:37 is HIGH (combined=0.704, jaccard=0.293, outlier=Arberry). All four translators agree that Zachariah finds provisions; they diverge on `bi-ghayri ḥisāb`: Saheeh "without account," Pickthall "without stint," Khattab "without limit," Arberry "without reckoning." Arberry's `reckoning` is closest to the eschatological-`ḥisāb` resonance; Pickthall's `stint` (limit-of-quantity) is the most this-worldly. The Arabic carries both senses; the divergence is real.

## 4. The angelic visitation (19:17-21, 3:42-47)

Two presentations of one event:

**3:42-47 (Medinan).** The angels speak in formulaic declaratives: `inna llāha ṣṭafāki wa-ṭahharaki wa-ṣṭafāki ʿalā nisāʾi l-ʿālamīn` — God has CHOSEN you, PURIFIED you, CHOSEN you above all women. The choosing is twice (`ṣṭafā` repeats). Then 3:45: `inna llāha yubashshiruki bi-kalimatin minhu smuhu l-Masīḥu ʿĪsā bnu Maryam` — Allah gives you good news of a WORD from Him whose name is the Messiah. The angel's speech is announcement-oriented; no description of the angel's body, no fear-response from Maryam beyond the question (3:47) `annā yakūnu lī waladun wa-lam yamsasnī basharun` — how can I have a child when no man has touched me?

**19:17 (Meccan).** The same event told with bodily detail: `fa-ttakhadhat min dūnihim ḥijāban fa-arsalnā ilayhā rūḥanā fa-tamaththala lahā basharan sawiyyan`. She took, apart from them, a curtain. WE SENT to her our Spirit. He TOOK A FORM before her of a perfect human. Maryam responds with fear (19:18 `innī aʿūdhu bi-l-Raḥmāni minka in kunta taqiyyan` — "I take refuge in the Most Merciful from you, if you are God-fearing"), and the angel reassures her.

Four points stack here:

(a) **fa-arsalnā ilayhā rūḥanā — We SENT to her our Spirit.** The verb `arsala` (rsl) is the standard verb of prophetic-mission, of God-to-prophet revelation-dispatch. Here it sends a Spirit (`rūḥ`) to a non-prophet woman. The vector is vertical (heaven-to-earth) intersecting horizontal (Maryam's withdrawn-eastern-place). The Quran is comfortable using the prophet-dispatch verb for a delivery TO Maryam.

(b) **fa-tamaththala lahā basharan sawiyyan — he took a form before her of a perfect human.** The verb `tamaththala` (root mvl) is a form-V "take-on-a-likeness." This is the visibility-state-change verb. The Spirit's native state is non-visible/non-anthropomorphic; for Maryam he becomes `bashar sawī` — a perfect (`sawī`, well-formed) human male. This is APPEAR-AS vocabulary. Cross-reference concept-Zhr (manifestation) for the Quran's broader visibility-state lexicon. The angel is not reduced; he is RE-PRESENTED. The translation `well-proportioned man` (Saheeh) and `perfectly formed` (Khattab) preserve the bodily-perfection register; Pickthall's `the likeness of a perfect man` keeps the form-shifting; Arberry's `presented himself to her a man without fault` reads `sawiyyan` morally rather than physically.

(c) **ḥijāb — the curtain.** Maryam withdraws BEHIND a curtain. The curtain is the seclusion boundary. Despite the curtain, the angel APPEARS in her presence. This is the mid-miracle: the curtain blocks human vision but NOT angelic descent. The miḥrāb-pattern (3:37) and the ḥijāb-pattern (19:17) are identical — a female interior, a sealed space, a divine appearance through unbroken seal. Maryam's body and Maryam's room follow the same architecture: closed to human entry, open to divine arrival.

(d) **The two presentations.** Why does the Quran narrate this twice? Hypothesis: the Medinan version (3:42-47) is the DOCTRINAL anchor — Maryam is chosen, Jesus is the Word, the conception is decreed. The Meccan version (19:17-21) is the NARRATIVE elaboration — with curtain, Spirit-form, fear-response, reassurance. The two versions are not redundant; they perform different rhetorical work. The Meccan-Medinan pair on this event is one of the cleanest cases of dual-presentation in the Quran.

Translation divergence on 19:17 is HIGH (combined=0.663). 19:18 is HIGHER (0.701, outlier Arberry — Arberry truncates the verse, splitting `in kunta taqiyyan` into the next ayah). The angelic-visitation passage is among the more lexically-contested in surah 19 — translators struggle with `tamaththala` (representational verb, not standard prose) and the curtain-set-up.

## 5. The withdrawal (19:22) and birth

19:22: `fa-ḥamalathu fa-ntabadhat bihi makānan qaṣiyyan`. Three verbs in one short ayah: she conceived him, she withdrew with him, [implied] to a far place.

This is the densest mobility-verse in the Maryam corpus and one of the densest in the entire Quran. Three observations:

(a) **fa-ḥamalathu — she carried him.** The Hml root is the Quran's all-purpose carrying-verb, covering pregnancy, the Ark, the Throne, beasts of burden, the Trust laid on humanity (33:72). Here it specifically denotes conception. 19:22's `ḥamalathu` is the moment of conception; 19:27's `taḥmiluhu` is the physical carrying of the infant on her return. Same verb, two phases. (See concept-Hml.md §1.)

(b) **fa-ntabadhat — she withdrew (reflexive).** The form-VIII reflexive of `nb*` (cast/throw). The form-VIII reflexive of this root appears in the entire Quran ONLY for Maryam, and only twice — both in surah 19 (16 and 22). Maryam is the ONLY person in the Quran who self-ejects. The same root that hurls Pharaoh into the sea (28:40, 51:40) and casts the slanderer into the Crusher (104:4) is, in its reflexive form, reserved for Maryam alone. Her withdrawal is structurally a self-throw — a violent self-removal from the social field. (See concept-nb*.md §1.)

(c) **makānan qaṣiyyan — a far place.** The qṣy/qṣw root means far, distant, remote. It is the same root as `al-masjid al-aqṣā` in 17:1 — Muhammad's Night Journey destination. Both Maryam (19:22) and Muhammad (17:1) reach a "FAR place" without described transit; both events are flagged with the qṣy lexeme. The Quran's qṣy-marker is a shared label for teleportation-class destinations. Maryam's far place is unnamed; Muhammad's is named (the Furthest Mosque); both are defined by their REMOTENESS rather than their identity. Tradition reads Maryam's far place as Bethlehem, but the Quran is silent.

Translation divergence on 19:22 is LOW (combined=0.272, jaccard=0.819, outlier=Pickthall — Pickthall says "far place," the others say "remote" or "distant"). All four translators agree on `withdrew with him` for the verb. The verse is one of the most resolved Hml-verses in the corpus (see concept-Hml.md §translation divergence). The 19:16 first withdrawal verse, by contrast, is much more divergent (combined=0.556, outlier Pickthall on `chamber looking East`).

The qualifier shift between 19:16 and 19:22 — `sharqiyyan` (eastern) → `qaṣiyyan` (far) — is intensification. Before the conception, Maryam withdraws to an EASTWARD place (an oriented space, perhaps the temple's eastern alcove, perhaps further east). After the conception, she withdraws to a FAR place (a non-oriented, simply-distant space). The second withdrawal is a magnification of the first. The narrative skips the journey: 19:21 ends with the angel's announcement; 19:22 announces conception-and-withdrawal in three verbs; 19:23 opens with her ALREADY at the trunk of the palm. There is no `sāra` (s-y-r), no `mashā` (walked), no described travel. Maryam withdraws and she IS THERE.

## 6. The palm tree (19:23-26)

19:23: `fa-ajāʾahā l-makhāḍu ilā jidhʿi l-naxlati qālat yā laytanī mittu qabla hādhā wa-kuntu nasyan mansiyyā` — the pangs of childbirth drove her to the trunk of the palm. She said: would that I had died before this and been forgotten utterly.

19:24: `fa-nādāhā min taḥtihā allā taḥzanī qad jaʿala rabbuki taḥtaki sariyyan` — and HE called to her from BENEATH her: do not grieve; your Lord has placed beneath you a stream.

19:25: `wa-huzzī ilayki bi-jidhʿi l-naxlati tusāqiṭ ʿalayki ruṭaban janiyyā` — and shake the trunk of the palm toward you; it will drop fresh ripe dates upon you.

Three points:

(a) **The dry palm yielding fresh dates.** Palms in winter (the traditional reading-context) do not yield. The miracle is OUT-OF-SEASON FRUIT — a temporal-displacement food event. Maryam shakes a TREE; the tree responds. The miracle works through a natural object obeying her command. Compare with: Solomon's wind subdued (21:81), Abraham's birds called (2:260), David's mountains and birds (38:18-19). Maryam's palm is part of the same Quranic typology — natural objects responding to a chosen human's gesture. The palm-trunk miracle joins Maryam to the prophet-typology, again, without the prophet-vocabulary.

(b) **The voice from beneath.** `fa-nādāhā min taḥtihā` — and he/it called to her from beneath her. WHO calls? The Arabic has no overt subject. Tradition splits: (i) Gabriel, calling from below the elevation/cliff/tree; (ii) the infant Jesus speaking immediately from the womb or moments after birth. Both readings are textually defensible. The translators reflect this: Saheeh "But he called her from below her" (ambiguous he); Pickthall "Then (one) cried unto her" (deliberately ambiguous); Khattab "So a voice reassured her" (depersonalizes); Arberry "But the one that was below her called to her" (deictic but unspecified). All four refuse to disambiguate. The Quran's silence here is preserved across translations. (See §12 on this divergence point.) Translation divergence is HIGH (combined=0.687, jaccard=0.321) — the unspecified subject is a major uncertainty axis.

(c) **The palm + the stream (`sariyy`).** The verse pairs DATES from the tree with WATER from beneath. Two food-class miracles in adjacent ayahs: out-of-season dates AND a stream appearing under her. Compare with the miḥrāb's `rizq` (provisions) — three episodes of food-appearance around Maryam's body across her narrative arc:
1. Provisions in the miḥrāb (3:37) — recurring, indoor.
2. Stream beneath the palm (19:24) — single, outdoor.
3. Dates from the dry palm (19:25) — single, outdoor.

Three food-miracles for one woman. The Quran's most concentrated food-miracle locus is Maryam, full stop.

## 7. Infant Jesus speaking (19:29-33)

When Maryam returns to her people carrying the infant (19:27 `taḥmiluhu`), they accuse her: `lā yā Maryamu laqad jiʾti shayʾan fariyyā` — "Mary, you have done a strange thing." She POINTS at the infant (19:29 `fa-ashārat ilayhi`). They protest: `kayfa nukallimu man kāna fī l-mahdi ṣabiyyan` — "how can we speak to one who is in the cradle, a child?"

The infant SPEAKS:
`qāla innī ʿabdu llāhi ātāniya l-kitāba wa-jaʿalanī nabiyyan · wa-jaʿalanī mubārakan ayna mā kuntu wa-awṣānī bi-l-ṣalāti wa-l-zakāti mā dumtu ḥayyan · wa-barran bi-wālidatī wa-lam yajʿalnī jabbāran shaqiyyan · wa-l-salāmu ʿalayya yawma wulidtu wa-yawma amūtu wa-yawma ubʿathu ḥayyan` (19:30-33).

Three points:

(a) **An infant performing prophet-speech.** Jesus does not babble; he produces the standard prophet-formula of self-identification, mission-statement, and peace-blessing. The peace-formula `al-salāmu ʿalayya yawma wulidtu wa-yawma amūtu wa-yawma ubʿathu ḥayyan` (19:33) is structurally identical to the John-the-Baptist formula `wa-salāmun ʿalayhi yawma wulida wa-yawma yamūtu wa-yawma yubʿathu ḥayyan` (19:15) — birth, death, resurrection. The infant Jesus claims the prophet-of-the-three-days formula immediately upon birth. This is TIME-DISPLACED COGNITION: the cognitive architecture of an adult prophet inhabiting the body of a newborn.

(b) **The defense by speech.** Maryam cannot defend herself — she has VOWED a fast (19:26 `innī nadhartu li-l-Raḥmāni ṣawman fa-lan ukallima l-yawma insiyyan` — "I have vowed to the Most Merciful a fast; I will not speak today to any human"). Her silence is voluntary; the infant's speech is divine compensation. Maryam's silence + Jesus's speech = a single defense-act distributed across two bodies. The infant becomes Maryam's voice. (Compare 3:46 `wa-yukallimu l-nāsa fī l-mahdi wa-kahlan` — he will speak to people in the cradle and in maturity. The cradle-speech is FOREWARNED at 3:46 and FULFILLED at 19:30.)

(c) **Cradle-speech and Cave Sleepers' awakening.** Both involve TIME-DISPLACED CONSCIOUSNESS. The Cave Sleepers wake after 309 years (18:25) and perceive only `yawman aw baʿḍa yawmin` — "a day or part of a day" (18:19). Their conscious time-sense is dilated. Jesus's cradle-speech runs the inverse: an adult-grade discourse compressed into a newborn body. Both pair WITHDRAWAL with TEMPORAL ANOMALY of consciousness. The Cave's withdrawal yields long-sleep + compressed-perception; Maryam's withdrawal yields conception + compressed-prophethood-into-infant. Same template, different phase of life. (See §9.)

Translation divergence on 19:29-33 is moderate to high. 19:33's salām-formula is the LOWEST in this passage (combined=0.327) — the formula is fixed, all four translators converge. The variability concentrates in 19:30-32 where the prophet-self-identification is rendered in slightly different registers.

## 8. The seal — 21:91 and 66:12 (the in-blowing nafakha)

Both verses describe Maryam in a single relative-clause formula: SHE WHO GUARDED HER CHASTITY (`aḥṣanat farjahā`), and We breathed of Our spirit INTO her (`fa-nafakhnā fīhā/fīhi min rūḥinā`).

The contrast between the two ayahs is grammatical:
- 21:91: `fa-nafakhnā fīhā min rūḥinā` — "We breathed into HER (feminine pronoun, referring to Maryam herself).
- 66:12: `fa-nafakhnā fīhi min rūḥinā` — "We breathed into IT" (masculine pronoun, referring back to `farjahā` — her chastity/private-part — or to the garment/sealing as antecedent).

Translators handle this divergence-point with insertions:
- Saheeh 21:91: "We blew into her [garment] through Our angel [Gabriel]."
- Saheeh 66:12: "We blew into [her garment] through Our angel."
- Pickthall 21:91: "therefor We breathed into her (something) of Our Spirit."
- Pickthall 66:12: "We breathed therein something of Our Spirit."
- Khattab 21:91: "We breathed into her through Our angel, [Gabriel]."
- Khattab 66:12: "We breathed into her [womb] through Our angel [Gabriel]."
- Arberry: preserves the bare "into her" / "into her" without insertion in both.

Three of four English translations INSERT either "garment" (Saheeh) or "angel/Gabriel" (Saheeh, Khattab) to dispose the in-blowing toward an indirect mechanism. This is interpretive — the Arabic does not say "into her garment" or "through an angel"; it says baldly "We breathed into her of Our spirit." Arberry alone preserves the directness. The interpretive layer worries about the literal-physical reading; the Arabic does not.

Two cross-cutting connections:

(a) **n-f-x as the resurrection verb.** The Trumpet of resurrection at 39:68, 50:20, 78:18 is `nufikha fī l-ṣūr` — "[the trumpet] is BLOWN into." The same root and same verbal grammar as `nafakhnā fīhā min rūḥinā`. The verb that BLOWS Spirit into Maryam (producing life) is the verb that BLOWS the Trumpet (raising the dead). Lifegiving and resurrection-summoning share their core verb. (See research-trumpet.md in the time investigation for the Trumpet vocabulary.)

(b) **n-f-x and Adam.** The same verb appears for the original creation of Adam: `wa-nafakhtu fīhi min rūḥī` (15:29, 38:72) — "and I blew into him of My spirit." Maryam's conception of Jesus uses the SAME verb as the creation of Adam. Jesus is the second Adam, by lexical match, not by inference. 3:59 makes this explicit: `inna mathala ʿĪsā ʿinda llāhi ka-mathali ādama xalaqahu min turābin thumma qāla lahu kun fa-yakūn` — Jesus before God is like Adam: He created him from dust, then said `BE` and he was. The Adam-Jesus parallel is built on a verb (`nafakha`) and a phrase (`kun fa-yakūn`). Maryam stands at one of the two creative anchor-points of human existence in the Quran: the n-f-x of Adam (origin) and the n-f-x of Jesus (re-origin via her). She is structurally placed at a creation-point.

## 9. The Maryam-Cave Sleepers structural parallel

The Cave Sleepers narrative (18:9-26) and the Maryam narrative (19:16-37) follow the same five-phase template:

| Phase | Cave Sleepers | Maryam |
|---|---|---|
| WITHDRAWAL | `iʿtazalūhum` (18:16) — withdrew from people | `intabadhat min ahlihā` (19:16) — withdrew from family |
| ISOLATION | `awā ilā l-kahf` (18:10) — took refuge in cave | `intabadhat bihi makānan qaṣiyyan` (19:22) — withdrew with him to a far place |
| DIVINE INTERVENTION | `fa-ḍarabnā ʿalā ādhānihim` (18:11) — God struck their ears with sleep | `fa-arsalnā ilayhā rūḥanā` (19:17) — God sent His Spirit |
| MIRACLE | 309 years compressed to `a day or part of a day` (18:19, 18:25) | virgin conception + birth + cradle-speech (19:22-33) |
| SOCIAL RE-ENTRY | one Sleeper sent to buy food (18:19) | Maryam returns carrying Jesus (19:27) |

The parallel is structural, not lexical. Cave Sleepers use the Awy + iʿtazala lexicon; Maryam uses the nb* lexicon. But the EVENT-SEQUENCE is identical: withdraw → be isolated → be divinely intervened upon → undergo a non-ordinary causal event → return to society and present the miracle.

23:50 then loops Maryam INTO the Awy lexicon: `āwaynāhumā ilā rabwatin` — "We sheltered them on a height." The form-IV `āwā` (give shelter) is the exact verb the Cave Sleepers are described with at 18:10 (form I `awā` — take refuge). Maryam's narrative crosses BOTH lexicons: she self-withdraws (nb*, surah 19), and she is sheltered (Awy, surah 23). The Cave Sleepers do the first via Awy; Maryam does it via nb*. The two narratives are structural twins with different verbal palettes.

The deeper claim: the Quran's pattern is WITHDRAWAL → MIRACLE → RETURN. Whatever the specific mobility-verb (nb*, Awy, or iʿtazala), the social-clearing must precede the divine intervention. God's most extraordinary acts happen when the witness has already cleared the social field. (See concept-Hml.md cross-cutting #4.)

## 10. Connection to other teleportation phenomena

**The brought-present pattern (HDr).** The eschatological `muḥḍar` (deeds, hosts, and the resurrected brought-present at Judgment) shares a structural feature with the miḥrāb provisions: things appear-in-place without described transit. 19:68 `la-nuḥḍirannahum ḥawla jahannama jiṯiyyan` — "We will surely bring them present around Hell on their knees" — is the divine-passive of teleportation. Maryam's miḥrāb provisions are an inverted version: divine provisions brought-present to a chosen body. (See concept-HDr.md.)

**Solomon's throne (27:38-44).** The Solomon-throne miracle uses Aty/jyA/qrr/Hml — different lexemes than the Maryam miracles but the same architectural type: an object (or a body, in Maryam's case) appearing in a place across a distance with no described physical transit. Solomon: throne brought from Sheba in less than the blink of an eye (27:40). Maryam: provisions brought into a sealed miḥrāb without delivery agent (3:37). Both involve a divine bringer (the man-with-knowledge / God himself) and a destination-of-presence. The vocabulary differs; the phenomenon overlaps.

**Jesus's raising (3:55, 4:158).** Maryam's son is later raised (`rāfiʿuka ilayya`, 3:55) and raised-up (`rafaʿahu llāhu ilayhi`, 4:158). The family arc spans two opposite-direction transits: incoming Spirit (n-f-x INTO Maryam, conception) and outgoing body (Jesus raised UP TO God, ascension). Maryam's body is the ENTRY-POINT; Jesus's raising is the EXIT-POINT. Together they bracket the family-mobility-architecture.

**Abraham's birds (2:260).** Abraham distributes pieces of four birds across mountaintops; calls them; they ASSEMBLE flying back to him. This is distributed-call-then-arrival. Maryam's miḥrāb is the inverse: centralized deposit. Both miracles bend the geometry of arrival, in opposite directions: Abraham draws scattered pieces TO a center; Maryam receives provisions AT a center from invisible elsewhere.

## 11. The female teleportation locus theology

Five distinct miracles converge on Maryam in a single narrative arc. No other single human in the Quran accumulates this density. Even Muhammad — the most narratively-elaborated human — has the Night Journey (17:1) and the Receiving of the Quran as his major direct supernatural events; the rest of his mobility is ordinary-historical. Even Moses — with the staff, the parting of the sea, the manna, the cloud, the rock-spring — has his miracles distributed across decades and across the people of Israel. Maryam's miracles are concentrated on her body and her immediate space, in a single short narrative.

The reading: Maryam's body is a PORTAL. The Spirit enters her (`nafakhnā fīhā`); Jesus emerges through her (`fa-ḥamalathu`); food appears around her (`rizqan`, miḥrāb); a tree responds to her (palm-trunk); a stream appears under her (`sariyyan`); the infant speaks for her. She is the geometric center of an unusual local concentration of miracle-events. The Quran's word for what she has done — `ʿabdu llāhi` is what JESUS calls himself (19:30), but Maryam's status is given via different markers: `ṣiddīqa` (truthful, 5:75), `iṣṭafāki ʿalā nisāʾi l-ʿālamīn` (chosen above all women, 3:42), `mina l-qānitīn` (of the devoutly obedient, 66:12). She is not called `nabī` or `rasūl`. The prophet-vocabulary is held back — but the prophet-suite of supernatural events is fully delivered.

Two readings, both worth holding:

(a) **Implicit female prophet-equivalent.** The Quran preserves the male monopoly on prophet-titles while granting Maryam the supernatural infrastructure usually attached to a major prophet (food-miracle, angelic descent, displacement, infant-witness, divine sheltering). The structural argument: Maryam IS the prophet-equivalent without the title. The Quran does the work of the prophet-suite around her; the title is not granted because the Quran's prophet-typology is gendered. The concept-set is broader than the concept-tag.

(b) **Maryam as the unique vessel.** Maryam is not a prophet-equivalent but a structural singularity — the Quran's only example of a NON-PROPHET who is both (i) the destination of an angelic mission and (ii) the bearer of a Word-of-God-becomes-human. She is unique, not iterable. The Quran's prophet-typology is not gendered-restricted but role-restricted; Maryam's role is not prophethood but vesselship.

The text underdetermines between (a) and (b). The miracle-density is uncontested.

## 12. Translation divergence

Selected key contested points within the Maryam corpus:

**3:37 (combined=0.704, jaccard=0.293, outlier=Arberry).** `bi-ghayri ḥisāb` — Saheeh "without account," Pickthall "without stint," Khattab "without limit," Arberry "without reckoning." Arberry preserves the eschatological-`ḥisāb` resonance; Pickthall obscures it. This is the eschatology↔provision link that most translators dampen.

**19:16 (combined=0.556, outlier=Pickthall).** `makānan sharqiyyan` — Saheeh "a place toward the east," Khattab "a place in the east," Arberry "an eastern place," Pickthall idiosyncratically "a chamber looking East." Pickthall reads architectural interior; the others read bare geography. The Arabic supplies no architectural cue. (Pickthall's reading aligns with the miḥrāb-pattern of 3:37, but the Arabic at 19:16 does not name the miḥrāb.)

**19:18 (combined=0.701, outlier=Arberry).** Arberry truncates `in kunta taqiyyan` ("if you fear God") onto the next ayah, splitting Maryam's refuge-utterance unnaturally. Saheeh, Pickthall, Khattab keep the verse intact.

**19:22 (combined=0.272 — LOW, outlier=Pickthall).** `makānan qaṣiyyan` — Pickthall "far place," others "remote / distant." The most resolved verse in the cluster.

**19:24 (combined=0.687).** `fa-nādāhā min taḥtihā` — the unspecified caller. Saheeh "But he called her" (he-ambiguous), Pickthall "Then (one) cried unto her" (anonymized), Khattab "So a voice reassured her" (depersonalized — the most interpretive), Arberry "But the one that was below her called to her" (most literal). The Arabic refuses to disambiguate Gabriel-vs-infant-Jesus; Arberry alone preserves the ambiguity intact.

**19:25 (combined=0.491, outlier=Arberry).** `tusāqiṭ ʿalayki ruṭaban janiyyan` — the dropping-of-dates phrase. Arberry "there shall come tumbling upon thee dates fresh and ripe" — the only translation where the dates "tumble." The verb `tusāqiṭ` is the form-VI continuous-falling; Arberry's `tumble` captures the iterative, the others (`drop`, `fall`) lose it.

**21:91 / 66:12 (combined=0.641 / 0.617).** The grammatical gender shift between `fīhā` (into HER) and `fīhi` (into IT) is handled with insertions by three of four translators (Saheeh and Khattab insert "garment"/"womb"/"angel/Gabriel"; Pickthall inserts "(something) of Our Spirit"). Arberry alone preserves the bare prepositional structure. The interpretive layer worries about the literal in-blowing; the Arabic does not.

The translation-pattern across the Maryam corpus: where the Arabic is bald, translators insert. The bareness of the Arabic is a feature, not a defect — the Quran is willing to leave the in-blowing literal, the caller unspecified, the place unnamed, the angel non-described-beyond-`bashar sawiyyan`. The translators keep filling in.

## 13. Negative space

What the Quran withholds about Maryam:

- **No naming of the "far place"** (19:22). Tradition: Bethlehem. Quran: silent.
- **No description of the angel.** Only `bashar sawī` (perfect human). No height, age, dress, voice quality.
- **No specification of the provisions in the miḥrāb** (3:37). What food? Out-of-season fruits? Bread? Quran: only `rizq`.
- **No description of HOW Maryam carried Jesus.** `Ḥamalathu` is the generic conception-verb. No gestation-period given (compare 46:15's `thalāthūna shahran` for ordinary carrying — Maryam gets no number).
- **No description of HOW long the withdrawal lasted.** Days? Months? The full pregnancy? Quran: silent. (Some traditions read the pregnancy as compressed to hours; the Arabic is non-committal.)
- **No mention of Maryam's later life.** No marriage, no adulthood, no death. She has the birth-arc only.
- **No mention of HER death.** Idrīs is raised (19:57). Jesus is raised (3:55). Maryam simply disappears from the narrative. She is the only figure of the Quran's miracle-cluster to neither die-on-record nor be-raised-on-record.
- **No specification of who the angel was.** Tradition: Gabriel (Jibrīl). The Quran calls him `rūḥanā` (our Spirit) and `rasūl rabbiki` (a messenger of your Lord) — not by name.
- **No description of the actual moment of conception.** 19:21 ends with the announcement; 19:22 opens with `fa-ḥamalathu` (already conceived). The conception itself is verb-only, no scene.
- **No description of the actual moment of birth.** 19:23 has the labor pangs; 19:24-26 have the post-birth voice and dates and stream. The birth itself is between the verses.
- **No description of the angelic-form's exit.** The angel appears (19:17), speaks (19:19, 21), and the next verse (19:22) is Maryam alone, conceived. The angel's departure is unrecorded.

This is a NARRATIVE BUILT ON OMISSIONS. The events are the joints between unrecorded transits. Maryam's mobility-set is a sequence of arrivals without journeys.

## 15. Rabbit holes

1. **Maryam as the implicit female prophet.** Compile every formula the Quran applies to (a) named prophets and (b) Maryam. Where do they overlap? Where do they not? The hypothesis: the prophet-suite of vocabulary (revelation, scripture-reception, mission, sign, etc.) is shared with Maryam at the EVENT level but withheld at the LEXICAL level. The structural argument can be made by mapping which prophet-formulas attach to her: `iṣṭafā` (chose) — applied to Maryam (3:42), Adam, Noah, the family of Imran (3:33), Moses (7:144). Maryam shares this verb with the patriarchs.

2. **The 21:91 / 66:12 grammatical-gender shift.** Why `fīhā` in 21:91 but `fīhi` in 66:12? Hypothesis: the antecedent shifts with the verse's structural context. 21:91 names her chastity in a relative clause (`al-latī aḥṣanat farjahā`) and the antecedent of `fīhā` reads as Maryam herself. 66:12 names her chastity in the same relative clause but the closer-antecedent `farj` (masculine in some readings) governs the masculine `fīhi`. Worth a focused syntax-check across classical commentaries.

3. **The voice from beneath (19:24).** The Gabriel-vs-Jesus split is one of the oldest Quranic interpretive cruxes. Compile the textual evidence on each side. The `min taḥtihā` (from beneath her) deictic favors Gabriel-below-the-tree (Maryam is at a height, the angel below). The pre-cradle-speech interpretation (immediate Jesus-speech) requires the infant to be already-born-and-speaking by 19:24, which contradicts the natural reading of 19:23 (still in labor).

4. **Maryam's silence vs Jesus's speech.** The vow-of-fast (19:26 `lan ukallima l-yawma insiyyan`) and the cradle-speech (19:30) form an inverse pair. Maryam's silence is the precondition for Jesus's speech. Her muteness is divine economy: the speech-act is divinely-allocated to the more-improbable speaker. Compare with Zachariah's three-day muteness (19:10) — both Zachariah and Maryam undergo a temporary silence that frames a miraculous-birth narrative. The Zachariah-Maryam parallel has more layers than typically read.

5. **The miḥrāb as architectural lexeme.** 3:37 (Maryam's miḥrāb) and 3:39, 19:11 (Zachariah's miḥrāb) put Zachariah and Maryam in the SAME architectural space. Each uses the miḥrāb. Each receives a divine event there (Maryam's provisions, Zachariah's angel-announcement). The miḥrāb is the Quran's hidden site of supernatural-encounter. Compile every miḥrāb-occurrence and map. The four occurrences are 3:37, 3:39, 19:11, 38:21 (David's miḥrāb — the litigants visit; the visitors enter unannounced through the wall, another spatial-anomaly miracle).

6. **The qṣy-marker and teleportation destinations.** `Aqṣā` (17:1, the Furthest Mosque) and `qaṣiyy` (19:22, far place) share the root q-ṣ-w. Both mark teleportation-class destinations. Compile every q-ṣ-w occurrence and check for the destination-of-non-ordinary-transit pattern. (See teleportation-cooccurrence.json for the data layer.)

7. **The food-miracle typology.** Maryam (3:37, 19:25), the Israelites' manna (2:57, 7:160), Jesus's table (5:114), Solomon's banquets (implicit), Zachariah's miḥrāb (implicit echo). Map the corpus. The hypothesis: Quranic food-miracles are gendered — Maryam receives provisions (passive, indoor); the Israelites receive manna (collective, outdoor); Jesus receives the table (collective, requested). Maryam's is the ONLY female-individual food-miracle.

8. **The n-f-x typology.** 15:29, 38:72 (Adam), 21:91, 66:12 (Maryam/Jesus), 39:68, 50:20, 78:18 (Trumpet). The verb anchors the THREE creation-events of the Quran's anthropology: the original creation, the unique conception, and the resurrection. Worth a dedicated cross-cutting study tying the n-f-x corpus together.

9. **The Cave + Maryam + Muhammad triple.** Three withdrawal-then-miracle narratives in the Quran: Cave Sleepers (surah 18, awā), Maryam (surah 19, intabadhat), Muhammad's Night Journey (surah 17, asrā). Three surahs in sequence (17, 18, 19) each anchored to a withdrawal-class miracle, each with a different mobility-verb. The 17-18-19 sequence may be the Quran's most concentrated cluster of teleportation-class narratives. Worth a focused study on whether the surah-ordering is meaningful here.

10. **Maryam's disappearance.** Idrīs is raised (19:57); Jesus is raised (3:55, 4:158); Muhammad dies-on-record (the Companions know). Maryam has no exit. What happens to her? The Quranic silence may reflect a deliberate refusal to give Maryam an apocalyptic-marker. She is the un-raised, un-died figure of the miracle-cluster. The Catholic dogma of the Assumption fills this gap; the Quran leaves it open. Worth a comparative-tradition study.
