# Narrative: The Night Journey (al-Isrāʾ) and the Miʿrāj question

> A disciplined text-only reading of 17:1 and 53:1-18, with strict separation between what the Qurʾān asserts and what hadith-tradition adds. The composite "Isrāʾ-Miʿrāj" of Muslim devotional memory is, at the level of the canonical text, two passages — one explicit horizontal night-transfer, one celestial vision — which the Qurʾān does **not** glue together. The famous narrative architecture (Burāq, prophet-meetings at each heaven, the prayer-bargaining with Moses, the staircase of light) is hadith-anchored, not Qurʾānic. The Qurʾān is so terse that the Night Journey, the most cinematically elaborated event in Islamic devotional life, occupies one verse of 28 words.

---

## 1. Headline finding

Two passages, one ostensible event in tradition, two distinct rhetorical operations in the text. **17:1 is a transit verse**: it names a verb (asrā), a carried party (ʿabdihi), a time (laylan), a source (al-Masjid al-Ḥarām), a destination (al-Masjid al-Aqṣā), and a purpose (li-nuriyahu min āyātinā). **53:1-18 is a vision verse**: it names no verb of motion at all for Muḥammad. It says Muḥammad *saw* (raʾā / raʾāhu, three times) — and what he saw was *another descent* (nazlatan ukhrā) — i.e., a descent of the teaching-figure (Jibrīl), not an ascent of Muḥammad. The Qurʾān gives Muḥammad a journey in 17:1 and a vision in 53; it does **not** give him an ascent (ʿuruj). The whole tradition called *miʿrāj* operates with the vocabulary of root **Erj** — a vocabulary the Qurʾān reserves for angels and the Spirit (70:4) and uses *of Muḥammad nowhere* (cf. `concept-Erj.md` §"What the Quran does NOT say about the Miʿrāj"). The single verb that would have been needed to fuse 17:1 and 53 into "first horizontal then vertical" — yaʿruju with Muḥammad as subject — is the missing word. The fusion is post-Qurʾānic.

A second finding sits beneath this: 17:1's PURPOSE-clause and 53:18's PURPOSE-clause use **identical sign-disclosure architecture** — *li-nuriyahu min āyātinā* (17:1) / *la-qad raʾā min āyāti rabbihi al-kubrā* (53:18). The same syntactic move ("show some of [Our / his Lord's] signs") frames both passages. This is the strongest *internal* textual argument that the two passages share a function, even if not an event. Both are about a prophet being shown some-of-the-greatest-signs by a divine causation. Whether they were the same night the Qurʾān refuses to say.

A third finding, methodologically central: 17:1's translation-divergence score is **0.5906** — *moderate*, not high (cf. `teleportation-divergence.md` §7.1). The verse most readers expect to be the most contested teleportation verse in the Qurʾān is in fact a site of remarkable convergence. Pickthall is the lone outlier and only on the *naming* of the two mosques ("Inviolable Place of Worship" / "Far distant place of worship") — a stylistic choice, not a theological one. **Not a single one of the four translators reads asrā as "in vision" or "by spirit."** The body-vs-vision controversy is a *commentarial* dispute layered on top of a translation-consensus that the verse describes physical transit. The doxological *subḥān* at the head is the only marker that "physical" may not be the right ontological category — and even that is implicit.

## 2. 17:1 deep read

### Arabic

> سُبۡحَٰنَ ٱلَّذِيٓ أَسۡرَىٰ بِعَبۡدِهِۦ لَيۡلࣰا مِّنَ ٱلۡمَسۡجِدِ ٱلۡحَرَامِ إِلَى ٱلۡمَسۡجِدِ ٱلۡأَقۡصَا ٱلَّذِي بَٰرَكۡنَا حَوۡلَهُۥ لِنُرِيَهُۥ مِنۡ ءَايَٰتِنَآۚ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلۡبَصِيرُ

### Four translations

- **Saheeh:** "Exalted is He who took His Servant by night from al-Masjid al-Haram to al-Masjid al-Aqsa, whose surroundings We have blessed, to show him of Our signs. Indeed, He is the Hearing, the Seeing."
- **Pickthall:** "Glorified be He Who carried His servant by night from the Inviolable Place of Worship to the Far distant place of worship the neighbourhood whereof We have blessed, that We might show him of Our tokens! Lo! He, only He, is the Hearer, the Seer."
- **Khattab:** "Glory be to the One Who took His servant ˹Muḥammad˺ by night from the Sacred Mosque to the Farthest Mosque whose surroundings We have blessed, so that We may show him some of Our signs. Indeed, He alone is the All-Hearing, All-Seeing."
- **Arberry:** "Glory be to Him, who carried His servant by night from the Holy Mosque to the Further Mosque the precincts of which We have blessed, that We might show him some of Our signs. He is the All-hearing, the All-seeing."

### Word-by-word

| Word | Gloss | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **subḥāna** | Glory/Exalted-be | Doxological accusative. The verb at the heart of the verse is encased in worship before it is even named. (See §2.1.) |
| **alladhī** | He who | God is *described*, not named. |
| **asrā** | took-by-night | Form IV (causative). The same verb that takes Lot and Moses to safety; here it takes the Prophet to vision. (See concept-sry.md §3-5.) |
| **bi-ʿabdihi** | with His servant | bi- is **comitative**, not "for." The servant is *carried with* God, not *sent by* God. (See §2.3.) |
| **laylan** | by night | Indefinite singular — "a (some) night," a portion of a night, not "the night." Pleonastic with asrā but specifies a sub-portion. |
| **min al-Masjid al-Ḥarām** | from the Sacred Mosque | Explicit source. Definite, specific. |
| **ilā al-Masjid al-Aqṣā** | to the Farthest Mosque | Explicit destination. al-Aqṣā = superlative of qaṣw "far"; literally "the most-distant." (See §2.4.) |
| **alladhī bāraknā ḥawlahu** | whose surroundings We blessed | The blessing is on the *surroundings*, not on the building itself. (See §2.5.) |
| **li-nuriyahu min āyātinā** | so that We may show him some-of Our signs | Purpose-lā + Form IV imperfect of *raʾā* (cause-to-see). The partitive *min* — *some*, not *all*. (See §2.6.) |
| **innahu huwa al-samīʿ al-baṣīr** | Indeed He is the Hearing, the Seeing | Closing divine-name pair. (See §2.7.) |

### 2.1. *Subḥān* as the verse's only theological warning

*Subḥāna alladhī …* opens almost no verses in the Qurʾān. The construction *subḥān* + *alladhī* + verb appears at:

- **17:1** — asrā (the Night Journey)
- **36:36** — khalaqa al-azwāj kullahā ("created all the pairs")
- **36:83** — bi-yadihi malakūt kulli shayʾ ("in whose hand is the dominion of all things")
- **43:13** — sakhkhara lanā hādhā ("subjected this for us")

The pattern: **subḥān + alladhī always frames a divine act that crosses creaturely category** — universal pair-creation, total-dominion, the subjugation of natural forces, and night-transfer of a prophet. It is the Qurʾān's marker that what follows resists ordinary causation. The doxology is not ornamental; it is a pre-emptive defence. The verse opens by saying *do not impute limitation to Him for what you are about to read*. This is the strongest internal signal that the Qurʾān itself anticipates objections to the Night Journey's ontology — and shields the unsaid by bracketing the said inside *subḥān*.

### 2.3. *bi-ʿabdihi* — why "His servant," not "His messenger"?

This is one of the densest theological compressions in the Qurʾān. At the verse most about Muḥammad's exalted status, he is called *ʿabd*, not *rasūl* or *nabī*. The pattern is structural: the Qurʾān uses *ʿabdihi* of Muḥammad in three places of supreme honour:

- **17:1** — the Night Journey
- **18:1** — sending down of the Book
- **53:10** — the second-vision passage's revelation-clause

In all three, the Prophet is at his most exalted and the Qurʾān reaches for *ʿabd*. Servanthood, not messengerhood, is the relational category that permits being-carried (17:1), being-given-the-Book (18:1), and being-revealed-to (53:10).

The grammar of *bi-* matters. *bi-ʿabdihi* is comitative — "with His servant." Compare to the Moses-rescue verses: *asri bi-ʿibādī* ("travel by night *with* My servants," 20:77, 26:52, 44:23) and the Lot-rescue: *fa-asri bi-ahlika* ("travel by night *with* your family," 11:81, 15:65). In every Form-IV asrā verse, the prepositional phrase *bi- + person* makes the moved party an *attached object of divine motion*, not an independent traveller. **The agent of motion is God; the human is the carried.** This is the grammar of teleportation in the Qurʾān: Lot is *taken with* his family; Moses is *taken with* his followers; Muḥammad is *taken with* — alone. He is the only ʿabd-singular asrā in the corpus. The other five Form-IV occurrences carry *plural* companions; only 17:1 carries a single servant.

### 2.4. *al-Masjid al-Aqṣā* — what does the text actually say?

The Arabic says "the most-distant place-of-prostration" (Aqṣā = elative of qaṣw — *the farthest*). It does **not** say Jerusalem. It does **not** say the Temple Mount. It does not name a city. The classical reading equates it with Jerusalem, but the Qurʾān uses a *descriptor*, not a proper noun. Pickthall is the only translator who refuses the proper-noun anachronism: he renders "the Far distant place of worship." The other three translators import "Mosque" as a quasi-toponym (Saheeh transliterates *al-Masjid al-Aqsa*; Khattab "Farthest Mosque"; Arberry "Further Mosque"). Pickthall's refusal is itself the source of his outlier status on this verse (cf. `teleportation-divergence.md` §7.1).

The textual evidence *for* a Jerusalem reading sits in §2.5 below — *bāraknā ḥawlahu*. The Holy Land is the Qurʾān's other "blessed land" (7:137, 21:71, 21:81). If "blessed surroundings" = "the Holy Land," then *al-Aqṣā* is in the Holy Land. But the Qurʾān never closes the loop with a name — it leaves the destination as a *function* (the most-distant mosque whose surroundings are blessed) rather than a *fact* (Jerusalem). The reader who knows the Holy Land's blessedness reads it; the reader who does not is given a description.

The structural rhyme: *makānan qaṣiyyan* (19:22) is the place Maryam withdraws to give birth — using the same root **qSw** as al-Aqṣā. Maryam goes horizontally to "a far place" to bring forth Jesus; Muḥammad is taken horizontally to "the farthest mosque" to be shown signs. *Both prophet-events are framed as transit to the q-S-w destination.* (See `concept-sry.md` §10 and `concept-rfE.md` cross-cutting on the q-S-w family as a teleportation marker.)

### 2.5. *bāraknā ḥawlahu* — the blessed surroundings

The blessing is on what is **around** the mosque, not on the mosque itself. *ḥawlahu* — "around it." This subtle preposition does work: the destination is named as a *region*, not a *building*. The architectural fact (a mosque) is anchored inside a geographical fact (a blessed surrounding). Cross-references for "the blessed land":

- **7:137** — God caused the oppressed Israelites to inherit *the eastern regions and the western ones, which We had blessed* (mashāriqa al-arḍi wa magharibahā allatī bāraknā fīhā).
- **21:71** — God delivered Abraham and Lot *to the land which We had blessed for the worlds* (ilā al-arḍi allatī bāraknā fīhā lil-ʿālamīn).
- **21:81** — Solomon's wind blows toward *the land which We had blessed* (ilā al-arḍi allatī bāraknā fīhā).

These three Bāraka-locations point to the Levant. The blessed-region tradition coheres around the Holy Land. If 17:1 is part of this same family of "the land We blessed," then *al-Aqṣā* sits inside the Bāraka-region — i.e., in or near Jerusalem. This is the Qurʾān's strongest *implicit* identification of the destination, and it is implicit only.

### 2.6. *li-nuriyahu min āyātinā* — the purpose-clause

The purpose of the journey is not commandment-receiving (no laws are given), not message-delivery (Muḥammad is not sent to a people), and not negotiation (the prayer-reduction is hadith). The purpose is **vision** — *li-nuriyahu* ("so that We may *show* him") + *min āyātinā* ("of Our signs," partitive *min*).

Two structural features:

1. **Vision, not transit-as-end.** The transit is *instrumental*. The verb pair is asrā→nuriyahu — God moves him, then God shows him. The motion serves the showing. The showing is the verse's telos.
2. **Partitive *min*.** *Some of* Our signs, not all. The vision is bounded; the disclosure is partial. *Min āyātinā* echoes Moses (20:23 — *li-nuriyaka min āyātinā al-kubrā*, "so We may show you of Our greatest signs"). The same purpose-clause structure appears at the burning bush. Two prophets, two transits-to-vision: Moses to the valley of Ṭuwā (20:12) for his great signs; Muḥammad to al-Aqṣā for his signs.

The *li-nuriyahu* / *raʾā* link is the textual hinge between 17:1 and 53:18. See §4.

### 2.7. *al-samīʿ al-baṣīr* — the closing names

The verse closes with two divine names: the Hearing, the Seeing. Each is doing specific work at this verse-ending:

- **al-samīʿ** answers the *vocal* dimension. God hears what others do not — the prayers, the secret pleas, the doctrinal debates, the night's audible-but-unwitnessed events.
- **al-baṣīr** answers the *visual* dimension just opened by *li-nuriyahu* (showing). God sees, *and* He causes-to-see. The cause-to-see verb of the purpose-clause is *uri* (Form IV of raʾā); the closing name *baṣīr* is from a different root (b-ṣ-r), but both belong to the visual register. The verse opens and closes inside the visual.

The hearing-seeing pair is a structural close-out: God's two perceptual modes bracket the Prophet's one perceptual event (vision). The Prophet *saw*; God *hears-and-sees*. The asymmetry is a subtle theological signal — divine perception is bi-modal and total; prophetic perception is uni-modal and partial (vision of *some* signs).

### Translations of the load-bearing verses (53:13-18)

- **53:13 raʾāhu nazlatan ukhrā** — Saheeh: "And he certainly saw him in another descent." Pickthall: "And verily he saw him yet another time." Khattab: "And he certainly saw that ˹angel descend˺ a second time." Arberry: "Indeed, he saw him another time."
- **53:14 ʿinda sidrati al-muntahā** — Saheeh: "At the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary." Pickthall: "By the lote-tree of the utmost boundary." Khattab: "at the Lote Tree of the most extreme limit ˹in the seventh heaven˺." Arberry: "by the Lote-Tree of the Boundary."
- **53:15 ʿindahā jannatu al-maʾwā** — Saheeh: "Near it is the Garden of Refuge." Pickthall: "Nigh unto which is the Garden of Abode." Khattab: "near which is the Garden of ˹Eternal˺ Residence." Arberry: "nigh which is the Garden of the Refuge."
- **53:18 la-qad raʾā min āyāti rabbihi al-kubrā** — Saheeh: "He certainly saw of the greatest signs of his Lord." Pickthall: "Verily he saw one of the greater revelations of his Lord." Khattab: "He certainly saw some of his Lord's greatest signs." Arberry: "Indeed, he saw one of the greatest signs of his Lord."

### 3.1. Is 53 about Muḥammad's ascent, or about Jibrīl's descent?

The text-only reading: **53 is about a descent, not an ascent.** The key word at 53:13 is *nazlatan ukhrā* — "another *descent*." From root **n-z-l** (to come down). The party who is descending is the *taught-one of mighty powers* introduced at 53:5 — *ʿallamahu shadīd al-quwā* — i.e., the teaching-figure traditionally identified as Jibrīl. The grammar of the entire passage tracks an *encounter* in which the descended figure approaches Muḥammad — not Muḥammad ascending to him.

Trace the verbs:
- **53:5** — *ʿallamahu shadīd al-quwā* — "one mighty in power **taught him**" (the figure is the teacher, Muḥammad the taught).
- **53:6** — *fa-stawā* — "and he stood poised" (the figure assumed his form).
- **53:7** — *wa-huwa bi-l-ufuqi al-aʿlā* — "while **he** was at the highest horizon" (the *he* is the figure, on the horizon — not Muḥammad on the horizon).
- **53:8** — *thumma danā fa-tadallā* — "then **he** drew near and **lowered himself**" (tadallā is causative-reflexive of d-l-w "to dangle/lower" — the descended motion).
- **53:9** — *fa-kāna qāba qawsayni aw adnā* — "and he was at two-bow-lengths or nearer" (the proximity is achieved by *his* descent, not by Muḥammad's ascent).
- **53:10** — *fa-awḥā ilā ʿabdihi mā awḥā* — "then **he** revealed to His servant what he revealed" (the figure reveals; Muḥammad receives).
- **53:11-12** — *mā kadhaba al-fuʾādu mā raʾā / a-fa-tumārūnahu ʿalā mā yarā* — "the heart did not lie about what it saw / will you dispute with him over what he sees?" (Muḥammad sees; the figure is seen.)
- **53:13** — *wa-la-qad raʾāhu nazlatan ukhrā* — "and he certainly saw **him** in another **descent**" (a *second* descent of the same figure; not a second journey of Muḥammad).
- **53:14** — *ʿinda sidrati al-muntahā* — "at the Lote-Tree of the Boundary" — the *location* of the second descent.

**The vertical motion in 53 is downward, not upward.** The Qurʾān says Jibrīl came-down (nazala) twice; it does not say Muḥammad went up. The *miʿrāj* tradition reads the second descent as occurring at a celestial location *to which Muḥammad has ascended* — but the Qurʾān places Muḥammad as the *seer*, the figure as the *descender*, and the meeting-point at the Lote-Tree. Whether Muḥammad reached the Lote-Tree by ascent or whether the Lote-Tree's vision came to him is exactly the question the Qurʾān does not answer.

### 3.2. *Sidrat al-muntahā* — the lote-tree of the boundary

The phrase appears once in the Qurʾān (53:14) and is one of its most elliptical locations. *Sidra* = lote-tree (a Levantine bramble/thorn-tree); *muntahā* = the place-of-ending (root n-h-y, "to end, prohibit"). Literally: *the lote-tree of the place-where-things-end*. What ends there? The Qurʾān does not say. Tradition fills the gap: the boundary between created knowledge and the divine; the upper limit of permitted ascent for created beings; the threshold beyond which only God knows. Khattab smuggles in the seventh-heaven gloss (*˹in the seventh heaven˺*); the other three translators leave the term un-glossed.

The Qurʾān's silence on what *muntahā* bounds is the most aggressive interpretive vacuum in the passage. The hadith tradition rushes to fill it (Bukhārī, Muslim — the lote-tree as the limit of Jibrīl's permitted ascent, beyond which Muḥammad alone proceeded). The Qurʾān gives only the name and the locative *ʿinda* ("at, near") and moves on.

### 3.3. *Jannat al-Maʾwā* — the Garden of Refuge

Located near (*ʿindahā*, "near it") the Lote-Tree. *Maʾwā* is from root **A-w-y** (to take shelter, to take in) — the same root behind the Sleepers' *ʾāwā* into the cave (18:10) and the eschatological *maʾwāhu jahannam* ("his refuge is Hell," 3:162). *Jannat al-Maʾwā* is "the garden of taking-in" — the eschatological *destination* of the righteous, here glimpsed near the Lote-Tree. This is the Qurʾān's single passage where the eschatological garden is *seen by a living person*. The vision is not a travel-through-Paradise tour (the hadith elaborates this); it is a single sighting of the garden's location.

### 3.4. *Raʾā* — the verb that runs the passage

The verb of *seeing* (Form I r-ʾ-y) appears four times in the passage:
- **53:11** — *mā kadhaba al-fuʾādu mā raʾā* — "the heart did not lie about what *it saw*"
- **53:12** — *a-fa-tumārūnahu ʿalā mā yarā* — "will you dispute with him over what *he sees*?"
- **53:13** — *wa-la-qad raʾāhu nazlatan ukhrā* — "and he certainly *saw him* in another descent"
- **53:18** — *la-qad raʾā min āyāti rabbihi al-kubrā* — "he certainly *saw* of the greatest signs of his Lord"

The Qurʾān is *insistent* — four times in eight verses — that this is a *seeing* event. Not hearing, not knowing, not feeling, not dreaming. *Seeing*. The repetition is rhetorical defence: 53:11 says the heart did not lie; 53:12 challenges the disputers; 53:17 says the *baṣar* (eye) did not swerve nor transgress; 53:18 closes by saying he saw. The Qurʾān stockades the visual claim. This is one of its rare visual-evidence passages, and it leans hard on the verb *raʾā* to defend its truth-value.

This is the load-bearing verb-link to 17:1's purpose-clause. 17:1: *li-nuriyahu min āyātinā* (so We may *show* him *some of* Our signs) — Form IV (causative) of *raʾā*. 53:18: *la-qad raʾā min āyāti rabbihi al-kubrā* (he saw *of* his Lord's *greatest* signs) — Form I of the same root. The same verb-root, same purpose, same partitive *min*. **The two passages share a syntactic spine even if they do not share an event.** This is the textual reason the post-Qurʾānic tradition fused them: their grammar invites the fusion. The Qurʾān itself does not perform it.

### 3.5. The two descents (nazlatan ukhrā) — the count that matters

53:13's *nazlatan ukhrā* tells us that **there were at least two descents of the teaching-figure**. The first is presumably the initial revelation (Iqraʾ — 96:1-5, hadith locates it at the cave of Ḥirāʾ); the second is the celestial-location encounter at the Lote-Tree. The Qurʾān's *count* matters: it explicitly numbers the second one. This is the Qurʾān's only *enumerated* prophetic vision-event. Most prophetic encounters are not counted; this one is.

If the count is taken seriously, then the structure of Muḥammad's revelation-history is:
- **First descent of the figure** — the initial Iqraʾ event (not described in 53)
- **Second descent of the figure** — at the Lote-Tree (described in 53:13-18)

Whether either of these is the same event as 17:1 is unspecified. The Qurʾān gives us *one transit verb* (asrā at 17:1), *one count of descents* (53:13's *ukhrā* implies two), and *one purpose-frame* (sign-disclosure, shared by both passages). It does not assert a one-to-one mapping among them.

## 4. The Isrāʾ-Miʿrāj fusion question

### What the Qurʾān HAS

- **17:1** — explicit horizontal night-transit. Verb: asrā (sry IV). Source: al-Masjid al-Ḥarām. Destination: al-Masjid al-Aqṣā. Time: laylan. Purpose: sign-disclosure.
- **53:1-18** — explicit celestial vision. Verb: raʾā (×4) + descent of the figure (nazlatan ukhrā). Location: ʿinda sidrati al-muntahā / ʿindahā jannatu al-maʾwā. Purpose: sign-disclosure (*raʾā min āyāti rabbihi al-kubrā*).
- **Shared syntactic spine** — both passages use *raʾā / nuriya min āyāt* purpose-architecture. The vision is the goal in both.
- **Shared *ʿabd* designation** — both passages call Muḥammad ʿabd (17:1: *bi-ʿabdihi*; 53:10: *ilā ʿabdihi*). The honorific reaches for the carried-relational title in both.

### What the Qurʾān does NOT have

- **No Erj of Muḥammad.** Not a single occurrence of the **Erj** root has Muḥammad as subject anywhere in the Qurʾān (cf. `concept-Erj.md` §"What the Quran does NOT say about the Miʿrāj"). The vertical-ascent vocabulary is reserved for angels, the Spirit, "the affair," and hypothetical disbelievers. The verb that would be needed to fuse 17:1 (horizontal asrā) into 53 (vertical-implied) is absent.
- **No miʿrāj as event-name.** The noun *miʿrāj* (singular of *maʿārij*) does not appear in the Qurʾān at all. The plural *maʿārij* appears at 70:3 (divine epithet *dhū al-maʿārij*) and 43:33 (counterfactual silver stairs). Neither passage attaches it to Muḥammad.
- **No Burāq.** The animal of transport is hadith-anchored (Bukhārī, Muslim). The Qurʾān supplies neither a mount nor a vehicle.
- **No prophet-meetings at each heaven.** The hadith narrative places Adam at the first heaven, Jesus and John at the second, Joseph at the third, Idrīs at the fourth, Aaron at the fifth, Moses at the sixth, Abraham at the seventh. The Qurʾān names none of these encounters at 17:1 or 53.
- **No prayer-bargaining with Moses.** The famous fifty-prayers-reduced-to-five negotiation is hadith. The Qurʾān is silent on the *content* of the encounter.
- **No description of celestial geography between Mecca/Aqṣā and the Lote-Tree.** The hadith elaborates seven heavens with gates and gatekeepers. The Qurʾān gives the start (al-Masjid al-Ḥarām), the named earthly destination (al-Masjid al-Aqṣā), and a separate celestial location (sidrat al-muntahā) — and does not connect them by an itinerary.
- **No travel duration.** No time-stamp inside the journey. Only *laylan* — "by night" — bounding the entire event to a portion of one night, but without internal divisions.
- **No return.** 17:1 narrates a one-way trip; the return to Mecca is unmentioned. The hadith assumes return; the Qurʾān leaves it.
- **No fusion-assertion.** Most decisively: the Qurʾān does not say the two passages describe the same night, the same event, or even sequential events. The fusion is an *inference* that the tradition makes; the Qurʾān supplies only the materials.

The asymmetry between the textual minimalism (one verse + 18 verses, both terse) and the traditional elaboration (Burāq, seven heavens, prophet-meetings, prayer-bargaining, the lote-tree's leaves like elephants' ears) is one of the cleanest textual-vs-extra-textual gaps in Islamic literature. The miʿrāj of devotional memory is *constructed* from the Qurʾān's vocabulary (Erj-root) and the Qurʾān's two thinnest references (17:1 + 53:13-18) by hadith elaboration — a process the Qurʾān neither mandates nor forbids.

## 5. Translation divergence on both passages

### 17:1

From `data/structural/translation-divergence.json`:

Combined score 0.5906 is moderate, but lexical disagreement is *normalized* very high (0.8947) — the four translators chose 34 distinct lexical realisations. The high lexical-spread does **not** translate into theological-spread; the translators all agree on the *event* (a real night-transit of God's servant from Mecca to a far mosque) and disagree on the *words*. Specific contestation sites:

| Word | Saheeh | Pickthall | Khattab | Arberry |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **subḥāna** | "Exalted is" | "Glorified be" | "Glory be to" | "Glory be to" |
| **asrā** | "took" | "carried" | "took" | "carried" |
| **bi-ʿabdihi** | "His Servant" (capitalised) | "His servant" | "His servant ˹Muḥammad˺" | "His servant" |
| **al-Masjid al-Ḥarām** | "al-Masjid al-Haram" | "the Inviolable Place of Worship" | "the Sacred Mosque" | "the Holy Mosque" |
| **al-Masjid al-Aqṣā** | "al-Masjid al-Aqsa" | "the Far distant place of worship" | "the Farthest Mosque" | "the Further Mosque" |
| **li-nuriyahu** | "to show him" | "that We might show him" | "so that We may show him" | "that We might show him some of" |
| **al-samīʿ al-baṣīr** | "the Hearing, the Seeing" | "the Hearer, the Seer" | "the All-Hearing, All-Seeing" | "the All-hearing, the All-seeing" |

The single deepest move is **Pickthall's refusal of "Mosque."** "The Inviolable Place of Worship" / "the Far distant place of worship" preserves the Arabic *masjid* as a *function* (place of prostration) rather than importing the seventh-century-architectural term. The other three import "Mosque" as a quasi-toponym. Pickthall is closer to the Arabic; the others are closer to modern devotional usage. This is also the source of his lone-outlier flag.

The most theologically loaded micro-decision is **Saheeh's capitalised "His Servant."** Capitalising flags *ʿabd* as a relational title — a uniqueness-marker. The other three translators leave *servant* lowercase, treating it as a generic noun. Saheeh's move is theologically pointed and is the only translator-marked theological gloss in the verse.

**Crucially: no translator uses "in vision" or "by spirit" or "in dream."** All four read the journey as physical transit at the surface. The body-vs-vision controversy is a *commentarial* dispute, not a translation dispute. The text reads as physical transit; only the doxology *subḥān* at the head signals that "physical" may not be the right ontological category — and that signal is *not* picked up by any translator at the level of word-choice. They convey the doxology and let the metaphysics rest.

### 53:13-18

The contestation pattern in 53:13-18 differs from 17:1. Here the divergences are *interpretive interpolations*:

**53:13 — *raʾāhu nazlatan ukhrā*.** Whose descent? Saheeh / Pickthall / Arberry leave the antecedent of *-hu* implicit (the figure of 53:5-12, presumably Jibrīl). Khattab fills the gap aggressively: *˹angel descend˺* — naming the descender. The contested point is *whose* descent is being recounted; tradition reads Jibrīl's, but the antecedent in the Arabic is technically the same *huwa* of 53:7 (the *shadīd al-quwā*). Khattab's bracketing equals "I am telling you it's the angel"; the others let the reader hold the ambiguity.

**53:14 — *ʿinda sidrati al-muntahā*.** Khattab inserts *˹in the seventh heaven˺* — a hadith gloss not found in the Arabic. The other three translators give literal renderings ("Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary" / "lote-tree of the utmost boundary" / "Lote-Tree of the Boundary"). This is the cleanest case of a translator importing a hadith reading into the text proper. Khattab's brackets are honest (they signal an interpolation), but the move is decisive: he tells the reader where the lote-tree is *located*. The Qurʾān does not.

**53:15 — *jannat al-maʾwā*.** All four translate *maʾwā* as "refuge" / "abode" / "residence" — convergent on the eschatological-garden meaning. Khattab inserts *˹Eternal˺* — emphasizing the garden's permanence (a hadith-aligned move; the Qurʾān does not specify *eternity* of this particular garden at this verse).

**53:18 — *raʾā min āyāti rabbihi al-kubrā*.** All four translate as a sign-vision; Pickthall uniquely chooses *revelations* (vs *signs* in the others). This is Pickthall's small interpretive lean: *āyāt* as *revelations* rather than *signs* makes the vision a content-disclosure rather than a sight-disclosure. The others keep *signs*.

The pattern across 53:1-18 is **Khattab the gloss-importer**, **Pickthall the archaizer**, **Saheeh the stable centre**, **Arberry the literalist with literary inversions**. The translators split on whether to import hadith into the translation; the text itself does not.

## 6. The body-vs-vision question (17:1 ↔ 17:60)

### The two classical positions

- **Bodily-Isrāʾ majority (Bukhārī, most Sunni tradition):** the journey was in body. ʿabd in 17:1 designates the whole person, body and soul. The hadith corpus describes Muḥammad waking, mounting Burāq, traversing Jerusalem, leading prayer in al-Aqṣā, ascending through the heavens.
- **Vision-Isrāʾ minority (some Muʿtazila, ʿĀʾisha-attributed report in Ibn Hishām):** the journey was in spirit / vision. The body remained in Mecca; the soul travelled.

The textual data each side draws from:

### The bodily reading's textual support

1. ***ʿabd* (servant) is an embodied term.** In all 8 asrā verses, *bi-ʿibādī / bi-ahlika / bi-ʿabdihi* refers to *bodies that are carried*. Lot's family is carried out of Sodom *in body*; Moses' followers cross the sea *in body*. By analogy, Muḥammad is carried *in body*.
2. **The doxology *subḥān*.** If the journey were merely a vision, the doxology pre-empting "implausibility" objections would be unmotivated. *Subḥān* is the marker that the act exceeds creaturely category — which fits a bodily transit better than a spirit-vision.
3. **The two specific endpoints.** A vision does not need named endpoints; only a transit does. The verse's specificity (*min al-Masjid al-Ḥarām ilā al-Masjid al-Aqṣā*) is the grammar of spatial change-of-state.
4. **No translator chooses vision-language at 17:1.** The translation consensus reads asrā as transit.

### The vision reading's textual support

1. **17:60 — *wa-mā jaʿalnā al-ruʾyā al-latī araynāka illā fitnatan li-l-nās*** — "And We did not make the *ruʾyā* which We showed you except as a trial for the people."

The textual proximity to 17:1 is suspicious. Sūra 17 opens with the Night Journey at v.1 and returns to a *ruʾyā* (vision/dream-vision) at v.60 — within the same sūra, separated by a long Israelite-history excursus. The classical case for the vision-reading rests on the hypothesis that 17:60's *ruʾyā* refers back to 17:1's journey. If so, the journey is *named a ruʾyā* by the Qurʾān itself.

The four translations of 17:60's *ruʾyā*:
- **Saheeh:** "the sight which We showed you"
- **Pickthall:** "the sight which We showed thee"
- **Khattab:** "what We brought you to see"
- **Arberry:** "the vision that We showed thee"

Three of four use "sight"; only Arberry uses "vision." All four leave the referent unspecified.

2. **The *raʾā* / *nuriya* family of verbs.** 17:1's *li-nuriyahu* (Form IV of *raʾā*) and 17:60's *araynāka* (also Form IV of *raʾā*) share the same root. Both verses are causative-vision verses with God as agent and Muḥammad as object. The lexical link is real.

### The interpretive crux

- **What is *ruʾyā*?** The word in classical Arabic spans *vision* (waking-vision, witnessed-sight) and *dream-vision* (sleep-vision). The Qurʾān uses it elsewhere of Joseph's dream (12:5) and of the dream of slaughtering Ishmael (37:105 — *ṣaddaqta al-ruʾyā*). The dream-reading is plausible. But *araynāka* (the cognate verb-form) is also used of God showing real things (e.g., 17:1's parallel *li-nuriyahu*).
- **Why would the Qurʾān call a real journey a *fitna* (trial)?** A *fitna* is a *test-event*: something whose interpretation the people will get right or wrong. If the Night Journey was real and bodily, the Meccans had the choice of believing or disbelieving — that *is* a *fitna*. The vision-reading is not required by the *fitna* claim; the bodily-reading also generates a *fitna* of belief.

### Where the data lands

The Qurʾān does not unambiguously say. The most honest reading is that **17:1 reads as physical transit at the surface; 17:60's *ruʾyā* may or may not refer back; the body-vs-vision controversy is a question the text does not settle.** Both classical positions are textually defensible. The translation-consensus on asrā as transit, combined with the absence of "in vision" language at 17:1, gives the bodily reading a slight surface-level advantage — but the *ruʾyā* of 17:60 keeps the vision reading textually anchored.

This is **the highest-stakes interpretive question in the Qurʾān's teleportation theology**, because the answer determines whether *asrā* is a Form-IV verb of physical-divine-transport (in which case the Qurʾān asserts physical teleportation of bodies) or a Form-IV verb of vision-induction (in which case the Qurʾān asserts vision-induction). The Qurʾān refuses to disambiguate. The doxological *subḥān* at the head is the verse's only metaphysical signal — and it works equally well for either reading.

## 7. The PURPOSE of the journey — vision-as-credentialing

Both passages frame the journey as VISION-OF-SIGNS. 17:1's purpose-clause is *li-nuriyahu min āyātinā* (so We may show him some of Our signs). 53:18's closing claim is *la-qad raʾā min āyāti rabbihi al-kubrā* (he certainly saw some of his Lord's greatest signs). The shared *raʾā / nuriya min āyāt* spine is the textual hinge.

Why does Muḥammad need this vision? The text does not explicitly say, but the structural cues converge on **credentialing**:

1. **17:1 is followed by 17:2** — *wa-ātaynā Mūsā al-kitāba wa-jaʿalnāhu hudan li-banī Isrāʾīl* ("We gave Moses the Book and made it a guidance for the Children of Israel"). The pivot from Muḥammad's transit to Moses' Book is structural: the surah moves from the new Prophet's vision to the old Prophet's revelation. The implicit argument: just as God showed Moses signs in the Sinai (cf. 20:23 *li-nuriyaka min āyātinā al-kubrā*), so He showed Muḥammad signs at the Aqṣā. **Muḥammad is being placed in the prophet-line by being given the prophet-vision.** The Night Journey is the credentialling event that transports him into the Mosaic-prophetic register.
2. **53:5-18 stockades the visual claim** — *mā kadhaba al-fuʾādu mā raʾā* (the heart did not lie about what it saw); *a-fa-tumārūnahu ʿalā mā yarā* (will you dispute with him over what he sees?); *mā zāgha al-baṣaru wa-mā ṭaghā* (the eye did not swerve nor transgress). The Qurʾān is *defending* the vision against challengers. This is the language of testimony-validation: the Prophet *saw*, his sight was true, his witness is reliable. The vision is *evidentiary*.
3. **Both passages refuse to elaborate the vision's content.** Partitive *min* in both: *some of* the signs, not all. *Greatest* (al-kubrā) signs in 53:18 — but no description of what the signs were. This is consistent with the credentialing reading: what matters is *that* he saw, not *what*. The witness is being established, not the content delivered.

The theological reading: the Night Journey is a *credentialing event*. It validates Muḥammad's prophethood by giving him a direct cosmic-order vision. He returns having SEEN. The community is asked to accept him on the basis of testimony to that vision (53:11-12). The Qurʾān's reticence about the vision's content is rhetorically effective: the *unsaid signs* function as warrant for further teaching — the Prophet saw what he saw, and the Qurʾān is what flows from that authority.

What this comparative table reveals:

1. **Muḥammad is the only prophet whose teleportation event has an *explicit purpose-clause of vision-disclosure*.** Idrīs is raised (verb only). Jesus is raised (verb only, with polemical bal-correction). Maryam withdraws (verb only). Solomon's throne arrives (verb only). Only 17:1 says *why* the transit happens — *li-nuriyahu min āyātinā*. The purpose-clause is unique to Muḥammad's transit.
2. **Muḥammad is the only prophet with a *round-trip*.** Idrīs and Jesus are raised and stay raised. Maryam withdraws and returns (implicit). Muḥammad is taken and returns (implicit by the *laylan* time-bound). The round-trip sits with the *living* prophets; the one-way trip sits with the *taken-up* prophets.
3. **Muḥammad's transit is HORIZONTAL.** Idrīs and Jesus are vertical; Muḥammad is *al-Masjid al-Ḥarām → al-Masjid al-Aqṣā* — terrestrial place-names. The Qurʾān does not give Muḥammad a vertical-completed event; what it gives him in 53 is a *vision* in which a celestial figure descends to meet him. The vertical motion in Muḥammad's career is *the figure's*, not his.
4. **The vision-event in 53 makes Muḥammad structurally analogous to Moses at the Mountain** — a prophet to whom God shows signs at a privileged site. Moses sees God's signs at Sinai (*nuriyahu min āyātinā al-kubrā*, 20:23-24); Muḥammad sees God's signs at the Lote-Tree (*raʾā min āyāti rabbihi al-kubrā*, 53:18). The two prophets occupy the same vision-syntax. This is the structural reason Muḥammad's mission can be presented as a continuation of Moses' (cf. 17:2's pivot from Isrāʾ to Mūsā's Book).

## 9. Negative space — the textual minimalism

The Qurʾān does **not** say:
- The mode of transport (no Burāq, no vehicle, no animal).
- The duration of the transit.
- What happened *inside* al-Aqṣā (no prayer, no meeting, no inscription).
- That there was a return journey (only implied by *laylan*).
- The names of beings encountered (no Adam, no Moses, no Idrīs, no Abraham as travel-stop).
- The geography between Mecca and al-Aqṣā (no terrain, no air, no sea).
- The geography between al-Aqṣā and the Lote-Tree (no heavens, no gates, no gatekeepers, no levels).
- The year, season, hour, or month of the event.
- The five-prayers-from-fifty-prayers prayer-bargaining narrative.
- The phrase *miʿrāj* (it does not occur in the Qurʾān).
- That 17:1 and 53:13-18 happened on the same night (or even the same year).
- That the journey continued vertically from al-Aqṣā.
- That the Lote-Tree is located in the seventh heaven (or any heaven).
- The number of bodies, eyes, wings, heights, or splendours of the *shadīd al-quwā*.

What it *does* say is dense but spare: a doxology, a verb, a carried party, a time-window, two named endpoints, a purpose-clause, and two divine-name closures. Eighteen verses of celestial-vision pericope: an oath, a defence of the messenger, three verses describing a teaching-figure, a proximity formula, a revelation-clause, three verses of vision-defence, the second-descent count, the Lote-Tree location, the Garden-of-Refuge proximity, a covering-of-the-tree clause, an eye-discipline clause, and the closing sign-disclosure summary.

The minimalism is the Qurʾān's chief rhetorical device on this material. The doxology *subḥān* shields the unsaid; the bi- of *bi-ʿabdihi* tells you the servant is carried but not how; the *min* … *ilā* names the endpoints but not the path; the *li-nuriyahu min āyātinā* names the purpose but not the content; *raʾā* names the seeing but not the seen. **The Qurʾān names the endpoints and the speed; it does not name the medium.** This is consistent with the Qurʾān's general pattern around teleportation-class events (cf. Solomon's throne at 27:40 — *qabla an yartadda ilayka ṭarfuk* "before your glance returns to you" — speed named, mechanism unsaid; Maryam at 19:22 — *intabadhat bihi makānan qaṣiyyan* — withdrawal asserted, journey sketched). The *miʿrāj* tradition fills in the medium — Burāq, gates, levels, encounters — that the Qurʾān leaves blank. This is one of the cleanest gaps between scriptural minimalism and devotional elaboration in Islamic literature.

## 11. Rabbit holes (what hadith adds that the Qurʾān doesn't say)

1. **Burāq.** The named winged steed of the Prophet's transport. Not in the Qurʾān. Hadith: Bukhārī, Muslim, Ibn Hishām. Rabbit hole: trace the earliest Burāq attestations and whether they predate or postdate the late-second-century hadith collections.
2. **Seven-heaven travel narrative.** The Prophet is hadith-described as ascending through seven heavens with prophet-encounters at each. Not in the Qurʾān. The Qurʾān's seven-heavens schema (67:3, 71:15, 78:12) is *cosmological*; the *miʿrāj* tradition appropriates it as a *travel-itinerary*. Rabbit hole: when does the seven-heavens-as-itinerary first appear?
3. **The fifty-to-five prayer negotiation.** Moses repeatedly counsels Muḥammad to return to God for prayer-reduction. Not in the Qurʾān. The Qurʾān's prayer-vocabulary is independent of any negotiation-narrative. Rabbit hole: whether the prayer-bargaining is a Mosaic-tradition borrowing.
4. **The Lote-Tree of the Boundary as the limit of created knowledge.** Hadith glosses *muntahā* as the threshold beyond which only God knows. The Qurʾān gives only the name. Rabbit hole: trace whether *muntahā* is glossed elsewhere in the Qurʾān as a knowledge-boundary or only as a place-of-ending.
5. **The Burāq's iconography.** Hadith describes a creature smaller than a mule and larger than a donkey, with wings on its sides. Not Qurʾānic. Rabbit hole: cross-tradition iconography (Apocalypse of Peter, Persian / Mesopotamian winged-mounts) that may have informed the image.
6. **The Aqṣā as identified with the Temple Mount.** Hadith and tradition identify *al-Aqṣā* with Jerusalem. The Qurʾān uses a descriptor (the most-distant). Rabbit hole: how early is the Temple-Mount identification — is it in pre-ʿUmar tradition or post-ʿUmar (i.e., post-conquest)?
7. **The connection of 17:1 and 53:13-18 as one event.** The Qurʾān refuses the connection. Tradition makes it. Rabbit hole: when does the fusion first appear in the hadith corpus — is it in the earliest Sīra (Ibn Isḥāq), or earlier, or later?
8. **The *ʿabd*-pattern for Muḥammad at moments of supreme honor.** 17:1, 18:1, 53:10. Why three? Are these the three *peak* honor-moments? Cross-link: any other Qurʾānic passages where Muḥammad is *ʿabd* and not *rasūl* / *nabī*?
9. **The 81:23 / 53:7 parallel.** Both passages place a vision-figure on a horizon. Are they the same sighting or two? Pull both side-by-side; check whether the *raʾā* clauses and the horizon-words match (*al-ufuq al-mubīn* vs *al-ufuq al-aʿlā*).
10. **The body-vs-vision question and its political stakes.** The Muʿtazila / Sunni split on Isrāʾ-bodily-vs-vision is connected to broader debates about the Prophet's epistemic access to the divine. Rabbit hole: trace whether translators' minor moves (Saheeh's capitalised "His Servant"; Khattab's hadith brackets at 53:14) align with confessional positions.
11. **The *ruʾyā* of 17:60 — what is the *cursed tree* (al-shajara al-malʿūna)?** 17:60 conjoins the *ruʾyā* with *al-shajara al-malʿūna fī al-Qurʾān*. The cursed tree is hadith-identified with the Zaqqūm-tree (37:62-65; 44:43-46). The textual juxtaposition of *ruʾyā* and *cursed tree* in one verse is a knot worth its own analysis — both are framed as *fitna* for the people.
12. **The structural rhyme between Moses at Sinai (20:9-23) and Muḥammad at the Aqṣā (17:1).** Both prophets are taken to a privileged site at night (Moses sees the fire at night, 20:10; Muḥammad is taken at night, 17:1). Both are shown signs (*li-nuriyaka* / *li-nuriyahu*). Both events are credentialing. Rabbit hole: is the Qurʾān deliberately structuring Muḥammad's Night Journey as a Mosaic-typological event?
13. **The methodological hook from `teleportation-divergence.md` §"Surprise 1":** the verse readers expect to be the most contested teleportation verse (17:1) is in the consensus envelope; the actual fight is in legal verses bearing teleportation roots. This needs to be folded into any future Phase-1 sweep — narrative-passage divergence is *lower* than root-only divergence on the same vocabulary.
