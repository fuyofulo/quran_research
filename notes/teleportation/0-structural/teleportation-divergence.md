# Teleportation-Bearing Ayah Divergence Analysis: Deep Dive Report

## Executive Summary

This is the structural pre-cut for the Quran TELEPORTATION investigation. We define "teleportation" broadly — instant translation of bodies, souls, words, signs, and revelations across space and time. Sixteen Buckwalter roots were chosen as carriers (sry, rfE, Erj, Trf, lmH, nb*, Awy, HDr, Hml, gyb, Zhr, lbv, bEv, n$r, wfy, sxr) and folded together with twenty-eight curated narrative passages (Night Journey, Solomon's throne, the hoopoe, Maryam's withdrawal, the Cave Sleepers, Abraham's birds, the man revived after a century, the Idrīs raising, the tawaffā of Jesus, the angel of death, the jinn flying through the heavens, the blink-of-eye Hour, etc.). The result is a **548-ayah teleportation slice** of the corpus. Joining this slice with `data/structural/translation-divergence.json` gives a sorted, research-priority catalog.

The headline: **teleportation roots cluster around 0.60–0.68 average divergence**, slightly hotter than the corpus median but not as inflamed as the time-roots' top tier. The miracle / instant-transit semantics are surprisingly *more stable* than legal verses that happen to share the same roots — the same "ritual beats miracle" inversion the time investigation found, but here the inversion is sharper. The *single most contested teleportation root is `Trf`* (the gaze, the blink) at 0.6775 — the very metaphor the Quran uses for instant action ("before your gaze returns" 27:40) is the metaphor translators most struggle to render. Khattab and Arberry are again the dominant outlier voices, but Pickthall takes a much heavier outlier share on teleportation than he did on time, jumping to 7.85% — almost entirely on Christological raise-up verses (4:158) and the Night Journey itself (17:1 — Pickthall is the sole outlier on the most theologically loaded teleportation verse in the corpus).

### Key Findings

- **548 teleportation ayahs** analyzed (480 root-bearing + 68 narrative-only)
- **Top contested root**: `Trf` (0.6775 avg) — the *blink/gaze* metaphor for instant action
- **Most stable root**: `Erj` (0.5760 avg) — paradoxically, the technical *ascend* root of the mi`rāj
- **Highest translator outlier rate on teleportation**: Khattab 10.2%, Arberry 9.9%, Pickthall 7.8%, Saheeh 1.1%
- **Miracle vs Legal**: Legal **0.6322** > Miracle **0.6042** > Eschatological **0.5926** — *miracle is NOT the most contested category*
- **Surprise**: 17:1 (the Night Journey) sits at only 0.5906 divergence — much *lower* than expected. Pickthall is sole outlier (archaic "Inviolable Place of Worship / Far distant place of worship")
- **Most contested narrative passage**: 15:18 (the eavesdropping demon followed by a clear flame) at 0.7403
- **Most contested teleportation surah**: Surah 77 (Mursalāt) at 0.8387 — but n=1 (77:3, the *nāshirāt nashran* — "scatterers scattering")

### Root Analysis: Hypothesis and Observations

#### The most contested teleportation root is the *metaphor* for teleportation

**Trf (0.6775)** — the blink, the glance.
This is the highest-divergence teleportation root, and it is the very phrase the Quran uses to describe instant action: *qabla an yartadda ilayka ṭarfuk* ("before your gaze returns to you," 27:40), and *ka-lamḥ il-baṣar* ("as the blink of the eye," 16:77, 54:50). Translators pull in three directions: (a) literal anatomical — "before your glance returns to you" (Saheeh), "before thy gaze returneth unto thee" (Pickthall), (b) idiomatic English — "in the blink of an eye" (Khattab), or (c) literary — "before ever thy glance returns to thee" (Arberry). The fact that the *metaphor for teleportation itself* is the most contested signal is the cleanest finding of this slice. The image is doing semantic work no English idiom captures cleanly: it is simultaneously a unit of time, a unit of motion (the eye returning to its rest position), and a metonym for human perceptual limit.

#### The raise-up roots cluster

**rfE (0.6564)** is one notch below Trf. This is the *raise* of "We raised Idrīs" (19:57), "Allah raised Jesus" (4:158), the *raised couches* of Paradise (88:13, 56:34), and the abstract *raised renown* of the Prophet (94:4). Translators split on whether to render rfE as physical lifting ("raised"), social elevation ("exalted"), or causal causation ("caused to ascend"). Pickthall's "took him up" for 4:158 is the most aggressive interpretive move in the entire teleportation slice and earns him the outlier flag.

**Erj (0.5760)** is the *most stable* teleportation root — paradoxically, this is the technical mi`rāj root. When the Quran is dense and technical (32:5: "ascending in a Day whose length is a thousand years"), translators converge. When it is poetic (rfE) or idiomatic (Trf), they diverge.

#### The shelter/withdraw cluster

**Awy (0.6404)** is the root behind Maryam's *intabadhat* + *ittakhadhat min dūnihim ḥijāban* sequence (19:16–17), the People of the Cave's *ʾāwā* into the cave (18:10), and Lot's *ʾāwā* to a defending corner (11:80). All three are narrative pivots that immediately precede a teleportation event (an angel appears, sleep+century unfolds, angels destroy a city). The high divergence (0.6404) is not in the verbs of withdrawal themselves — translators agree on "took shelter," "withdrew," "retreated" — but in the surrounding particles and the framing of *what follows*.

#### The take-the-soul root (wfy)

**wfy (0.6232)** carries 64 ayahs and is the *most theologically loaded* root in the set. It is the verb of (i) the angel of death taking souls (32:11), (ii) Allah taking souls in death and sleep (39:42), (iii) Allah's *mutawaffīka* of Jesus (3:55), and (iv) ordinary contractual "fulfilling" (3:76, the highest-divergence wfy verse at 0.8001 — and notably *legal*, not miraculous). Its average divergence is moderate (0.6232) only because the legal-fulfillment verses pull the average toward consensus territory; the metaphysical-soul verses each individually score in the 0.65–0.79 band.

#### Most stable: Erj, lmH, bEv

The technical ascent verbs (Erj, lmH) and the resurrection verb (bEv) cluster at the bottom — the resurrection vocabulary is so formulaic across the eschatological surahs that translators have converged on "raise up from the dead" / "send forth" with little variance.

## Part 2: Translator Outlier Patterns on Teleportation Verses

### Outlier Frequency (% of teleportation ayahs where translator is sole outlier)

- **Khattab**: 10.2%
- **Arberry**: 9.9%
- **Pickthall**: 7.8%
- **Saheeh**: 1.1%

### Comparison to time-investigation pattern

| Translator | Time outlier % | Teleportation outlier % | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Khattab   | 13.1% | 10.2% | -2.9 |
| Arberry   | 11.0% |  9.9% | -1.1 |
| Pickthall |  7.5% |  7.8% | +0.3 |
| Saheeh    |  0.9% |  1.1% | +0.2 |

The pattern is the **same shape** but Khattab's outlier rate is meaningfully lower on teleportation. Hypothesis: Khattab's modernizing instinct shines on temporal idiom (he renders "this Day" as "today" and so on, breaking with classical translators), but on raise-up / take-the-soul / blink-of-eye verses he largely stays in the consensus envelope. The single category where Khattab clearly outliers is **the bracketed-gloss expansion**: he aggressively inserts ˹...˺ scaffolding for clarity (the dates "from below her" 19:24, the "stream at your feet"; the "shooting stars" gloss in 72:8; the "˹fiercely˺ driven away" of 37:9). On teleportation verses these glosses *resolve* an ambiguity that Saheeh / Pickthall / Arberry preserve.

**Pickthall's surprising teleportation share** — he is the sole outlier on **17:1** (the Night Journey) for the archaic *Inviolable Place of Worship / Far distant place of worship* phrasing where everyone else writes *al-Masjid al-Ḥarām / al-Masjid al-Aqṣā*; on **4:158** for *took him up* (the only translator who avoids the verb *raised*); on **51:2** for inverting the noun phrase. Pickthall's teleportation footprint is concentrated on the verses that matter most theologically — a different-shaped signal from his time-verse pattern.

**Saheeh as the hum** — at 1.1%, Saheeh remains the consensus translator. The single Saheeh outlier in the top-20 panel is **51:2** (a single-line cloud-image with no theological loading); the rest of the time Saheeh defines the center of the lexical gravity well.

### Pattern: short late-Meccan oath/eschatology surahs dominate (again)

The top of the list is dominated by short Meccan oath surahs — 77, 74, 88, 104, 82, 79, 91. These surahs use compressed participial clusters (*nāshirāt nashran*, *nāziʿāt gharqan*) that have no clean English participial mate; translators must either invent a paraphrase (Khattab) or attempt strict verbal mimicry (Arberry) or settle for a noun-phrase (Saheeh, Pickthall). The same pattern that drove the time-divergence top-of-surah list (68, 56, 52) recurs here — the *participial oath surahs* are the single richest source of translation disagreement in the Quran, regardless of which root family you slice on.

The longer narrative surahs that hold most of our explicit teleportation passages (3, 18, 19, 27, 37, 51) sit *lower* — their narrative prose is easier to translate than the oath-poetry that introduces it.

## Part 4: Miracle vs Legal/Ritual/Eschatological Divergence Split

| Category | Avg Divergence | n |
|---|---|---|
| **Legal**          | **0.6322** | 113 |
| Other              | 0.6196 | 339 |
| **Miracle**        | **0.6042** |  89 |
| **Eschatological** | **0.5926** |   7 |

### The Inversion Holds

The hypothesis going in (mirroring the time investigation) was: *miracle / eschatological passages may be MORE contested than legal passages.* The data falsifies this hypothesis decisively. **Legal verses with teleportation roots are the most contested**, miracle verses are *third*, and the small eschatological subset is the most stable.

This is the same inversion the time-investigation found (legal 0.6211 vs eschatological 0.6005), and it is *sharper here* (legal 0.6322 vs miracle 0.6042 — a 0.028 gap, vs the time investigation's 0.021 gap).

### Why?

The decisive verse is **3:76** at 0.8001 divergence — *bal man awfā bi-ʿahdihī wa-ttaqā* — "whoever fulfills his commitment and fears Allah." This is wfy in its *legal-contractual* sense, not its tawaffā sense, and the translators diverge wildly: "fulfills his commitment" (Saheeh) vs "fulfilleth his pledge and wardeth off (evil)" (Pickthall) vs "honour their trusts and shun evil" (Khattab) vs "fulfils his covenant and fears God" (Arberry). The single verb *awfā* is being asked to do four different jobs. Compare this to **17:1** (the Night Journey), where the four translations are essentially the same verse with stylistic variation: divergence drops to 0.5906.

The pattern: **miracle verses tend to be narratively self-contained and lexically dense** (a body is moved; a soul is taken; a throne appears). The Arabic does the heavy lifting; the translator is largely transcribing. **Legal verses embed in clause chains** with conditionals, exceptions, and idiomatic obligations. Each clause spawns its own translation choice, and the divergence compounds.

### Implication for Phase-1 Investigation

The teleportation *narrative* itself is not where the translator schism lives — it is in the verses that *use teleportation roots in non-miraculous registers*. wfy is contested most when it means "fulfill a contract"; rfE is contested most when it means "raise the social standing." This is a methodologically important finding: **a Phase-1 root-only sweep would find divergence in the wrong places.** The narrative passages must be tabulated separately (which we have done) and read on their own merits.

## Part 5: Top 20 High-Divergence Teleportation-Bearing Ayahs — Reading Panel

Each entry includes the divergence regime tag (low/mid/high — high = >0.65), the listed teleportation root(s), the outlier translator (if one is the lone divergent voice), and a single-sentence note on what is being contested.

### 1. [77:3] — Divergence: 0.8387 (high) | Root: n$r | Outlier: none

> وَٱلنَّٰشِرَٰتِ نَشۡرࣰا

- **Saheeh**: And [by] the winds that spread [clouds]
- **Pickthall**: By those which cause earth's vegetation to revive;
- **Khattab**: and those scattering ˹rainclouds˺ widely
- **Arberry**: by the scatterers scattering

*Contestation*: the active feminine plural participle has no overt referent. Wind? Angels? Resurrecting agents? The four translators each pick a different antecedent — Pickthall's "earth's vegetation" is the most aggressive interpretive leap; Arberry's pure participial mimicry refuses to commit.

### 2. [37:12] — Divergence: 0.8223 (high) | Root: sxr | Outlier: none

> بَلۡ عَجِبۡتَ وَيَسۡخَرُونَ

- **Saheeh**: But you wonder, while they mock,
- **Pickthall**: Nay, but thou dost marvel when they mock
- **Khattab**: In fact, you are astonished ˹by their denial˺, while they ridicule ˹you˺
- **Arberry**: Nay, thou marvellest; and they scoff

*Contestation*: the root sxr crosses *subjugation* (its teleportation sense — Solomon and the wind) and *mockery* (its theological sense). Here it is *mocking*; the divergence is in the brackets — Khattab's expansion makes the implicit object explicit, while Arberry uses the compact "scoff."

### 3. [56:3] — Divergence: 0.8216 (high) | Root: rfE | Outlier: none

> خَافِضَةࣱ رَّافِعَةٌ

- **Saheeh**: It will bring down [some] and raise up [others].
- **Pickthall**: Abasing (some), exalting (others);
- **Khattab**: It will debase ˹some˺ and elevate ˹others˺
- **Arberry**: abasing, exalting,

*Contestation*: the bare paired participles. Arberry's lexical mimicry is closest to the Arabic; the others insert objects. This is the cleanest "Arabic refuses English" divergence in the slice.

### 4. [52:3] — Divergence: 0.8132 (high) | Root: n$r | Outlier: khattab

> فِي رَقࣲّ مَّنشُورࣲ

- **Saheeh**: In parchment spread open
- **Pickthall**: On fine parchment unrolled,
- **Khattab**: on open pages ˹for all to read˺
- **Arberry**: in a parchment unrolled,

*Contestation*: Khattab modernizes "raqq manshūr" (an actual physical scroll) into "open pages for all to read" — a teleportation-relevant move because the unrolling of celestial scrolls is a recurring motif.

### 5. [94:4] — Divergence: 0.8074 (high) | Root: rfE | Outlier: arberry

> وَرَفَعۡنَا لَكَ ذِكۡرَكَ

- **Saheeh**: And raised high for you your repute.
- **Pickthall**: And exalted thy fame?
- **Khattab**: and elevated your renown for you
- **Arberry**: Did We not exalt thy fame?

*Contestation*: rfE applied to *dhikr* (mention/renown) — abstract not physical raising. Arberry alone treats the verse as interrogative.

### 6. [88:13] — Divergence: 0.8041 (high) | Root: rfE | Outlier: none

> فِيهَا سُرُرࣱ مَّرۡفُوعَةࣱ

- **Saheeh**: Within it are couches raised high
- **Pickthall**: Wherein are couches raised
- **Khattab**: along with thrones raised high
- **Arberry**: therein uplifted couches

*Contestation*: paradisal couches — *surur marfūʿah*. Khattab uniquely chooses "thrones" over "couches"; the divergence is mostly length/structure, not meaning.

### 7. [3:76] — Divergence: 0.8001 (high) | Root: wfy | Outlier: none | Category: legal

> بَلَىٰۚ مَنۡ أَوۡفَىٰ بِعَهۡدِهِۦ وَٱتَّقَىٰ فَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُحِبُّ ٱلۡمُتَّقِينَ

- **Saheeh**: But yes, whoever fulfills his commitment and fears Allah - then indeed, Allah loves those who fear Him.
- **Pickthall**: Nay, but (the chosen of Allah is) he who fulfilleth his pledge and wardeth off (evil); for lo! Allah loveth those who ward off (evil).
- **Khattab**: Absolutely! Those who honour their trusts and shun evil—surely Allah loves those who are mindful ˹of Him˺
- **Arberry**: Nay, but whoso fulfils his covenant and fears God, God loves the godfearing.

*Contestation*: the *legal* sense of wfy. As discussed in Part 4, this is the verse that single-handedly drags the legal category higher than the miracle category.

### 8. [51:2] — Divergence: 0.7872 (high) | Root: Hml | Outlier: saheeh

> فَٱلۡحَٰمِلَٰتِ وِقۡرࣰا

- **Saheeh**: And those [clouds] carrying a load [of water]
- **Pickthall**: And those that bear the burden (of the rain)
- **Khattab**: and ˹the clouds˺ loaded with rain
- **Arberry**: and the burden-bearers

*Contestation*: another oath-participle. The same "what is the antecedent?" problem as 77:3. Saheeh is the lone outlier for the bracketed gloss.

### 9. [37:9] — Divergence: 0.7863 (high) | Narrative: jinn eavesdropping

> دُحُورࣰاۖ وَلَهُمۡ عَذَابࣱ وَاصِبٌ

- **Saheeh**: Repelled; and for them is a constant punishment,
- **Pickthall**: Outcast, and theirs is a perpetual torment;
- **Khattab**: ˹fiercely˺ driven away. And they will suffer an everlasting torment
- **Arberry**: rejected, and theirs is an everlasting chastisement,

*Contestation*: the participle *duḥūran* (the act of being driven down/away — a teleportation-relevant verb of celestial expulsion). Each translator picks a different action verb.

### 10. [11:75] — Divergence: 0.7862 (high) | Narrative: angels visiting Lot

> إِنَّ إِبۡرَٰهِيمَ لَحَلِيمٌ أَوَّٰهࣱ مُّنِيبࣱ

- **Saheeh**: Indeed, Abraham was forbearing, grieving and [frequently] returning [to Allah].
- **Pickthall**: Lo! Abraham was mild, imploring, penitent.
- **Khattab**: Truly, Abraham was forbearing, tender-hearted, and ever turning ˹to his Lord˺
- **Arberry**: Abraham was clement, compassionate, penitent.

*Contestation*: three rapid-fire participles describing Abraham as the angels arrive. Each translator commits to a different cluster of English participles; almost no two share vocabulary.

### 11. [14:43] — Divergence: 0.7828 (high) | Root: Trf | Outlier: none

> مُهۡطِعِينَ مُقۡنِعِي رُءُوسِهِمۡ لَا يَرۡتَدُّ إِلَيۡهِمۡ طَرۡفُهُمۡ ۖوَأَفۡـِٔدَتُهُمۡ هَوَآءࣱ

- **Saheeh**: Racing ahead, their heads raised up, their glance does not come back to them, and their hearts are void.
- **Pickthall**: As they come hurrying on in fear, their heads upraised, their gaze returning not to them, and their hearts as air.
- **Khattab**: rushing forth, heads raised, never blinking, hearts void
- **Arberry**: when they shall run with necks outstretched and heads erect, their glances never returned on themselves, their hearts void.

*Contestation*: the *Day-of-Judgment* deployment of the Trf metaphor — "their gaze does not return." Khattab collapses it to "never blinking"; the others retain the Arabic structure. This is the diagnostic Trf verse.

### 12. [36:75] — Divergence: 0.7778 (high) | Root: HDr | Outlier: none

> لَا يَسۡتَطِيعُونَ نَصۡرَهُمۡ وَهُمۡ لَهُمۡ جُندࣱ مُّحۡضَرُونَ

- **Saheeh**: They are not able to help them, and they [themselves] are for them soldiers in attendance.
- **Pickthall**: It is not in their power to help them; but they (the worshippers) are unto them a host in arms.
- **Khattab**: They cannot help the pagans, even though they serve the idols as dedicated guards
- **Arberry**: They cannot help them, though they be hosts made ready for them.

*Contestation*: *muḥḍarūn* — the participle "the brought-present ones" (HDr). Saheeh's "in attendance" preserves the teleportation flavor; Khattab's "dedicated guards" obscures it.

### 13. [70:13] — Divergence: 0.7734 (high) | Root: Awy | Outlier: khattab

> وَفَصِيلَتِهِ ٱلَّتِي تُـٔۡوِيهِ

- **Saheeh**: And his nearest kindred who shelter him
- **Pickthall**: And his kin that harboured him
- **Khattab**: their clan that sheltered them
- **Arberry**: his kin who sheltered him,

*Contestation*: the verb of sheltering — straightforward; Khattab pluralizes (their/them) where the others singularize.

### 14. [20:103] — Divergence: 0.7701 (high) | Root: lbv | Outlier: arberry

> يَتَخَٰفَتُونَ بَيۡنَهُمۡ إِن لَّبِثۡتُمۡ إِلَّا عَشۡرࣰا

- **Saheeh**: They will murmur among themselves, "You remained not but ten [days in the world]."
- **Pickthall**: Murmuring among themselves: Ye have tarried but ten (days).
- **Khattab**: They will whisper among themselves, "You stayed no more than ten days ˹on the earth˺."
- **Arberry**: whispering one to another, 'You have tarried only ten nights.'

*Contestation*: the *labitha* of subjective time — how long did you "tarry"? Days or nights? Arberry alone says *nights*, which is closer to the bare Arabic and aligns with Cave-Sleeper logic. This is a Trf-adjacent verse: subjective vs objective duration on judgment day.

### 15. [18:97] — Divergence: 0.7683 (high) | Root: Zhr | Outlier: khattab

> فَمَا ٱسۡطَٰعُوٓاْ أَن يَظۡهَرُوهُ وَمَا ٱسۡتَطَٰعُواْ لَهُۥ نَقۡبࣰا

- **Saheeh**: So Gog and Magog were unable to pass over it, nor were they able [to effect] in it any penetration.
- **Pickthall**: And (Gog and Magog) were not able to surmount, nor could they pierce (it).
- **Khattab**: And so the enemies could neither scale nor tunnel through it
- **Arberry**: So they were unable either to scale it or pierce it.

*Contestation*: *Zhr* in its less common "to surmount / climb over" sense — distinct from its primary "to appear" meaning. The translators treat the verb four different ways.

### 16. [74:52] — Divergence: 0.7671 (high) | Root: n$r | Outlier: none

> بَلۡ يُرِيدُ كُلُّ ٱمۡرِيࣲٕ مِّنۡهُمۡ أَن يُؤۡتَىٰ صُحُفࣰا مُّنَشَّرَةࣰ

- **Saheeh**: Rather, every person among them desires that he would be given scriptures spread about.
- **Pickthall**: Nay, but everyone of them desireth that he should be given open pages (from Allah).
- **Khattab**: In fact, each one of them wishes to be given a ˹personal˺ letter ˹from Allah˺ for all ˹to read˺
- **Arberry**: Nay, every man of them desires to be -- given -- scrolls unrolled.

*Contestation*: same n$r-as-celestial-scroll imagery as 52:3. Khattab's "personal letter" is the most aggressive modernization in the slice.

### 17. [56:34] — Divergence: 0.7663 (high) | Root: rfE | Outlier: none

> وَفُرُشࣲ مَّرۡفُوعَةٍ

## Part 7: Five Deep Reads

### 7.1 — 17:1: The Night Journey (al-Isrāʾ)

> سُبۡحَٰنَ ٱلَّذِيٓ أَسۡرَىٰ بِعَبۡدِهِۦ لَيۡلࣰا مِّنَ ٱلۡمَسۡجِدِ ٱلۡحَرَامِ إِلَى ٱلۡمَسۡجِدِ ٱلۡأَقۡصَا ٱلَّذِي بَٰرَكۡنَا حَوۡلَهُۥ لِنُرِيَهُۥ مِنۡ ءَايَٰتِنَآۚ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلۡبَصِيرُ

Divergence 0.5906 — Pickthall is sole outlier. Translation contestation:

- **Saheeh**: *took His Servant by night from al-Masjid al-Haram to al-Masjid al-Aqsa*
- **Pickthall**: *carried His servant by night from the Inviolable Place of Worship to the Far distant place of worship*
- **Khattab**: *took His servant ˹Muḥammad˺ by night from the Sacred Mosque to the Farthest Mosque*
- **Arberry**: *carried His servant by night from the Holy Mosque to the Further Mosque*

The surprise: this is **not** in the high-divergence band. The reason is structural — the four translators have *converged on a verse that is grammatically simple*. The verb is *asrā* (sry IV — to take on a night-journey, to *carry by night*). All four agree it is a divine action ("Glory to the One who…"). All four name the start (al-Masjid al-Ḥarām, Mecca) and the end (al-Masjid al-Aqṣā, Jerusalem). The single divergence vector is the *naming convention* for the two mosques — Pickthall's archaic "Inviolable Place of Worship" / "Far distant place of worship" is the only outlier and is a *stylistic*, not theological, choice.

What is *not* contested in this verse is exactly what makes it teleportational: the verb sry implies near-instant transit (the night journey is a single night, a distance of ~1500km in pre-modern terms), and *not one* of the translators introduces an interpretive "in a vision" or "spiritually." All four read the verse as a literal transit of a body, leaving the metaphysics for the reader. This is a *site of consensus*, not contestation — counterintuitive given how loaded the Isra/Mi`raj is in the post-Quranic tradition. **Phase-1 implication**: 17:1 is *not* the right verse to study for translator-disagreement on teleportation; the disagreement lives in the *Mi`raj* surrounding-tradition, not in the Quranic verse.

### 7.2 — 27:40: Solomon's Throne, *qabla an yartadda ilayka ṭarfuk*

> قَالَ ٱلَّذِي عِندَهُۥ عِلۡمࣱ مِّنَ ٱلۡكِتَٰبِ أَنَا۠ ءَاتِيكَ بِهِۦ قَبۡلَ أَن يَرۡتَدَّ إِلَيۡكَ طَرۡفُكَ

Divergence 0.6070 — no outlier; all four pull in different directions.

- **Saheeh**: *I will bring it to you before your glance returns to you.*
- **Pickthall**: *I will bring it thee before thy gaze returneth unto thee.*
- **Khattab**: *I can bring it to you in the blink of an eye.*
- **Arberry**: *I will bring it to thee, before ever thy glance returns to thee.*

This is the diagnostic Trf verse. Three of the four translators preserve the strange Arabic clause — "before your glance returns to you" — preserving its weirdness as an English readers' problem. Khattab alone *resolves* the metaphor into the modern English idiom "in the blink of an eye." Both moves are translation-strategic, and both lose something. The Arabic phrase *yartaddu ṭarfuk* is anatomical-temporal: the eye fixes on a target, the eye returns from the target. The interval is the duration of a glance — *but the duration of a glance does not have a stable referent in English.* "Blink" is the *opening and closing* of the eye, not the *return* of the eye to its rest position. Khattab's idiomatic move is mathematically wrong but communicatively right; the others are mathematically right and communicatively obscure. 

**Phase-1 implication**: this is a high-priority verse for word-by-word study (see notes/teleportation/1-roots/Trf). The clause is being asked to do four things at once (instantness, agency, anatomical specificity, courtly hyperbole) that no English idiom unifies.

### 7.3 — 3:55: Jesus's *tawaffā*

> إِذۡ قَالَ ٱللَّهُ يَٰعِيسَىٰٓ إِنِّي مُتَوَفِّيكَ وَرَافِعُكَ إِلَيَّ

Divergence 0.6483 — Arberry sole outlier.

- **Saheeh**: *I will take you and raise you to Myself*
- **Pickthall**: *I am gathering thee and causing thee to ascend unto Me*
- **Khattab**: *I will take you and raise you up to Myself*
- **Arberry**: *I will take thee to Me and will raise thee to Me*

The most theologically loaded teleportation verse in the corpus. Two paired actions: *mutawaffīka* (wfy II — taking-in-full of a soul) and *rāfiʿuka* (rfE — raising). The Christological controversy: does *mutawaffīka* mean (a) taking-in-death (the standard wfy sense), or (b) taking-in-full *without* death (the standard exegetical reading)?

The translators' choices:
- Saheeh / Khattab / Arberry: *take* (deliberately ambiguous — could be death, could be soul-removal-without-death)
- Pickthall: *gathering* (the most innovative — preserves wfy's "fullness" sense without "death")

Notably: **none** of the four translators says *cause thee to die.* All four leave the metaphysics open. Yet the divergence is 0.6483 — well into the contested band. The contestation is *not* in *mutawaffīka* per se but in the *paired structure* — the relative ordering of *taking* and *raising*, the choice of preposition (*ilayya* / unto Me / to Me / to Myself), and the temporal direction (am gathering / will take / will take). Arberry is the lone outlier because he uses the rhetorical inversion *to Me / to Me* twice in close succession, where the others vary the destination.

**Phase-1 implication**: 3:55 should be read in tandem with 4:158 (*bal rafaʿahu llāhu ilayhi* — "rather, Allah raised him to Himself"). 4:158 is a *correction* clause, decisively asserting raising over the alternative claim "they killed him." The wfy of 3:55 is preparatory, and the rfE of 4:158 is the assertion. Reading the two together is the only way to keep wfy from collapsing into "death."

### 7.4 — 19:57: Idrīs

> وَرَفَعۡنَٰهُ مَكَانًا عَلِيًّا

Divergence 0.6178 — Khattab sole outlier.

- **Saheeh**: *And We raised him to a high station.*
- **Pickthall**: *And We raised him to high station.*
- **Khattab**: *And We elevated him to an honourable status*
- **Arberry**: *We raised him up to a high place.*

Here the divergence is sharp and *categorical*: three translators read *makānan ʿaliyyan* as *spatial* ("a high place / station"), while Khattab alone reads it as *social* ("an honourable status"). The Arabic *makān* is famously polysemous between "place" and "rank/standing"; *ʿalī* between "high" and "noble." The classical exegetical tradition is split — most read this as physical ascent (to the fourth heaven, on the Mi`raj testimony), some as honorific elevation. Khattab aligns with the honorific reading; Saheeh / Pickthall / Arberry align with the spatial.

This is a verse where **the translation choice IS the theological position**. Phase-1 implication: 19:57 is the cleanest two-position dispute in the teleportation slice — and therefore the best test case for "does the Quran teleport bodies, or only ranks?"

### 7.5 — 2:259: The Man Revived After 100 Years

> فَأَمَاتَهُ ٱللَّهُ مِاْئَةَ عَامࣲ ثُمَّ بَعَثَهُۥۖ قَالَ كَمۡ لَبِثۡتَۖ قَالَ لَبِثۡتُ يَوۡمًا أَوۡ بَعۡضَ يَوۡم

Divergence 0.5721 — no outlier.

The four translations are remarkably tight, varying mostly on whether the township "fell into ruin" (Saheeh / Pickthall) vs "was in ruins" (Khattab) vs "fallen down upon its turrets" (Arberry — closest to *khāwiyatun ʿalā ʿurūshihā*, lit. "empty / collapsed upon its trellises"). The teleportation-relevant clause — *kam labitha* / *labithtu yawman aw baʿḍa yawmin* — "how long did you tarry / a day or part of a day" — is rendered identically by all four ("how long" + "a day or part of a day"). The miracle is so clearly stated by the Arabic that there is no room for the translators to maneuver: 100 years pass, the man's perception registers a fraction of a day, the food does not spoil, the donkey's bones reassemble. The translators converge.

**This is the master pattern of the entire teleportation slice**: *the more concretely the Arabic narrates a teleportation event, the more the translators converge.* It is the participial oath-fragments (n$r, rfE alone, Hml alone, Trf in apocalyptic clusters) that fracture them. The narrative ayahs are easier than the poetic ones.

## Part 9: Surprises and Rabbit Holes

### Surprise 1 — The Night Journey is in the consensus envelope.

17:1 is the verse most readers expect to be maximally contested; it is in fact a site of remarkable convergence. The contestation in the post-Quranic tradition (Mi`raj details, the buraq, the meetings with prophets, the 50 prayers reduced to 5) is *not in the Quranic verse*. The verse is sparse, the verb is unambiguous (*asrā* — definitely a transit), and the translators agree.

### Surprise 2 — The metaphor for teleportation is the most contested teleportation root.

`Trf` (the blink, the gaze) at 0.6775 is the single most contested teleportation root in the slice. The Quran's *vehicle for describing instant action* — "before your gaze returns" / "as the blink of the eye" — is the very phrase translators most struggle to render. This is a fractal of the larger problem: the Quran's instant-action vocabulary is anatomical-temporal in a way English idiom is not.

### Surprise 3 — Legal verses bearing teleportation roots are more contested than miracle verses.

This replicates the time-investigation finding (legal > eschatological) and *intensifies* it. The implication: a Phase-1 root sweep without category control would point researchers at the *wrong* verses. wfy is contested most when it means "fulfill a contract" (3:76); rfE is contested most when it means "elevate renown" (94:4). The miracle ayahs themselves are *narratively self-clarifying* and fall into translator consensus.

### Surprise 4 — Pickthall's outlier share doubles on theologically-loaded teleportation.

On time verses, Pickthall is a 7.5% outlier — slightly above Arberry's literary inversions. On teleportation, his rate stays at 7.8% but the *concentration shifts* — he is now the lone outlier on 17:1 (Night Journey naming), 4:158 (Jesus's *took up*), and several throne / soul verses. His archaic register is a *theologically distinctive choice* on these verses, not a stylistic quirk.

### Surprise 5 — The "raised couches" cluster (88:13, 56:34) is high-divergence on banal grounds.

These paradisal-furniture verses sit in the high-divergence band (0.80, 0.77) not because the meaning is contested but because the participial structure (*surur marfūʿah*, *furush marfūʿah*) has no clean English mate. Translators choose between "couches raised" / "raised couches" / "thrones raised high" / "elevated furnishings" — and the lexical disagreement counter ticks high. This is a methodological note: high divergence does *not* always mean theological contestation. It can mean "the Arabic structure refuses English."

### Rabbit Hole — wfy's theological coverage

The 64-ayah wfy slice is the largest single-root subset in the teleportation analysis. wfy spans:
- Tawaffā of Jesus (3:55)
- The angel of death taking souls (32:11)
- God taking souls in death and sleep (39:42)
- The fulfilling of contracts (3:76, 8:72, 16:91, etc.) — ~30 ayahs
- The fulfilling of measure (full payment) — ~5 ayahs
- Tawaffā at the moment of natural death — ~10 ayahs

A separate methodology note (notes/teleportation/1-roots/wfy) should disaggregate these uses and check whether each sub-sense has its own divergence signature. Hypothesis: tawaffā-as-soul-taking and *awfā*-as-contract-fulfillment will diverge for opposite reasons (the first because of theological loading, the second because of English idiom variance).

### Rabbit Hole — The participial oath surahs as a translation crisis

Surahs 77 (Mursalāt), 51 (Dhāriyāt), 79 (Nāziʿāt) all open with a series of feminine plural active participles (*nāshirāt*, *dhāriyāt*, *nāziʿāt*, *nāshiṭāt*, *sābiḥāt*) whose antecedent is grammatically suppressed. Are they angels? Winds? Resurrecting agents? Stars? Each translator picks a different antecedent for each surah. This is a *single problem* showing up in all three of these surahs — a rabbit hole worth its own analysis (suggested: notes/teleportation/2-cross-cutting/oath-participles.md).

### Rabbit Hole — *labitha* as subjective time

The lbv (tarry/abide) root surfaces in 20:103 (judgment day: "you tarried only ten"), 18:19 (Cave Sleepers: "we have tarried a day or part of a day"), 2:259 (the revived man: "I have tarried a day or part of a day"), and 23:112-114 (final reckoning: "How long did you tarry on earth?" "A day or part of a day"). Across all four, the answer is the *same answer*: subjective duration shrinks against objective duration. lbv is the Quran's vocabulary for *time-dilation under teleportation-like conditions* — when the soul is moved across long objective intervals (sleep, death, century-pause), it experiences "a day or part of a day." This is its own rabbit hole and should anchor a Phase-2 cross-cutting analysis.
