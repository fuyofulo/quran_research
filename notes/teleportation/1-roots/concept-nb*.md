# Concept: nb* — withdraw, cast, fling

**Headline.** The root نبذ governs ejective motion: throwing something away from oneself, casting a thing into a place, withdrawing oneself from a society. It has two faces. (1) Twelve out of twelve verbal occurrences are active or passive forms of throwing/casting/discarding — covenants flung behind backs, Pharaoh's host hurled into the sea, Jonah cast onto the bare shore, the slanderer cast into the Crusher. (2) The reflexive eighth-form `intabadha` (ٱنتَبَذَ) appears just twice — both for Maryam in surah 19 — meaning "she cast HERSELF away," i.e. withdrew. The same root that hurls Pharaoh into the sea (28:40, 51:40) is the root that hides Maryam to give birth (19:22). Ejection from one place is the structural unit; whether the agent is God, a covenant-breaker, or a withdrawing virgin only changes who is throwing whom.

## Stats
- 12 occurrences across 12 ayahs in 10 surahs.
- Pure verbal root: every occurrence is a verb (V=12). No nominal forms in the Quran.
- 2 lemmas: `naba*a` (form I, 10×, transitive throw) and `{ntaba*ato` (form VIII, 2×, reflexive withdraw — both Maryam).
- Distribution: 4 Medinan, 8 Meccan. Surah concentration: Al-Baqara 2 (covenant breach), Maryam 19 (the only reflexives), then a long Meccan tail in 3, 8, 20, 28, 37, 51, 68, 104.

## Lemma table

| Lemma | Form (Buckwalter) | Stem | Count | Semantic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| naba*a | نَبَذَ | I, transitive | 10 | someone throws X away |
| {ntaba*ato | ٱنتَبَذَتْ | VIII, reflexive (mutāwiʿ) | 2 | someone casts herself away → withdraws |

The form-VIII reflexive is morphologically the SAME action turned inward: instead of throwing a treaty behind one's back, one throws oneself into the wilderness. The Quran reserves this reflexive entirely for Maryam (19:16, 19:22). Maryam is the only person in the Quran who self-ejects.

## Distribution: who/what gets thrown

Two structural buckets emerge from the 10 transitive verbs.

**Bucket A — covenants/scripture flung behind backs** (3 ayahs, all Medinan, all about Banū Isrāʾīl):
- 2:100 `nabaḏahu farīqun minhum` — every time they made a covenant, a faction flung it.
- 2:101 `nabaḏa farīqun … kitāba llāhi warāʾa ẓuhūrihim` — they flung the Book behind their backs.
- 3:187 `fa-nabaḏūhu warāʾa ẓuhūrihim` — same formula again. Plus 8:58 `fa-nbiḏ ilayhim ʿalā sawāʾ` — throw the treaty back at them, openly.

The "behind their backs" formula is fixed: the act of `nb*` happens to a written/spoken commitment, with a directional adverbial phrase locating where the thrown object lands.

**Bucket B — bodies thrown into a place** (5 ayahs, all Meccan):
- 28:40 / 51:40 — Pharaoh and his hosts: `fa-nabaḏnāhum fī l-yamm` — "We flung them into the sea." Active divine verb, the sea as receptacle.
- 37:145 / 68:49 — Yūnus: `fa-nabaḏnāhu bi-l-ʿarāʾ wa-huwa saqīm` — "We cast him onto the open shore." Same verb, opposite valence: not punitive drowning but rescue-by-jettison.
- 20:96 — the Sāmirī: `fa-qabaḍtu qabḍatan min aṯari l-rasūli fa-nabaḏtuhā` — "I took a fistful from the messenger's track and flung it" (into the calf-mould). Here `nb*` is the technical verb of the magical-causation gesture.
- 104:4 — the slanderer: `la-yunbaḏanna fī l-ḥuṭamah` — "he will surely be flung into the Crusher." Same syntax as Pharaoh's hosts.

**Bucket C — Maryam (the reflexives, both 19):**
- 19:16 `i*i ntabadhat min ahlihā makānan šarqiyyan` — "when she withdrew from her family to an eastern place."
- 19:22 `fa-ḥamalathu fa-ntabadhat bihi makānan qaṣiyyan` — "so she conceived him and withdrew with him to a far place."

## Key formula: `intabadha + min/bi + makān + adjective`

The two reflexive uses are identical in structure: withdraw + with(him) / from(family) + a place + qualifier. Note the qualifier shift between 19:16 and 19:22: BEFORE the conception the place is `šarqiyy` (eastern, oriented); AFTER the conception it is `qaṣiyy` (far, distant, removed). The qualifier moves from cardinal-direction to remoteness — the second withdrawal is greater than the first. Maryam withdraws TWICE in the same surah, and the second withdrawal is a magnification of the first. The teleportation-relevant move is the second one: she carries the conceived child INTO the remote place, and the next ayah (19:23) opens with her already at the trunk of a palm-tree, alone, in a place the surah never names. The narrative skips the journey.

This is the negative-space teleportation pattern. The verb `intabadha` performs the "and now she is somewhere else" work that surahs about Solomon and Sulaymān-class miracles do with explicit speed-language. There is no "she walked," no "she traveled," no `sāra` (s-y-r) in the passage. She withdraws and she is far.

## Semantic field — co-occurring roots

The richest co-occurrence pattern (at the ayah level): `ktb` (3 ayahs — covenant/scripture as throwable object), `Ax*` (3 — taking-and-then-throwing pair), `Alh` (3), `kwn` (3). Notable specific co-occurrences:
- 19:22 pairs `nb*` with **`Hml`** (carrying) and `qSw` (remoteness). The Maryam-withdrawal verse is the ONLY ayah where `nb*` co-occurs with `Hml`. The two roots of the situational-mobility cluster collide here on a single ayah, on the same actor, on the same direct object: she carries-him, she withdraws-with-him.
- 19:16 pairs `nb*` with `$rq` (east) and `Ahl` (family) — withdrawal AS departure-from-kin.
- 28:40 / 51:40 pair with `ymm` (sea) and `jnd` (hosts) — the Pharaoh formula.
- 37:145 / 68:49 pair with `Ery` (bareness, the open shore) and `sqm`/`*mm` (illness/condemnation) — the Jonah formula.

## Translation divergence on the Maryam reflexives

The translation-divergence layer scores 19:22 as comparatively LOW divergence (combined=0.272, jaccard=0.819) and 19:16 noticeably HIGHER (combined=0.556, jaccard=0.559). All four translators converge on "withdrew" for 19:22 — Saheeh, Khattab, Arberry, Pickthall all use "withdrew with him." Divergence appears in 19:16 around what kind of "place" Maryam withdraws TO: Saheeh "a place toward the east," Khattab "a place in the east," Arberry "an eastern place," Pickthall idiosyncratically "a chamber looking East" — Pickthall alone reads `makānan šarqiyyan` as an architectural interior. He is the divergence outlier on 19:16. The Arabic supplies no architectural cue; only `makān` (place) plus an adjective. Pickthall is interpreting; the others are gloss-adjacent.

For the transitive uses, the divergence is more lexical: `nabaḏa`/`nabaḏūhu` becomes "threw away" (Saheeh), "set aside / fling" (Pickthall), "cast aside" (Khattab), "reject" (Arberry). Arberry's "reject" is the most interpretive — it loses the physical-throwing valence the verb carries. Pickthall's "fling" most preserves the violence.

The 8:58 imperative `fa-nbiḏ ilayhim` is divergent in pragmatic register: Saheeh "throw [their treaty] back," Pickthall "throw back to them … fairly," Khattab "openly terminating your treaty," Arberry "dissolve it with them equally." Khattab abandons the throwing image entirely. The Arabic literalism is a physical gesture — toss the treaty back across the line `ʿalā sawāʾ` (on equal terms, openly).

## Negative space — what `nb*` does NOT mean

It is never used for ordinary travel (no `nb*` for caravans, journeys, migration). It is never used for divine descent of revelation (that is `nzl`). It is never used for the angels' carrying motion (that is `Hml`). It is never used for refuge-taking (that is `Awy`). The verb's scope is sharp: VIOLENT/DECISIVE EJECTION of a thing from one location to another. When applied reflexively, that violence becomes a self-removal — and only Maryam performs it.

## Verses for direct study

- 19:16 + 19:22 — the only reflexives. Read together; treat 19:22 as an intensification of 19:16. Track the qualifier shift `šarqiyy → qaṣiyy`.
- 28:40 (Qasas) + 51:40 (Dhāriyāt) — Pharaoh's drowning, IDENTICAL verb-construction `fa-nabaḏnāhum fī l-yamm`. Two surahs use the same formula across Meccan eras; this is a fixed Quranic phrasal unit.
- 37:145 + 68:49 — Yūnus on the bare shore, also a paired/repeated formula `nabaḏnāhu bi-l-ʿarāʾ`.
- 104:4 — eschatological flinging (`la-yunbaḏanna fī l-ḥuṭamah`). Same verb that flings Pharaoh into the sea flings the slanderer into the fire.
- 20:96 — the Sāmirī's gesture, the only `nb*` performed by a human onto a small object for magical effect.

## Rabbit holes

1. **Who in the Quran self-withdraws?** The form-VIII reflexive is unique to Maryam (2× in surah 19). Compare to other "withdraw-by-going-aside" verbs: `Etl` (`iʿtazala` — used in 18:16 of the Cave Sleepers: "when you have withdrawn from them") and `Etzl` more generally. The Quran has at least two distinct "withdrawal" lexemes — `intabadha` (Maryam) and `iʿtazala` (Cave Sleepers, also Ibrāhīm in 19:48). They are not interchangeable; track the distribution.

2. **The Pharaoh-drowning twin** (28:40 / 51:40). Why is the SAME verbal formula reused across two surahs? Worth checking the surah signatures and ayah-echo data — does the corpus treat these two ayahs as a paired echo?

3. **Yūnus's `ʿarāʾ` and Maryam's `qaṣiyy`.** Both are bare/remote places that receive a thrown body. Is the receptacle-of-the-cast a recurring spatial type? Compare with the sea (28:40/51:40), the Crusher (104:4), and the calf-mould (20:96). Five different "throw-into" receptacles; Maryam's is the only one she walks into voluntarily.

4. **Sāmirī's gesture (20:96) as inverse-Maryam.** The Sāmirī takes a fistful of dust from the messenger's track and FLINGS it INTO the calf, animating it. Maryam withdraws, conceives, returns carrying. Both involve `nb*` + a generative supernatural event. The Sāmirī uses the transitive throw to make life from dust; Maryam uses the reflexive throw to gestate life from breath. Worth a paired study with the Hml v.27 (`taḥmiluhu`) and 20:96 verses.
