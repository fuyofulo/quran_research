# Erj (عرج) — "to ascend, climb, mount"

> The Quran's narrow-bore vertical-ascent vocabulary. Where rfE is "to raise" (often by an external agent), Erj is "to climb" (the agent moves itself upward). Nine occurrences only — but the root names a whole surah (Surah 70, *al-Maʿārij*, "The Ascending Stairways") and is central to the post-Quranic *miʿrāj* tradition. Erj is the verb of angels and the Spirit ascending to God; it is also the verb of crippled gait (`aʿraj` = "the lame," from limping/uneven movement).

## Stats

- **Occurrences:** 9
- **Unique lemmas:** 3 (`yaʿruju` V×5; `aʿraj` N×2; `maʿārij` N×2)
- **POS:** V 5 / N 4
- **Surahs:** 8 — distribution 6 Meccan, 3 Medinan
- **Top co-occurring roots:** `smw` ("heaven," 4 ayahs), `kwn` ("be"), `Alh` ("Allah"), `ArD` ("earth"), `ywm` ("day"), `Alf` ("thousand"), `nzl` ("descend"), `xrj` ("emerge"), `wlj` ("penetrate")

The collocation `Erj` + `nzl` + `wlj` + `xrj` (climb up + come down + penetrate in + come out) appears compactly in **34:2** and **57:4**. These two ayahs lay out a full **four-direction cosmology**: out of the earth, into the earth, down from the sky, up into the sky. Erj is one cardinal direction in this four-axis grid.

## Lemma table

| Lemma | Buckwalter | Arabic | POS | Count | Function |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Imperfect verb | `yaEoruju` | يَعْرُجُ | V | 5 | "ascends, mounts up" |

The two `aʿraj` occurrences (24:61, 48:17) are a different semantic node: not vertical ascent but **uneven-gait disability**. The connective tissue between "ascend" and "lame" is the underlying notion of *raised step* — climbing involves a raising of one foot above the other; lameness is when that step is uneven. Both senses preserve the asymmetric-elevation core. We treat the "lame" sense as a parallel branch; the ascent senses are the focus here.

## The two `maʿārij` (the ascending paths)

- **70:3** — `min Allāhi dhī al-maʿārij` — "from Allah, owner of the ways of ascent." A divine epithet — God as Lord-of-the-stairways. The surah is named after this verse.
- **43:33** — `wa maʿārija ʿalayhā yaẓharūn` — "and stairways upon which to mount" — silver staircases that, the verse says, God *would have given* to disbelievers in their houses if not for the risk that humanity would all turn to disbelief. **A counterfactual.** Worldly silver stairways are denied, but the cosmic *maʿārij* (70:3) are real.

This is a sharp inversion: physical *maʿārij* (43:33) are explicitly **withheld** as worldly luxury; cosmological *maʿārij* (70:3) are God's possession. The Quran reserves the ascent-architecture for the heaven-earth cosmos.

## The 50,000-year day — 70:3-4

`مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ ذِي ٱلۡمَعَارِجِ` / `تَعۡرُجُ ٱلۡمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ وَٱلرُّوحُ إِلَيۡهِ فِي يَوۡمࣲ كَانَ مِقۡدَارُهُۥ خَمۡسِينَ أَلۡفَ سَنَةࣲ`

> "From Allah, owner of the ways of ascent: the angels and the Spirit ascend to Him in a Day whose extent is fifty thousand years."

Translation convergence: all four translators agree on the structure ("ascend to Him in a Day of fifty thousand years"). Khattab adds an interpretive bracket: "˹through which˺" — making the maʿārij the *path* of ascent. Saheeh, Pickthall, Arberry let the relation stand undefined.

Cross-reference with **32:5** (the rfE/Erj-adjacent verse): `yudabbiru al-amra mina al-samāʾi ila al-arḍ thumma yaʿruju ilayhi fī yawmin kāna miqdāruhu alfa sanatin mimmā taʿuddūn`.

> "He directs the affair from the heaven to the earth; then it ascends to Him in a Day whose measure is a thousand years of those you count."

Two different ascent-verses, two different time-scales: **1,000** (32:5) and **50,000** (70:4). The literature has long noted this discrepancy. Possible structural readings:

1. **Different objects ascending at different rates.** 32:5: `al-amr` (affair) ascends at 1,000-year-day. 70:4: angels and the Spirit ascend at 50,000-year-day. The *rate* depends on what is climbing.
2. **Different days entirely.** 32:5's "day" is a normal management cycle; 70:4's "day" is the Day of Judgment (the surah context — earlier verses describe the punishment, the questioner asking about it).
3. **Increasing scale toward the eschaton.** The 50,000-year day is the *final* day; the 1,000-year day is the *operating* day.

The Quran does not adjudicate. Both numbers stand without harmonization. This is a *deliberate* placement of two different time-distances on the same vertical axis. (See the time investigation's `research-year-and-scale.md` for the full development.)

The structural fact that matters for teleportation: **moving along the Erj axis dilates time massively**. To ascend to God is not just to traverse space — it is to enter a different temporal regime. The Erj root encodes a cosmology where vertical distance and temporal distance are convertible.

## The four-axis cosmology of 34:2 and 57:4

`يَعۡلَمُ مَا يَلِجُ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ وَمَا يَخۡرُجُ مِنۡهَا وَمَا يَنزِلُ مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ وَمَا يَعۡرُجُ فِيهَا` (34:2)

> "He knows what penetrates into the earth, what emerges from it, what descends from the heaven, and what ascends into it."

This is the **complete cardinal grid**:

|  | Toward earth | Toward heaven |
|---|---|---|
| Verb of going-in | `yaliju` (penetrates) | `yaʿruju` (climbs into it) |
| Verb of going-out | `yakhruju` (emerges) | `yanzilu` (descends from it) |

Erj here is paired with `nzl` (descent) as a strict opposite. Both sentences (34:2 and 57:4) place the four verbs in identical order. This is a **fixed schema** — the Quran's compass-rose for vertical and intra-earth movement. Erj is the upward-into-heaven cardinal.

Note the asymmetry: things **descend FROM** heaven (`yanzilu min al-samāʾ`) but **ascend INTO** heaven (`yaʿruju fīhā`). The preposition shift (`min` vs `fī`) suggests heaven is reached by *entering* (climbing into) but is left by *coming forth* (descending from). The earth is *entered into* (`fī`) by penetration and *emerged from* (`min`) — exactly inverted. Each domain has an "into" and an "out of," and the prepositions track which side is the source.

## The 15:14 hypothetical — disbelievers told to climb

`وَلَوۡ فَتَحۡنَا عَلَيۡهِم بَابࣰا مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ فَظَلُّواْ فِيهِ يَعۡرُجُونَ`

> "And [even] if We opened to them a gate from the heaven, and they continued ascending therein..."

Conditional/concessive — the disbelievers, even given a literal gate of heaven through which they could climb, would still not believe (15:15: they would say they have been bewitched). Two structural notes:

1. The **only** Erj-occurrence with **human** subjects (and only conditionally — they don't actually do it).
2. Frames Erj as something the Meccan disbelievers theoretically *could* be granted but won't believe even if granted. Erj is presented as the *maximal sign* — if even ascending into heaven won't convince them, nothing will.

## What the Quran does NOT say about the Miʿrāj

The post-Quranic *miʿrāj* tradition (the Prophet's night ascent through the seven heavens) is named after this root — `miʿrāj` (singular of `maʿārij`) is "the ascent." But:

1. **The verb `yaʿruju` is never used of Muhammad** in the Quran. Of the five verbal occurrences, none has the Prophet as subject.
2. **The noun `miʿrāj` itself does not appear** in the Quran. Only the plural `maʿārij` appears (43:33 of silver stairs; 70:3 of God's stairways).
3. **Surah 17:1's Isrāʾ uses `sry`, not `Erj`.** The horizontal leg is *Quranic*; the vertical leg is *post-Quranic*.

So the *miʿrāj* tradition is a **hadith-anchored extension** that gives Muhammad an ascent of the kind the Quran ascribes only to angels and to the Spirit (and counterfactually to disbelievers given a gate). The Quran's own architecture leaves Erj for non-human or never-actually-realized human ascents. The tradition cuts a vertical leg by drawing on the *vocabulary* of Erj while not having a Quranic verbal anchor.

## The angel-ascent pattern + the rfE/Erj UP / nzl DOWN binary

Erj and `nzl` form a tight functional pair:

- **Angels descend** (`tanazzala al-malāʾikatu wa al-rūḥ` in 97:4) at moments of revelation/destiny.
- **Angels ascend** (`taʿruju al-malāʾikatu wa al-rūḥ` in 70:4) on the great Day.

The same actors (angels + the Spirit) do both. The Spirit (`al-rūḥ`) is the universal vertical-traverser. nzl is the verb of bringing-down; Erj is the verb of going-back-up.

What the verbs do NOT share:
- `nzl` is used of the **Quran** descending (`al-Qurʾān al-munazzal`). Erj is **never** used of the Quran returning.
- `nzl` is used of provision (rain, food). Erj is **never** used of return-of-provision.

The asymmetry: the Quran encodes a *one-way descent* of revelation/provision and a *cyclical ascent-and-descent* of angelic personnel. Information and goods come down; messengers come down and return.

## Translation divergence

| Verse | Term | Spread |
|---|---|---|
| 70:3 | `dhī al-maʿārij` | "owner of the ways of ascent" / "Lord of the Ascending Stairways" / "Lord of pathways of ˹heavenly˺ ascent" / "Lord of the Stairways" |
| 43:33 | `maʿārij` | "stairways" / "stairs" / "stairways to ascend" |
| 32:5 | `yaʿruju ilayh` | "ascend to Him" / "ascendeth unto Him" / "ascends to Him" / "goes up to Him" |
| 70:4 | `taʿruju ... ilayh` | "ascend to Him" — convergent |
| 24:61, 48:17 | `al-aʿraj` | "the lame" / "the disabled" — Khattab modernizes |

Convergence is high on Erj — translators agree it means ascending. Divergence is mostly on rendering of the divine epithet `dhī al-maʿārij`. Pickthall capitalizes ("Ascending Stairways") to signal divine architecture; Arberry stays terse ("the Stairways"). All four leave the term concrete (not spiritualized to "ranks" or "stations") — unlike the Idrīs case in rfE 19:57.

This is itself a finding: Erj is more **concrete** in translator hands than rfE. "Stairways" stays a stairway; "ascend" stays vertical motion. rfE is the term that gets honorifically softened ("status," "rank"); Erj resists that softening.

## Negative space — what's NOT in the data

1. **No human-subject completed ascent.** All five verbal ascents are either of angels/Spirit (70:4), of "the affair" (32:5), of unspecified things (34:2, 57:4), or hypothetical disbelievers (15:14). The Quran does not narrate any human as actually performing Erj.

2. **Idrīs and Jesus use rfE, not Erj.** The two prophets explicitly raised to God's presence are *raised by God* (passive/agentive rfE), not *climbing themselves* (active Erj). This is grammatically marked agency: the prophets are receivers of divine action; angels are autonomous ascenders.

3. **No `descended-after-ascending` for any Erj-subject.** Once something climbs to God in 32:5 or 70:4, the Quran does not narrate a return. Angels descend *separately* (97:4) — they are never said to "ascend then descend." The two motions are independently motivated.

4. **The miʿrāj as Quranic text — absent.** Not a single verbal occurrence of `Erj` has Muhammad as subject. The Quran provides the *vocabulary* for the tradition but not the *narrative*.

5. **No earthly "ladder" gets a Quranic Erj-verbal claim.** Even 43:33 — silver stairways — uses `yaẓharūn` ("they mount upon") with verb root `Zhr`, not `Erj`. The Erj-verb is never used of climbing a worldly ladder. This preserves Erj as a **specifically heavenward** vocabulary.

## Rabbit holes

1. **The two times: 1,000 vs 50,000.** Cross-link to `notes/time/concept-time-deep.md`. Why does 70:4 specify a number 50× the 32:5 day? Hypothesis: 32:5 is the management cadence; 70:4 is the eschatological cadence. Or: 32:5's 1,000 ≈ a single divine "operating day"; 70:4's 50,000 ≈ the duration of the entire Day of Judgment. (See [`notes/time/0-structural/`] for divine-creature time scaling.)

2. **`al-aʿraj` (the lame).** Two occurrences (24:61, 48:17), both in lists granting permission to the disabled (eat from family's houses, stay back from war). The semantic bridge between "lame" and "ascend": both involve **uneven elevation** (one foot higher than the other). Worth a dedicated note — the embodied connection between climbing-motion and limping-motion in early Arabic semantic space.

3. **`miʿrāj` and the 7-heavens cosmology.** Hadith elaborates the heavenly stages; Quran has Erj-vocabulary but not the staged-encounter narrative. What does the Quran say about the **structure** of what is ascended *through*? Not much. Surah 67:3 mentions seven heavens (`sabʿa samāwāt ṭibāqā` "seven heavens in layers") but doesn't connect this to Erj-verbs. The structural cosmology and the ascent vocabulary are not joined in the Quran — they are joined later.

4. **`al-rūḥ` (the Spirit) in Erj.** The Spirit ascends with the angels in 70:4, but the Spirit also descends in 97:4 (`tanazzalu al-malāʾikatu wa al-rūḥu fīhā`). The Spirit is the vertical-traverser par excellence. Cross-link with the Spirit's role in 19:17 (sent to Mary), 16:102 (Holy Spirit revealing), 17:85 (the Spirit is from the affair of my Lord).

5. **`Erj` and `Er$` (throne).** Phonetically close, semantically distinct. `Erj` = ascent; `Er$` = throne. But ascent is ascent *toward the Throne* (cf. 32:5's `yaʿruju ilayh` after `istawā ʿala al-ʿarsh` in 13:2 / 57:4). Both roots cluster around the same divine destination. Worth charting whether the proximity is coincidental or generative.
