# rfE (رفع) — "to raise, lift, elevate"

> The Quran's primary vocabulary for vertical agency. God raises heavens, raises mountains over a covenant, raises ranks of prophets, raises the foundations of the Kaʿba, raises the soft furnishings of paradise, raises Idrīs to a "lofty place," and raises Jesus "to Himself." Almost every active subject is God; the verb is overwhelmingly directional-upward, but the *content* of "up" splits into at least three distinct registers — physical, honorific, and trans-cosmic.

## Stats

- **Occurrences:** 29
- **Unique lemmas:** 6 (`rafaʿa` V×22; `marfūʿah` ADJ/N×3; `rāfiʿ` N×1; `rafīʿ` ADJ×1; `marfūʿ` N×1; `rāfiʿah` N×1)
- **POS:** V 22 / N 4 / ADJ 3
- **Surahs:** 21 — broad distribution, not clustered
- **Revelation:** Meccan and Medinan in roughly equal weight (rfE is too foundational to be a phase-locked term)
- **Top co-occurring roots:** `Alh` (Allah, 8 ayahs), `fwq` ("above," 8), `drj` ("degrees, ranks," 7), `rbb` ("Lord," 6), `Er$` ("Throne," 3), `smw` ("heaven," 4)

The `rfE` + `drj` pairing is the load-bearing collocation: 7 of 29 ayahs (24%) speak explicitly of "raising in **degrees**." This is a structured-rank cosmology, not a generic lift.

Note the absence of an internal-passive imperfect (no *yurfaʿ*-frequency analogue to *yunzal* for nzl). The passives are nearly all participial — *marfūʿ/marfūʿah* — describing **states**, not events. When rfE is an event, almost always God is the active agent. This is grammatically marked: rfE is an act, not a process.

## The three registers

Lay out all 22 verbal occurrences and they sort into three semantic groups:

### A. Physical raising (cosmological / topographical)
- **2:63, 2:93, 4:154** — "وَرَفَعۡنَا فَوۡقَكُمُ ٱلطُّورَ" — raising the Mount over the Children of Israel at the covenant. Three near-identical passive-of-divine-power constructions. Pickthall: "caused the mount to tower above you." This is brute physical lifting *as coercion* — the mountain is the threat backing the oath.
- **2:127** — Abraham (with Ishmael) "raising the foundations of the House" (`yarfaʿu Ibrāhīmu al-qawāʿida min al-bayt`). The only ayah where the active subject is a human, not God. Even here it's foundation-laying — building upward, anchored in the earth.
- **13:2, 79:28, 88:18, 55:7** — God raises the heavens (`raʿafa al-samāwāt`, `rafaʿa samkahā`, `rufiʿat`, `wa al-samāʾa rafaʿahā`). 13:2 specifies "without pillars you can see" — the cosmological architecture, an emphatic miracle.
- **52:5** — `al-saqf al-marfūʿ` — "the canopy raised [high]" — the heaven as roof.
- **94:4** — `wa rafaʿnā laka dhikrak` — "We raised your remembrance/fame for you" — Muhammad's reputation, raised. Slightly metaphorical but still in physical register.

### B. Honorific raising (ranked)
- **2:253** — `wa rafaʿa baʿḍahum darajāt` — "He raised some [messengers] in degrees." The structural verse on prophet-tiering.
- **6:83** — Abraham: `narfaʿu darajātin man nashāʾ` — "We raise by degrees whom We will."
- **6:165** — `wa rafaʿa baʿḍakum fawqa baʿḍin darajāt` — humanity arranged in social ranks as a *test*.
- **7:176** — `wa law shiʾnā la-rafaʿnāhu bihā` — "We could have raised him by them [Our signs]." About a man who clung to earth (`akhlada ila al-arḍ`) instead. The *opposite* of rfE here is `khulūd` — clinging earthward.
- **12:76** — Joseph: `narfaʿu darajātin man nashāʾ`.
- **12:100** — Joseph "raised his parents on the throne" — honorific but spatially literal.
- **35:10** — `wa al-ʿamalu al-ṣāliḥu yarfaʿuh` — righteous deeds raise good speech (subject of raising is **action**, not God directly).
- **43:32** — ranked-livelihood: `wa rafaʿnā baʿḍahum fawqa baʿḍin darajāt`.
- **49:2** — prohibitive: don't *raise* your voices above the Prophet's.
- **58:11** — Allah will raise believers "and those given knowledge" by degrees.
- **24:36** — mosques God has ordered to be raised (`an turfaʿ`) — both physical construction and honorific elevation; the active middle ground.

### C. Trans-cosmic raising (the teleportation register)
- **3:55** — Jesus: `innī mutawaffīka wa rāfiʿuka ilayya wa muṭahhiruka mina alladhīna kafarū` — "I will *take you* and *raise you* to Me and *purify you*." Three sequential operations.
- **4:158** — Jesus again, more terse: `bal rafaʿahu Allāhu ilayh` — "rather, God *raised him* to Himself." `bal` (rather, but instead) is the corrective — set against 4:157's `mā qatalūhu wa mā ṣalabūh` ("they did not kill him and did not crucify him").
- **19:57** — Idrīs: `wa rafaʿnāhu makānan ʿaliyyā` — "We raised him to a lofty place."

These three verses are the **vertical-teleportation core**. They behave differently from A and B:
1. The destination is not a place on earth or a rank among humans, but a directional-divine location: **`ilayya` / `ilayh`** ("to Me / to Him") for Jesus, and **`makānan ʿaliyyā`** ("a lofty place") for Idrīs.
2. The subject is a specific named prophet, not a class.
3. There is no "back" — neither prophet is described in the Quran as descending afterward.

## The 19:57 puzzle — "a lofty place"

`وَرَفَعۡنَٰهُ مَكَانًا عَلِيࣰّا` — five words. Translation divergence:

| Translator | Rendering of `makānan ʿaliyyā` |
|---|---|
| Saheeh International | "to a high station" |
| Pickthall | "to high station" |
| Khattab | "to an honourable status" |
| Arberry | "to a high place." |

Khattab is the outlier — he reads `makān` *honorifically* ("status") rather than spatially ("place"). Saheeh, Pickthall, and Arberry all keep the spatial reading, with "station" leaning slightly more toward rank in English while "place" stays concrete.

The Arabic word `makān` is **strongly spatial** — it derives from `kawn` (being, location) and almost always denotes a physical location elsewhere in the Quran (e.g., 19:16 `makānan sharqiyyā` "an eastern place" for Mary's seclusion; 19:22 `makānan qaṣiyyā` "a far/distant place" for the birth of Jesus; 11:93 `makānatikum` "your position/place"). Khattab's "status" is a theological softening.

`ʿaliyyā` means "lofty, elevated, exalted" — the same root as `Allāh al-ʿAlīy` ("the Most High"). Crucially the term `ʿaliyy` is also used as a divine epithet (2:255 in Āyat al-Kursī).

So the literal reading is: **"We raised him to an exalted place."** The reading-deeply move: this is the same construction as `makānan qaṣiyyā` (19:22, "a far place") — concrete, directional, spatial. Mary withdraws to a far place horizontally; Idrīs is raised to a lofty place vertically. The Quran *does not say where* — and the absence of further information is theologically and structurally meaningful (see Negative space below).

## The 3:55 / 4:158 puzzle — Jesus's "raising"

3:55 stacks three operations: `mutawaffīka` + `rāfiʿuka` + `muṭahhiruka` — "taking you" + "raising you" + "purifying you." 4:158 is bare: `bal rafaʿahu Allāhu ilayh`.

The theological storm is around `tawaffā` (root: `wfy`, "to fulfill, take in full," idiomatically "to take in death"). Translation divergence on `mutawaffīka`:

| Translator | 3:55 rendering of `mutawaffīka` |
|---|---|
| Saheeh | "I will take you" |
| Pickthall | "I am gathering thee" |
| Khattab | "I will take you" |
| Arberry | "I will take thee to Me" |

Notice that **none** translates `tawaffā` as "cause to die" here, even though that is the standard idiomatic meaning elsewhere (e.g., 39:42, where `Allāhu yatawaffā al-anfusa ḥīna mawtihā` clearly means "takes the souls at the time of death"). The translators are reading `tawaffā` here as **completion of mission** or **reception**, not death — driven by 4:157-158, which states the disbelievers did not kill him.

The grammatical layering matters. In 3:55, the sequence `tawaffā` *then* `rafaʿa` *then* `ṭahhara` is a temporal stack: take → raise → purify. The standard exegetical move is either (a) `tawaffā` = sleep/temporary completion, then bodily raising, or (b) `tawaffā` = end of earthly mission (also a non-death reading), then raising. Either way, the *raising* is preserved as an event distinct from a generic death-and-soul-ascent.

4:158 strips it down: just `rafaʿahu Allāhu ilayh`. Pickthall: "Allah took him up unto Himself." `ilayh` ("to Him") is the same destination as 3:55 — and the same construction as the Erj-root usage in 70:4 (`taʿruju al-malāʾikatu wa al-rūḥu *ilayh*`). The angels' ascent and Jesus's raising terminate at the same divine destination.

What's the relationship between 3:55 (full sequence) and 4:158 (bare)? Two readings:
1. **4:158 is a summary** — both *tawaffā* and *rafʿ* collapsed into "raised."
2. **4:158 corrects a different claim** — the polemical context (4:157, "they did not kill him") makes *rafʿ* the load-bearing word: it explicitly substitutes for *qatl* (killing). Whatever else Jesus underwent, the Quran insists the **terminus** was upward, not graveward.

The data-grounded conclusion is that **rfE in the Jesus passages performs the structural function of replacing earthly death with vertical transit**. Whether or not `tawaffā` denotes death is contested; that `rafaʿa` denotes upward transit to God's location is not.

## Honorific raising — what does "raising in degree" mean?

`darajāt` (degrees) is the unit. The verb rfE + `darajāt` appears in 2:253, 6:83, 6:165, 12:76, 43:32, 58:11, and (with `rafīʿ al-darajāt`) 40:15. This is a **graded ontology** — not just "high" and "low," but a stepped vertical structure into which:
- prophets are placed (2:253)
- the special-knowledge-given are placed (6:83, 12:76)
- humans are placed for testing (6:165, 43:32)
- believers and the knowledgeable are raised (58:11)
- God Himself sits at the top (40:15: `rafīʿ al-darajāt dhū al-ʿarsh`)

The *vertical-cosmology metaphor* and the *honorific-rank metaphor* are not separate semantic fields — they are the same field. Raising in rank is movement on the same axis as raising of heavens. This is what makes Idrīs's raising ambiguous: "lofty place" can be read on either reading because they are not actually distinct registers in Quranic Arabic.

## Marfūʿ — the "raised state"

Three lemmas of the participial form encode standardised raised-status:

- **52:5** — `wa al-saqf al-marfūʿ` — "the canopy raised" (the heaven as elevated roof, in oath-clusters). The cosmological structure as standing-raised-thing.
- **56:34, 88:13** — `furush marfūʿah`, `surur marfūʿah` — "raised couches/beds" of paradise. Furniture in paradise is raised: a mark of honor, status.
- **80:14** — scriptures: `marfūʿah muṭahharah` — "exalted, purified" pages of revelation.

Standardised marfūʿ-state = the property of being-in-a-raised-condition. It collapses honorific and physical: paradise furniture is *both* literally elevated *and* honored. The Quranic worldview does not separate these.

## Translation divergence flags

| Verse | Term | Spread | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 19:57 | `makānan ʿaliyyā` | "high station" / "high place" / "honourable status" | Khattab spiritualizes; others keep spatial |
| 3:55 | `mutawaffīka` | "take you" (all four converge) | All decline literal "cause you to die" |
| 4:158 | `rafaʿahu ... ilayh` | "raised him to Himself" / "took him up" | Convergent — direction is "to God" |
| 49:2 | `lā tarfaʿū aṣwātakum` | "do not raise voices" (all four convergent) | Convergent |
| 56:3 | `rāfiʿah` | "exalting" / "raise up" | Convergent (paired with `xāfiḍah` "abasing") |
| 13:2 | `rafaʿa al-samāwāt` | "raised the heavens" (universal) | Convergent |

The two divergence sites both involve trans-cosmic raising of named prophets (Idrīs, Jesus). This is consistent — translators converge on routine rfE and diverge on the metaphysically loaded cases.

## Negative space — what's NOT in the data

1. **Nobody descends.** The Quran has no dedicated verb for a person coming back DOWN from a raised place. Idrīs goes up at 19:57 and never returns in the text. Jesus is raised in 3:55 and 4:158 and the Quran is silent on his return. The Quran does have a lexicon of descent (`nzl`, `hbT`), but it is applied to the Quran-as-revelation, to angels, to rain, to provision, to Adam (`hbT` "go down" — the fall), but never to a prophet returning from God's presence to earthly mission as the inverse of rfE. *(The hadith corpus elaborates Jesus's return in the end-times, but the Quran does not.)*

2. **No miʿrāj of Muhammad in rfE.** The Prophet's night journey uses *sry* (horizontal: 17:1) and the night-ascent tradition is bound to *Erj*-derived terminology (the *miʿrāj*) — but **`rafaʿa` is never used of Muhammad** as a vertical-cosmic event. The closest is 94:4 (`rafaʿnā laka dhikrak`) — raising of *fame*, not of body.

3. **God is rāfiʿ; the human is being-raised.** The active participle `rāfiʿ` occurs only at 3:55, of God toward Jesus. Humans are never agents of trans-cosmic rfE.

4. **`tarfaʿ` (you, plural, raise) appears once: 49:2** — and it's a *prohibition*. Humans actively raising is regulated negatively. The agency of the verb is reserved.

## Cross-cutting: The vertical cosmology — rfE / Erj / nzl

The teleportation vocabulary in the Quran sorts onto **two perpendicular axes**:

**Vertical axis (between heaven and earth)**
- `rfE` — raising up. Agent overwhelmingly God. Patients: heavens, mountains, prophets (Idrīs, Jesus), ranks, voices, deeds-raising-words.
- `Erj` — climbing up. Used of angels and the Spirit (32:5, 70:4) and unnamed "what ascends" (34:2, 57:4). The path itself is `maʿārij` (70:3, "ladders/staircases") — Surah 70 is named after these.
- `nzl` — sending/coming down. Reverse of rfE/Erj. Patient in nzl is information (Quran descends), beings (angels descend), provision (rain, food).

**Horizontal axis (within earth, but extraordinarily long)**
- `sry` — the *night journey* / Isrāʾ. 17:1: `subḥāna alladhī asrā bi-ʿabdihi laylan min al-masjidi al-ḥarāmi ila al-masjidi al-aqṣā`. From Mecca to Jerusalem. This is the horizontal-teleportation vocabulary.

The two axes do not share verbs. `rfE` is never used of horizontal motion (Mecca-to-Jerusalem); `sry` is never used of vertical motion (heaven-to-earth). They are **distinct miracles with distinct lexicons**. This is structurally significant.

### Why are the prophet-miracles RAISING-up rather than TRAVELING-across?

Look at the named prophet-relocations:
- **Idrīs (19:57)** — vertical, rfE
- **Jesus (3:55, 4:158)** — vertical, rfE
- **Muhammad (17:1)** — horizontal (Isrāʾ), sry; vertical (Miʿrāj) is *traditionally* placed within the same night but the Quranic verse covers the horizontal leg explicitly

The Quran reserves **vertical-permanent** raising for two prophets (Idrīs, Jesus) — both of whom in tradition are "alive" (taken up rather than dying). It reserves **horizontal-round-trip** movement (sry — going and presumably returning) for Muhammad, whose mission is to live and die on earth.

The architecture: vertical motion correlates with **non-return** (Idrīs, Jesus). Horizontal motion correlates with **return** (Muhammad goes by night and is back in Mecca by morning — 17:1's framing implies this). Vertical = exit from earthly bounds; horizontal = extraordinary intra-earthly transit.

### Connection to the time investigation

In `notes/time/concept-time-deep.md` and the divine-vs-creature time research note, the Quran explicitly couples vertical motion with time-distortion:

- **32:5** — affairs are managed `min al-samāʾi ila al-arḍ` then `yaʿruju ilayh` (ascend to Him) — in a day = 1,000 years.
- **70:4** — angels and the Spirit `taʿruju ilayh` in a day = 50,000 years.

Crossing the vertical axis = crossing into different time-scales. Horizontal motion (sry) does *not* invoke this time-distortion language. The night-journey of 17:1 is "by night" — earthly time. So:

> **Vertical movement = time-scale crossing. Horizontal movement = earthly time preserved.**

This means rfE is not just spatial. Raising Idrīs to a "lofty place" or raising Jesus "to Himself" is *also* a temporal relocation — they are removed from the temporal frame in which earthly people die and decompose. Which is the structural reason both can be alive in tradition while not being "on earth" — they are *somewhere else on the time-axis*, not just on the height-axis.

### Theological architecture summary

| Axis | Verbs | Used of | Time-frame | Reversibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vertical UP | `rfE`, `Erj`, `SEd` (rare) | heavens, prophets (Idrīs, Jesus), angels, deeds | Cross-temporal (1,000y / 50,000y day) | One-way for prophets |
| Vertical DOWN | `nzl`, `hbT` | revelation, angels, rain, Adam (fall) | Cross-temporal | Bidirectional for angels |
| Horizontal extraordinary | `sry` (Isrāʾ) | Muhammad (17:1), Lot/Moses (with families, by night) | Earthly time | Round-trip |
| Horizontal ordinary | `s^yr`, `m^$y`, `safar` | humans walking, traveling | Earthly time | Bidirectional |

## Rabbit holes

1. **Idrīs = Enoch?** Tradition equates Idrīs with biblical Enoch (Gen 5:24, "Enoch walked with God; then he was no more, because God took him"). The same one-way vertical translation pattern. Cross-tradition convergence. Not a Quranic claim, but a research-priority for inter-textual layering.
2. **Why is `wfy` (`tawaffā`) the verb paired with `rfE` for Jesus, not for Idrīs?** Idrīs is just *raised*. Jesus is *taken-then-raised*. The asymmetry deserves its own analysis. Could mean (a) Idrīs's raising is bodily without prior process; (b) Jesus's raising is the second of two operations because there's a polemical death-claim to refute; (c) the two events are categorically different.
3. **The mountain-raising at the covenant (2:63, 2:93, 4:154, 7:171).** Three (and a fourth in 7:171 with `nataqnā` "shook," not rfE) repetitions of the *same* event. Why does the Quran repeat this with rfE? Hypothesis: rfE here is not just topographical, it's juridical — the mountain raised over the head as a *signed-under-duress* signal.
4. **`rafīʿ al-darajāt` (40:15) as a divine name.** Possibly the most explicit identification of God as the *ground of the vertical-rank order*. He is "Exalter of ranks" not just "the Exalted." The cosmology is ranked because He ranks it. Cross-reference with `dhū al-maʿārij` (70:3, the Erj-root divine epithet). God is named TWICE by his vertical-architecture role.
5. **Why never `yarfaʿ` of the Resurrection?** The Day of Resurrection is `yawm al-qiyāmah` (root: `qwm`, "to stand up"), not "the day of being raised." The dead *stand up*, they are not lifted. rfE retains its God-as-active-agent specificity even at the eschaton.
6. **The Maḥdī / Jesus-return tradition.** The Quran is silent on Jesus's return; hadith fills it. The Quranic text gives us a one-way trip. The descent-return corpus is post-Quranic. Worth tracing what Quranic vocabulary, if any, the hadith corpus appropriates for the *return* — and whether it uses `nzl` (descent) of Jesus, completing the rfE/nzl pair.
