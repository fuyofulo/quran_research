# Concept: HDr — to be present, to be brought-present

**Headline.** The root حضر governs presence in three flavors: (1) `ḥaḍara` form I — "to come to / to attend / to draw near" (a person attending a place, or death drawing near a person); (2) `ḥāḍir`, `ḥāḍira` — active participles, "present, attending, dwelling near"; (3) `muḥḍar`, `aḥḍara`, `muḥtaḍar` — form II/IV/VIII passive/causative, "brought-present, made-to-attend." This third bucket is the eschatological backbone: of 25 occurrences, 10 are the passive participle `muḥḍar` and 3 are causative `aḥḍara`, almost all in scenes where deeds, hosts, or reward-recipients are MADE PRESENT before God on Judgment Day. The Quran has a dedicated lexeme for the appearance-of-something-where-it-was-not. **Important corrective:** the Queen of Sheba's throne in 27:42 is NOT carried by the HDr root in the Quranic text; the verb there is `jāʾat` (root `jyA`) and `āṭīka bihi` (root `Aty`). The HDr root does NOT cover the Solomon-throne miracle directly. Where this root LIVES is the Day-of-Judgment "brought-present" pattern. The teleportation-relevant connection between Solomon's throne and HDr is one of structural analogy — both are scenes where things appear-where-they-were-not — not lexical overlap.

## The three semantic buckets

**Bucket A — `muḥḍar`, the eschatological brought-present (10×, all passive participles, Meccan-dominant).** This is the lexeme of beings/things made to appear at the Day-of-Reckoning scene.
- 3:30 `yawma tajidu kullu nafsin mā ʿamilat min xayrin **muḥḍaran** wa-mā ʿamilat min sūʾin` — "the Day every soul will find what it has done of good BROUGHT-PRESENT." The DEED appears as a thing.
- 28:61 `yawma l-qiyāmati humma mina l-**muḥḍarīn**` — they are among those-brought-present.
- 30:16, 34:38, 36:32, 36:53, 36:75, 37:57, 37:127, 37:158 — the formulaic plural `muḥḍarūn`/`muḥḍarīn`. These are the DAMNED-OR-REWARDED parties brought into the Judgment scene. 36:75 sharpens it: the false gods cannot help; the worshippers are TO THEM `jundun muḥḍarūn` — a host brought-present (Saheeh: "soldiers in attendance"; Khattab: "dedicated guards"). Translation divergence on this verse is high.

**Bucket B — `ḥaḍara`, drawing near (7× verbs, Medinan-dominant).** Most of this bucket is the death-presence formula:
- 2:180 `i*ā ḥaḍara aḥadakumu l-mawt` — "when death draws near to one of you." The fixed formula.
- 2:133 `i*ā ḥaḍara yaʿqūba l-mawt` — "when death drew near to Yaʿqūb."
- 4:18 `i*ā ḥaḍara aḥadahumu l-mawt` — same formula.
- 5:106 `i*ā ḥaḍara aḥadakumu l-mawt ḥīna l-waṣiyya` — same again, in the bequest-witness context.
- 4:8 `i*ā ḥaḍara l-qisma` — "when the division [of inheritance] is attended."
- 23:98 `aʿūdhu bika rabbi an yaḥḍurūn` — "I take refuge with You lest they (the devils) come to me."
- 46:29 `wa-i*i ṣarafnā ilayka nafaran mina l-jinni yastamiʿūna l-qurʾāna fa-lammā ḥaḍarūhu` — "when they ATTENDED it (the recitation)."

The death-formula `i*ā ḥaḍara aḥadakumu l-mawt` is one of the Quran's most stable phrasal units (4 instances of essentially identical syntax). Death is grammatically the AGENT that "draws near" the dying person — death goes TO them, not the reverse.

**Bucket C — `ḥāḍir`, on-the-spot presence (4× participles).** 2:282 (a "present" cash transaction, `tijāratan ḥāḍiratan`), 2:196 (`ḥāḍirī l-masjidi l-ḥarām` — those whose families dwell near the Sacred Mosque), 7:163 (the village `ḥāḍirata l-baḥr` — present-by-the-sea, the Sabbath-breakers' town), 18:49 (`wajadū mā ʿamilū **ḥāḍiran**` — they will find what they did present-before-them on the Day).

18:49 is the bridging case: the active participle `ḥāḍir` here describes the SAME phenomenon that elsewhere uses the passive participle `muḥḍar` — DEEDS appearing at the Reckoning. The two participles run in parallel for 3:30 and 18:49. The Quran has BOTH "the deed is present" (`ḥāḍir`) and "the deed is brought-present" (`muḥḍar`); the latter foregrounds an external bringer (Allah/angels), the former foregrounds the as-found state.

## Form-VIII outlier — `muḥtaḍar` (54:28)

Single instance: `kullu širbin muḥtaḍar` — "every drink is attended" (each turn at the water is overseen). This is the Ṣāliḥ camel narrative; the water-share between camel and people. The form-VIII reflexive/intensive shifts the sense from "brought-present (by an external agent)" to "(the drink) attended-upon (by the takers)." Rare lemma, semantically anchored to the camel scene.

## Form-IV causative — `aḥḍarat` (3×)

- 4:128 `wa-uḥḍirati l-anfusu š-šuḥḥ` — "souls are made-present-with stinginess" (i.e. greed is innate). Passive form, abstract.
- 19:68 `la-nuḥḍirannahum ḥawla jahannama jiṯiyyan` — "We will surely BRING THEM-PRESENT around Hell on their knees." The DIVINE BRINGING — Allah teleports the resurrected to the perimeter of Hell.
- 81:14 `ʿalimat nafsun mā **aḥḍarat**` — "a soul will know what it has BROUGHT-PRESENT." Each soul has presented its own deeds.

19:68 is decisive for the teleportation-vocabulary thesis. The Day-of-Reckoning is described as a mass spatial transfer: the resurrected are not said to walk, ride, or be carried; they are `nuḥḍirannahum` — brought-present. This is the divine-passive of teleportation. No transit-verb; the destination is named (`ḥawla jahannam`); the body simply IS THERE on its knees.

## On 27:42 and the Solomon throne — the corrective

The user-prompt described the Queen of Sheba's throne as "muḥḍar al-ʿarsh" in 27:42. THE QURANIC TEXT IS NOT THIS. The actual sequence:
- 27:38 — Sulaymān asks `ayyukum yaʾtīnī bi-ʿaršihā` — "which of you will BRING me her throne" (root: `Aty`).
- 27:40 — the man with knowledge says `anā āṭīka bihi` (root `Aty`); then `fa-lammā raʾāhu mustaqirran ʿindahu` — "when he saw it SETTLED with him" (root `qrr`, not HDr).
- 27:42 — `fa-lammā jāʾat qīla a-hākadhā ʿaršuki` — "when SHE (the Queen) came, it was said: is your throne like this?" The verb `jāʾat` is from root `jyA`. `Hml` and `Aty` are the carrying/bringing verbs of the throne-scene; HDr is not present in surah 27 at all (zero occurrences across the whole surah).

The structural-analogy holds (deeds brought-present and a throne brought-present share the "appearance-where-it-was-not" pattern), but the LEXICAL realization is different. This is exactly the kind of cross-checking that prevents false-friend conclusions. The HDr root's eschatological-bringing is its own self-contained pattern; the Solomon-throne miracle is built from `Aty + jyA + qrr + Hml`. The teleportation cluster is broader than the user-prompt schematic suggested.

## Translation divergence

3:30 (combined=0.637, jaccard=0.406) — `muḥḍar` becomes "present [before it]" (Saheeh), "confronted with" (Pickthall), "presented with" (Khattab), "brought forward" (Arberry). Arberry's "brought forward" is closest to the morphological passive; Pickthall's "confronted with" foregrounds the experiential clash; Saheeh and Khattab settle on "present(ed)." All four preserve the visibility motif but only Arberry preserves the brought-by-someone implicit subject.

19:68 (combined=0.651, jaccard=0.389) — `la-nuḥḍirannahum` becomes "We will surely bring them" (Saheeh, similar Khattab), "We shall bring them, crouching" (Pickthall), "We shall parade them about" (Arberry). Arberry's "parade" is the most spatial/dynamic; Pickthall's adverbial placement reads `jiṯiyyan` (on their knees) as the manner-of-arrival; Saheeh keeps the bare-bones action.

81:14 (combined=0.617, jaccard=0.396) — `mā aḥḍarat` becomes "what it has brought [with it]" (Saheeh), "what it hath made ready" (Pickthall), "what ˹deeds˺ it has brought along" (Khattab — adds the gloss [deeds]), "what it has produced" (Arberry). Arberry's "produced" loses the bringing-to-presence valence.

For the death-formula, divergence is low and convergent: "death approaches/draws near" is universal across translators. This is a stable phrasal unit.

For 36:75 (`hum lahum jundun muḥḍarūn`) the divergence is conceptually high — Saheeh "soldiers in attendance," Pickthall "a host in arms," Khattab "dedicated guards," Arberry "hosts made ready." The phrase is opaque; each translator chooses a different military/service register. The Arabic literally is "for them, an army-brought-present" — the IDOLS' worshippers are brought-present FOR the idols, who cannot save them. Khattab's "dedicated guards" is the most interpretive (adds an agency the Arabic does not). Saheeh "in attendance" is best.

## Negative space — what HDr does NOT cover

It does not cover ordinary travel (`s-y-r`, `nzl`). It does not cover the bringing-by-physical-carriage (that is `Hml` — angels carrying the throne; mothers carrying children). It does not cover descent of revelation (`nzl`). It does not cover the angel-borne presentation (Mūsā's tablet, etc.). The HDr root is specifically the ABSTRACT BRINGING-INTO-PRESENCE — making something/someone APPEAR before a witness, often with no described physical transit. This is precisely why it functions as the Quran's quiet teleportation-vocabulary: the deed is brought-present, the host is brought-present, the resurrected are brought-present — no journey is described, only the resulting presence.

## Verses for direct study

- 3:30 — Day every soul finds its good `muḥḍaran` and wishes its evil far. The deed IS the brought-present object.
- 19:68 — `la-nuḥḍirannahum ḥawla jahannam` — divine causative bringing of the damned.
- 81:14 — `ʿalimat nafsun mā aḥḍarat` — each soul's presented deeds. The form-IV active.
- 36:32, 36:53, 36:75, 37:57, 37:127, 37:158 — the formulaic `muḥḍarūn` plural. Read as a cluster.
- 18:49 — the active participle `ḥāḍir` for found-deeds (parallel to `muḥḍar`).
- 2:133, 2:180, 4:18, 5:106 — the death-presence formula `ḥaḍara aḥadakumu l-mawt`.
- 54:28 — `kullu širbin muḥtaḍar` — the form-VIII outlier in the Ṣāliḥ camel scene.

## Rabbit holes

1. **The death-as-agent formula.** `ḥaḍara aḥadakumu l-mawt` makes DEATH grammatically the subject that "draws near" a person. Compare with `jāʾa` (came), `idrāku l-mawt` (overtaking by death, see 4:78), `tawaffā` (taking the soul). The Quran has multiple lexemes for death's arrival, each with different agency. Worth a focused study.

2. **`muḥḍar` and the "things appear-where-they-were-not" pattern.** Compile every Quranic case of an object/agent appearing in a scene without described transit. The list includes: the Solomon throne (27:42, root `Aty`/`jyA`), the deeds at Judgment (HDr), the Maryam-conception (no transit verb), the Ṣāliḥ camel emerging from the rock (root `n-q-h`?), Maryam's provisions in the miḥrāb (3:37, root `rzq`/`Aty`). HDr is one node in a larger pattern.

3. **18:49 / 3:30 parallel — `ḥāḍir` vs `muḥḍar`.** Both name DEEDS appearing at Judgment. Why does the Quran sometimes use the active participle (the deed IS present) and sometimes the passive (the deed IS BROUGHT-present)? Hypothesis: the passive foregrounds a divine bringer; the active foregrounds the deed-as-evidence. Worth checking translator consensus on which valence dominates.

4. **The form-IV `aḥḍara` with the soul as subject (81:14).** Each soul brings its OWN deeds present. This is a striking inversion of the usual passive — the doer is here, for once, the doer of the bringing. Pair with the 3:30 passive (deeds brought-present TO the soul) and you get a chiasm: deeds appear before the soul (3:30, passive divine subject) / soul brings the deeds present (81:14, active soul subject). Both true; the Quran uses both voices for the same eschatological event.

5. **The corrective for the Solomon throne.** The teleportation thesis must be lexically careful: 27:42 does not use HDr. The "quiet vocabulary of teleportation" is built from at least four roots — `Aty` (bring), `jyA` (come), `Hml` (carry), and `HDr` (bring-present) — not just one. Each root has its own habitat. See cross-cutting in `concept-Hml.md`.
