# Concept: ط-ر-ف (Trf) — glance, blink, extremity

## Headline

The root **ط-ر-ف** is the Quran's **gaze-and-edge** root: it names what the eye does in the smallest possible movement (*ṭarf* — a glance, the flicker of a lid) AND what the world is at its outermost border (*ṭaraf* / *aṭrāf* — an extremity, a margin, an end). Same triliteral, two semantic poles — and **the Quran exploits the fact that they are the same word**. With *ṭarf* it measures the smallest unit of perceived time. With *aṭrāf* it measures the largest unit of inhabited space. **One root spans the eye-blink and the horizon.**

The single most consequential occurrence is **27:40** — *qabla an yartadda ilayka ṭarfuk*, "before your gaze returns to you" — the explicit teleportation comparator used by "the one who had knowledge from the Book" when the Queen of Sheba's throne is brought across continents. This is the Quran's most literal teleportation phrase. And it is paired in the same surah, two ayahs prior, with the ʿifrīt's offer of *time*-bounded speed (*qabla an taqūma min maqāmik*, "before you rise from your place"). The Quran chooses the gaze-comparator to *outrank* the time-comparator. **The gaze is faster than the act.**

## Stats

- 11 occurrences, 11 ayahs, 11 surahs (uniformly distributed — not concentrated)
- 2 lemmas: **Tarof** (طَرْف, "glance/gaze") = 6× ; **Taraf** (طَرَف, "edge/extremity") = 5×
- All 11 are nouns (no verbal forms — this is striking; see Negative Space)
- Revelation split: 8 Meccan / 3 Medinan
- Surahs: 3, 11, 13, 14, 20, 21, 27, 37, 38, 42, 55 — one occurrence each

## Lemma table

| Lemma | Arabic | Sense | Count | Locations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tarof** (ṭarf) | طَرْف | the gaze, the flicker of the eye | 6 | 14:43, 27:40, 37:48, 38:52, 42:45, 55:56 |
| **Taraf** (ṭaraf) | طَرَف | a part, an end, a margin (sg./pl.) | 5 | 3:127, 11:114, 13:41, 20:130, 21:44 |

The split is clean: *Tarof* is always **the gaze** (singular, definite or possessive); *Taraf* is always **a portion or edge** of something larger (a body of unbelievers, a stretch of day, the borders of the earth). The two never overlap and are never conflated by translators.

## The teleportation formula — 27:40 in detail

> قَالَ ٱلَّذِى عِندَهُۥ عِلْمٌ مِّنَ ٱلْكِتَـٰبِ أَنَا۠ ءَاتِيكَ بِهِۦ **قَبْلَ أَن يَرْتَدَّ إِلَيْكَ طَرْفُكَ**
>
> *"Said the one who had knowledge from the Book: 'I will bring it to you **before your gaze returns to you**.'"*

Grammatical parse:
- *qabla* — "before" (preposition + verbal-noun construction)
- *an yartadda* — "that it return" (subjunctive of √rdd, form VIII *iftaʿala*, reflexive return)
- *ilayka* — "to you"
- *ṭarfuk(a)* — "your gaze" (subject of *yartadda*)

So literally: *before your-gaze returns to you*. The sense is the moment between when you cast a glance outward and when it rebounds — the round-trip flicker of attention. **Less than a blink. The interval of optical perception itself.**

Translation behaviour for the formula:
- Saheeh: *"before your glance returns to you"*
- Pickthall: *"before thy gaze returneth unto thee"*
- Arberry: *"before ever thy glance returns to thee"*
- Khattab: ***"in the blink of an eye"*** ← **collapses the formula entirely** into the lmH idiom (16:77 / 54:50). Khattab is doing a sense-translation: he reads 27:40 as already meaning what 16:77 says, and renders it with the more familiar English idiom. **This is a translator declaring the equivalence I will argue is structurally there.**

The cooccurring roots inside 27:40 are revealing: *qrr* (settled), *fDl* (favour), *blw* (test), *krm* (generous), *gny* (self-sufficient), *Elm* (knowledge), *ktb* (Book), *$kr* (gratitude), *qbl* (before), *End* (with/possess), *nfs* (self), *rdd* (return). The verse is dense with the Solomonic reflex: the throne arrives, *and the immediate response is gratitude, not awe of the agent*. The instantaneity is foregrounded by the comparator, then dissolved into a theology of *fDl* and *blw* (favour and test).

## The ʿifrīt's offer — 27:39 — sets up the comparator

> قَالَ عِفْرِيتٌ مِّنَ ٱلْجِنِّ أَنَا۠ ءَاتِيكَ بِهِۦ **قَبْلَ أَن تَقُومَ مِن مَّقَامِكَ**
>
> *"An ʿifrīt of the jinn said: 'I will bring it to you **before you rise from your place**.'"*

The ʿifrīt's clock is **agent-centred**: it measures by Solomon's bodily action (rising from his seat). The "one who had knowledge from the Book" measures by **a sub-bodily, optical event**: the return-flicker of the gaze, an interval shorter than any volitional movement. The two offers are syntactically parallel — *qabla an + verb + min + nominal* — but the verbs differ: *taqūma* (rise) vs *yartadda* (return). One is muscular; the other is pre-muscular.

The escalation is structurally encoded. The Quran does not say "X was faster than Y"; it lets two parallel sentences encode the gap. **And the gaze-comparator wins.** The throne arrives "settled before him" (*mustaqirran ʿindahu*) — already at rest — by the time the eye-flicker would have completed.

## ṭarf as gaze elsewhere — the eschatology of looking

The other 5 *Tarof* occurrences cluster around two scenes: **(a) the look of the damned** and **(b) the modesty of paradise companions**.

(a) **14:43** — *muhṭiʿīn muqniʿī ru'ūsihim **lā yartaddu ilayhim ṭarfuhum** wa-af'idatuhum hawā'*. *"Hurrying with heads upraised, **their gaze does not return to them**, and their hearts are void."*

Note the **inverted teleportation formula**. 27:40 says "before your gaze returns to you" — the throne beats the return. 14:43 says "their gaze **does not return** to them" — the gaze is permanently outbound, frozen on the horror. Same root *rdd* + *ṭarf* combination. **The Day-of-Judgment scene is built from the negation of the Solomonic clock.** Where the Book-knower runs faster than the gaze can return, the damned's gaze never returns at all. Same operator (*yartaddu ilayhim ṭarfuhum*), opposite truth-value. This is one of the most precise structural echoes in the corpus.

(b) **37:48, 38:52, 55:56** — *qāṣirātu al-ṭarf*, "those of restrained gaze" — paradise-companions whose eye stays modestly on their partner. The phrase is repeated almost verbatim three times. Here *ṭarf* is **the gaze as social/erotic restraint** — modesty as not letting the eye rove.

(c) **42:45** — *yanẓurūna min ṭarfin khafiyy*, "they look from a furtive/concealed glance." The damned, exposed to the Fire, steal glances at it sideways, ashamed. *Khafiyy* = hidden, faint. The gaze-of-shame.

So the *Tarof* cluster has three modes:
1. **Speed-comparator** (27:40, 14:43 negation)
2. **Erotic restraint** (37:48, 38:52, 55:56 — paradise)
3. **Shame** (42:45 — hell)

All three concern **what the gaze does or fails to do** — outward motion (27:40), failed return (14:43), restrained departure (37:48 etc.), furtive sideways (42:45). The Quran's eye is always going somewhere or refusing to.

## ṭaraf as edge — the ends-of-the-earth formula

The five *Taraf* occurrences split as follows:

| Loc | Form | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| 3:127 | ṭarafan | "a part" of the disbelievers (cut off) |
| 11:114 | ṭarafay al-nahār | "the two ends of the day" (prayer times) |
| 13:41 | aṭrāfihā | "its (earth's) extremities" |
| 20:130 | aṭrāfa al-nahār | "the ends of the day" |
| 21:44 | aṭrāfihā | "its (earth's) extremities" |

The **"min aṭrāf al-arḍ"** formula — 13:41 and 21:44 — is **identical wording** in both verses:

> أَوَلَمْ يَرَوْاْ أَنَّا نَأْتِى ٱلْأَرْضَ نَنقُصُهَا مِنْ أَطْرَافِهَا
>
> *"Do they not see that We come to the land, reducing it from its extremities?"*

This is **a Quranic spatial-totality formula**: the act of God consuming the inhabited world from its margins inward. Translators read it as either **(i)** geopolitical attrition (Pickthall, Khattab — "outlying parts," "borders" — Muslim conquest reducing pagan territory) or **(ii)** geological/eschatological erosion (Arberry — "extremities" — vague enough to read either way). Saheeh splits the difference ("borders"). **No translator pursues a literal cosmic reading** (the earth itself shrinking from the edges), though the Arabic *nanquṣuhā* (we diminish *it*) supports it.

What matters for the teleportation thread: 13:41 also contains *sarīʿ al-ḥisāb*, **"swift in the reckoning"** — the only co-occurrence in this set of an explicit divine-speed term. The verse pairs **edge-eating geography** with **swift reckoning**. The ends of the earth come in fast.

The "two ends of the day" (11:114, 20:130) is a different register — ritual time, the prayer-bracket. *Aṭrāf* there means dawn-and-dusk as the day's hinge points. Worth noting only to keep the lemma table honest.

## Negative space

1. **No verb of √Trf.** Eleven occurrences, all nouns. There is no *ṭarafa / yaṭrifu* verb anywhere in the corpus. The gaze is always **a thing that does** (returns, doesn't return, is restrained, is furtive) — never an action one performs. This is grammatically marked: the Quran does not say "I glanced" or "he looked-with-a-flicker." It says "his gaze did X." **The eye is treated as a semi-autonomous agent**, not as a tool the self uses.

2. **No co-occurrence between *Tarof* and *Taraf*.** No verse uses both senses. The Quran does not pun on the root. The gaze-meaning and the edge-meaning are partitioned by ayah, even though they share the triliteral. (This makes Khattab's sense-translation at 27:40 — "blink of an eye" — even more striking, because he is not just collapsing 27:40 into 16:77; he is also tacitly resisting any pun-reading that the original Arabic might support.)

3. **No "lowering the gaze" with ṭarf.** The famous gender ethic of 24:30–31 — *yaghuḍḍū min abṣārihim* — uses √bṣr, not √Trf. The two roots' division of labour: *bṣr* = vision-as-faculty, the seeing-organ; *Trf* = gaze-as-event, the optical micro-act. *Bṣr* can be turned away; *ṭarf* is what flickers. They are not synonyms. **This is the same partition the lmH formulas exploit (kalamḥi al-baṣar, "as a flicker of-the-vision"): lmH is the verb-noun of flickering, baṣar is the substrate it flickers across.** ṭarf collapses both into one nominal — which is why 27:40 doesn't need lmH; *yartadda ṭarfuk* already encodes the flicker-and-return.

4. **No paradisal *ṭarf*-as-speed.** The paradise companions have restrained-*ṭarf* (37/38/55). But the Quran does not reuse the speed-of-*ṭarf* in paradise scenes. The Solomonic flicker-clock is **a one-off** in this lemma; the eschatological echo is in 14:43, not in any reward-side verse.

## Translation divergence — research signal

| Verse | Saheeh | Pickthall | Arberry | Khattab |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 27:40 | "before your glance returns to you" | "before thy gaze returneth unto thee" | "before ever thy glance returns to thee" | **"in the blink of an eye"** |
| 14:43 | "their glance does not come back to them" | "their gaze returning not to them" | "their glances never returned on themselves" | **"never blinking"** |
| 13:41 | "from its borders" | "of its outlying parts" | "in its extremities" | "from its borders" |

Pattern: **Khattab idiomatises systematically.** At 27:40 he replaces the Solomonic formula with the famous Hour-formula ("blink of an eye"). At 14:43 he replaces "gaze does not return" with "never blinking." Both moves erase the *yartadda* (return) verb and keep only the eye-event. **This is a translator silently merging Trf and lmH.** The Arabic keeps them lexically distinct; Khattab folds them into one English idiom. The other three translators preserve the *return* motif faithfully, which is required for the 27:40 ↔ 14:43 echo to register.

This is a recurrent finding (see methodology log Entry 5 §6): translator *idiomatisation* erases lexical structure that the Arabic uses to encode comparison. The 27:40 / 14:43 mirror is invisible in Khattab; visible in the other three.

## The instantaneity vocabulary catalog

Building the full inventory by integrating Trf with lmH and adjacent roots:

| Root/idiom | Sense | Key verse | Speed-target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **lmH** (√l-m-ḥ, *lamḥ*) | the flicker, the twinkle | 16:77, 54:50 | the Hour, divine command |
| **Trf** (√ṭ-r-f, *ṭarf*) | the gaze (especially its return) | 27:40 | throne teleportation |
| **kun fa-yakūn** | "Be — and it is" | 2:117, 3:47, 36:82, etc. (×8) | creation/decree |
| **sarīʿ al-ḥisāb** | swift-in-reckoning | 2:202, 13:41, etc. | judgment-day calculation |
| **aqrab** ("nearer/closer") | hyperbolic qualifier | 16:77 (paired with lmḥ) | the Hour faster than fast |
| **wāḥida** ("a single one") | one-act qualifier | 54:50 (paired with lmḥ) | divine command as monolithic |

Notes on the inventory:

- **kun fa-yakūn** is not a "speed" formula in the same sense — it is **causal collapse** (no gap between command and effect). lmH and Trf measure speed *against a perceptual baseline* (the eye, the body); kun-fa-yakūn says there is no gap to measure. They are different rhetorical moves. The Quran uses *both*: kun-fa-yakūn for cosmological creation; lmḥ-and-ṭarf for actions that are still inside narrative time but at its limit.

- **sarīʿ al-ḥisāb** is the only "speed" attribute in the divine names (8+ occurrences). It says God is fast at *judgment*, not at *creation*. The thing being modulated by speed is the **moral accounting**, not the act. So sarīʿ + lmḥ + Trf + kun-fa-yakūn together cover four distinct domains: judgment-speed, eschatology-speed, action-speed, creation-instantaneity.

- **aqrab** in 16:77 is the **hyperbolic capstone**: even the blink-of-eye comparator is too slow. The Quran adds *aw huwa aqrab* — "or it is closer/nearer" — to outpace its own speed-image. Lane glosses *aqrab* there as temporal nearness. This is the Quran's only instance of a speed-image being *self-overrun*.

The catalog reveals: **the Quran has a dedicated grammar for instantaneity, and Trf is the human-perceptual end of it**. lmH is the same (eye-flicker) but typically in *amr* (command) contexts. Trf is in *narrative* contexts (Solomon, the damned). kun-fa-yakūn is in *creation/decree* contexts. The four formulas are domain-partitioned.

## Teleportation theology + eschatology — the binding

The 27:40 throne event uses *ṭarf* (gaze-flicker as the metric of how fast the throne arrives). The 16:77 Hour-prophecy uses *lamḥ al-baṣar* (gaze-flicker as the metric of how fast the Hour arrives). **The same metric** — the eye-flicker — bounds **both the teleporting object and the arriving Hour**.

This is a structural binding: whatever power moved the throne in 27:40 is the same power that will bring the Hour in 16:77, and the Quran is consistent about the *speed-image* used for both. The "one with knowledge from the Book" (27:40) is performing, at narrative scale, the same kind of act God describes Himself doing at cosmic scale (16:77 / 54:50). **Teleportation is eschatology run on a smaller object.**

This binding is invisible if you read 27:40 in isolation. It is visible only when you notice: (i) the lexical link is the eye-flicker, (ii) the comparator-grammar is the same (*ka-/qabla-* + flicker-event), (iii) the hyperbolic move (*aqrab* in 16:77) implies that even the throne-comparator was an understatement.

## Verses for direct study (priority)

1. **27:40** — the explicit teleportation comparator. The throne, the Book-knower, the gratitude-reflex.
2. **27:39** as set-up — the ʿifrīt's *qabla an taqūma min maqāmik*. The contrasted comparator.
3. **14:43** — the inverted formula. *lā yartaddu ilayhim ṭarfuhum*. Same operator, negated. Day of Judgment.
4. **13:41 / 21:44** — *min aṭrāf al-arḍ*. Earth eaten from the edges. *Sarīʿ al-ḥisāb* attached.
5. **42:45** — furtive gaze of the damned. The shame-clause of the eye.
6. **37:48, 38:52, 55:56** — *qāṣirātu al-ṭarf*. Paradise modesty.

## Rabbit holes opened

1. **The √rdd + √Trf collocation.** 27:40 has *yartadda + ṭarfuk*; 14:43 has *yartaddu + ṭarfuhum*. These are the only two such collocations in the corpus. Worth a dedicated √rdd profile to see what else *return* does — the data shows *rdd* is a common verb but its pairing with *ṭarf* is a 2-verse exclusive.
2. **The "knowledge from the Book" agent in 27:40.** Not named, not described. Translators read him variously as Asaf b. Barakhya (rabbinic), as al-Khiḍr, as an angel. The Arabic just says *alladhī ʿindahu ʿilmun min al-kitāb*. The agent is **knowledge-anchored** and **anonymous** — not a prophet, not a jinn. A category of one. Worth a separate entry under 1-narratives.
3. **The "min aṭrāf al-arḍ" reading.** Geological? Geopolitical? Cosmological? Translators are split. A dedicated probe of √nqS (to diminish, decrease) co-occurrences could clarify whether 13:41/21:44 sits in a "shrinking-world" idiom-cluster or a "conquering-edges" idiom-cluster.
4. **Why no verb of Trf?** Compare to roots like √nZr (look) or √rAy (see) where verbs dominate. The Trf nominal-only profile is unusual. Hypothesis: *ṭarf* names not an action but **the smallest unit of perception**, and the Quran refuses to verbalize it because to do so would commit a subject — and the smallest unit of perception belongs to no one. (Speculative — flagged for testing.)
5. **The aqrab clause in 16:77 — does the Quran ever exceed its own comparators elsewhere?** A search for *aw aqrab* / *aw a-X* hyperbolic capstones would tell us whether 16:77 is unique or part of a pattern.
