# Zhr (ظهر) — "to appear, become manifest, surface"

> The Quran's primary vocabulary for **becoming-visible** — but with a twist. The same root supplies (a) the verb "to appear/be manifest," (b) the noun "back" (as in the back of a body or a camel), (c) a kinship-formula for divorce ("you are like my mother's back"), (d) the noun for "helper/supporter," and (e) one half of the divine-name pair *al-Ẓāhir / al-Bāṭin* at 57:3. Five semantic territories from one root. Where *gyb* is monolithic (49 of 60 occurrences are *al-ghayb*), *Zhr* is pluralistic — 10 lemmas, 59 occurrences, with the load distributed across at least four distinct senses. The unifying intuition is *the back-or-outside-surface*: what is on the *outer face* of something, what backs something up, what comes to the surface, what protrudes outward into view.

## Stats

- **Occurrences:** 59
- **Unique lemmas:** 10 (the highest lemma-count of any root in the teleportation cluster so far)
- **POS split:** N 31 / V 26 / ADJ 2 — much more verbal than `gyb` (which was 59 N / 1 V)
- **Ayahs:** 57
- **Surahs:** 30
- **Top co-occurring roots:** `Alh` (Allah, 27 ayahs / 47%), `qwl` (saying, 23 / 40%), `kwn` (being, 13 / 23%), `Elm` (knowledge, 12 / 21%), `rbb` (Lord, 10 / 18%), `$rk` (associate-partners, 8 / 14%), `kfr` (disbelieve, 8 / 14%), `Hqq` (truth, 8 / 14%)

The `Alh` co-occurrence at 47% indicates that nearly half of all *Zhr* verses are *theologically valenced* — about manifestation in religious context. The `qwl` co-occurrence at 40% is from the *zihar* divorce-formula verses (saying "you are to me as my mother's back") and from the prophets-saying-things verses. The fact that *Zhr* and *Elm* (knowledge) co-occur only at 21% — versus *gyb* and *Elm* at 58% — is itself a finding: **what is *manifest* is not particularly knowledge-coded. What is *unseen* is heavily knowledge-coded.** Manifestation in the Quran is a register of *appearance*, not of *epistemic claim*.

The *time-noun* at 24:58 is striking: *al-ẓahīrah* = "noon," when the sun is *most manifest* (highest, brightest, casting no shadow). Even the time-of-day for "midday rest" derives from the manifestation root. The *adverbial* at 11:92 (*ittakhadhtumūhu warāʾakum ẓihriyyā*) is the inverse: God taken "behind your back, neglected" — the back is what you *don't face*. The same root captures both *most-visible* (noon) and *placed-out-of-view* (behind-the-back). The unifier is *back/outer-surface as a directional vector*: noon is the visible-side of the day; behind-back is the visible-side of the body, but turned away from the viewed object.

## Five semantic territories

### Territory 1 — The body/object back (the concrete sense, 15× + derivatives)
- **2:189** — "It is not righteousness that you enter houses from their *backs* (*ẓuhūrihā*)."
- **6:31, 9:35, 21:39, 84:10** — burdens carried *on backs*; punishment branding their *backs*; record given *behind the back* on Judgment Day.
- **6:138, 6:146, 43:13** — backs of cattle (food laws, riding).
- **2:101, 3:187** — Scripture flung *behind their backs* (*warāʾa ẓuhūrihim*) — turning away from revelation.
- **94:3** — Muhammad's burden which "weighed upon your *back*" (*anqaḍa ẓahrak*).
- **35:45, 42:33** — *the surface*, e.g., God leaving no creature on *the surface* of the earth (35:45).
- **7:172** — children of Adam taken from *the loins/backs* of Adam.

This territory is **the most concrete and literal**. The "back" is the spinal-rear surface, and by extension *the riding-surface* (43:13), *the carrying-surface* for burdens, *the rejecting-direction* (2:101, 3:187 — flinging behind-back as iconic disregard). Notice: *ẓahr* is never the spiritual or hidden side. It is always the *physical, surface, weight-bearing, visible-but-turned* face.

### Territory 2 — The verb "to appear" (10× *ẓahara* + 8× *aẓhara*)
- **6:151, 7:33** — "*indecencies, what is apparent (mā ẓahara) and what is hidden (mā baṭana)*" — the *ẓhr/bṭn* paired binary at the *moral-act* level.
- **9:8** — *yaẓharū*, "if they prevail [over you]." Manifest = come out on top.
- **9:33, 48:28, 61:9** — the canonical *li-yuẓhirahu ʿalā al-dīni kullihi* ("to make it manifest above all religion"). This is **the manifestation-as-prevailing formula**, repeated three times in the corpus.
- **9:48** — *wa ẓahara amrullāh* — "the command of Allah was made manifest" — manifestation = victory of the divine decree.
- **18:20, 18:97** — *yaẓharū / yaẓharūhu*, "they cannot scale [the wall]" — manifest = surmount, top, overcome. This is the *ẓhr* root using "appear above" to mean "physically rise over." Built into the verb is the up-and-over geometry.
- **24:31** — *illā mā ẓahara minhā* — "except what [necessarily] appears" — appears = becomes externally visible despite intent to conceal.
- **30:41** — *ẓahara al-fasād fī al-barri wa al-baḥr* — "corruption has appeared on land and sea." Manifestation = surfacing, becoming evident.
- **40:26** — Pharaoh: "I fear he will *make manifest* (*yuẓhira*) corruption in the land."
- **66:3** — "Allah *made it manifest* to him" (*aẓharahu Allāhu ʿalayhi*) — disclosure of a hidden conversation.

The verb has at least three sub-senses: (a) *to appear* (24:31, 30:41), (b) *to prevail / overcome* (9:33, 18:97), (c) *to disclose* (66:3). All three trace back to the back-as-protrusion: what comes onto the back-surface, what rises above, what is brought to the visible side.

### Territory 3 — The active-participle *ẓāhir* (8×)
- **6:120** — *ẓāhira al-ithm wa bāṭinahu* — "outward sin and inward."
- **18:22** — *jidālan ẓāhirā* — "an obvious dispute."
- **30:7** — *yaʿlamūna ẓāhiran min al-ḥayāti al-dunyā* — "they know what is *outward* of the worldly life." This verse alone is a textbook of *ẓāhir* as *surface-knowing*: the disbelievers' epistemic horizon stops at the visible/outer face of this world.
- **31:20** — *niʿamahu ẓāhiratan wa bāṭinah* — "His blessings, outward and inward."
- **34:18** — *quran ẓāhirah* — "[towns] visible [from one another]" — the only place *Zhr* is used for *spatial* visibility. Sheba's structured travel-network: cities placed within line-of-sight.
- **40:29** — *ẓāhirīn fī al-arḍ* — "dominant/uppermost in the land" — the prevalent-ones.
- **57:3** — *huwa al-Awwal wa al-Ākhir wa al-Ẓāhir wa al-Bāṭin* — the divine-name verse.
- **61:14** — *fa-aṣbaḥū ẓāhirīn* — "and they became uppermost" (the believers prevailing over their enemies).
- **57:13** — *ẓāhiruhu min qibalihi al-ʿadhāb* — "its outer side is from the direction of punishment" (the wall between believers and hypocrites on Judgment Day).

**Territory 3 is the divine-name territory** — and it pairs *ẓāhir* with *bāṭin* (root `bṭn`) **three times** at the same syntactic level: 6:120 (sin), 31:20 (blessings), 57:3 (divine names). The *ẓāhir/bāṭin* pair is therefore a *Quranic structural binary* — not just a divine attribute pair, but a vocabulary the Quran uses for any phenomenon with an outer and inner face. The divine names are the **theological apex** of this binary, not its origin.

### Territory 4 — The helper/supporter (*ẓahīr*, 6×; *taẓāhara/ẓāharu*, 8×)
- **17:88, 25:55, 28:17, 28:86, 34:22, 66:4** — *ẓahīrā* / *ẓahīrun* in the sense of "helper, backer, support."
- **2:85, 9:4, 33:26, 60:9, 66:4** — Form III/VI verbs of "to back each other up" / "to gang up."
- **28:48** — *siḥrāni taẓāharā* — Pharaoh's people: "two magics supporting each other" (their dismissal of Moses + Muhammad's revelations as mutually-reinforcing magic-acts).

The semantic logic: a *ẓahīr* is one who *stands at your back*, has your back, supports you from behind. From the same body-part image as Territory 1, but functionalized into a social role.

### Territory 5 — The *ẓihār* divorce-formula (6×)
- **33:4** — definitional: God has not made your wives whom you say are *ka-ẓahri ummahātikum* (like your mother's back) into actual mothers. The formula refers to a pre-Islamic divorce act where the husband says "you are to me as the back of my mother" — invoking an incest-prohibition to functionally divorce. The Quran rejects this: words don't unmake reality.
- **58:2-3** — the legal correction: those who pronounce *ẓihār* must atone (free a slave, fast, feed) before resuming marriage.

This is a **specialized semantic offshoot**. The use of *ẓahr* (mother's back) in the formula is itself a metaphor — invoking the back as the *non-sexual, kinship-marked* surface of the body, vs. the front (sexual access). The formula uses *ẓhr* as the asexual face of the body. The Quran preserves the formula's vocabulary while overriding its legal consequence.

## The 57:3 anchor — *al-Ẓāhir wa al-Bāṭin*

The single most theologically dense use of *Zhr* is at 57:3:

> هُوَ ٱلْأَوَّلُ وَٱلْآخِرُ وَٱلظَّاهِرُ وَٱلْبَاطِنُ
> *huwa al-Awwal wa al-Ākhir wa al-Ẓāhir wa al-Bāṭin*

Two pairs of divine names, totaling four. The first pair is *temporal* (First / Last); the second pair is *visibility-modal* (Manifest / Hidden). Translation divergence (Jaccard 0.52, regime "mid"):

| Translator | *al-Ẓāhir* | *al-Bāṭin* |
|---|---|---|
| Saheeh International | "the Ascendant" | "the Intimate" |
| Pickthall | "the Outward" | "the Inward" |
| Khattab | "the Most High" | "the Most Near" |
| Arberry | "the Outward" | "the Inward" |

**Saheeh and Khattab take the divine-name reading allegorical/honorific** (Ascendant, Most High; Intimate, Most Near). **Pickthall and Arberry take it literal/structural** (Outward / Inward). The literal reading aligns with the *ẓhr/bṭn* binary as it appears in 6:120 and 31:20 — manifest-surface and inner-recess. The allegorical reading converts the surface/depth metaphor into a transcendence/immanence pairing.

The *literal* reading is structurally consistent with the rest of the Quran's *ẓāhir/bāṭin* uses. The *allegorical* reading inserts theological commentary. **For the teleportation investigation, the literal reading matters more**: 57:3 says God is *both* the manifest face and the inner reality of all things. There is no place — visible or hidden — where God is not. **This is the cosmic anchor for why teleportation works without contradiction**: the moment between A and B, when an object is "in-transit-and-unseen," is fully covered by *al-Bāṭin*. Nothing falls outside the manifest-and-hidden coverage.

Cross-reference: this matches the time-investigation's research-divine-vs-creature-time finding that the *al-Awwal / al-Ākhir* pair is the temporal version of the same coverage logic. **God's attribute-coverage works the same way in both registers — temporal (first-and-last) and visibility (manifest-and-hidden).** Both pairs use the *X-and-not-X* structure to assert "no gap in this dimension where God is not."

## The "make-it-prevail-above-all-religion" formula (9:33, 48:28, 61:9)

Three near-identical verses use *li-yuẓhirahu ʿalā al-dīni kullihi*:

- **9:33** — *huwa alladhī arsala rasūlahu bi al-hudā wa dīni al-ḥaqqi li-yuẓhirahu ʿalā al-dīni kullihi*
- **48:28** — same, with *kafā bi-llāhi shahīdā* (and Allah suffices as witness) appended
- **61:9** — same as 9:33, with anti-polytheist wrap

The verb *li-yuẓhirahu* — "that He may make it manifest" — uses the Form IV causative *aẓhara*. The translations diverge significantly:

| Translator | rendering of *li-yuẓhirahu ʿalā al-dīni kullihi* |
|---|---|
| Saheeh | "to manifest it over all religion" |
| Pickthall (9:33) | "that He may cause it to prevail over all religion" |
| Pickthall (48:28) | "that He may cause it to prevail over all religion" |
| Pickthall (61:9) | "that He may make it conqueror of all religion" |
| Khattab | "making it prevail over all others" |
| Arberry | "that He may uplift it above every religion" |

Saheeh keeps the *manifestation* sense; Pickthall slides to *prevail* (most political); Khattab slides to *prevail over*; Arberry to *uplift above*. The Arabic *aẓhara ʿalā* is **idiomatically two-faced**: it can mean *to make manifest above* (visibility) or *to make prevail over* (dominance). The Quran's choice of *Zhr* for this central political-religious claim leverages exactly this ambiguity — the religion's *manifestation* and its *prevalence* are not separable. To be visible above all religion is also to be supreme over all religion, because *ẓuhūr* is structurally the *up-and-over* geometry.

**The strongest divergence cluster is on the *ẓāhir/bāṭin* surface/depth pairs** at 6:120, 7:33, 31:20. Modern translators (Khattab) flatten the metaphor into "open/secret" or "seen/unseen"; older translators (Pickthall, Arberry) preserve the topological metaphor "outward/inward." **This means the metaphysical reading at 57:3 is partially erased in modern English versions** — by the time the reader gets to "Most High and Most Near" (Khattab), the cross-link to the surface/depth verses elsewhere is no longer textually visible. Pickthall and Arberry preserve the consistent vocabulary; Saheeh and Khattab modernize it.

## Negative space — what Zhr does NOT do

1. **It does not refer to the divine essence as "manifest" in the visual sense.** Despite *al-Ẓāhir* being a divine name, the Quran never says God is *seen*. *Ẓuhūr* is structural, not perceptual: God is the manifest *register* — the surface-side of being itself — but no visible object is identified with God.
2. **It is rarely paired with *gyb*.** The natural English pair "manifest / unseen" would invite the Arabic pair *ẓāhir / ghāʾib*. **The Quran does not use this pair.** Its pairs are *ẓāhir / bāṭin* (manifest / hidden, 6:120, 31:20, 57:3) and *ghayb / shahādah* (unseen / witnessed, the eight-verse *ʿālim al-ghayb wa al-shahādah* formula). The two visibility-binaries operate in parallel but do not cross. **This is a structural finding**: the Arabic of the Quran maintains two distinct visibility-binaries for two distinct standpoints. *Ẓāhir/bāṭin* is the *object's own* outside-and-inside; *ghayb/shahādah* is the *observer's* access-and-no-access.
3. **It is never used for revelation as such.** The verb-of-disclosure for the Quran is *anzala* (sent down) or *waḥā* (inspired), not *aẓhara*. *Aẓhara* is for political prevalence (9:33), for showing what was concealed (66:3), for sin appearing (30:41). The *coming-to-the-surface* is in human or worldly registers, not in revelation.
4. **It is not used for resurrection.** The natural metaphor "appear from the dead" might use *Zhr*, but the Quran prefers *baʿatha* (raise) and *aḥyā* (give life). *Ẓuhūr* stays bound to outer-surface manifestation, not to coming-back-from-non-being.
5. **It is rarely linked to *Elm* (knowledge).** Only 21% co-occurrence vs 58% for *gyb*. **What appears does not need to be known**; what is unseen is the *content* of knowledge. Manifestation is a register of *being-out-there*; knowledge belongs more to the inner side.

## Cross-cutting note (for full development see the gyb file)

The teleportation arc is *visible at A → out-of-view → visible at B*. Three roots, three different jobs:

- **Visible at A and at B**: marked with *Zhr* (manifest) or *raʾā* ("saw").
- **The transit gap (out-of-view)**: marked implicitly with *gyb* (the unseen) — but the Quran refuses to *narrate* this phase. The hoopoe leaves and arrives; the throne departs and reappears. The gap is the *empty space* in the text.
- **Always-visible-to-God**: marked with *al-Ẓāhir* + *al-Bāṭin* (57:3), or with *ʿālim al-ghayb wa al-shahādah*. Both pairs assert: there is no register where God is not.

The asymmetry between the divine-name pairs *Ẓāhir/Bāṭin* and the formula *ghayb/shahādah* is the **load-bearing distinction for teleportation**. The throne is *bāṭin* during transit (interior to itself, unseen by humans) — but never *ghāʾib* in the divine register, because *al-ghayb* is precisely what God *knows the keys to* (6:59). What teleportation reveals is that **the human-side gap (*ghayb*) and the object's interiority (*bāṭin*) overlap in the moment of transit, but the divine-coverage spans both**.

## Rabbit holes

1. **The *li-yuẓhirahu ʿalā al-dīni* triple-repetition (9:33, 48:28, 61:9).** Three near-identical verses across different sūrahs. Why repeat? Compare to *al-Awwal/al-Ākhir* (only one occurrence at 57:3): some claims get hammered, others stated once. The triple statement of *manifestation-as-prevalence* may itself be a textual *ẓuhūr*-act — making the claim outwardly visible by repetition.
2. **The *ẓāhir/bāṭin* pairing as a structural template.** It surfaces at three levels: moral acts (sin, indecency — 6:120, 7:33, 6:151), divine blessings (31:20), divine names (57:3). Is the Quran teaching by analogy? Sin has an outer face and an inner; favors have an outer and inner; God Himself is the manifest and the hidden. **The structure may be: every real thing has a *ẓāhir* and a *bāṭin*, and the divine-name verse asserts that God is the maximally-saturating instance of this binary.**
3. **The 18:97 *yaẓharūhu* — Gog and Magog couldn't surmount the wall.** The verb here means *climb over*, in a literal physical sense. This is the "back" geometry of *Zhr* most plainly: to *appear* is to *climb up onto the back/top of*. Worth comparing to the *fawq* (above) verses to see if they collide.
4. **The hoopoe's report to Solomon does not use *Zhr*.** When the hoopoe finds Sheba's people "prostrating to the sun" (27:24), the verb is *yasjudūn*, not *yaẓharūn*. The hoopoe's *visible discovery* is not narrated as a *manifestation*. **This may suggest *Zhr* is reserved for *self-disclosure* rather than *being-discovered*.** Sheba's idolatry was always there; the hoopoe just reported it. Nothing about its becoming-visible-to-Solomon is *aẓhara*-coded. Compare to 66:3 where God *aẓhara* a private conversation to the Prophet — this is *active disclosure*, not passive being-found-out.
5. **The 30:7 disbelievers' epistemic horizon.** *Yaʿlamūna ẓāhiran min al-ḥayāti al-dunyā wa hum ʿan al-ākhirati hum ghāfilūn* — they know an *outer* of this life and are heedless of the next. **This verse explicitly couples *ẓāhir-knowledge* with *ghaflah* (heedlessness about the unseen).** Surface-knowing is the disbelievers' epistemic ceiling. Compare to the believer at 2:3 who *believes in al-ghayb*. **The contrast is: believers' epistemics span ghayb + shahādah; disbelievers' epistemics stop at the *ẓāhir*.** This is one of the cleanest verse-pair contrasts in the corpus.
6. **The *ẓhr / Awwal-Ākhir* parallel at 57:3.** The verse runs four divine names back-to-back. The temporal pair (First/Last) is followed by the visibility pair (Manifest/Hidden). **Both pairs use the same logical move**: extreme + opposite-extreme = total coverage. Pull the cross-investigation file from the time concept-set: this same template *also* appears in 57:1-3 in the cosmogonic framing. Sura 57 (al-Ḥadīd) opens with three coverage-formulas in three verses. It may be the corpus' densest concentration of "no-gap-where-God-is-not" claims.
7. **The 24:58 noon-as-ẓahīrah.** Why is *noon* a *Zhr*-derivative? Because at noon the sun is *ẓāhir* in two senses — at its highest (apex) and casting no shadow (most exposed). Time-of-day vocabulary mapping onto manifestation-vocabulary: a small detail but the kind of root-extension that suggests the Arabic conceptual web treats the *up-most* as the *most-visible* by definition. Worth checking against *fajr* (dawn), *ʿaṣr* (afternoon), *maghrib* (sunset) — do other prayer-times map to other phenomenological registers?
