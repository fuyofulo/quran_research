# Concept: Awy — take refuge, give shelter, abode

**Headline.** The root أوي governs sheltering motion. It has two sides — one verbal (an actor takes refuge or another shelters them), one nominal (the place itself: `maʾwā`, refuge-place). Of 36 occurrences, 22 are the noun `maʾwā` and 14 are verbs (`āwā` form IV "to give shelter," 9×; `awā` form I "to take refuge," 5×). The verbal side is intransitive plus a place-preposition: `awā ilā l-kahf` — "took refuge IN/TO the cave" (18:10). The nominal side is overwhelmingly eschatological: of 22 `maʾwā` instances, the majority designate Hell or Paradise as the final dwelling-place. The same lexeme that tells you where the Cave Sleepers HID is the lexeme that tells you where the unbelievers WILL DWELL. Refuge in time is structurally identical to refuge in eschatology.

## Stats
- 36 occurrences across 36 ayahs in 23 surahs.
- POS: 22 N, 14 V.
- 3 lemmas: `maʾwā` (مَأْوَىٰ — refuge-place, 22×); `āwā` (ءَاوَىٰ — form IV, give shelter, 9×); `awā` (أَوَى — form I, take refuge, 5×).
- Distribution: 17 Medinan, 19 Meccan — almost evenly split. Surah concentration: Anfāl 8 (4 occurrences — covers BOTH the historical hijra-shelter and the eschatological refuge), Āl ʿImrān 3 (3 — eschatological), Kahf 18 (3 — the Cave verbs), Maʾārij 70, Naziʿāt 79 (2, eschatological), Sajda 32 (2 — both maʾwā), and a long tail.

The morphology is clean. Form I is the actor entering the refuge; form IV is someone (host, parent, God) putting an actor INTO refuge; the nominal `maʾwā` is the locus.

## Distribution: the two halves

**Half A — verbs of historical refuge-taking.** Concentrated in the prophetic narratives of Yūsuf, Lūṭ, Mūsā/ʿĪsā, the Cave Sleepers, and Muhammad himself. Form-I `awā` is concentrated in Kahf (18:10, 18:16, 18:63 — three of the five form-I occurrences). Form-IV `āwā` is the verb of the Yūsuf reunions (12:69 — Yūsuf takes Binyāmīn aside; 12:99 — Yūsuf receives his parents into Egypt), of the rabwa for Maryam-and-ʿĪsā (23:50), of the orphan-Muhammad (93:6 `alam yajidka yatīman fa-āwā`), and of the believers' shelter after hijra (8:26, 8:72, 8:74). 11:80 has Lūṭ wishing he could `āwī ilā ruknin šadīd` — "take refuge in a strong support" — under the pressure of his people.

**Half B — `maʾwā` as eschatological terminus.** Of 22 `maʾwā` occurrences, the dominant collocation is `wa-maʾwāhumu n-nār` / `… al-jaḥīm` (and their dwelling is the Fire / Hellfire). 3:151, 3:162, 3:197, 4:97, 4:121, 5:72, 8:16, 9:73, 9:95, 10:8, 13:18, 17:97, 24:57, 29:25, 45:34, 57:15, 66:9 — the formulaic Hell-as-refuge appears 17 times. Inversely, 32:19 `jannātu l-maʾwā` (Gardens of Refuge), 79:41 `fa-inna l-jannata hiya l-maʾwā` (then Paradise is the refuge), and the high-station `maʾwā` of 53:15 (`ʿindahā jannatu l-maʾwā` — by the Lote-tree, the Garden-of-Refuge). 79:39 makes the negative pole explicit: `fa-inna l-jaḥīma hiya l-maʾwā` — Hell is the refuge. The matched-pair 79:39/79:41 is decisive: the same word names BOTH destinations within two ayahs of each other. The `maʾwā` is THE final dwelling-place, valence-neutral.

## The Cave Sleepers passage — 18:10 + 18:16

Both verses use form I. 18:10: `i*i awā l-fityatu ilā l-kahfi` — "when the youths took refuge IN the cave." 18:16 is uttered BY the youths to one another: `wa-i*i ʿtazaltumūhum wa-mā yaʿbudūna illā llāha fa-ʾwū ilā l-kahf` — "and when you have withdrawn (`iʿtazala`) from them and what they worship except Allah, then take refuge (`ʾwū`, imperative) in the cave."

Two things to notice. First, 18:16 stacks `iʿtazala` (a SECOND withdrawal-verb) WITH `awā`: the sequence is "first you withdraw from the people, THEN you take refuge in the cave." Withdrawal is the first move; taking-refuge is the second. The cave is not entered cold; it is entered after a separation. Second, the verb `awā` here precedes the ENTIRE 309-year sleep; the surah uses no transit-verb between 18:10 and 18:11 (where God then "strikes their ears" with sleep). The refuge IS the time-displacement event's threshold. This is the pattern: refuge → unconscious passage of time → re-emergence in a different age. The verb `awā` performs the threshold work that explicit teleportation language would in a different scripture.

18:63 is the only form-I `awā` outside the Cave-Sleepers passage in this surah, used by Mūsā's young companion: `i*i awaynā ilā l-ṣaxra` — "when we took refuge by the rock." The fish-leaving-into-the-sea miracle happens AT this refuge-point. Refuge-then-miracle is the local pattern.

## The "place of refuge" — `maʾwā`

The surface meaning of `maʾwā` is "the place one resorts to" — a destination noun derived from the verb. But in Quranic deployment it has shifted almost entirely toward the eschatological terminus. A reader could compile a study just from the `wa-maʾwāhum jahannam` formula (17 of the 22 nominals fit this skeleton). The five non-eschatological nominals are 3:197 (a feeble worldly enjoyment, then their `maʾwā` is Jahannam — still Hell-bound but framed as the next stop after worldly comfort), 7:163 (the Sabbath-breakers — implicit), and 9:73 / 9:95 / 24:57 / 57:15 / 66:9 — most of these still resolve to Hell. The Quran has effectively REPURPOSED the everyday word "shelter-place" as the standard expression for "final dwelling." This is a major semantic shift documented in the data.

## Co-occurring roots

`Alh` (14 ayahs), `qwl` (11), `bAs` (10 — wretched), `kwn` (8), `nwr` (8 — Hell-fire), `Syr` (7 — destination/becoming), `kfr` (7), `nSr` (6), `Amn` (4), `jnn` (4 — Paradise/Garden). The eschatological co-occurrences (`bAs`, `nwr`, `Syr`, `kfr`, `jnn`) dominate. Notice that `Hml` (carry) does NOT appear in the top co-occurring roots — the carrying-and-the-sheltering rarely pair on the same ayah, suggesting these are two different mobility moves.

23:50 is the exception worth flagging: `wa-jaʿalnā bna Maryama wa-ummahu āyatan wa-āwaynāhumā ilā rabwatin* dhāti qarārin wa-maʿīn` — "We made Maryam's son and his mother a sign, and gave them refuge upon a height (rabwa) with a stable resting-place and a flowing spring." This is the form-IV `āwā` applied directly to Maryam-and-ʿĪsā post-birth. The grammatical subject is God; the object is the dyad; the destination is a `rabwa` (height) — a different lexeme than Maryam's earlier `makānan qaṣiyyan` (19:22). This verse joins the Maryam strand to the Awy root: she is THEN sheltered after withdrawing. Read 19:22 (`intabadhat`) → 23:50 (`āwaynāhumā`) as a sequence: Maryam's self-removal is followed by a divine sheltering.

## Translation divergence

18:10 and 18:16 are HIGH-divergence verses (combined=0.635, 0.648; jaccard=0.385, 0.391). On 18:10, Saheeh "retreated to the cave," Pickthall "fled for refuge to the Cave," Khattab "took refuge in the cave," Arberry "took refuge in the Cave." Pickthall is hardest on the verb (FLED — adds urgency the Arabic does not specify), Saheeh's "retreated" is the weakest (military overtones). "Took refuge" is the cleaner gloss — it preserves the verb's intentional, sheltering valence without reading panic.

On 18:16, the imperative `fa-ʾwū` is rendered "retreat" (Saheeh), "seek refuge" (Pickthall), "take refuge" (Khattab), "take refuge" (Arberry). Saheeh's "retreat" is again the outlier; the others converge on "take refuge."

For 11:80 (Lūṭ wishing he could `āwī ilā ruknin šadīd`), divergence is interesting: Saheeh "could take refuge in a strong support," Pickthall "had some strong support (among you)," Khattab "could rely on a strong supporter," Arberry "might take refuge in a strong pillar." Pickthall and Khattab both abandon the spatial refuge image and read it as an abstract "support/supporter"; Saheeh and Arberry preserve the spatial figure (refuge in a pillar). This is the classic divergence between literal-spatial and abstract-pragmatic readings of Quranic refuge-language.

For 93:6 (the Muhammad orphan-shelter verse `alam yajidka yatīman fa-āwā`), all four translators agree on a sheltering verb ("give refuge" / "shelter" / "protect"). Low divergence. The most theologically dense of the Awy verses is also the least translation-disputed.

## Negative space — what Awy does NOT do

It is never used for journey or migration as such (that is `hjr`, `sry`, `s-y-r`). It is never used for the descent of revelation. It is never used for the carrying-motion (`Hml`) — a sheltered person is in the place; a carried person is in transit. The Awy root marks the END-STATE of a movement, not the movement itself. This is critical for the teleportation cluster: when a body lands in a new place, two distinct lexemes can describe the resulting situation — `maʾwā` (it is now their refuge/dwelling) or `mustaqarr` (it is now their settled-place). The Quran's vocabulary distinguishes the action of taking-refuge from the action of being-carried; the same body, the same place, but different roots cover different facets.

## Verses for direct study

- 18:10 + 18:16 — Cave Sleepers' refuge (form I, threshold of time-displacement).
- 18:63 — Mūsā's companion at the rock (refuge → miracle pattern).
- 23:50 — Maryam-and-ʿĪsā sheltered on the rabwa (form IV, divine subject).
- 12:69 + 12:99 — Yūsuf welcoming brother / parents (form IV, intra-family shelter).
- 11:80 — Lūṭ's wish for a `rukn šadīd` (the abstract-vs-spatial divergence point).
- 93:6 — Allah finding Muhammad orphaned and sheltering him (form IV, divine).
- 79:39 + 79:41 — Hell-is-the-maʾwā / Paradise-is-the-maʾwā (the ambivalent terminus).
- 32:19 — `jannātu l-maʾwā` (Gardens of Refuge) — positive eschatological maʾwā.

## Rabbit holes

1. **Cave + Maryam parallel.** Both Cave Sleepers and Maryam withdraw, then a miracle of suspended/altered time happens. Cave Sleepers: `iʿtazala` + `awā` → 309 years compressed to a perceived day. Maryam: `intabadha` (`nb*`) → conception without male contact. Both pair WITHDRAWAL with a time/causality miracle. See cross-cutting in `concept-Hml.md`.

2. **The orphan-Muhammad parallel with Yūsuf.** 93:6 (`fa-āwā`) uses the same form-IV verb as 12:69 / 12:99. Allah's sheltering of the orphan Prophet parallels Yūsuf's gathering of his exiled family. The structural echo is intentional and worth tracing across surahs.

3. **`maʾwā` as eschatological default.** Why has the Quran's everyday word for "shelter-place" become almost exclusively the Hell-or-Paradise word? The semantic pressure of recurring formulaic ayahs (`wa-maʾwāhum jahannam`) has narrowed the noun. Compare to `dār` (abode) and `qarār` (resting-place) — does each of these undergo the same semantic narrowing?

4. **Form I vs Form IV agency.** Form I `awā` puts the SUBJECT in motion toward refuge (Cave Sleepers acting). Form IV `āwā` puts an OBJECT into refuge by another's action (Allah sheltering Muhammad, Yūsuf sheltering brother). The Quran's choice of form encodes WHO is the agent of refuge. Track the distribution of subjects: who self-shelters and who is sheltered? Form I appears with prophets and seekers (Lūṭ, Mūsā's companion, Cave Sleepers, Ibrāhīm in 11:43); form IV's subject is overwhelmingly God (8:26, 23:50, 93:6) or a senior protector (Yūsuf, 12:69/99). The morphology encodes a theology of refuge.

5. **Hijra and Awy.** 8:72 / 8:74 use form-IV plural participles `āwaw wa-naṣarū` ("sheltered and helped") — the technical formula for the Anṣār of Madīna receiving the muhājirūn. The Awy root carries the Quran's name for the host community of the hijra. Worth pursuing in a separate study tying the historical sheltering act to the eschatological maʾwā.
