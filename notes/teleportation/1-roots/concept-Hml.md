# Concept: Hml — to carry, bear, lift, undertake

**Headline.** The root حمل governs every kind of bearing-of-a-burden in the Quran: a mother carries a fetus, an Ark carries a remnant, angels carry a Throne, a donkey carries books, a wife carries firewood, the heavens-and-earth-and-mountains REFUSE to carry the Trust and humanity carries it instead. With 64 occurrences, Hml is the largest of the four situational-mobility roots in this cluster — it is roughly equal in size to nb*, Awy, and HDr COMBINED. The lexeme covers physical conveyance, gestational carrying, ethical bearing, and cosmological transport. The Throne-of-the-Lord and the Ark-of-Noah share the same verb; the burdened sinner and the burdened mother share the same verb; the angels who bear the Throne and the donkey who carries unread books share the same verb. This is the cosmic-utility lexeme of the Quran's transfer-between-places.

## Stats
- 64 occurrences across 50 ayahs in 29 surahs.
- POS: 49 V, 15 N.
- 10 lemmas — the most morphologically diverse of the four cluster-roots.
- Distribution: 23 Medinan, 41 Meccan. Surah concentration: Baqara 2, Ṭā-Hā 20, ʿAnkabūt 29, Ṭalāq 65 (4 each), then Anʿām 6, Aʿrāf 7, Maryam 19, Aḥzāb 33, Fāṭir 35, Jumuʿa 62, Ḥāqqa 69 (3 each). Hml is broadly distributed across both eras, more strongly Meccan but with substantial Medinan use (especially in the family-/inheritance-context surahs).

The pattern: form I dominates (41×) and is the workhorse. Form II `ḥummila` is the divine/social ENTRUSTING (Torah given to the Jews, 62:5; the Trust laid on humanity in 33:72; commands placed on prophets/Muḥammad in 24:54). Form VIII `iḥtamala` is the moral SELF-BURDENING (4:112, 13:17, 33:58 — bearing slander, foam-on-the-flood, and falsehood). Each form keeps a distinct domain.

## The semantic field — five domains

**Domain 1 — Gestation and birth (≈10 occurrences).** Mothers conceiving and carrying.
- 19:22 `fa-ḥamalathu` — Maryam conceives.
- 19:27 `taḥmiluhu` — she returns to her people CARRYING him. Note: 19:22's `ḥamalathu` is conception, 19:27's `taḥmiluhu` is physical carrying — the same root spans the two.
- 31:14 `ḥamalathu ummuhu wahnan ʿalā wahn` — "his mother carried him in weakness upon weakness."
- 46:15 `ḥamalathu ummuhu kurhan wa-waḍaʿathu kurhan wa-ḥamluhu wa-fiṣāluhu thalāthūna šahran` — "carried with hardship, gave birth with hardship, his bearing and weaning thirty months."
- 7:189, 22:2 (the pregnant put down their loads on the Day), 35:11, 41:47, 65:4, 65:6 — pregnancy-and-its-load formula. The verbal noun `ḥaml` doubles as "the pregnancy" AND "the burden." Biological carrying is grammatically identical to mechanical carrying. This is a deliberate Quranic flatness.

**Domain 2 — Vehicular transport / divine conveyance (≈15 occurrences).**
- 11:40 `iḥmil fīhā min kullin zawjayni thnayn` — Noah's Ark, IMPERATIVE to load. The Ark carries the remnant.
- 17:3 `dhurriyyata man ḥamalnā maʿa Nūḥ` — descendants of those We CARRIED with Noah.
- 17:70 `wa-laqad karramnā banī ādama wa-ḥamalnāhum fī l-barri wa-l-baḥri` — "We honored the children of Adam and CARRIED them on land and sea."
- 23:22, 36:41, 40:80, 54:13, 69:11 — variants of the Ark/carrying-on-water formula.
- 2:248 `tāʾūtu … taḥmiluhu l-malāʾika` — "the Ark (of the Covenant) … the angels carrying it."
- 40:7 `alladhīna **yaḥmilūna l-ʿarš** wa-man ḥawlahu` — "those who CARRY the Throne and those around it." Angelic Throne-bearing.
- 69:17 `wa-yaḥmilu ʿarša rabbika fawqahum yawmaʾi*in **ṯamāniya**` — "EIGHT will bear the Throne of your Lord above them on that Day."
- 16:7 `wa-taḥmilu aṯqālakum` — beasts that carry your loads.
- 9:92 `lā ajidu mā **aḥmilukum** ʿalayh` — "I find nothing on which to mount you" (Tabūk camel-shortage).

**Domain 3 — Ethical/spiritual burden (≈12 occurrences).**
- 33:72 `wa-ḥamalahā l-insān` — "and humanity carried it (the Trust)." The cosmic-ethical burden ALL OTHER CREATION refused.
- 35:18 `wa-in tadʿu muthqalatun ilā ḥimlihā lā **yuḥmal** minhu šayʾun` — no one will carry another's burden.
- 6:31, 6:164, 16:25 (`la-yaḥmilunna aṯqālahum wa-aṯqālan maʿa aṯqālihim`), 29:12 (`wa-l-naḥmil xaṭāyākum wa-mā hum bi-**ḥāmilīn** min xaṭāyāhum min šayʾ` — disbelievers offering to bear the sins, refused), 29:13 — the burden-of-sin lexicon.
- 4:112 `wa-man yaksib xaṭīʾatan aw iṯman ṯumma yarmi bihi barīʾan **fa-qadi ḥtamala** buhtānan` — form VIII for the slander-bearer.
- 24:54 `fa-innamā ʿalayhi mā **ḥummila** wa-ʿalaykum mā **ḥummiltum**` — "on him is what was placed on him, on you what was placed on you" — passive form II for the apostolic-and-communal duty.
- 62:5 `mathalu lladhīna **ḥummilū** t-tawrāta ṯumma lam **yaḥmilūhā** ka-mathali l-ḥimāri **yaḥmilu** asfārā` — "the example of those entrusted with the Torah and then did not carry it is like the donkey carrying volumes." Three Hml forms in a single ayah. The simile makes book-on-back parallel to law-on-soul.
- 2:286 `wa-lā tuḥammilnā mā lā ṭāqata lanā bih` — "do not LAY upon us what we have no power to bear."

**Domain 4 — The cosmological / anomalous bearing (3 occurrences).**
- 51:2 `fa-l-**ḥāmilāti** wiqrā` — "by those (winds/clouds/ships?) carrying a load." The mysterious oath in Sūrat al-Dhāriyāt.
- 69:14 `wa-**ḥumilati** l-arḍu wa-l-jibālu` — "and the earth and the mountains will be LIFTED" on the Day. The geological cataclysm uses Hml passive.
- 22:2 `taḍaʿu kullu **ḥāmla** ḥamlahā` — "every pregnant one will lay down her burden." Pregnancy and apocalypse share the verb.

**Domain 5 — Animal and curse-figures (4 occurrences).**
- 6:142 `wa-mina l-anʿāmi **ḥamūlatan** wa-faršā` — "of livestock, beasts-of-burden and (those for) bedding."
- 7:176 (the dog-figure `in **taḥmil** ʿalayh yalhath`).
- 12:36 (`innī arānī **aḥmilu** fawqa raʾsī xubzan` — Yūsuf's prison-companion's dream of carrying bread on his head, which birds will eat).
- 12:72 (`wa-li-man jāʾa bihi **ḥimlu** baʿīr` — a camel-load-of-grain reward).
- 111:4 `wa-mraʾatuhu **ḥammālata** l-ḥaṭab` — "and his wife (Abū Lahab's), the carrier of firewood." Intensive participle, infamous epithet.

## Co-occurring roots

`Alh` (14 ayahs), `qwl` (12), `qwm` (11), `kwn` (9), `rbb` (8), `ywm` (6 — the Day), `wDE` (6 — laying-down, the gestational pair-verb), `Elm` (6), `Amn` (6), `$yA` (6), `kll` (5), `rḥm` (5). The pairing with `wDE` (set down, give birth) is significant: `ḥamala` and `waḍaʿa` form a fixed verb-pair for pregnancy → birth, used in 22:2, 35:11, 46:15, 65:4, 65:6. The `ḥamala`/`waḍaʿa` pair is the Quran's gestation-formula.

The pairing with `nb*` is unique to **19:22** — the only ayah where the four-root cluster has its tightest collision: Maryam **carries** (Hml) and **withdraws** (nb*) in a single verse. See cross-cutting #4 below.

## Translation divergence on key verses

- **40:7** (combined=0.59, jaccard=0.43) — `yaḥmilūna l-ʿarš` → "carry the Throne" (Saheeh, Khattab), "bear the Throne" (Pickthall, Arberry). Low semantic divergence.
- **69:17** (combined=0.47, jaccard=0.51) — `yaḥmilu ʿarša rabbika fawqahum yawmaʾi*in **ṯamāniya**` → all four converge on "eight (angels) will bear/uphold the Throne of your Lord above them, that Day." Pickthall is the most archaic; Khattab adds the interpretive `[mighty angels]`. The number EIGHT is undisputed.
- **11:40** (combined=0.65, jaccard=0.37) — `iḥmil fīhā min kullin zawjayni ṯnayn` → "Load upon the ship of each [creature] two mates" (Saheeh), "Load therein two of every kind, a pair" (Pickthall), "Take into the Ark a pair from every species" (Khattab — abandons the load-verb!), "Embark in it two of every kind" (Arberry — also abandons load-verb). Khattab and Arberry both translate `iḥmil` (imperative of carry) as TAKE/EMBARK rather than LOAD. The gloss "embark" in particular hides the carrying agency: it is the Ark that carries; the loading is the human side of that. Arberry's choice obscures the lexical link with 17:70 (`ḥamalnāhum fī l-barri wa-l-baḥri`).
- **31:14** (combined=0.61, jaccard=0.42, outlier=khattab) — `ḥamalathu ummuhu wahnan ʿalā wahn` → "carried him in weakness upon weakness" (Saheeh), "beareth him in weakness upon weakness" (Pickthall), "bore them through hardship upon hardship" (Khattab — pluralizes; outlier), "bore him in weakness upon weakness" (Arberry). Khattab's pluralization is a stylistic choice that obscures the direct mother-child concrete image.
- **62:5** (combined=0.69, jaccard=0.31 — VERY HIGH divergence) — three Hml forms in a single ayah, and translators diverge in HOW to render the donkey-comparison. Saheeh "entrusted with … did not take it on … donkey who carries volumes" preserves three distinct verbs. Pickthall "entrusted … apply it not … ass carrying books" — drops the "carrying" verb in the middle clause. Khattab "entrusted … failed to do so … donkey carrying books" similarly. Arberry "loaded with … not carried it … ass carrying books" preserves the carry-verb across all three. **Arberry alone preserves the Quran's repetition of `ḥummila → yaḥmil → yaḥmilu`.** This is one of the few cases where Arberry's literalism is decisive.
- **33:72** (combined=0.63, jaccard=0.37) — `wa-ḥamalahā l-insān` → "but man [undertook to] bear it" (Saheeh, brackets), "And man assumed it" (Pickthall — abandons "bear"!), "humanity assumed it" (Khattab), "and man carried it" (Arberry). Pickthall and Khattab use "assume/assumed"; Saheeh interpolates `[undertook to]`; Arberry alone keeps the literal `carried`. The translation choice is THEOLOGICALLY loaded — "assumed" makes the act sound voluntary-but-consequential; "carried" makes it concrete and burdensome.
- **46:15** (combined=0.69, jaccard=0.30 — also very high) — pregnancy formula. Translators diverge on `kurhan` (hardship vs reluctance). Pickthall "with reluctance" is the outlier; the others read "with hardship."
- **19:22** (combined=0.27, LOW divergence; outlier=pickthall) — `fa-ḥamalathu fa-ntabadhat bihi makānan qaṣiyyan` — all four say "she conceived him and withdrew with him." Pickthall's "far place" vs Saheeh/Khattab "remote place" / Arberry "distant place" is the only difference. The most resolved Hml verse in the corpus.

## Cross-cutting findings (the cluster lives here)

### 1. The QUIET VOCABULARY of teleportation

When the Quran wants to describe a body landing in a NEW LOCATION without the explicit speed-language of `sry` (night-journey), `rfʿ` (raising), or `ʿrj` (ascending), it reaches for FOUR situational-mobility roots:

- **nb*** — to be cast/cast oneself away (Maryam withdraws; Pharaoh hurled into the sea; Yūnus on the bare shore).
- **Awy** — to take refuge, to be sheltered (Cave Sleepers; Maryam-and-ʿĪsā on the rabwa; orphan Muhammad).
- **HDr** — to be made-present (deeds at Judgment; the resurrected around Hell; the soul's record).
- **Hml** — to be carried (angels bear the Throne; the Ark bears the remnant; the womb bears the child; the Trust on humanity's back).

Each root targets a different facet of "ending up somewhere." `nb*` is the violent ejective transfer. `Awy` is the sheltered settlement after transfer. `HDr` is the abstract appearance-without-described-transit. `Hml` is the borne carrying ITSELF — the conveyance verb. Together they form the implicit-transfer lexicon. **The Quran has no single verb of teleportation; it has a distributed vocabulary of FOUR transfer-aspects.**

### 2. The "brought-present" pattern lives at HDr, NOT at the Solomon throne

The user-prompt schematic claimed `muḥḍar al-ʿarsh` for 27:42. The Quranic text does not say this. 27:42 uses `jāʾat` (jyA) for the Queen's coming and `āṭīka` (Aty) for the bringing of the throne. **The throne-teleportation in surah 27 is built from `Aty` + `jyA` + `qrr` (settled) + `Hml`-adjacent — not HDr.** The structural parallel between Solomon's throne and the deeds-brought-present at Judgment is REAL (both are appearance-without-transit), but it is realized through DIFFERENT lexemes. This is a critical correction to the cluster thesis: the Quran's quiet vocabulary of teleportation includes at least SIX roots (the four above plus `Aty` and `jyA`), and each root's habitat must be examined separately. HDr is the eschatological-bringing root; `Aty`/`jyA` are the throne-bringing roots; the structural analogy holds at the SCENE level, not the WORD level.

### 3. The Ark + Throne parallel — Hml does cosmic and historical work with the same verb

40:7 and 69:17 — angels CARRY the Throne (cosmic, recurring, and at the End). 11:40 and 17:3 and 23:22 and 36:41 and 40:80 and 54:13 and 69:11 — God CARRIES (or commands the Ark to carry) Noah's people across the Flood (historical, unique, mid-time). 2:248 — angels CARRY the Ark of the Covenant to the Banū Isrāʾīl. **Three categories of cargo: a Throne, a remnant of humanity, an Ark.** All use the same verb `yaḥmilu` / `ḥamalnā`. The Quran has not differentiated cosmic and historical conveyance lexically; it uses Hml for both. This is significant: the act of God-or-angels-bearing-something is one act, regardless of whether the something is cosmological furniture (Throne) or biological remnant (Noah's family) or sacred relic (Ark of the Covenant). The verb flatness is theologically deliberate — God's transport-action is a single action with multiple objects.

The 69:17 specification of EIGHT throne-bearing angels is the only numerical specification in the Hml corpus. Eight is THE number for Throne-bearing on the Day. (40:7 does not specify a number for ordinary-time Throne-bearing.) Worth pursuing in eschatology study.

### 4. The Cave + Maryam parallel — withdrawal triggers miracle

The two great time-displacement / virgin-birth narratives share a structural pattern:

- **Cave Sleepers (18:10, 18:16):** `iʿtazala` (withdraw from people) + `awā` (take refuge in cave) → 309 years compressed to a perceived day.
- **Maryam (19:16, 19:22):** `intabadhat` (withdraw to an eastern place; later, withdraw with the conceived child to a far place) → virgin birth.

Both follow: WITHDRAWAL → ISOLATION → MIRACULOUS EVENT. The withdrawal is the TRIGGER; the miracle is the YIELD. The Quran's pattern: God's most extraordinary interventions happen when a witness has already cleared the social field. The Cave Sleepers' `awā` and Maryam's `intabadhat` are doing the same narrative work — preparing the empty space the miracle will fill.

The pair is reinforced by 23:50, where Maryam-and-ʿĪsā are sheltered (`āwaynāhumā`) on the rabwa — bringing Maryam's narrative INTO the Awy root. So the sequence for Maryam is: withdraw (`nb*`) → carry (`Hml`) → return-carrying (`Hml`) → be-sheltered (`Awy`). Across surah 19 and surah 23, three of the four cluster-roots act on Maryam's body. She is the ROOT-CONNECTING figure of the situational-mobility cluster.

### 5. The Hml-Trust verses reframe the cluster theologically

33:72 (the Trust offered to heaven, earth, mountains; refused; humanity carried it) is the philosophical anchor of the entire root. 2:286 (the prayer "do not LAY upon us what we have no power to bear" — `lā tuḥammilnā mā lā ṭāqata lanā bih`) is its ethical mirror. Together these say: the deepest meaning of `ḥamala` is the assumption-of-responsibility-that-no-one-else-could-bear. The Throne-carrying angels bear an OBJECT; humanity bears an ETHICAL ENTRUSTMENT. Same verb. Same morphological family. The Quran has chosen ONE root to cover physical, biological, ethical, and cosmic carrying — and the choice is itself a statement that all four are ONE KIND OF ACTION.

## Rabbit holes

1. **The `ḥamala` / `waḍaʿa` pair as gestation-formula.** Compile every ayah where the two verbs co-occur: 22:2, 35:11, 46:15, 65:4, 65:6. The pair is fixed; it always means conceive→deliver. Compare to other birth-language in the Quran (`waladat`, `naša'a`, etc.) — does the `ḥml/wDE` pair carry a particular register?

2. **The eight Throne-bearers (69:17).** The number eight appears once in the corpus in this context. Compare with Ezekiel's four living creatures and Revelation's twenty-four elders. What does the number ENCODE? Is there structural data on numbers in eschatological scenes (the seven heavens, eight gates of paradise in hadith, etc.)?

3. **Hml + nb* on 19:22 — the only collision.** The two roots co-occur in exactly ONE ayah: Maryam's withdrawal-with-the-conceived-child. This is the densest moment of the situational-mobility cluster in the entire Quran. Worth a dedicated micro-study.

4. **The form-II passive `ḥummila` and the apostolic burden.** 24:54 uses it for the messenger's task and the people's task. 62:5 uses it for the Torah-entrusted Jews. 33:72 uses an active `ḥamala` for humanity's Trust. Why the voice-shift between communities? Hypothesis: the passive marks an entrusting-from-above; the active marks a voluntary acceptance. Test on every form-II occurrence.

5. **The pregnancy-as-burden inversion.** In Quranic Arabic, `ḥaml` is BOTH the pregnancy AND the burden. The mother's body is a `ḥamūla`. This collapses a metaphor most languages keep separate. Check how the Saheeh, Pickthall, Khattab, Arberry translators handle the polysemy in 7:189, 22:2, 65:4, 65:6 — do any preserve the pun? (The 22:2 verse, where pregnant women cast off their loads on the Day, is the most poetically charged — the load is the unborn child PLUS the metaphysical weight of the End.)

6. **The Throne-bearing as inversion of the Trust-bearing.** Angels bear the Throne (40:7, 69:17). Humans bear the Trust (33:72). Both are `ḥamala` events. Both are vertical/structural — angels uphold heaven, humans uphold ethical responsibility. The cosmological architecture is mirrored in the ethical architecture, lexically. This is the deepest Hml insight: the Quran's word for "carry" carries the universe TO ITSELF.
