# Root sry (سري) — to travel by night

## 1. Headline finding

The root **sry** is the Quran's *named* teleportation verb. Unlike `nql` (Solomon's throne — "transferred"), `Hml` (carrying), or the unspoken raisings of Idrīs and ʿĪsā, sry is the Quran's only root that *thematises night-travel as its lexical core* — and the only root whose **Form IV (asrā)** is reserved exclusively for **divinely-commanded relocation under existential threat or for divinely-orchestrated revelation-display**. Its 8 occurrences (all Meccan) form a remarkably tight semantic field: **6 of 8 are God moving His servants by night**; the 7th (19:24) is the cognate noun *sariyy* (a stream/rivulet under Maryam) that punningly compresses Maryam's own withdrawal-narrative; the 8th (89:4) is the night personified as itself "travelling" — the universe doing what God commands prophets to do.

The decisive structural fact: every occurrence of asrā has a *human-direction-object marked by the preposition bi-* (bi-ahlika, bi-ʿibādī, bi-ʿabdihi) — God does not *send* people by night, He *takes them with* Him. The bi- is a comitative-instrumental: it makes the moved party an *attached* object of divine motion, not an independent traveller. This is the grammar of teleportation: *the agent of motion is God; the human is the carried.* The Night Journey at 17:1 is therefore not an outlier — it is the climactic instance of a six-verse pattern that begins with Lot in Sūrat Hūd and arcs through Moses (4×) before culminating in Muḥammad.

The pattern's purpose-clause — at 17:1 only — is **li-nuriyahu min āyātinā** ("so We may show him *some of* Our signs"). This single phrase distinguishes 17:1 from the other five Form-IV occurrences: the Lot/Moses cases are *rescue-by-night*; 17:1 is *revelation-by-night*. Same verb, two functions — both involve transfer, but only 17:1 makes the transfer instrumental to a vision.

## 3. 17:1 deep read

### Arabic
> سُبْحَٰنَ ٱلَّذِىٓ أَسْرَىٰ بِعَبْدِهِۦ لَيْلًا مِّنَ ٱلْمَسْجِدِ ٱلْحَرَامِ إِلَى ٱلْمَسْجِدِ ٱلْأَقْصَا ٱلَّذِى بَٰرَكْنَا حَوْلَهُۥ لِنُرِيَهُۥ مِنْ ءَايَٰتِنَآ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلْبَصِيرُ

### Four translations side-by-side

- **Saheeh:** "Exalted is He who took His Servant by night from al-Masjid al-Haram to al-Masjid al-Aqsa, whose surroundings We have blessed, to show him of Our signs. Indeed, He is the Hearing, the Seeing."
- **Pickthall:** "Glorified be He Who carried His servant by night from the Inviolable Place of Worship to the Far distant place of worship the neighbourhood whereof We have blessed, that We might show him of Our tokens! Lo! He, only He, is the Hearer, the Seer."
- **Khattab:** "Glory be to the One Who took His servant ˹Muḥammad˺ by night from the Sacred Mosque to the Farthest Mosque whose surroundings We have blessed, so that We may show him some of Our signs. Indeed, He alone is the All-Hearing, All-Seeing."
- **Arberry:** "Glory be to Him, who carried His servant by night from the Holy Mosque to the Further Mosque the precincts of which We have blessed, that We might show him some of Our signs. He is the All-hearing, the All-seeing."

### Word-by-word with commentary

| Word | Gloss | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **subḥāna** | Glory/Exalted-be | A doxological accusative — never used for any Quranic event opening except this one. The verb at the heart of the sūra is encased in worship before it is even named. |
| **alladhī** | He who | Relative pronoun replacing *Allāh* — the actor is described, not named. |
| **asrā** | took-by-night | Form IV — causative night-travel. The *He* moves the servant, but the action verb is one normally used by humans of themselves. The grammar elevates the transfer into divine agency. |
| **bi-ʿabdihi** | with His servant | bi- is comitative; the servant is *carried*, not sent. ʿabd, not rasūl. (See §4.) |
| **laylan** | by night | Indefinite — "a (some) night." Pleonastic with asrā, but specifies the kind of night-portion. |
| **min al-Masjid al-Ḥarām** | from the Sacred Mosque | Explicit source. |
| **ilā al-Masjid al-Aqṣā** | to the Farthest Mosque | Explicit destination. al-Aqṣā = "the most-distant" — superlative of qaṣw (cf. co-occurring root **qSw**). |
| **alladhī bāraknā ḥawlahu** | whose surroundings We blessed | The blessing is on *surroundings*, not on the mosque itself — a deliberate spatial vagueness; the destination is a region, not a building. |
| **li-nuriyahu min āyātinā** | so We may show him *some of* Our signs | li- (purpose) + nuriya (Form IV imperfect — *cause-to-see*). Crucially **min** — *some of*, partitive. He saw a portion. (See §6.) |
| **innahu huwa al-samīʿ al-baṣīr** | Indeed He is the Hearing, the Seeing | Closing divine-name pair: *hearing* answers a vocal/audited dimension of revelation; *seeing* answers the visual dimension just opened. |

### What subḥān is doing

*Subḥān* (root sbḥ) is the language of declaring God transcendent over what is being asserted about Him — almost always in *defence* of God's oneness or majesty against an implicit charge. Its placement here is not ornamental: it pre-empts the objection that physical transfer of a body across a night is implausible or unbecoming. The verse opens by saying *whatever you are about to read, do not impute limitation to Him for it.* The doxology does theological work the verse content cannot: it ring-fences the event from being read as a small claim. It is the Qurʾān's own warning that the Night Journey is the kind of fact on which the language of normal causation breaks.

## 4. The other seven occurrences

### The five **rescue-asrā** events (Form IV)

[**11:81**] (Lot, in Hūd): "*fa-asri bi-ahlika bi-qiṭʿin min al-layli*" — "Travel by night with your family in a portion of the night ... Indeed their appointment is the morning. Is not the morning near?" The *qiṭʿ min al-layl* ("watch/portion of the night") is the explicit time-window: leave before dawn, because dawn is when the city is destroyed.

[**15:65**] (Lot, in Ḥijr): same imperative, same *qiṭʿ min al-layl*, same prohibition on looking back.

[**20:77**] (Moses, in Ṭā-Hā): "*asri bi-ʿibādī fa-iḍrib lahum ṭarīqan fī al-baḥri yabasan*" — "Travel by night with My servants and strike for them a dry path through the sea." Here asrā is paired with another teleportation-class action: the splitting-and-drying of the sea (the path is **opened by command**, i.e. the second teleportation is a wormhole through water).

[**26:52**] (Moses, in Shuʿarāʾ): "*asri bi-ʿibādī innakum muttabaʿūn*" — "Travel by night with My servants; indeed you will be pursued."

[**44:23**] (Moses, in Dukhān): "*fa-asri bi-ʿibādī laylan innakum muttabaʿūn*" — same content, *laylan* explicit.

The pattern: **asrā always appears at the rescue-edge of a destruction narrative.** Lot's destruction is at dawn (*inna mawʿidahum al-ṣubḥ*, 11:81); Pharaoh's destruction is at the sea-crossing's end. The Quran's *night-rescue / dawn-destruction* dual pattern (cf. `notes/time/concept-time-deep.md` §7.2 and the cross-cutting note `research-dawn-destruction.md`) is the structural background against which 17:1 must be read.

### The one cognate-noun event

[**19:24**] (Maryam): "*qad jaʿala rabbuki taḥtaki sariyyan*" — "Your Lord has placed beneath you a stream/rivulet (sariyyan)." This is the noun **sariyy** — etymologically tied to motion, here water-that-travels. The placement is striking: Maryam has just *withdrawn* (intabadhat — root nb*) to a *makānan qaṣiyyan* ("a far place" — same root **qSw** as al-Aqṣā). She is the only female protagonist whose narrative employs a sry-cognate, and the verse compresses three teleportation-class motifs into 24 words: withdrawal, far-place, and a flowing-thing produced beneath her by command. The pun is structural: she travels, the water travels, the date-palm yields.

### The one Form-I event

[**89:4**] (Al-Fajr): "*wa-l-layli idhā yasri*" — "By the night when it journeys/passes." The night itself is the agent. This sūra opens with a sequence of oaths *by* dawn, *by* ten nights, *by* even and odd, *by* the night when it travels. That the night itself "travels" while everything else is being sworn-by reframes asrā as a verb belonging not just to humans-under-divine-command but to *cosmic time-states*. The night does what prophets are commanded to do: it *moves* — but it moves *itself*, by Form I. Only the carriage of a divine-servant requires Form IV (causative) — the night does it intransitively.

## 5. The night-as-rescue pattern

In every Form-IV case, the structure is:
1. **Destruction is imminent.** A people-or-army will be destroyed at *dawn* (Lot) or by a *pursuit* (Moses).
2. **The rescued are commanded to travel by night.** asrā + bi- + companions.
3. **The look-back is forbidden** (11:81, 15:65) — looking-back becomes the lone exception by which Lot's wife is destroyed. *Looking back* is the inverse of *travelling by night*: the latter is forward-trust, the former is backward-attachment.
4. **The dawn delivers judgement** on the unrescued.

17:1 inserts itself into this pattern but **swaps the function**: there is no destruction at dawn in Mecca on the night of the Isrāʾ. The threat is *political-spiritual* (the Meccan rejection of Muḥammad), and the journey's purpose has shifted from *escape from destruction* to *vision of signs*. Same verb, same grammar — but the destination is no longer *escape* but *sight*. The Quran has redeployed its rescue-vocabulary for revelation-vocabulary: when God needs to *show* His Prophet what other prophets needed to *flee toward*, the same verb is used. This is a major theological signal: revelation and rescue share a syntax.

## 6. The Isrāʾ-Miʿrāj question: 17:1 vs 53:1-18

The Quran's *Night-Journey-and-Ascent* tradition is almost entirely a hadith construction. The Quran-proper has only two passages that could plausibly underwrite it: 17:1 (asrā) and 53:1-18 (the second-vision pericope).

**17:1** is unambiguously **horizontal** — *min al-Masjid al-Ḥarām ilā al-Masjid al-Aqṣā*. It names a source and a destination on the earth (or at least with earthly toponymy). No ascent through heavens is mentioned.

**53:1-18** is reading-ambiguous on its vertical-or-not character. The text says:

- 53:7: *wa-huwa bi-l-ufuqi al-aʿlā* — "while he was on the highest horizon" — this is the **Apostle's vision**, but the *he* is contested: is it Muḥammad on the horizon, or Gabriel? The ufuq al-aʿlā (highest horizon) is a sky-reference but not necessarily heavenly geography.
- 53:8-9: *thumma danā fa-tadallā / fa-kāna qāba qawsayni aw adnā* — "then he approached and descended / and was at two-bows-length or nearer." Approached *whom*? Descended *toward what*? The text leaves it open.
- 53:13-14: *wa-laqad raʾāhu nazlatan ukhrā / ʿinda sidrat al-muntahā* — "he saw him in another descent, at the Lote-Tree of the Boundary." The **Lote-Tree of the Boundary** is the only Quranic location that *could* be read as celestial geography — and even here, the verb *raʾāhu* ("he saw him") is about *vision*, not *transit*. The Prophet *sees*; the Quran does not say he *travels*.

Comparison of translations of 53:13-14 confirms the ambiguity:
- **Saheeh:** "And he certainly saw him in another descent / At the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary"
- **Khattab** *interpolates a hadith reading*: "˹in the seventh heaven˺" — bracketed, signalling the editor knows the text does not say this.
- **Pickthall:** "yet another time / By the lote-tree of the utmost boundary"
- **Arberry:** "another time / by the Lote-Tree of the Boundary"

Three of four translators avoid the seven-heavens claim entirely. Only Khattab smuggles it in via square brackets — which is itself a translation-divergence finding.

**Are 17:1 and 53:1-18 the same event?** The text neither asserts nor denies. 17:1 has motion (*asrā* — Form IV); 53:1-18 has vision (*raʾā* — Form I, repeated). They could be one composite event narrated twice; they could be two separate events; they could be an event-and-its-vision. The Qurʾān's *refusal to glue them together* is itself a finding. The hadith tradition glued them; the Qurʾān did not.

A **lexical-disagreement-normalised score of 0.8947** is among the highest in any teleportation-class verse — the four translators chose 34 distinct lexical realisations. The word-by-word comparison shows where:

- **subḥāna**: Saheeh "Exalted is He"; Pickthall "Glorified be He"; Khattab "Glory be to the One"; Arberry "Glory be to Him." Saheeh's "Exalted" leans toward transcendence; the others lean toward worship.
- **asrā**: Saheeh "took"; Pickthall "carried"; Khattab "took"; Arberry "carried." A 2-2 split between *took* (extraction) and *carried* (transport). Pickthall and Arberry both chose *carried*, foregrounding the comitative bi-.
- **al-Aqṣā**: Saheeh transliterates ("al-Masjid al-Aqsa"); Pickthall renders ("Far distant place of worship"); Khattab "Farthest Mosque"; Arberry "Further Mosque." Pickthall's "place of worship" instead of "Mosque" (used for both al-Ḥarām and al-Aqṣā) is the source of his outlier-status — he refuses the anachronism of "mosque" for a 7th-century event.
- **Crucially: no translator uses "in vision" or "by spirit."** All four translate as if the journey is real motion. This is itself a finding: at the level of literal translation, *all four converge on physical transfer*. The body-vs-vision controversy is a *commentarial* dispute, not a translation dispute. The Quran's text reads as physical transit at the surface; only the doxology subḥān at the head signals that "physical" may not be the right ontological category.

Saheeh's "His Servant" (capitalised) is the one translator-marked theological gloss: by capitalising, Saheeh uniquely flags ʿabd as a relational title (cf. §4 below).

## 8. The textual minimalism (negative space)

What the Qurʾān does **not** say about the Night Journey:

- **No mention of a mount.** The hadith tradition's Burāq is absent. The Qurʾān's verb does its own work without an animal.
- **No mention of seven heavens being traversed.** The seven-heavens schema is in the Qurʾān (e.g. 67:3, 71:15) but is *never* attached to 17:1 or 53:1-18.
- **No mention of meeting prior prophets.** The hadith's prophet-by-prophet ascent is absent.
- **No mention of receiving the five-prayers ordinance.** The textual link to ritual law is absent.
- **No description of al-Aqṣā's interior.** The destination is named and its surroundings blessed — its appearance is silent.
- **No mention of duration.** No "in one night" claim — though *laylan* (a night) implicitly bounds it.
- **No mention of Muḥammad's bodily condition during transit.** No sleeping, no waking, no *between*.
- **No mention of return.** The verse names a one-way trip; the return is unmentioned.

The textual minimalism is so pronounced that **17:1 is, by word-count, one of the shortest "miraculous-event" verses in the Quran**. The Quran narrates *that* the journey happened, *whose servant* was carried, *from where* and *to where*, *for what purpose*, and *whom to glorify*. Everything else — the *how*, the *interior*, the *return* — is unspoken. This minimalism is itself the verse's chief rhetorical device: the doxology *subḥān* shields the unsaid; the bi- of *bi-ʿabdihi* tells you the servant is carried, but not how; the *min ... ilā ...* tells you the endpoints, but not the path.

This is consistent with the Quran's general pattern around teleportation-class events (cf. Solomon's throne at 27:40 — *qabla an yartadda ilayka ṭarfuk* "before your glance returns to you" — the speed is named but the mechanism is not; Maryam at 19:22 — *intabadhat bihi makānan qaṣiyyan* — the withdrawal happens but the journey is sketched). **The Qurʾān names the endpoints and the speed; it does not name the medium.**

## 9. bi-ʿabdihi: why "His servant," not "His messenger"?

This is one of the densest theological compressions in the Qurʾān. At 17:1 — the verse most about Muḥammad's exalted status — he is called *ʿabd*, not *rasūl* or *nabī*. The pattern is structural: the Qurʾān uses *ʿabd* of Muḥammad in three places of supreme honour (17:1, 18:1 *ʿabdihi*, 53:10 *ʿabdihi*) and almost nowhere else. The grammar: *bi-ʿabdihi* — "with His servant" — places the carried Prophet under the same word that the Qurʾān uses for Moses' followers in the four asrā-Moses verses (*bi-ʿibādī*, "with My servants").

The relational compression: when God moves a man through space-time, the man's title is *ʿabd*, not *rasūl*. Servanthood is the ontological category that permits being-carried. Messengerhood is the *function* of speech. To be carried is to be *belonging*; to be sent is to be *commissioned*. 17:1 elevates the Prophet by demoting him — by reaching for the title that other rescued bodies share.

## 10. Connection to other teleportation phenomena

The sry pattern *contains* — by verbal class — the Quran's teleportation lexicon, but does not exhaust it. Cross-references for the cross-cutting agents:

- **Solomon's throne** (27:38-40, root **nql/jnn/Etr**): instant transfer; explicit source and destination; "before your glance returns" — *speed is named but mechanism is not*. Same minimalism as 17:1.
- **Maryam's withdrawal** (19:22-24, roots **nb*/qSw/sry**): *makānan qaṣiyyan* echoes *al-Masjid al-Aqṣā*; the cognate noun *sariyyan* sits inside her narrative.
- **Idrīs's raising** (19:57, root **rfʿ**): *rafaʿnāhu makānan ʿaliyyan* — "We raised him to a high place." Same partitive vagueness as al-Aqṣā: a place is named but not located.
- **ʿĪsā's raising** (4:158, root **rfʿ**): *bal rafaʿahu Allāhu ilayh* — "rather, God raised him to Himself." Direction *to God* — vertical without coordinate.
- **The Sleepers of the Cave** (18:11-25, root **DRb**): time-teleportation rather than space-teleportation; same divine-agency grammar; same negative space about mechanism.
- **The man at the ruined town** (2:259, root **mwt/bʿv**): 100-year sleep; food unspoiled; donkey reassembled. Time-displacement teleportation.

The sry pattern is the **horizontal** teleportation cluster; the rfʿ pattern is the **vertical** teleportation cluster; the time-displacement cluster (Cave, Uzayr) is the **temporal** cluster. 17:1 sits at the **junction** because *al-Aqṣā* is named horizontally but *the showing of signs* implies a vertical opening. The Quran's grammar permits 17:1 to be read as either horizontal-only or as a horizontal-plus-vertical event with the vertical part collapsed into *li-nuriyahu min āyātinā*.

## 12. Rabbit holes

1. **The qSw / Aqṣā / qaṣiyyan family.** Both Maryam (19:22) and Muḥammad (17:1) are sent to a "farthest place" using the same root **qSw**. Pull every qSw occurrence; is "the farthest" a teleportation marker?
2. **subḥān as event-frame.** Map every Quranic occurrence of *subḥān + alladhī* (e.g. 36:36, 36:83, 43:13). Does the construction always frame an act of divine power that exceeds creaturely category? If yes, *subḥān* is the Quran's miracle-marker.
3. **The bi- of comitative motion.** Does *bi-* + person + verb-of-motion always entail divine-agency in the Quran, or does it appear in non-divine contexts?
4. **Why does the noun isrāʾ not occur?** The sūra is named for a verbal noun the Quran never uses. Does this match the pattern of other sūra-name absences (e.g. is *Najm* — the named sūra-noun of 53 — likewise rare)?
5. **Look-back as the failure mode.** The "do not look back" prohibition (11:81, 15:65) parallels the Lot-of-Genesis tradition; what other Quranic narratives forbid looking-back?
6. **The 53:1-18 and 81:23 second-vision question.** 81:23 (*wa-laqad raʾāhu bi-l-ufuq al-mubīn*) parallels 53:7. Are these two passages the same event, two sightings, or one sighting reported twice? Pull both and compare the *raʾā* clauses.
7. **The Lote-Tree's location.** *Sidrat al-muntahā* (53:14) is the only place in the Quran's vocabulary that *might* be eschatological geography. What is *al-muntahā* the boundary *of*? Knowledge? Permitted ascent? The created-realm's edge?
8. **Pickthall's outlier-status.** Pickthall is the structural outlier on 17:1 (mean Jaccard 0.3982). Why? His refusal of "Mosque" is one cause; what others?
9. **The rescue-asrā / revelation-asrā switch.** What happened — narratively and theologically — that allowed the Quran to redeploy its rescue-verb for a revelation-event? Does this happen with any other rescue-vocabulary?
10. **Maryam's sariyy and 17:1's asrā as adjacent events.** Sūra 19 (Maryam) and Sūra 17 (Isrāʾ) are textual neighbours. The sry root, used twice in two cognate forms, threads them. Is this an editorial signal — sry as the theme-thread of 17-19?
