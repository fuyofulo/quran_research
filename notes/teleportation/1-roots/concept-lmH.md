# Concept: ل-م-ح (lmH) — the flicker, the twinkle of the eye

## Headline

The root **ل-م-ح** occurs only **twice** in the entire Quran — and both times in the same idiomatic frame: **ka-lamḥ + bi/al-baṣar**, "as a flicker of/with the vision." Two occurrences, one formula, two immense theological loads. **16:77** uses it for the arrival of the Hour. **54:50** uses it for the issuing of God's command. Despite the tiny lemma count, lmH is one of the highest-density roots in the corpus by *theological-load-per-occurrence*: it has no everyday senses, no narrative side-uses, no derivational drift. Both occurrences are speed-comparators for divine action. **lmH is purpose-built vocabulary.**

A lemma that appears only in a single canonical formula, twice, is a structural marker. The Quran is signaling: *this is the maximum-speed image. The eye-flicker. There is no shorter unit.*

## Stats

- 2 occurrences, 2 ayahs, 2 surahs (16, 54)
- 1 lemma: **lamoH** (لَمْح, the twinkle/flicker), N
- Both Meccan
- Forms: *ka-lamḥ-i* (16:77) and *ka-lamḥ-in bi-* (54:50) — minor inflection difference, see Grammatical Note
- Co-occurring roots in BOTH ayahs: √bṣr (vision), √Amr (command/matter). 100% co-occurrence with *both*. This is a closed lexical circuit.

## The two verses in full

### 16:77 — the Hour as flicker

> وَلِلَّهِ غَيْبُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ ۚ وَمَآ أَمْرُ ٱلسَّاعَةِ إِلَّا **كَلَمْحِ ٱلْبَصَرِ** أَوْ هُوَ أَقْرَبُ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَىْءٍ قَدِيرٌ
>
> *"To God belongs the unseen of the heavens and the earth, and the matter of the Hour is but **as a flicker of the vision** — or it is nearer. Surely God is over all things capable."*

### 54:50 — the divine command as flicker

> وَمَآ أَمْرُنَآ إِلَّا وَٰحِدَةٌ **كَلَمْحٍۭ بِٱلْبَصَرِ**
>
> *"And Our command is but a single one, **as a flicker with the vision**."*

## Grammatical note: ka-lamḥi al-baṣar vs ka-lamḥin bi-l-baṣar

This is **the most interesting micro-fact about the lmH dataset**: the two occurrences differ in their construct.

- 16:77: **كَلَمْحِ ٱلْبَصَرِ** — *ka-lamḥ-i al-baṣar-i* — *iḍāfa* (genitive construct chain). "As a flicker **of** the vision." Definite. The flicker that vision makes.
- 54:50: **كَلَمْحٍۭ بِٱلْبَصَرِ** — *ka-lamḥ-in bi-l-baṣar-i* — indefinite *lamḥ* + preposition *bi-* "with/by." "As a flicker **with/by** the vision." Indefinite. A flicker performed by vision.

The semantic shading:
- *of the vision* (16:77) frames lamḥ as **a property of the eye-event** — the flicker that belongs to vision.
- *with the vision* (54:50) frames lamḥ as **an act performed using vision** — vision as the instrument by which the flicker happens.

Why the difference?
- **16:77 is descriptive** ("the matter of the Hour *is*..."): the Hour is *like the kind-of-flicker that vision has*. The Hour belongs to a category of speed; vision's flicker exemplifies that category.
- **54:50 is performative** ("Our command *is*..."): God's command is *like an act-of-flickering done with the eye*. The command is an *event*, and the eye-flicker is the closest event-comparator.

The shift from *iḍāfa* to *bi-* tracks the shift from **noun-of-property** (the Hour = a kind of speed) to **verbal noun-of-act** (the command = a kind of action). This is the Quran being microscopically careful about which side of the noun/verb boundary the comparator is doing work on. **No translator captures the distinction in English** — they all flatten both to "blink of an eye" or "twinkling of an eye." The Arabic is making a choice the English cannot register.

## Theological context

### 16:77 — knowledge of the unseen, capacity over all things

The verse follows 16:75–76, two parables of the disabled-versus-capable (a slave with no power vs a free agent who commands; a mute who is a burden vs one who orders justice). The parables are about **divine asymmetry**: God is to creatures as the capable to the incapable. 16:77 caps that asymmetry by saying God owns the *unseen* and brings the *Hour* in less time than the eye flickers. The *aqrab* ("nearer") qualifier is the rhetorical detonator: it says the comparator is itself an understatement.

The verse closes with *innallāha ʿalā kulli shay'in qadīr* — "God is capable of all things." The pairing of **lamḥ + qadīr** is the speed-attribute paired with the capacity-attribute. **Speed presupposes capacity.** You cannot move fast in a domain you cannot move in.

### 54:50 — the indivisible command after creation-by-measure

54:49 is the immediately preceding verse: *innā kulla shay'in khalaqnāhu **bi-qadar*** — "everything We created with *qadar* (measure/decree)." Then 54:50: *and Our command is but a single one, like a flicker with the vision*.

The pairing is theological dynamite. **Qadar** is *measure / pre-determination*: a careful pre-ordained quantity. **Lamḥ** is *the flicker*: an instantaneous unindivisible event. **Wāḥida** ("a single one") is the bridge: God's command is **wāḥida** = unitary, monolithic, not parsed-out. So creation is *measured* (qadar — quantitative), but the act-of-commanding-it is *single* (wāḥida) and *flicker-fast* (lamḥ). The Quran is partitioning two roles:

- Created reality: structured, measured, quantitative (54:49)
- Divine command that creates it: unitary, instant, flicker-paced (54:50)

The lamḥ-formula is the speed-signature of the *act*; qadar is the structural-signature of the *result*. **Two different metaphysical clocks meet at the lamḥ ↔ qadar interface.**

## The aqrab qualifier — hyperbolic instantaneity

Only 16:77 carries *aw huwa aqrab* — "or it is nearer" / "or even closer." This is the Quran's only instance where a speed-comparator is **explicitly outpaced by the thing it claims to measure**.

The semantics: the Hour is so fast that even the flicker of the eye is too slow as a comparator. The Quran adds the disclaimer in real-time: *or — actually — it's closer than that*. The blink is the smallest-perceived unit, but the Hour is sub-perceptual. **The Hour will arrive faster than perception can register that it arrived.**

What this implies operationally: 16:77 is **hyperbolic** about the eye-flicker; 54:50 is **typological** ("Our command is a *kind* of flicker"). The Hour-verse pushes past the comparator; the command-verse uses the comparator as a category-marker. Same lemma, different rhetorical pressure.

54:50 has no *aqrab*. Its scope is bounded — it claims the command is *as fast as* the flicker, no more. The asymmetry is consistent: **eschatology is hyperbolic, ontology is typological**.

## Translation divergence

| Verse | Saheeh | Pickthall | Arberry | Khattab |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16:77 | "as a glance of the eye or even nearer" | "as a twinkling of the eye, or it is nearer still" | "as a twinkling of the eye, or nearer" | **"would only take the blink of an eye, or even less"** |
| 54:50 | "like a glance of the eye" | "as the twinkling of an eye" | "as the twinkling of an eye" | **"done in the blink of an eye"** |

Patterns:
1. **"Glance"** (Saheeh) vs **"twinkling"** (Pickthall, Arberry) vs **"blink"** (Khattab). Three lexical choices. *Twinkling* is archaic-elevated; *glance* implies directionality; *blink* implies the lid closing-and-opening. The Arabic *lamḥ* is closer to **"the flicker"** — it can mean either an outward darting of the eye or the moment of the lid's closure. English has no single word that captures the ambiguity.
2. **Khattab adds verbs.** "Would only take," "done in." He **performs the act-frame** that the Arabic 54:50 *bi-* construction implies, but at 16:77 he also performs it where the Arabic *iḍāfa* is more property-like. He flattens the construct distinction in the same direction the others do, just more aggressively.
3. **No translator preserves the iḍāfa / bi- distinction.** The micro-grammar of 16:77 vs 54:50 is invisible in English.
4. **Pickthall renders "nearer still"** at 16:77 — the only translator who marks *aqrab* with an emphatic *still*. He is registering the hyperbole more sharply than the others.

The translation behaviour confirms a methodological pattern (see Trf entry §Translation divergence + methodology log Entry 5 §6): **English translation tends to standardise the speed-formula across verses, erasing the per-verse grammatical micro-choice the Arabic makes**. To do source-text-grounded analysis on the lmH formulas, the Arabic distinction must be reconstructed by hand.

## The ka-lamḥ + bi-l-baṣar formula — internal anatomy

| Element | Function |
|---|---|
| *ka-* | "as / like" — comparison particle |
| *lamḥ* | the flicker — verbal-noun (*maṣdar*) of the unattested verb *lamaḥa* |
| *al-baṣar* / *bi-l-baṣar* | "the vision" / "with the vision" — the substrate or instrument |

Note that *lamḥ* is a *maṣdar* (verbal noun) — it names **the act of flickering** itself, not the eye doing it. The formula's logic:
- The eye (*baṣar*) is the substrate.
- The flickering (*lamḥ*) is the act.
- Together they name the smallest event the human perceptual system can register.

This is **the same architecture as the Trf formula** (27:40: *yartadda ilayka ṭarfuk* — your gaze returns to you), but distributed differently:
- *ṭarf* is **a single noun** that bundles "gaze + flicker" together. It needs no co-noun.
- *lamḥ* is **a verbal noun** that requires *baṣar* to specify *the flicker of what*. It is grammatically dependent.

Why two architectures for the same concept?
- **lmH (lamḥ + baṣar)** = decomposed: act + organ. Used for divine action that can be decomposed into command + execution.
- **Trf (ṭarf alone)** = atomic: gaze-as-event-bundle. Used for narrative that needs the comparator to be **a single thing the human eye does**.

This is consistent with the partition: lmH for divine command (16:77, 54:50); Trf for human/narrative speed (27:40 throne; 14:43 the damned). **Divine speed is decomposable; human-perception speed is atomic.** A speculative reading, but supported by the lemma distribution.

## Negative space

1. **No verb of √lmḥ.** Like √Trf, lmH appears only as a noun. The verb *lamaḥa* exists in classical Arabic but does not appear in the Quran. The Quran will not say "He flickered" — only "as the flicker." This is the **comparator-only** profile.
2. **No use outside the speed-formula.** Two occurrences, one formula. lmH does not appear in any other context — not as eye-anatomy, not in paradise/hell scenes, not in metaphors of light. It is a **single-purpose root**.
3. **No co-occurrence with √Trf in any verse.** The two roots cover the same conceptual space but are never deployed together. The Quran does not mix them. This is a **lexical partition**: pick one comparator-architecture per verse, never blend.
4. **No use for prophetic miracles.** The Quran has narratives of staffs becoming serpents (Moses), the dead being raised (Jesus), birds being formed (Jesus), the throne being moved (Solomon). Of these, only **the throne** uses a flicker-comparator (and via Trf, not lmH). Moses' staff is given no speed-comparator. Jesus' raisings are given none. **lmH is reserved for divine eschatology and the divine command itself**, not for prophet-mediated miracles.
5. **No co-occurrence with √Hsb (sarīʿ al-ḥisāb).** The other major divine-speed motif — *swift in reckoning* — never shares a verse with lmH. Two speed-attributes, distinct domains: lmH for the *act* of the Hour/command; *sarīʿ al-ḥisāb* for the *judgment* that follows.

## Verses for direct study (priority)

1. **16:77** — Hour-as-flicker, with *aqrab* hyperbolic capstone. Embedded in capacity-discourse (*qadīr*) and unseen-discourse (*ghayb*).
2. **54:50** — Command-as-flicker, paired with **54:49** (creation by *qadar*). The qadar/lamḥ partition.
3. **16:75–76** — set-up parables for 16:77 (the disabled vs capable). Why the speed-image *follows* the asymmetry-parables.
4. **54:49** — *kullu shay'in khalaqnāhu bi-qadar*. The measured-creation that 54:50 then says is commanded in a flicker.

## Rabbit holes opened

1. **The iḍāfa / bi- minimal pair.** Worth a dedicated probe across other "ka-X" speed-comparators in the Quran. Does the Quran systematically use *iḍāfa* for property-comparisons and *bi-* for act-comparisons? Or is 16:77/54:50 a one-off variation?
2. **The aqrab capstone.** Is there a corpus pattern of "X *aw aqrab* / *aw shadīdan*" — speed-comparators that overrun themselves? Quick search of *aqrab* + qiyāma/sāʿa contexts would surface or refute the pattern.
3. **The qadar ↔ lamḥ partition (54:49–50).** This is potentially **the most theologically dense verse-pair in the corpus** for the question of *deterministic creation versus instantaneous command*. The two verses sit adjacent and stage a metaphysical contrast. Worth a full dedicated entry under 2-cross-cutting.
4. **lmH ↔ Trf as paired vocabulary.** The hypothesis (see lmH §The ka-lamḥ formula) that **decomposable architecture = divine, atomic architecture = human-perceptual** could be tested by checking if other Quranic divine-action verbs are similarly *decomposed* into act + substrate, while human-narrative verbs are *atomic*.
5. **Why 16:77 places the Hour-comparator in an unseen-discourse?** *Lillāhi ghaybu al-samāwāt wa-l-arḍ* opens the verse. Unseen + Hour + flicker + capable = a **single thought**. The Hour is unseen *because* it arrives flicker-fast — perception can't catch it. Worth a probe of √gyb (unseen) for whether *unseen* is structurally tied to *fast* in the corpus.
