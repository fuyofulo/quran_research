## 1. Headline finding

The Quran does not have *a* word for "instantaneous." It has **a graded, domain-partitioned grammar of speed-comparators**, each surgically calibrated to a specific category of divine action. There are at least seven distinct lexical-grammatical formulas in this system — *kun fa-yakūn* (the creation-and-decree formula, 8 ayahs), *ka-lamḥ al-baṣar* (the eye-flicker, 16:77 / 54:50), *qabla an yartadda ilayka ṭarfuk* (the gaze-return, 27:40), *nafkhatun wāḥida* / *Ṣayhatun wāḥida* / *zajratun wāḥida* (the single-blast trigger, 7+ ayahs), *sarīʿ al-ḥisāb* (swift-in-reckoning, 8 ayahs), *baghta* / *baghtatan* (suddenly, 13 ayahs), and the hyperbolic capstone *aw huwa aqrab* (or even closer, 16:77 only) — and these formulas **never co-occur in the same ayah** in the way one might expect of synonyms. They are not seven names for one speed. They are **seven slots in a coordinate system** that maps creation, divine command, eschatological trigger, judgment-speed, sudden-arrival, narrative-teleportation, and self-overrunning hyperbole onto orthogonal axes.

The decisive integrating move: every one of these formulas is a **comparator without a mechanism**. The Quran tells us *how fast* a divine action arrives by referencing a perceptual baseline (the eye, the body, the breath) — it never describes *how* the speed is achieved. There is no traversal, no portal, no mechanism. Instantaneity is reported by **juxtaposition** — claim, then settled-arrival — and the comparator is the only narrative connective. This is consistent with the larger teleportation finding (*concept-Trf.md*, §The throne event): Quranic teleportation is **arrival without journey**. The instantaneity catalog is the lexical underwriting of that theological commitment.

The corollary the Phase-1 agents could not state on their own: **creation theology, eschatological theology, and teleportation theology share the same grammar of divine instantaneous action.** The throne in 27:40 arrives by the same kind of operation that brings the Hour in 16:77, that kills-and-resurrects in 39:68, that spoke the universe into being in 36:82. Different formulas; one underlying grammar. The Quran has a unified theory of divine cross-cosmic operation, and the catalog of instantaneity vocabulary is the place to read it.

## 2. The catalog of formulas

### 2a. *kun fa-yakūn* — "Be! and it is" (8 ayahs)

Verified by direct Arabic search of `data/arabic/quran.json` (after stripping diacritics): exactly 8 ayahs contain the *kun fa-yakūn* formula:

| Ayah | Antecedent | What is being created/decreed |
|---|---|---|
| **2:117** | Originator (*badīʿ*) of heavens and earth + decreed matter (*qaḍā amran*) | cosmic creation |
| **3:47** | Maryam's question on virginal conception | Jesus's birth |
| **3:59** | the *parallel* of Jesus to Adam | Adam's creation (re-stated) |
| **6:73** | "the Day He says *kun fa-yakūn*" + the Trumpet (*yawma yunfaxu fī al-Ṣūr*) | the eschatological speech-act |
| **16:40** | "Our word to a thing when We intend it" | divine intention-to-existence |
| **19:35** | refutation of the divine son | Jesus's miraculous-not-divine origin |
| **36:82** | "His command (*amruhu*), when He intends a thing" | the formal definition of divine command |
| **40:68** | "He gives life and causes death" + decree | resurrection-as-creation |

Two of these (3:47, 19:35) are about Jesus; one (3:59) compares Jesus to Adam — so the formula is the **doctrinal hinge** for Quranic Christology. Two (2:117, 36:82) are general statements of divine causal collapse. One (6:73) folds *kun fa-yakūn* and the Trumpet (Ṣūr) into the same ayah — and this is **structurally important**: it is the only ayah where the creation-formula and the eschatological-trigger meet. We will return to it.

The Arabic for the canonical form (36:82):

> إِنَّمَآ أَمۡرُهُۥٓ إِذَآ أَرَادَ شَيۡـًٔا أَن يَقُولَ لَهُۥ كُن فَيَكُونُ
>
> *"His command (*amruhu*), when He wills a thing, is to say to it 'Be!' and it is."*

Grammar: *kun* is the imperative of *kāna* (to be); *fa-yakūn* is the *fa-* of immediate consequence + the present-tense indicative. The structural punch is in the *fa-* — there is **no temporal interval** between the imperative and the actualized state. *Kun* and *yakūn* are the same root (k-w-n) inflected as command and effect. The formula is a closed loop: God says "be," and being-happens. There is no reported gap.

### 2b. *ka-lamḥ al-baṣar* — "as a flicker of the vision" (2 ayahs)

| Ayah | Construction | Domain |
|---|---|---|
| **16:77** | *ka-lamḥ-i al-baṣar-i* (iḍāfa, definite construct) + *aw huwa aqrab* | the Hour (eschatology) |
| **54:50** | *ka-lamḥ-in bi-l-baṣar-i* (indefinite + bi-) | divine command (*amrunā*) |

Both Meccan; both pair with √Amr (command); both pair with √bṣr (vision). The micro-grammatical distinction (iḍāfa vs bi-) is treated in §7. See `concept-lmH.md` for the full root profile.

### 2c. *qabla an yartadda ilayka ṭarfuk* — "before your gaze returns to you" (1 ayah)

> قَبۡلَ أَن يَرۡتَدَّ إِلَيۡكَ طَرۡفُكَ — 27:40

A single occurrence — but **the only explicit teleportation comparator in narrative context** in the entire Quran. The formula uses √rdd (return) + √Trf (gaze) in a way that occurs nowhere else except its mirror at 14:43 (where the same pair appears negated). See `concept-Trf.md`. Structural anchor for §6.

### 2d. *nafkhatun wāḥida* / *Ṣayhatun wāḥida* / *zajratun wāḥida* — "a single blast/cry/shout" (8+ ayahs)

The single-event eschatological trigger. Verified directly:

| Lemma + *wāḥida* | Ayahs | Domain |
|---|---|---|
| *nafkhatun wāḥida* | 69:13 | the Trumpet (cosmic) |
| *Ṣayhatun wāḥida* | 36:29, 36:49, 36:53, 38:15, 54:31 | both worldly destruction and eschatological gathering |
| *zajratun wāḥida* | 79:13 | resurrection-blast |
| *dakkatan wāḥida* | 69:14 | the **leveling** that follows the blast (one-shot crushing) |

Eight ayahs. Structurally identical: in each, *wāḥida* (single, feminine, indefinite) qualifies a sound-or-shock-noun, and the consequence-clause is introduced by *fa-iḏā hum* (then-suddenly-they) followed by an immediate state-change. The *wāḥida* tag insists that the divine signal is **not iterated**: one blast suffices. The 69:13–14 pair is the densest — a single blast and a single leveling.

### 2e. *sarīʿ al-ḥisāb* — "swift in the reckoning" (8 ayahs)

Verified directly: 8 ayahs containing *sarīʿ + ḥisāb*: **2:202, 3:19, 3:199, 5:4, 13:41, 14:51, 24:39, 40:17**. Always predicated of God (*Allāh sarīʿ al-ḥisāb*), almost always at verse-end as a clausula.

The domain is **judgment-speed**, not action-speed. God is fast at *reckoning* (calculating moral accounts), not at *acting* (which uses *kun fa-yakūn*) and not at *signaling* (which uses the *wāḥida* formulas). This is a **divine attribute**, not an event-comparator.

Critical co-occurrence: **13:41 contains both *min aṭrāf al-arḍ* (Trf-as-edge formula) and *sarīʿ al-ḥisāb***. The earth being eaten from its margins is paired with the swift reckoning. See `concept-Trf.md` §ṭaraf as edge for the geographical reading. This is the single co-occurrence between the *speed-attribute* and the *teleportation-root* family.

### 2f. *baghta* / *baghtatan* — "suddenly" (13 ayahs)

Verified directly: 13 ayahs use *baghtatan* (the adverbial "suddenly"): **6:31, 6:44, 6:47, 7:95, 7:187, 12:107, 21:40, 22:55, 26:202, 29:53, 39:55, 43:66, 47:18**.

The dominant collocation is with the Hour and with punishment. 7:187: *lā taʾtīkum illā baghta* — "it [the Hour] does not come upon you except suddenly." 22:55: *ḥattā taʾtiyahum al-sāʿatu baghta* — "until the Hour comes upon them suddenly." 6:31 / 6:44 / 6:47: punishment arrives *baghta*.

*Baghta* names **the modality of arrival from the recipient's side**. The Hour comes *baghta* — the disbelievers do not see it coming. It is the **epistemic complement** of the speed-formulas: where *lamḥ* / *ṭarf* describe how *fast* the event is, *baghta* describes how *unwarned* the recipient is. A thing can be slow and *baghta* (a long-prepared punishment that arrives without warning); a thing can be fast and not *baghta* (the throne in 27:40 is fast-but-Solomon-asks-for-it). The Quran keeps these distinct.

### 2g. *aw huwa aqrab* — "or even closer" (1 ayah)

The hyperbolic capstone, exclusive to 16:77. Discussed in §4, §10. The Quran's only instance of a speed-comparator that explicitly outpaces itself.

### 2h. The *fa-iḏā* construction — "and-suddenly-they" (the narrative pivot)

Not a vocabulary item but a **syntactic instantaneity-operator**. In all the *wāḥida* verses and many post-Trumpet verses, the consequence-clause begins with *fa-iḏā hum + state* — "and at-once they are [extinguished / brought-present / standing-looking]." This is the Arabic grammar of *narrative-jump-cut*: the connective collapses the duration between cause and effect.

Examples:
- 36:29: *fa-iḏā hum xāmidūn* — "and at once they were extinguished"
- 36:53: *fa-iḏā hum jamīʿun ladaynā muḥḍarūn* — "and at once they are all brought-present before Us"
- 79:14: *fa-iḏā hum bi-l-sāhira* — "and at once they are upon the awakened plain"

The *fa-iḏā* construction is the Quran's **default grammar for instantaneous arrival** and it operates *under* the lexical instantaneity formulas. Wherever the lexical comparators appear, *fa-iḏā* tends to be near.

## 3. Domain partitioning — each formula has its own register

The most important integrating finding: **the Quran's instantaneity formulas are partitioned by domain**, almost without exception. Each formula has a register and stays in it.

| Formula | Domain | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| *kun fa-yakūn* | CREATION + DECREE | universe (2:117), Jesus (3:47, 3:59, 19:35), Adam (3:59), divine command-formula (16:40, 36:82), resurrection-as-creation (40:68), eschatological speech-act (6:73) |
| *ka-lamḥ al-baṣar* | DIVINE COMMAND + ESCHATOLOGY (the Hour) | the Hour's matter (16:77), the divine command (54:50) |
| *qabla an yartadda ilayka ṭarfuk* | TELEPORTATION (single narrative event) | Solomon's throne (27:40); negated mirror in eschatological terror (14:43) |
| *nafkhatun wāḥida* + family | ESCHATOLOGICAL TRIGGER | one-blast destruction (54:31), one-blast resurrection (36:53, 79:13), one-blast leveling (69:13–14) |
| *sarīʿ al-ḥisāb* | DIVINE ATTRIBUTE (judgment) | God-as-fast-reckoner (8 ayahs), always predicative |
| *baghta* | EPISTEMIC MODALITY (recipient's experience) | Hour's arrival (7:187, 22:55), punishment-arrival (6:31, 6:44 etc.) |
| *aw huwa aqrab* | HYPERBOLIC CAPSTONE | Hour as faster-than-its-own-comparator (16:77) |

The partition is striking. **No two formulas overlap in domain**. *Kun fa-yakūn* is never used for the Trumpet event. The single-blast formulas are never used for creation. The eye-flicker is never used as a divine attribute. *Sarīʿ al-ḥisāb* is never used for an actual event (only as God's standing quality). *Baghta* is never used for the throne-event (the throne is fast but asked-for).

The single exception that proves the rule: **6:73 unifies *kun fa-yakūn* with the Trumpet** in a single ayah. The verse is doing a deliberate cross-categorical move — fusing the creation-formula with the eschatological-trigger to make a metaphysical point: "His is the dominion the day He says 'Be!' and it is — the day the Horn is blown." 6:73 is the only place the corpus puts these two registers in one breath. (See §8.)

This domain-partitioning is the same architectural pattern the eschatological-architecture synthesis identified for *al-Sāʿa* / *yawm* / *al-Ṣūr* / *mīqāt* / *ajal* / *al-ākhira* / *xulūd* / *abadan* — a **non-overlapping coordinate system, not a thesaurus**. The instantaneity vocabulary is one slice of the broader corpus-pattern: *the Quran refuses to merge formulas that look synonymous*.

## 4. The graduated speed-ladder

The Quran's instantaneity vocabulary is *not uniform*. It is calibrated. Reading the formulas together produces a **graduated ladder**, from the most-decomposable to the most-collapsed:

```
SLOWER ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────► FASTER
                                                                      
[bird-flight]  →  [wind-flight]  →  [jinn-flight]  →  [gaze-return]  →  [eye-flicker]  →  [Be-and-it-is]
   hoopoe         Solomon's wind     ʿifrīt          Book-knower      the Hour          divine command
   (27:20)        (34:12)            (27:39)         (27:40)          (16:77)           (36:82)
                                                                      ↓
                                                                  + aw aqrab
                                                                  (16:77)
                                                                      ↓
                                                                  even faster
```

The bottom three rungs are the **Solomon-suite hierarchy** (see `narrative-solomon-suite.md` §6). The top three are **divine-action formulas**. The whole ladder has an internal logic: each rung is **explicitly faster than the one below** by Quranic juxtaposition. Solomon's own court contains rungs 1–4 (hoopoe, wind, jinn, Book-knower); the eschatological vocabulary supplies rungs 5–6 (Hour, divine command). The rungs differ by *what they measure their speed against*:

- **Hoopoe** — measured against ordinary bird-flight (he is fast for a bird, but absent for unknown periods).
- **Wind** — measured against ordinary travel-time (Solomon's wind covers a month per half-day, 30×).
- **Jinn (ʿifrīt)** — measured against bodily volition (*qabla an taqūma min maqāmika* — "before you rise from your seat").
- **Book-knower** — measured against the optical micro-act (*qabla an yartadda ilayka ṭarfuk* — "before your gaze returns to you").
- **The Hour / divine command** — measured against the eye-flicker itself (*ka-lamḥ al-baṣar*), and explicitly *outpaces* it (*aw huwa aqrab* — "or even closer").
- **Kun fa-yakūn** — measured against *nothing*. The formula does not use a comparator; it asserts no-gap-at-all between command and actualization.

The progression is from **comparator-grounded** (the lower rungs) to **comparator-overrun** (16:77's *aqrab*) to **comparator-refused** (36:82's *kun fa-yakūn*, which has no comparator because there is no interval to measure). At the top of the ladder, the Quran abandons the comparator-grammar and switches to a different rhetorical move: the **closed loop of imperative + immediate-effect**.

This is one of the Quran's most precise rhetorical structures. The lower the rung, the more the speed is *narrated against a baseline*. The higher the rung, the more the baseline *itself* fails. By 16:77 even the eye-flicker is too slow as a comparator (*aw huwa aqrab*). By 36:82 the comparator is gone entirely (*kun fa-yakūn* — no comparison, just causal-loop closure). The graduation tracks the **withdrawal of the perceptual baseline**: as we approach the divine end of the ladder, the human eye can no longer measure the speed.

## 5. The 27:38–40 inversion — knowledge outpaces strength

In a single three-ayah block, the Quran stages **a speed-contest between strength-based and knowledge-based transport**, and uses the speed-comparator to encode the winner.

> **27:38** — Solomon: "O council, which of you will bring me her throne before they come to me submitted?"
> **27:39** — *ʿifrīt of the jinn*: "I will bring it to you **before you rise from your place**, and I am for it strong, trustworthy."
> **27:40** — *one who had knowledge from the Book*: "I will bring it to you **before your gaze returns to you**." Then when [Solomon] saw it settled before him, he said: "This is from the favor of my Lord…"

The two offers are **syntactically parallel** — *anā ātīka bihi qabla an + verb*. The grammars are identical. What differs is the comparator-noun:

| Offer | Comparator | Body-stratum |
|---|---|---|
| ʿifrīt (strength) | *taqūma min maqāmika* | macro-bodily volition (rising) |
| Book-knower (knowledge) | *yartadda ilayka ṭarfuk* | optical micro-event (gaze-return) |

The comparator is the *only* differentiator. The Quran does not say "X is better than Y," does not name a winner, does not stage a confrontation. It lets the **comparator-noun** do the work. The ʿifrīt's clock ticks at sit-to-stand cadence; the Book-knower's ticks at the cadence of an eye's flicker-return. The latter is **faster by orders of magnitude**, and the third clause confirms: *fa-lammā raʾāhu mustaqirran ʿindahu* — "when he saw it settled before him." Past tense. The throne is *already* at rest. The Book-knower's claim is adjudicated by the result.

What this encodes:

1. **Knowledge outpaces strength.** The ʿifrīt is qualified as *qawiyyun amīn* (strong, trustworthy); the Book-knower has only *ʿindahu ʿilmun min al-kitāb* (knowledge from the Book). The strong-and-trustworthy is outranked by the knowledgeable.
2. **The hierarchy is encoded in the speed-comparator.** The Quran chose the ʿifrīt's body-clock (rising) and the Book-knower's eye-clock (gaze-return) precisely to scale the gap.
3. **The Book-knower is not described or named.** The Quran refuses to identify the agent of the faster transport. He is a **category-of-one**: an anonymous, knowledge-anchored, single-instance carrier (see `narrative-solomon-suite.md` §1).
4. **Strength is *bodily*; knowledge is *sub-bodily*.** The ʿifrīt's comparator is a macro-bodily act. The Book-knower's comparator is the smallest unit of human perception. The hierarchy moves from *body* → *eye* → (later, in 16:77) *faster than eye*.

This is the cleanest **grammatically-encoded value-inversion** in the teleportation corpus. The Quran does not preach "knowledge is better than strength"; it *demonstrates* it with two parallel sentences and one grammatical-cadence measurement. The same passage shows that the upper rungs of the speed-ladder belong to **whatever has access to *al-ghayb* (the unseen)**: the ʿifrīt, however strong, cannot exceed his bodily clock because his cognition is bound to *al-shahāda* (the witnessed); the Book-knower has knowledge of the Book and is therefore *ghayb*-adjacent. The 34:14 death-of-Solomon scene (jinn cannot detect Solomon's death because they do not know *al-ghayb*) confirms the binding from the negative side: **transport-power is bounded by knowledge-access**.

## 6. The 27:40 ↔ 14:43 mirror — same operator, opposite truth-value

The Phase-1 Trf agent surfaced this. The integration sharpens it.

The verb *yartadda* (form VIII of √rdd, "to return") + *ṭarf* (gaze) is a **two-verse exclusive collocation in the entire Quran**. Both occurrences:

> **27:40** — *qabla an yartadda ilayka ṭarfuk*
> "**before your gaze returns to you**" — Solomon-throne, FUTURE-NEGATED (the throne arrives *before* the return completes)

> **14:43** — *muhṭiʿīn muqniʿī ru'ūsihim, lā yartaddu ilayhim ṭarfuhum, wa-af'idatuhum hawā'*
> "Hurrying, heads upraised, **their gaze does not return to them**, and their hearts are void." — Day of Judgment, NEGATED (the damned's gaze is permanently outbound)

Same operator (*yartadda + ṭarf*). Opposite truth-value. The Quran is using the **same lexical-grammatical mechanism** to encode two opposite cosmic events:

- 27:40: *a divine-knowledge event* moves an object faster than the gaze-return can complete.
- 14:43: *a divine-judgment event* freezes the gaze so that no return ever happens.

In 27:40, the eye-flicker is *outrun*. In 14:43, the eye-flicker is *suspended*. Both verses use the gaze-return as the **temporal unit being denied** — denied on different sides. 27:40 denies that the gaze can complete *before* the throne arrives. 14:43 denies that the gaze can complete *at all*.

This is one of the corpus's cleanest lexical mirrors. The two ayahs are 13 surahs apart, with very different rhetorical contexts (a court anecdote of miraculous transport vs an apocalyptic vision of the damned). The Quran is using the operator to bracket the ENDPOINTS of a single conceptual axis — **what divine action does to perception**:

- Divine-favorable action: *operates faster than perception can register*. (27:40)
- Divine-judging action: *freezes perception so the damned cannot look away*. (14:43)

The mirror is invisible to translators who idiomatize. **Khattab erases it twice**: at 27:40 he renders "in the blink of an eye" (collapsing into the lmH idiom), at 14:43 he renders "never blinking" (collapsing into a generic English idiom for fixed gaze). The other three translators (Saheeh, Pickthall, Arberry) preserve "gaze returns / does not return" in both verses, keeping the lexical bridge intact. The mirror is **only visible if the translator preserves the *yartadda*-structure**. Khattab does not, and so a Khattab-only reader cannot see the mirror at all.

This is one of two empirically-confirmable cases in the teleportation corpus where translator-idiomatization deletes a structural echo (the other being the 16:77/54:50 *iḍāfa*-vs-*bi-* distinction, §7).

## 7. The *iḍāfa* vs *bi-* distinction in 16:77 vs 54:50

The two *lamḥ* occurrences differ in **a one-letter grammatical move** that reflects a real semantic distinction:

- **16:77** — *ka-lamḥ-i al-baṣar-i* — *iḍāfa* (genitive construct chain). Definite. "As **the** flicker **of** the vision."
- **54:50** — *ka-lamḥ-in bi-l-baṣar-i* — indefinite *lamḥ* + preposition *bi-* "with/by." "As **a** flicker **with/by** the vision."

The semantic shading (see `concept-lmH.md` §Grammatical Note for the full treatment):
- *of the vision* (16:77) frames *lamḥ* as **a property of the eye-event** — the kind-of-flicker that vision has.
- *with the vision* (54:50) frames *lamḥ* as **an act performed using vision** — vision as the instrument by which the flicker happens.

This is a **noun-vs-verb cline**:
- 16:77 is *descriptive*: the matter of the Hour *belongs to the category* of speeds-of-which-the-eye-flicker-is-an-example.
- 54:50 is *performative*: the divine command *is an act* of the same kind as a flicker performed-with-the-eye.

The shift from *iḍāfa* to *bi-* tracks **the shift from noun-of-property (the Hour = a kind of speed) to verbal-noun-of-act (the command = a kind of action)**. It is the Arabic doing one micro-grammatical job: signaling whether the comparator is doing **categorial** work (16:77) or **dynamic** work (54:50).

**No English translation captures the distinction.** All four translators (Saheeh, Pickthall, Arberry, Khattab) flatten both verses to "as / like a glance / twinkling / blink of the eye." The Arabic is making a choice the English cannot register, and translation-divergence captures only the *style* split (glance / twinkling / blink), not the *grammar* split.

This is a **methodologically important finding** that recurs across the corpus (see methodology log on translator-idiomatization). The Quran's *iḍāfa*-vs-*bi-* contrast is one of the most micro-grammatical lexical-structural moves available in Arabic, and it is invisible in any English rendering. Source-text-grounded analysis must reconstruct it by hand.

The interpretive consequence: the two *lamḥ* verses are doing **different rhetorical jobs**, even though they look like duplications. 16:77 *categorizes* the Hour (it belongs to a class of speeds); 54:50 *enacts* the command (it is an act of a kind). The Quran is partitioning the speed-formula by grammatical mood: descriptive vs performative. To collapse them is to lose the partition.

## 8. *kun fa-yakūn* as foundational formula — and its convergence with the Trumpet at 6:73

*Kun fa-yakūn* sits at the top of the speed-ladder (§4) — but its rhetorical character is different from the other formulas. It is not a comparator. It is **a closed grammatical loop**.

The canonical instance, 36:82:

> إِنَّمَآ أَمۡرُهُۥٓ إِذَآ أَرَادَ شَيۡـًٔا أَن يَقُولَ لَهُۥ كُن فَيَكُونُ
>
> *"Indeed, His command (*amruhu*), when He wills (*arāda*) a thing, is to say to it 'Be!' (*kun*) and it is (*fa-yakūn*)."*

The grammar:
- *amr* (command) is the **noun** of the divine speech-act.
- *idhā arāda shayʾan* (when He wills a thing) is the **conditional** that triggers the command.
- *yaqūla lahu kun* (saying to it 'Be') is the **imperative**.
- *fa-yakūn* (and it is) is the **immediate effect**, where *fa-* signals **no temporal gap**.

The two verbs in the loop are *both from √k-w-n* (to be). The command-form (*kun*) and the actualized-form (*yakūn*) are **the same root inflected differently**. There is no second linguistic event between them. God says "be," and being-happens — and the *being* is grammatically tied back to the *be*-command by sharing its consonants.

This is the Quran's **theory of divine action stripped to its minimum**: a speech-act in the imperative whose effect is the actualization of the imperative's content, with the actualization-verb morphologically derived from the imperative-verb. There is no causal arrow with an interval; there is just an imperative-and-its-completion as one grammatical event.

### The 8 occurrences and their domains

| Ayah | Domain | Notes |
|---|---|---|

The eight occurrences cluster into **three theological functions**:
1. **Cosmogonic** (2:117) — creation of the universe.
2. **Christological** (3:47, 3:59, 19:35) — Jesus's miraculous birth, Adam-parallel, refutation of son-of-God doctrine. Three of the eight, all on Jesus.
3. **Eschatological-resurrective** (6:73, 40:68) — the resurrection as a re-creation. Two of the eight.
4. **Formal definition** (16:40, 36:82) — abstract statements of how divine command works.

The implication: *kun fa-yakūn* is the **single formula the Quran uses for every act of divine creative-causation**, from the Big Bang to virgin birth to resurrection. **The Quran asserts that all four are the same kind of act**: a divine imperative whose effect is immediate and formally guaranteed by the imperative's grammar.

### 6:73 — the convergence ayah

> وَهُوَ ٱلَّذِي خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضَ بِٱلۡحَقِّۖ وَيَوۡمَ يَقُولُ كُن فَيَكُونُۚ قَوۡلُهُ ٱلۡحَقُّۚ وَلَهُ ٱلۡمُلۡكُ يَوۡمَ يُنفَخُ فِي ٱلصُّورِۚ عَٰلِمُ ٱلۡغَيۡبِ وَٱلشَّهَٰدَةِۚ وَهُوَ ٱلۡحَكِيمُ ٱلۡخَبِيرُ
>
> *"And He is the One who created the heavens and the earth in truth. And the day He says 'Be!' and it is — His word is the truth. And His is the dominion the Day the Horn is blown. Knower of the unseen and the witnessed; and He is the Wise, the Aware."*

This is the **only ayah in the Quran** that puts *kun fa-yakūn* and the Trumpet (*yawma yunfaxu fī al-Ṣūr*) in the same breath. Note also that it explicitly mentions *al-ghayb wa-l-shahāda* (the unseen and the witnessed) — the two epistemic categories that 27:40 / 34:14 turn on (see §5).

The structural significance: 6:73 is the Quran's **integrating doctrinal statement** of the speed-architecture. It says: the creation that happened by *kun fa-yakūn* AND the eschaton that begins at the Trumpet AND the dominion that is disclosed on that Day AND the knowledge of unseen-and-witnessed are **one continuous act of divine sovereignty**. The verse refuses to splice the cameras apart for once. This is the **only seam** between the creation-formula and the eschatological-trigger in the corpus, and the seam itself is doctrinal: the same *amr* that said "Be!" at creation will be enacted again at the Trumpet.

The eschatological-architecture synthesis identified the systematic non-overlap of the Trumpet (Ṣūr) with the Hour (Sāʿa). 6:73 confirms that the Trumpet *can* meet *kun fa-yakūn* — but the Hour cannot. This is a **finer-grained partition** than the Phase-1 work surfaced: the partitioning is not just Hour-vs-Trumpet, it is also creation-formula-meets-Trumpet-but-not-Hour. The Quran allows the creation-and-judgment continuum to be stated in one breath; it does not allow the Hour and the Trumpet to be stated in one breath.

## 9. The eschatological connection — the same vocabulary handles end-times

The 16:77 *ka-lamḥ al-baṣar* attached to *al-sāʿa* is the **single most consequential cross-domain link** in the catalog. The Hour (the eschatological trigger) gets the same speed-comparator as the divine command (54:50) and the same eye-flicker family as the throne-event (27:40). One vocabulary, three domains.

### The unified-instantaneity hypothesis

Reading the formulas together produces a coherent claim: **the same kind of divine action that brings the throne in 27:40 brings the Hour in 16:77 brings the universe-and-Jesus in *kun fa-yakūn*.** Different formulas calibrated to different rhetorical contexts, but **one underlying grammar of divine-act-without-interval**.

Evidence for the unified reading:
1. **Lexical overlap**: 16:77 and 27:40 both reference the eye-event (*lamḥ al-baṣar* / *ṭarf*). The lexical overlap signals the same conceptual zone.
2. **Comparator-overrun**: 16:77 adds *aw huwa aqrab* (or even closer) — the Hour is even faster than the comparator that 27:40 uses for the throne. The two events sit on the same speed-axis, with the Hour at the top.
3. **No-interval grammar**: *kun fa-yakūn* asserts no-gap; *fa-iḏā* (and at-once) does the same syntactic work in the *wāḥida* verses. Both reduce the temporal gap to zero by grammatical means.
4. **Cross-domain co-occurrence at 6:73**: creation-formula and Trumpet-formula in one ayah. The seam is real.
5. **Negative space**: there is no Quranic narrative that uses a *slow* divine action. Every reported divine act — creation, prophecy-revelation, miracle-performance, throne-transport, soul-extraction, resurrection-trigger — uses one of the instantaneity formulas or the *fa-iḏā* construction.

### The eschatological speed-cluster

Grouping the eschatological ayahs by speed-formula:

| Event | Formula | Ayahs |
|---|---|---|
| The Hour's arrival | *ka-lamḥ al-baṣar (aw huwa aqrab)* | 16:77 |
| The Hour's surprise | *baghta* | 7:187, 22:55, etc. |
| The first/destruction blast | *Ṣayhatun wāḥida* | 36:29, 36:49, 38:15, 54:31 |
| The resurrection blast | *Ṣayhatun wāḥida*, *zajratun wāḥida* | 36:53, 79:13 |
| The cosmic blast + leveling | *nafkhatun wāḥida + dakkatan wāḥida* | 69:13–14 |
| Resurrection-as-creation | *kun fa-yakūn* | 40:68 |
| God's judgment-speed | *sarīʿ al-ḥisāb* | 8 ayahs (incl. 13:41, 40:17) |

Every phase of the eschatological event has a dedicated instantaneity-formula. The Quran does not narrate the eschaton with verbs of duration; it narrates it with **verbs of immediate-state-change** (*xāmidūn*, *muḥḍarūn*, *yansilūn*) sutured by **single-event triggers** (*nafkhatun wāḥida*, etc.) and **immediate-effect connectives** (*fa-iḏā*).

The implication: **the speed of divine eschatology IS the speed of divine creation IS the speed of teleportation.** All three sit on one ladder, and the formulas are domain-specific calibrations of the same underlying claim — that divine action does not have an interval.

## 11. Decomposable vs atomic speed-architectures

The lmH and Trf concept-files identified a structural distinction in the architecture of the comparators themselves:

- **lmH formula**: *ka-lamḥ + al-baṣar* — **decomposable** (act + substrate). *Lamḥ* is the verbal-noun of the act (the flickering); *al-baṣar* is the substrate (the eye/vision). The two are separately named.
- **Trf formula**: *ṭarf* — **atomic** (single noun bundling gaze + flicker). *Ṭarf* names both the eye-event and the substrate in one nominal.

These two architectures **never co-occur in the same ayah**. The Quran picks one or the other per verse:

- **lmH (decomposed)** is used for **divine command** (16:77 Hour, 54:50 amr).
- **Trf (atomic)** is used for **narrative speed** (27:40 throne, 14:43 the damned).

The hypothesis from `concept-lmH.md` §The ka-lamḥ formula: **decomposable architecture = divine; atomic architecture = human-perceptual.**

The integrated reading sharpens this. *Decomposable* speed needs two grammatical pieces because the divine act involves both the *event* (lamḥ) and the *substrate* (baṣar) — God's command is the *event* that uses the *eye-substrate* as its measure. The decomposition tracks the ontological partition between divine-event-side and creature-substrate-side. *Atomic* speed bundles event-and-substrate into one because the human-perceptual measurement does not need to make that partition — the gaze (*ṭarf*) is just the eye-event, no substrate needed.

This connects to a deeper pattern. The Quran systematically **decomposes divine action into act + substrate** (lamḥ + baṣar; amr + shay'; rūḥ + fīhi; nfx + Ṣūr). It systematically **bundles human action into atomic verbs** (ṭarf, qiyām, jalsa, niyya). The lexical architecture mirrors the theological one: divine action is *act-on-creation*, requiring two roles; human action is *act-in-itself*, requiring one.

The decomposition has a downstream consequence: **the Quran can describe divine speed by reference to a creature-substrate** (the divine command is *as-fast-as-the-eye-can-flicker*). It cannot describe creature-speed by reference to a divine substrate. This is grammatical asymmetry encoding the ontological asymmetry. The eye exists; it can be referenced as a yardstick. There is no created yardstick fast enough to measure divine speed except in metaphor.

Why two architectures for the same conceptual zone? The data supports the hypothesis: **the Quran reserves the decomposable architecture for divine acts that need to be calibrated against creation, and the atomic architecture for narrative events whose speed is being reported within the human frame**. The throne in 27:40 is a narrative event (Solomon experiences it); the Hour in 16:77 is a divine act (transmitted to humans as a description). The architectures track the rhetorical register.

## 12. The mechanism-silence pattern

The most striking thing about the entire instantaneity catalog: **none of the formulas describe a mechanism**. They are all comparators or pure grammar.

- *kun fa-yakūn* — closed grammatical loop, no mechanism.
- *ka-lamḥ al-baṣar* — comparison to the eye-flicker, no mechanism.
- *qabla an yartadda ilayka ṭarfuk* — comparison to the gaze-return, no mechanism.
- *nafkhatun wāḥida* — single blast, no mechanism (no description of how the blast moves, who hears it first, what happens between blast and effect).
- *sarīʿ al-ḥisāb* — abstract attribute, no mechanism.
- *baghta* — modal description of unwarning, no mechanism.

Every formula in the catalog **reports the speed without reporting the means**. There is no Quranic equivalent of "the angel transported the throne by ___" or "the universe was created by ___ taking ___ amount of energy/material/time." The Quran refuses to populate the mechanism slot.

This is consistent with the broader teleportation finding in `narrative-solomon-suite.md` §9 (the throne event has no portal, no dematerialization, no transit-haze) and with the Trumpet's silence about the agent (`research-trumpet.md` §4 — the blower is unnamed). The instantaneity-formulas are the **lexical articulation** of this refusal: the Quran has a vocabulary for *how fast* divine action is, and it has no vocabulary for *how* divine action is.

The methodological implication: **instantaneity-formulas are comparators, not mechanisms.** Reading them as descriptions of mechanism is a category-error. They are the Quran's way of saying "the interval is zero" — they are not its way of saying "and here is the bridge that closes the interval." The bridge does not exist in the text. It exists only in extra-Quranic harmonization (folktales of teleportation, hadith on the Burāq, kalām on divine causality, etc.).

This has a doctrinal consequence: **the Quran is an apophatic theology of mechanism**. It asserts speed; it withholds means. The withholding is the doctrine. To fill the mechanism slot is to convert the Quran's structure-of-sovereignty into a description-of-process, and lose the sovereignty.

## 13. Translation divergence

Pulling divergence and outlier patterns across the catalog:

| Verse | Saheeh | Pickthall | Arberry | Khattab | Outlier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **16:77** | "as a glance of the eye or even nearer" | "as a twinkling of the eye, or it is nearer still" | "as a twinkling of the eye, or nearer" | "would only take the blink of an eye, or even less" | none (Khattab adds verb-frame) |
| **54:50** | "like a glance of the eye" | "as the twinkling of an eye" | "as the twinkling of an eye" | "done in the blink of an eye" | none |
| **27:40** | "before your glance returns to you" | "before thy gaze returneth unto thee" | "before ever thy glance returns to thee" | **"in the blink of an eye"** | Khattab idiomatizes |
| **14:43** | "their glance does not come back to them" | "their gaze returning not to them" | "their glances never returned on themselves" | **"never blinking"** | Khattab idiomatizes |
| **36:82** | "He says to it 'Be,' and it is" | "He saith unto it: Be! and it is" | "He but says to it 'Be,' and it is" | "simply tells it 'Be!' And it is" | uniform |
| **2:117** | "He says to it 'Be,' and it is" | "He saith unto it: Be! and it is" | "He but says to it 'Be,' and it is" | "simply tells it 'Be!' And it is" | uniform |
| **69:13** | "the Horn is blown with one blast" | "the trumpet shall sound one blast" | "the Trumpet is blown with a single blast" | "the Trumpet will be blown with one blast" | uniform |
| **6:73** | "the Day He says 'Be,' and it is… the Day the Horn is blown" | (similar) | (similar) | (similar) | uniform on the formulas |
| **13:41** | "He is swift in account" | "He is swift at reckoning" | "swift at the reckoning" | "swift in reckoning" | uniform |
| **7:187** | "It will not come upon you except unexpectedly" | "It cometh not to you save unawares" | "it shall come on you suddenly" | "will only take you by surprise" | uniform on baghta |

### Patterns

**(1) Translators are uniform on *kun fa-yakūn*.** All four render the formula as "Be! and it is" with minor stylistic variation. The closed grammatical loop survives translation cleanly because it is propositionally simple — there is no idiom to translate, just two short verbs. **The formula's speed-claim survives intact.**

**(2) Translators are uniform on the *wāḥida* formulas.** All four render *nafkhatun wāḥida* as "one blast / single blast." The numeral *wāḥida* is unambiguous in English. **The single-event grammar survives.**

**(3) Translators diverge on the *lamḥ*-and-*ṭarf* formulas in two distinct ways.**
- *Lexical*: glance (Saheeh) vs twinkling (Pickthall, Arberry) vs blink (Khattab). Three different English images for the eye-event.
- *Structural*: Khattab uniquely idiomatizes 27:40 as "in the blink of an eye" and 14:43 as "never blinking" — collapsing both verses into the *lamḥ*-formula even though the Arabic uses *Trf*. **This erases the 27:40 ↔ 14:43 mirror and erases the Trf/lmH partition.**

**(4) No translator preserves the 16:77 vs 54:50 *iḍāfa*-vs-*bi-* distinction.** All four flatten both to "(as) a glance/twinkling/blink of the eye." The grammatical micro-fact is invisible in English.

**(5) Translators are uniform on *sarīʿ al-ḥisāb*** ("swift in account/reckoning"). The divine-attribute formula does not provoke disagreement.

**(6) Translators are uniform on *baghta*** ("suddenly" / "unexpectedly" / "unawares" / "by surprise"). Minor stylistic variation, no substantive disagreement.

### The translator-asymmetry summary

- *Kun fa-yakūn*, *wāḥida*, *sarīʿ al-ḥisāb*, *baghta* — **stable across translators**.
- *Ka-lamḥ al-baṣar* (16:77, 54:50) — **stable in meaning, divergent in lexical choice**, with the *iḍāfa*-vs-*bi-* distinction lost in all renderings.
- *Yartadda ṭarf* (27:40, 14:43) — **fractured**. Three translators preserve the structure; Khattab idiomatizes both, erasing the cross-verse mirror.

The pattern is consistent with the larger finding from `teleportation-divergence.md` and the methodology log: **translator idiomatization erases lexical structure that the Arabic uses to encode comparison**. The 27:40 ↔ 14:43 mirror is the cleanest single example. The 16:77 vs 54:50 *iḍāfa*-vs-*bi-* distinction is the second cleanest. Both are invisible to anyone working from English alone.

## 14. Rabbit holes for Phase 3

**(1) The *fa-iḏā* construction as the syntactic instantaneity-operator.** A dedicated study of every *fa-iḏā hum + state* clause in the eschatological corpus would map the Quran's grammar of narrative-jump-cut. The *fa-iḏā* construction operates *under* the lexical formulas and is arguably the deeper grammar of divine instantaneity — every *wāḥida* verse uses it; many *kun fa-yakūn* contexts have it nearby; even 27:40's *fa-lammā raʾāhu mustaqirran ʿindahu* is a kindred move (the *fa-* of immediate consequence). Build `notes/teleportation/2-cross-cutting/research-faidha-grammar.md`.

**(2) 6:73 as the doctrinal hinge.** The single ayah unifying *kun fa-yakūn* and the Trumpet deserves its own deep read. Why 6:73 specifically? What is the surrounding rhetorical frame in surah al-Anʿām? Does the convergence imply that the eschaton is itself an act of *kun fa-yakūn* (re-creation), making the Trumpet not a separate event but a dramatized rendering of the same divine speech-act? This is the most theologically dense single-verse intersection in the catalog.

**(3) The *aw huwa aqrab* hyperbolic capstone — does the corpus contain other "X *aw* X" speed-overruns?** A search for *aw + comparative* across the speed-vocabulary would test whether 16:77 is unique or part of a pattern. Hypothesis: 16:77 is the only place where a speed-comparator outpaces itself, and this uniqueness is doctrinally load-bearing — the Hour is the only event explicitly faster than its own image.

**(4) *Sarīʿ al-ḥisāb* and the 13:41 binding.** The single co-occurrence between the speed-attribute and a Trf-edge formula (*min aṭrāf al-arḍ*) at 13:41 is suspicious. Why does the Quran pair the geographical edge-eating with the swift reckoning in this one verse? Is the connection that *both* are operations of the same divine speed — the world is consumed from its margins fast, and the moral accounting moves fast? A dedicated probe of *sarīʿ + neighbor-noun* co-occurrences would test whether 13:41 is a one-off or a structural pairing.

**(5) The Christological cluster of *kun fa-yakūn* (3:47, 3:59, 19:35).** Three of the eight occurrences are about Jesus. This is over-representation: Jesus's miraculous birth (3:47), the Adam-parallel (3:59), the refutation of divine son (19:35) — all use the same formula. The Quran is doing **substantial Christological work** through the instantaneity-vocabulary: it places Jesus's birth in the same ontological category as creation-of-Adam-from-dust, and it uses *kun fa-yakūn* to assert that miraculous origin does not entail divine status. A dedicated study of the Christological *kun fa-yakūn* cluster would unpack this.

**(6) Decomposable-vs-atomic across other root-pairs.** The lmH/Trf partition (act+substrate vs single noun) may be a corpus-wide pattern. Other candidate pairs to test: *amr + shay'* (command + thing) vs *qaḍāʾ* (decree, atomic); *nfx + Ṣūr* (blow + horn) vs *Ṣayḥa* (cry, atomic). If the pattern holds, the Quran may be systematically using decomposable lexicons for divine acts and atomic lexicons for human/narrative events.

**(7) The mechanism-silence as a corpus-wide doctrine.** The instantaneity catalog refuses to describe mechanism. Does this refusal extend to other domains where mechanism would be expected? Examples: prophetic revelation (the Quran does not describe how Jibrīl conveys); soul-extraction (the verbs of *tawaffā* are reported, but the mechanism is silent); creation of Adam (clay is mentioned but not the assembly process); resurrection (the bodies are reassembled but no description of the process). A corpus-wide audit of *mechanism-silence* would test whether instantaneity-vocabulary is one expression of a broader apophatic-grammar of divine causation.

**(8) The Trumpet-vs-Hour and the *kun fa-yakūn*-meets-Trumpet seam.** The eschatological-architecture synthesis identified the Trumpet/Hour zero-overlap. The instantaneity catalog identifies a single *kun fa-yakūn*/Trumpet co-occurrence at 6:73. Why does the Quran allow this seam but forbid the other? Hypothesis: *kun fa-yakūn* is a meta-statement *about* divine action, and as such can name any divine event in the same ayah; *al-Sāʿa* and *al-Ṣūr* are domain-specific naming-conventions whose partition is preserved at ayah-level. A dedicated investigation would test the seam-vs-partition rule.

**(9) The Khattab-idiomatization pattern.** Khattab consistently collapses Arabic structural micro-distinctions into familiar English idioms. He erases the 27:40 ↔ 14:43 mirror; he flattens the 16:77 vs 54:50 *iḍāfa*-vs-*bi-*; he renders the throne-event with the Hour-formula. **Every Khattab idiomatization in the instantaneity catalog moves the verse toward the canonical English speed-image.** A focused study of Khattab's idiomatization choices across the catalog would surface the pattern's edges and may reveal a coherent translation-theology behind it. This connects to a broader methodology-log entry about translator-side erasure of structural echoes.

**(10) The negative-space audit.** The Quran has *no* slow-divine-action verses. Every reported divine action is instantaneous or brought-near-instantaneous via comparator. Test: enumerate all verbs with God as subject; check whether any has a duration-attached. Hypothesis: the corpus has zero exceptions. If true, this is the strongest possible case for a unified instantaneity doctrine.
