# Cross-cutting research: Agency, Comitative Grammar, and Mechanism-Silence — the Quran's theory of divine action

> **Phase-2 synthesis.** Three convergent findings from Phase 1 — the KNOWLEDGE-OVER-STRENGTH inversion at 27:38–40, the bi-ʿabdihi COMITATIVE pattern in horizontal teleportation, and the corpus-wide MECHANISM-SILENCE — describe, when read together, the Quran's coherent theory of divine action. Knowledge is the medium; comitative bi- marks accompanying-care; silence about mechanism is theological discipline. This document integrates the three threads and reads them as one structure.

---

## 1. Headline finding — three convergent patterns

Three patterns that surfaced independently in Phase 1 fit together as one architecture:

1. **The 27:38–40 inversion**: when a fast-jinn-strength offer (*qawiyyun amīn*) is set against a knowledge-from-Book offer in the same court, the speed-comparator encodes the winner — and **knowledge wins by an order of magnitude**. The Quran does not assert a doctrine of "knowledge beats strength"; it stages it grammatically inside a single passage and lets the comparator do the work.
2. **The bi-ʿabdihi grammar**: every horizontal extraordinary teleportation event in the Quran uses a comitative *bi-* prepositional phrase — God moves *with* the human, not *of* or *for* the human. The vertical extraordinary events (Idrīs, Jesus) use direct accusative + *ilayya / ilayh*. **Two grammars, two modes of divine action: comitative-accompanying (horizontal) vs unilateral-relocation (vertical).**
3. **The mechanism-silence**: across every teleportation event in the Quran — Solomon's throne, Idrīs's raising, Jesus's translation, Maryam's provisions, the angels' arrivals, the Cave Sleepers' duration — the trajectory is unstated. The Quran reports juxtaposition, never traversal. There is no portal, no flight, no flash, no transit-haze. Two endpoints; nothing between them.

Each finding is structurally independent (they operate on different levels: lexical-content, grammatical-preposition, narrative-event). Together they describe a coherent theory:

- **Divine action is knowledge-mediated** — the fastest mode of cosmic operation is not strength but Book-knowledge.
- **Divine action toward humans-on-earth is accompanying-with-care** — the comitative *bi-* makes the human a carried-companion, not a relocated-object.
- **Divine action's mechanism is reserved** — the Quran refuses to populate the slot where a mechanism would go. The withholding is the doctrine.

The decisive integrating move is to recognize that the three findings encode one underlying theological discipline: **divine causation does not map onto creaturely process-vocabulary**. The Quran will name the speed (*qabla an yartadda ilayka ṭarfuk*); the carried party (*bi-ʿabdihi*); the result (*mustaqirran ʿindahu*). It will not name the bridge that connects start to end — because to name the bridge would be to suggest that the bridge has its own agency, and the only agent the Quran will grant in such events is God acting through knowledge.

## 2. The 27:38–40 inversion in detail

### The two parallel offers

The grammatical parallelism is exact, which sharpens the comparator-difference:

> **27:39** — قَالَ عِفۡرِيتࣱ مِّنَ ٱلۡجِنِّ أَنَا۠ ءَاتِيكَ بِهِۦ قَبۡلَ أَن تَقُومَ مِن مَّقَامِكَ وَإِنِّي عَلَيۡهِ لَقَوِيٌّ أَمِينࣱ
>
> *"An ʿifrīt of the jinn said: 'I will bring it to you before you rise from your place, and I am for it strong, trustworthy.'"*

> **27:40** — قَالَ ٱلَّذِي عِندَهُۥ عِلۡمࣱ مِّنَ ٱلۡكِتَٰبِ أَنَا۠ ءَاتِيكَ بِهِۦ قَبۡلَ أَن يَرۡتَدَّ إِلَيۡكَ طَرۡفُكَۚ فَلَمَّا رَءَاهُ مُسۡتَقِرًّا عِندَهُۥ قَالَ هَٰذَا مِن فَضۡلِ رَبِّي
>
> *"Said the one who had knowledge from the Book: 'I will bring it to you before your gaze returns to you.' Then when he saw it settled before him, he said: 'This is from the favor of my Lord…'"*

The framing is *anā ātīka bihi qabla an + verb* in both cases. Same subject pronoun, same main verb (√Aty — to bring, to come), same prepositional structure, same temporal adverb. **What differs is exactly two things**:

| Element | ʿIfrīt | Book-knower |
|---|---|---|
| Comparator-clock | *taqūma min maqāmika* (you rise from your place) | *yartadda ilayka ṭarfuk* (your gaze returns to you) |
| Credentials | *qawiyyun amīn* (strong, trustworthy) — TWO | *ʿindahu ʿilmun min al-kitāb* (he had knowledge from the Book) — ONE |

The credentialing-clauses are themselves asymmetric. The ʿifrīt offers two credentials (a strength claim, a trust claim), each itself a claim about the agent's reliability as a transport-operator. The Book-knower offers one credential — and it is not about strength or reliability at all. It is about *knowledge*. The credential-clause is shorter and the credential-quality is different: *epistemic*, not *physical-moral*.

### What the result tells us

The narrative does not stage a contest, does not name a winner, does not allow the offers to be debated. The next clause is *fa-lammā raʾāhu mustaqirran ʿindahu* — "when he saw it settled before him." The throne is *already there*, *already at rest*. The Book-knower's claim has been adjudicated by the result: he succeeded; the throne is mustaqirran; this is from the favor of my Lord.

What the Quran is encoding:

1. **Knowledge outpaces strength as a divine medium.** The ʿifrīt's strength is real (he is *qawiyyun*), and his moral reliability is presumed (he is *amīn*). But the Book-knowledge agent is faster by orders of magnitude. The speed-gap is not closed by stronger strength — it is closed by *different category of access*.
2. **The credentialing-asymmetry mirrors the speed-asymmetry.** Two credentials yield slower transport; one credential of higher class yields faster transport. The Quran is encoding a theological principle through grammatical economy: more is not better; *kind* matters more than *quantity*.
3. **Knowledge of the BOOK specifically — not knowledge in general.** The text is precise: *ʿilmun min al-kitāb*. This is not generic intellectual knowledge; it is partial-knowledge from a divine register. The fastest mode of cosmic operation is access (even partial) to divine inscription.

This is one of the cleanest grammatically-encoded value-inversions in the corpus. The teaching is not propositional ("knowledge beats strength"); it is *demonstrated* by a comparator-difference between two parallel sentences, and validated by a third sentence reporting the result.

### Why this matters for divine action theology

The 27:38–40 passage tells us *how the Quran thinks about divine causation through created agents*: speed is a function of epistemic access. The ʿifrīt's transport-power is bounded by his cognitive horizon — he is creature-of-*al-shahāda* (the witnessed), strong but not Book-knowing. The Book-knower is *ghayb*-adjacent — he has access to a divine register, and that access translates directly into transport-velocity. **What can be commanded is bounded by what can be known**, and the highest-velocity transport is reserved for the highest-class knowledge.

This is the structural opening for the broader theory: the medium of divine action toward humans is *knowledge*. Strength can deliver fast bringing; only knowledge can deliver gaze-speed bringing.

## 3. The Book-knower as a category — anonymity and the type-pattern

The agent of 27:40 is named only as *al-ladhī ʿindahu ʿilmun min al-kitāb* — "the one who had knowledge from the Book." The Quran does not give a personal name. Tradition supplies several candidates: Āṣaf b. Barakhyā (the rabbinic-derived Solomonic vizier), al-Khiḍr, an angel, even Solomon himself reading his own inscription.

The Quran's silence is not a missing detail. **It is a deliberate type-pattern** that the corpus uses across multiple narratives. When a story needs an agent whose authority is *direct knowledge from God's side*, the Quran withholds the proper name and supplies only the function:

| Anonymous figure | Verse | Function |
|---|---|---|
| The one with knowledge from the Book | 27:40 | Brings Solomon's throne in less than a gaze-return |
| The servant Moses meets | 18:65 | Possesses *ʿilm min ladunnā* (knowledge from Our presence); demonstrates apparent injustices that resolve into divine justice |
| The man who passes by the destroyed town | 2:259 | Doubts resurrection; is made-to-die 100 years; is revived; sees food unspoiled, donkey reassembled |
| The Sleepers of the Cave | 18:9–26 | Sleep 309 years; wake to find the world changed; their names are not given (the text rebukes those who guess: 18:22) |
| The "perfect human" form Gabriel takes for Maryam | 19:17 | *Bashar sawiyy* — well-formed human; brings the annunciation |

The Quran has a recurring anonymity-of-key-figures pattern. Tradition rushes to name them; the Quran resists. The reason this matters for the divine-action theory: **anonymity preserves the function as the focus.** If the Book-knower had a name, the reader would attach the speed-claim to the agent's biography (his reputation, his powers, his lineage). With no name, the speed-claim attaches to the *category* — knowledge-from-the-Book. The *type* is more important than the *character*.

Compare the al-Khiḍr passage explicitly. 18:65 introduces the figure as *ʿabdan min ʿibādinā ātaynāhu raḥmatan min ʿindinā wa-ʿallamnāhu min ladunnā ʿilman* — "a servant from among Our servants whom We had given mercy from Us and taught knowledge from Our presence." This is structurally identical to 27:40's category-of-one: an anonymous, knowledge-anchored, single-instance carrier of divine-side knowledge. **The Quran has a recurring type — the unnamed knowledge-bearer — whose function is to embody the principle that some operations require divine-side access.**

The implication for the divine-action theory: knowledge-from-the-divine-side is the highest-class transport medium, and the Quran will not let the medium be reduced to a character. **The Book-knower at 27:40 is a category, not a person.** The type-pattern protects the principle.

## 4. The bi-ʿabdihi catalog

Pulling every Form-IV occurrence of *asrā* in the corpus to verify the comitative pattern:

| Verse | Subject | Comitative phrase | Carried party |
|---|---|---|---|
| **17:1** | God | *bi-ʿabdihi* (with His servant) | Muhammad |
| **11:81** | God (imperative to Lot) | *bi-ahlika* (with your family) | Lot's family |
| **15:65** | God (imperative to Lot) | *bi-ahlika* | Lot's family |
| **20:77** | God (imperative to Moses) | *bi-ʿibādī* (with My servants) | Moses' Israelites |
| **26:52** | God (imperative to Moses) | *bi-ʿibādī* | Moses' Israelites |
| **44:23** | God (imperative to Moses) | *bi-ʿibādī* | Moses' Israelites |

Six occurrences. Six comitatives. Zero exceptions. **Every single Form-IV asrā in the Quran uses a comitative bi- + person.**

The grammatical force of the comitative *bi-* in Arabic is **accompaniment with carrying**. It is not "for" (which would be *li-*), not "to" (which would be *ilā*), not "from" (which would be *min*). The comitative *bi-* makes the human the *attached-object* of divine motion. God is the agent of the motion; the human is *carried-with*.

Compare these constructions to non-asrā motion-with-people in the corpus. When God's prophets travel ordinarily, they go (root *ḏhb*) with their people (e.g. 27:28, *adhhab bi-kitābī hādhā*); the comitative *bi-* there is the same accompaniment grammar but applied to *the prophet's letter*, not to a divinely-carried person. The *asrā bi-* construction reserves the comitative-carrying for divine-agency moments.

### Comparison with the vertical register

Now the comparison that the Phase-1 vertical-horizontal axis paper made explicit. The vertical extraordinary teleportation events — Idrīs and Jesus — use a different grammar entirely:

| Verse | Subject | Construction | Patient | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **19:57** (Idrīs) | God (1pl: *rafaʿnā-*) | direct accusative pronoun + *makānan ʿaliyyā* | *-hu* (Idrīs) | "to a lofty place" |
| **3:55** (Jesus) | God (1sg: *rāfiʿu-*) | direct accusative pronoun + *ilayya* | *-ka* (Jesus) | "to Me" |
| **4:158** (Jesus) | God (3sg: *rafaʿa-*) | direct accusative pronoun + *ilayhi* | *-hu* (Jesus) | "to Him" |

**Zero comitatives.** No *bi-Īsā*, no *bi-Idrīs*. The construction is direct accusative attachment to the verb. The destination is named (a lofty place, to Me, to Him), but the patient is *acted upon directly* — the Arabic does not insert the *with*-relation.

The grammatical contrast is sharp:

- **Horizontal extraordinary**: God moves *with* the human (comitative *bi-*). The grammar foregrounds the accompaniment-relation.
- **Vertical extraordinary**: God *raises* the human (direct accusative). The grammar foregrounds the patient-relation.

These two modes of divine-action-on-human are encoded by different prepositional architectures. **The grammatical asymmetry is total.**

## 5. Why bi-ʿabdihi for horizontal but direct accusative for vertical

Why does Arabic — and the Quran's deliberate choice within Arabic — use *bi-* (with) for horizontal events and direct accusative for vertical ones?

Three hypotheses, each grammatically and theologically grounded:

### Hypothesis 1: Horizontal is interpersonal; vertical is extractive

In horizontal events, both source and destination are *on the same plane* — the plane of human geography. Lot's family travels from Sodom (a place humans live) to safety (another place humans live). Moses' Israelites cross the sea from Egypt to the wilderness — both points in the human-political world. Muhammad goes from al-Masjid al-Ḥarām to al-Masjid al-Aqṣā — both consecrated mosques. The geometry is *across* the human plane. **God *accompanies* the human across human space.**

In vertical events, the destination is *out of* the human plane. Idrīs is raised to *makānan ʿaliyyā* — a lofty place that is no longer in earthly geography. Jesus is raised *to Me / to Him* — a destination that is the divine presence itself. The geometry is *out* of the human plane. **God *removes* the human from human space.**

The grammatical fit: comitative *bi-* (accompaniment) belongs to motion *across* a shared plane; direct accusative belongs to motion *out of* a plane (one party operating, one party operated-on). The horizontal is a journey-with; the vertical is a translation-up.

### Hypothesis 2: Comitative bi- implies care

The comitative *bi-* in Arabic is not merely instrumental ("by means of") and not merely associative ("along with"). When applied to a person being moved by another's motion, it carries a connotation of *protective accompaniment*. The carried party is *with* the carrier in a way that implies the carrier is responsible for them.

This fits the observed pattern that the *asrā bi-* events are universally rescue-events or honor-events:
- Lot is rescued from Sodom's destruction (11:81, 15:65 — carrier responsible for survival).
- Moses' Israelites are rescued from Pharaoh's pursuit (20:77, 26:52, 44:23 — carrier responsible for survival).
- Muhammad is taken on the Isrāʾ for sign-disclosure (17:1 — carrier responsible for reception of the vision).

In every case, the carried party survives by virtue of the carrying. **The comitative *bi-* encodes a caring escort.**

The vertical register does not need this protective construction because the *destination is the divine presence itself*. Idrīs is raised to a lofty place; Jesus is raised to Me. There is no separate destination requiring divine protection en route — the destination *is* the divine presence. The patient-relation grammar suffices because there is no *across* to be cared-for-during.

### Hypothesis 3: Horizontal is mission-continuing; vertical is mission-closing

Building on the vertical-horizontal axis paper (§5): horizontal extraordinary events are round-trip; vertical events are one-way for prophets. Lot returns to a new life (his family is preserved); Moses returns to lead his people; Muhammad returns to Mecca (implicit in *laylan*); Maryam returns to her people with infant Jesus. Idrīs and Jesus do not return.

The comitative *bi-* fits round-trip: God *accompanies* the prophet through an extraordinary intra-earthly transit, and the prophet returns to mission. The direct accusative + destination-*ilayhi* fits one-way: God *takes* the prophet out of earthly mission to Himself.

The comitative is a *continuing-relation* construction; the direct accusative + *ilayya* is a *destination-as-terminus* construction.

### Synthesis

These three hypotheses are not mutually exclusive. They identify three aspects of the same structural distinction. The horizontal-comitative-care-mission-continuing cluster fits together; the vertical-accusative-extraction-mission-closing cluster fits together. **The grammar is theologically loaded all the way down.**

The downstream prediction: any Quranic event that uses *asrā bi-* should be characterized by (a) intra-earthly transit, (b) divine-accompaniment, (c) survival/honor of the carried party, (d) round-trip or rescue-from-destruction. Any event that uses *rafaʿa* + accusative should be characterized by (a) trans-earthly translation, (b) unilateral divine action, (c) one-way removal from earthly mission, (d) destination-as-divine-presence.

The data verifies both predictions. The bi-ʿabdihi grammar is not a stylistic choice; it is the Quran's *grammatical theology of horizontal divine action*.

## 6. The mechanism-silence pattern — a catalog of cases

The Quran is consistent across every teleportation event: **the trajectory is unstated**. The text reports endpoints; it does not narrate transit. Pulling every case to make the pattern audible:

### Solomon's throne (27:40)

> فَلَمَّا رَءَاهُ مُسۡتَقِرًّا عِندَهُۥ
>
> *"Then when he saw it settled before him."*

The Book-knower said *anā ātīka bihi qabla an yartadda ilayka ṭarfuk* — "I will bring it before your gaze returns to you." The next narrative move is *raʾāhu mustaqirran ʿindahu* — Solomon saw it *already settled* before him. The verb *istaqarra* (Form X of √qrr — to come-to-rest, to settle) is a state-verb; the throne is *at rest at B*. There is no transit-haze, no portal-effect, no flash, no dematerialization, no description of the trajectory between Sheba and Jerusalem. The throne is *not at Solomon's court*; then it is *at Solomon's court at rest*. The mechanism is annihilated.

### Idrīs's raising (19:57)

> وَرَفَعۡنَٰهُ مَكَانًا عَلِيًّا
>
> *"And We raised him to a lofty place."*

Three words for the entire event. No description of the ascent. No vehicle (no chariot, no flight, no light-tunnel). No companions. No duration. No description of the destination beyond *makānan ʿaliyyā* (a lofty place — adjective only, no specifics). The mechanism is so absent that the verse is one of the shortest miracle-events in the entire corpus.

### Jesus's raising (3:55, 4:158)

> 3:55 — إِنِّي مُتَوَفِّيكَ وَرَافِعُكَ إِلَيَّ
>
> *"Indeed, I will take you fully and raise you to Me."*

> 4:158 — بَل رَّفَعَهُ ٱللَّهُ إِلَيۡهِ
>
> *"Rather, God raised him to Himself."*

Same minimalism. The verbs operate; the destination is named (*ilayya / ilayh*); the mechanism is silent. *Mutawaffīka* (form V of √wfy) names the soul-extraction event but does not describe how it occurs. *Rāfiʿuka / rafaʿahu* (form I of √rfE) names the raising but does not describe the ascent.

### Maryam's provisions (3:37)

> كُلَّمَا دَخَلَ عَلَيۡهَا زَكَرِيَّا ٱلۡمِحۡرَابَ وَجَدَ عِندَهَا رِزۡقࣰا
>
> *"Whenever Zachariah entered upon her in the prayer-niche, he found provisions with her."*

Zachariah enters the miḥrāb. Provisions are *there*. Maryam says *huwa min ʿindi llāh* — "it is from God." There is no description of how the provisions arrived. No angelic delivery is staged; no door is opened; no basket descends. The provisions are simply *found-at-her-side*. The mechanism is annihilated.

### Angels visiting Abraham (11:69, 15:51, 51:25)

> 11:69 — وَلَقَدۡ جَآءَتۡ رُسُلُنَآ إِبۡرَٰهِيمَ بِٱلۡبُشۡرَىٰ قَالُواْ سَلَٰمࣰا
>
> *"Our messengers came to Abraham with the good news; they said 'Peace.'"*

> 15:52 — إِذۡ دَخَلُواْ عَلَيۡهِ فَقَالُواْ سَلَٰمࣰا
>
> *"When they entered upon him and said 'Peace.'"*

The angels *arrive*. They *enter*. They *speak*. There is no description of their journey to Abraham's tent. No flight. No light. No materialization. They simply *are there*. The verb *dakhalū ʿalayhi* (they entered upon him) presumes a doorway but specifies no journey. As `narrative-angels-and-jinn.md` §3 made explicit: this is the **unmarked arrival**, the Quran's signature mode for angelic appearance.

### The Cave Sleepers' duration (18:25)

> وَلَبِثُواْ فِي كَهۡفِهِمۡ ثَلَٰثَ مِاْئَةࣲ سِنِينَ وَٱزۡدَادُواْ تِسۡعࣰا
>
> *"And they stayed in their cave three hundred years, and added nine."*

309 years. The verse states the duration. It does not describe what happened during those years. No dreams, no decay, no preservation-mechanism, no metabolic miracle. Their bodies are described as appearing awake (18:18 *taḥsabuhum ayqāẓan wa-hum ruqūd*), but the *temporal mechanism* — how 309 years passed for the world while their bodies were preserved — is not narrated. The Quran reports the start (they entered, were sealed asleep) and the end (they wake, they ask each other how long it has been). The middle is annihilated.

### The 100-year sleeper (2:259)

> فَأَمَاتَهُ ٱللَّهُ مِاْئَةَ عَامࣲ ثُمَّ بَعَثَهُۥ
>
> *"So God caused him to die a hundred years, then raised him."*

Same pattern. *Amātahu* (caused him to die) and *baʿathahu* (raised him) — two verbs naming start and end. Between them: a hundred years compressed to nothing. His food unspoiled, his donkey reassembled — but the mechanism of preservation/reconstruction is unnarrated. The Quran reports facts; the bridge is silent.

### The pattern across the catalog

In every single case, the Quran:
1. Names the start-state.
2. Names the end-state.
3. Names the speed (when speed matters) by comparator (*qabla an yartadda ṭarfuk*) or by simple temporal bound (*laylan*, *one night*).
4. Refuses to populate the mechanism slot.

The gap between start and end is not narrated as *traversal*; it is collapsed by *juxtaposition*. The Arabic *fa-* of immediate consequence carries the syntactic load (*fa-lammā raʾāhu mustaqirran ʿindahu* — "and at-once when he saw it settled"). The connective is grammatical, not mechanical.

This is consistent across every teleportation register — vertical, horizontal, in-place-temporal, angelic. The Quran's narrative grammar of cross-realm transit is **arrival without journey** (cf. `narrative-solomon-suite.md` §1, §10; `narrative-angels-and-jinn.md` §3, §13). The mechanism-silence is not an artifact of one narrative tradition; it is the *signature feature* of the Quran's teleportation theology.

## 7. The mechanism-silence as APOPHATIC THEOLOGY of causation

Consider what the Quran *does* have vocabulary for, and what it *does not* have vocabulary for, in the domain of divine action.

### What the Quran HAS

The Quran has elaborate vocabulary for **how fast** divine action is. The instantaneity-catalog (`research-instantaneity-catalog.md`) documented seven distinct lexical-grammatical formulas, each calibrated to a specific domain:

- *kun fa-yakūn* — closed-loop creation/decree (8 ayahs).
- *ka-lamḥ al-baṣar* — eye-flicker comparator (16:77, 54:50).
- *qabla an yartadda ilayka ṭarfuk* — gaze-return comparator (27:40).
- *nafkhatun wāḥida / Ṣayhatun wāḥida / zajratun wāḥida* — single-blast eschatological trigger.
- *sarīʿ al-ḥisāb* — swift-in-reckoning divine attribute (8 ayahs).
- *baghta* — sudden, unwarned arrival (13 ayahs).
- *aw huwa aqrab* — hyperbolic capstone (16:77 only).

Plus the syntactic instantaneity-operator *fa-iḏā hum + state* (and at-once they are…).

The Quran has vocabulary for **comparators of speed**: every formula above measures divine speed against a creature-substrate (the eye, the body, the breath, the moment of bodily volition). These comparators are precise; they are calibrated; they are partitioned by domain.

The Quran has vocabulary for **directional motion**: *ilayya / ilayh* (to Me / to Him); *min* … *ilā* (from … to); *fawqahum* (above them); *ḥawlahu* (around it); *ʿindahu* (with him / at him).

The Quran has vocabulary for **the carried party**: *bi-ʿabdihi*, *bi-ʿibādī*, *bi-ahlika*. The grammar of accompaniment.

### What the Quran does NOT have

The Quran has *zero vocabulary* for **how** divine action works. There is no:
- Description of a portal, gateway, threshold, or aperture between realms.
- Description of dematerialization, rematerialization, or any state-change mechanism.
- Description of a vehicle, mount, or conveyance for divine-action events (the Quran's silence on Burāq is structural, not stylistic).
- Description of a transit-medium (light, fire, water, air) used for cross-realm motion.
- Description of energy, force, or substance that effects the motion.
- Description of intermediate states between start and end.
- Description of the agent's own subjective experience of the transit (no angelic phenomenology, no prophetic interior of the Isrāʾ).

This is a category-wide silence, not a topic-specific one. The Quran has many vocabularies it could deploy (it can describe physical phenomena richly — the wind in 51:1–6, the night in 81:17, the fire in 56:71); it chooses not to deploy any of them for divine-action mechanisms.

### Why the silence is the doctrine

This is the Quran's **apophatic theology of causation**. To describe a mechanism would be to suggest that the mechanism has its own agency. If the throne arrives via a portal, the portal is doing work — the portal becomes the agent that moves the throne. If Idrīs is raised on a chariot, the chariot is the agent that lifts Idrīs. If angels arrive by flight, the flight-medium becomes the agent that brings the angels. **In each case, naming a mechanism would be naming a created agent that does the work of God.**

The Quran refuses this. The agent of every teleportation event is God. The mechanism slot is left blank because filling it would compromise the theology of unitary divine causation. Knowledge-from-the-Book brings the throne — and the *knowledge* is the medium; there is no portal, no vehicle, no intermediary. The medium is divine-side knowledge itself, and divine-side knowledge does not require a created bridge.

This connects to the time investigation's parallel finding: the lexical apartheid between divine-time vocabulary and creature-time vocabulary (see `notes/time/2-cross-cutting/research-divine-vs-creature-time.md`). The Quran reserves certain vocabulary-spaces for God alone. Divine eternity does not borrow creature-time words; divine action does not borrow creature-mechanism words. The silence is not absence of information; it is *protected vocabulary-space*.

### The doctrine articulated

**The Quran has a vocabulary for HOW FAST divine action is, and zero vocabulary for HOW divine action works. The withholding is the doctrine.**

The withholding does three theological jobs simultaneously:

1. **It preserves divine sovereignty.** The agent is God; no created mechanism gets co-credit. The mechanism-slot's emptiness is the preservation of the agent-slot's sole occupancy.
2. **It refuses the category-error.** Process-vocabulary belongs to creaturely action. Divine action is not a process in the creaturely sense; it is the closing of the will-effect loop. To describe divine action with process-vocabulary would be to apply creaturely categories to divine reality.
3. **It marks the limit of human description.** The Quran is forthright about what humans can know and what they cannot (cf. *al-ghayb* / *al-shahāda* partition, 6:73). Mechanism of divine action falls on the *al-ghayb* side. The silence is epistemological as well as theological.

This is a *positive* doctrine articulated by negative space. The Quran is not declining to speak about mechanism out of ignorance; it is declining to speak about mechanism because *the silence itself encodes the theology*.

## 8. Implications for divine action theology

Putting the three findings together:

1. **KNOWLEDGE IS THE MEDIUM.** The fastest mode of divine action is knowledge-from-the-Book (27:40). Strength can deliver fast; only Book-knowledge can deliver gaze-speed. The *medium* of divine cosmic operation is divine-side knowledge.

2. **COMITATIVE BI- MARKS DIVINE ACCOMPANIMENT.** When divine action moves a human across the earthly plane, the grammar foregrounds the *with*-relation: God *accompanies* the human as carried-companion. When divine action removes a human from the earthly plane, the grammar foregrounds the patient-relation: God *raises* the human as object-acted-on. Two modes; two grammars; one theology that distinguishes accompanying-care from translating-removal.

3. **MECHANISM IS SILENCED.** The trajectory of every teleportation event is unstated. The Quran has elaborate speed-comparators; it has no mechanism-vocabulary. The silence preserves divine sovereignty, refuses category-error, and marks the limit of human description.

These together describe the Quran's coherent theory:

> Divine action is **knowledge-mediated** (the medium is access to divine inscription), **accompanying-with-care** for human-axis events (the comitative *bi-* foregrounds the carrying-companion relation), and **deliberately mysterious** in mechanism (the trajectory is reserved as protected vocabulary-space).

This is not three separate doctrines. It is one doctrine articulated in three registers: *medium* (knowledge), *relation* (comitative), *epistemology* (silence about mechanism). Each register reinforces the others.

The doctrine has downstream consequences for how we read every divine-action event in the Quran:

- The Hour at 16:77 is a divine-knowledge event (operating at *ka-lamḥ al-baṣar* speed); it is described by speed-comparator and not by mechanism.
- The creation of universe at 2:117 uses *kun fa-yakūn* — closed grammatical loop; the mechanism is the imperative-and-effect cycle, no created medium intervenes.
- The Trumpet at 6:73 is named (*yawma yunfaxu fī al-Ṣūr*) but the mechanism of how the blast propagates is silent. The blower is unnamed; the medium is unnamed.
- Muhammad's Isrāʾ at 17:1 is *bi-ʿabdihi* (comitative, accompanying); the mechanism is reserved (no Burāq); the purpose is sign-disclosure (knowledge-event).
- The angel of death at 32:11 *yatawaffākum*; the mechanism of soul-extraction is silent; the destination is *ilā rabbikum*.

In every case, the same architecture: knowledge-medium, appropriate grammar (comitative or accusative depending on axis), mechanism-silence.

## 9. Comparison with the time investigation's findings

The time investigation surfaced three structural patterns that *exactly mirror* the teleportation findings here:

### Time-pattern 1: PROGRAMMATIC INDETERMINACY around large-scale time

The Quran refuses to date the year, century, or millennium. The Hour's date is unknown (*ʿinda rabbī ʿilmuhā*, 7:187, 31:34). There is no year-bridge between creation and the eschaton. There is no calendar of revelations. The structural move is **the same as mechanism-silence in teleportation**: the Quran refuses to populate slots whose population would compromise divine sovereignty over time/causation.

The parallelism: silence about the *mechanism* of teleportation = silence about the *date* of the eschaton. Both are reserved vocabulary-spaces.

### Time-pattern 2: LEXICAL APARTHEID — divine vs creature time vocabulary do not blend

The time investigation found that divine eternity uses a separate vocabulary register from creature time. *Abadan* (forever, divine) does not blend with *sana* (year, creature); *xulūd* (everlastingness, divine) does not blend with *ajl* (term, creature). These vocabularies are partitioned by domain and do not co-occur in the same construction.

The parallelism: the two-grammar split between *bi-ʿabdihi* (horizontal) and direct-accusative + *ilayhi* (vertical) is the **same kind** of lexical-grammatical apartheid. Two registers, no blending. The Quran systematically partitions vocabulary-spaces by ontological domain.

### Time-pattern 3: TRIPARTITE GRAMMAR (Ajl / wqt / Amd)

The time investigation found that creature-time has three coordinated grammatical categories — *ajal* (term), *waqt* (appointed moment), *amad* (extended duration) — each calibrated to a different aspect of bounded-time-experience.

The teleportation parallel: the three modes of divine action toward humans — vertical translation (one-way removal), horizontal extraordinary transit (round-trip with comitative), in-place displacement (Cave Sleepers, 100-year sleeper) — form a parallel tripartite grammar of *axes-of-divine-mobility*.

### The unifying picture

The Quran's broader theological grammar across the corpus is becoming clear:

- **Partitioned vocabularies** (divine vs creature; vertical vs horizontal; Ajl vs wqt vs amd).
- **Indeterminate mechanisms** (no Burāq; no Hour-date; no creation-process narration).
- **Reserved divine-operation spaces** (knowledge-from-Book is unnamed; the angel of death is anonymous; the Hour-date is *ʿinda rabbī*).

These three structural moves recur across the time and teleportation investigations. They are not coincidental — they are the *signature method* of the Quran's theology. The Quran uses **vocabulary-partition + mechanism-silence + reserved-space** as a unified architecture. Any future investigation can expect the same architecture to govern its domain (cosmology, eschatology, prophetology, ethics).

The teleportation findings sharpen the time findings (and vice versa). The decisive integrating move: **the Quran's apophatic theology is a vocabulary-architecture, not just a thematic stance**. The silence is enforced by grammatical partition. The reserved spaces are protected by lexical-class boundaries. The doctrine is articulated *by the structure of the lexicon itself*.

## 10. The hierarchy of transport-power in detail

From `narrative-solomon-suite.md` §6, the Solomon suite encodes a hierarchy of transport-power across four agent-classes that appear within Solomon's own court. Reading this hierarchy through the agency-mechanism-knowledge frame:

### The four-rung hierarchy

| Rung | Agent | Comparator | Knowledge-class |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hoopoe (animal flight) | bird-physics, time-bound | Knows distant kingdoms by sight; reports back; no access to *al-ghayb* |
| 2 | Wind (cosmic-scale natural force) | 1 month per half-day | No knowledge attribute; pure delegated power |
| 3 | Jinn / ʿifrīt (spirits) | "before you rise from your seat" | Strong, trustworthy; access to *al-shahāda* but *not al-ghayb* (cf. 34:14) |
| 4 | Knowledge-from-the-Book | "before your gaze returns" | Partial knowledge of divine inscription; *ghayb*-adjacent |

The hierarchy reads from bottom to top as *increasing access to divine knowledge*. The bottom rung (hoopoe) operates by *animal observation*; the top rung (Book-knower) operates by *divine inscription*.

### The pattern

The fastest transport is via the highest-knowledge mode. **Speed is a function of epistemic access to divine inscription.** This is not a metaphor — it is the structural claim the 27:38–40 passage makes through its comparator-architecture.

The hierarchy validates the headline finding: knowledge is the medium of divine action. The lower the agent's epistemic access, the slower the transport. The higher the access, the faster. The wind is fast (30× ordinary travel) but bounded to the calendar; the jinn are fast but bounded to bodily volition; the Book-knower is fast at the cadence of an eye-flicker.

What sits *above* the Book-knower in the broader corpus speed-ladder? The Hour at 16:77 (*ka-lamḥ al-baṣar aw huwa aqrab*) and *kun fa-yakūn* (closed grammatical loop, no comparator). These are *direct divine* operations, not delegated. The hierarchy continues:

| Rung | Agent | Comparator | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Direct divine command (lamḥ family) | *ka-lamḥ al-baṣar* | The Hour, the divine command — same speed-class as Book-knower but uncomparated by any creature-substrate |
| 6 | *Kun fa-yakūn* | no comparator | Closed grammatical loop; no interval at all |

The full speed-ladder is from creaturely-knowledge-mediated (rungs 1–4) to direct-divine-operation (rungs 5–6). The Book-knower at 27:40 is the *highest creaturely rung* — the boundary between created-agent transport and divine-direct operation.

### Implication

This hierarchy is the Quran's account of how divine action propagates through created agents. It propagates *via knowledge*. The transport-power of any agent is bounded by the agent's epistemic access. **The Quran's cosmology of divine causation is fundamentally epistemic, not energetic.** Power, force, and strength are real categories — but they are *not* the categories that determine divine-action velocity. Knowledge is.

This converges with the time investigation's parallel finding: the reckoning of divine action is on *al-ghayb / al-shahāda* lines, not on power-magnitude lines. God acts according to knowledge-of-the-unseen; created agents act according to their epistemic class.

## 11. The 34:14 limit-case

The hierarchy of transport-power has its sharpest test at 34:14 — the death-of-Solomon scene:

> فَلَمَّا قَضَيۡنَا عَلَيۡهِ ٱلۡمَوۡتَ مَا دَلَّهُمۡ عَلَىٰ مَوۡتِهِۦٓ إِلَّا دَآبَّةُ ٱلۡأَرۡضِ تَأۡكُلُ مِنسَأَتَهُۥۖ فَلَمَّا خَرَّ تَبَيَّنَتِ ٱلۡجِنُّ أَن لَّوۡ كَانُواْ يَعۡلَمُونَ ٱلۡغَيۡبَ مَا لَبِثُواْ فِي ٱلۡعَذَابِ ٱلۡمُهِينِ
>
> *"When We decreed death for him, nothing indicated to them his death except the creature of the earth eating his staff. So when he fell, it became clear to the jinn that, had they known the unseen, they would not have remained in humiliating servitude."*

Solomon dies. He stands leaning on his staff. The jinn — who can build sanctuaries, dive for pearls, fly across continents, and fetch a Sheba throne in less than a man's standing-up — **cannot tell their own master is dead**. They keep working. A worm eats the staff; the body falls; *then* they know.

The Quran's own commentary closes the lesson: *law kānū yaʿlamūna al-ghayba mā labithū fī al-ʿadhābi al-muhīn* — "had they known the unseen, they would not have remained in humiliating servitude." This is a refutation, in narrative, of the doctrine that jinn have access to *al-ghayb*.

### Why this is the limit-case for the divine-action theory

34:14 caps the hierarchy from below. It says: even the strongest creature-transport agents are bounded by knowledge. The jinn have transport-power that exceeds anything human; they have *no access to al-ghayb*. The Book-knower at 27:40 has knowledge-from-the-Book; **his transport-velocity exceeds the jinn's by orders of magnitude**, and the ordering is precisely the ordering of knowledge-access.

The pair 27:40 + 34:14 is the Quran's tightest argument for the knowledge-mediation theory:

- **27:40** demonstrates positively: knowledge-from-Book outpaces strength.
- **34:14** demonstrates negatively: strength without knowledge is blind to what matters most (here: that the master is dead).

Together they make the structural claim: **transport-power is bounded by knowledge-access, both up and down.** Up: more knowledge yields faster transport (Book-knower beats ʿifrīt). Down: less knowledge yields irreducible blindness even at high transport-power (jinn miss Solomon's death despite their continental-scale carrying-power).

### Connection to the mechanism-silence

34:14 also confirms the mechanism-silence pattern. The Quran does not describe what the jinn were *doing* during Solomon's standing-dead period. It does not narrate their workshops, their construction, their lifts. It compresses the entire duration into the worm's gnawing-time. *We do not know if it was a day or a year.* The mechanism of "jinn working under a dead king" is unnarrated; only the start (he fell) and the relevant temporal marker (the worm completed eating the staff) are reported.

This is the same mechanism-silence as elsewhere. The Quran narrates *what mattered* (jinn ignorance, the worm-as-witness, the falling body); it does not narrate process. **The corpus is consistent.**

## 12. Translation divergence

How translators handle the bi-ʿabdihi formulas and the 27:39–40 throne-offers reveals where the Arabic grammatical theology gets erased in English.

### The bi-ʿabdihi formulas

| Verse | Saheeh | Pickthall | Khattab | Arberry |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **17:1** *asrā bi-ʿabdihi* | "took His Servant by night" | "carried His servant by night" | "took His servant ˹Muḥammad˺ by night" | "carried His servant by night" |
| **11:81** *fa-asri bi-ahlika* | "set out with your family" | "travel with thy household" | "set out with your family" | "set forth with thy family" |
| **26:52** *asri bi-ʿibādī* | "Travel by night with My servants" | "depart with my slaves by night" | "set out with My servants by night" | "set forth with My servants by night" |

**The comitative *bi-* is collapsed to plain "with" in English.** The Arabic comitative carries connotations of accompaniment-with-divine-care; English "with" is a generic association preposition. The grammatical-theological force of *bi-* is invisible in any translation.

A second observation: translators split on whether to render *asrā* as "took" or "carried." Saheeh (17:1: "took His Servant"; 11:81: "set out with"; 26:52: "Travel"). The split is partly extraction-vs-transport ("took" foregrounds removal-from-source; "carried" foregrounds the carrying-relation). The Arabic *asrā* is Form IV causative — "to make travel by night" — and *bi-* makes the prepositional party the carried object. **English does not have a single verb that captures both the causative-night-travel and the comitative-carrying simultaneously**, so translators choose one face of the verb and lose the other.

The deepest invisible move: in the Arabic, *bi-ʿabdihi* makes Muhammad the **attached comitative object of God's verb**; in English, "with His servant" makes Muhammad the **independent referent of an associative preposition**. The Arabic encodes Muhammad as carried; the English encodes Muhammad as accompanying. Almost the inverse relation. **The grammar of divine accompanying-care is invisible in any English rendering.**

### The 27:39–40 throne-offers

| Verse | Saheeh | Pickthall | Khattab | Arberry |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **27:39** *ʿifrīt min al-jinn* | "A powerful one from among the jinn" | "A stalwart of the jinn" | "A mighty jinn" | "An efreet of the jinn" |
| **27:39** *qawiyyun amīn* | "indeed I am for this strong and trustworthy" | "Lo! I verily am strong and trusty for the work" | "I am quite strong and trustworthy" | "I have strength for it and I am trusty" |
| **27:40** *ʿindahu ʿilmun min al-kitāb* | "who had knowledge from the Scripture" | "who had knowledge of the Scripture" | "who had knowledge of the Scripture" | "who had knowledge of the Book" |
| **27:40** *qabla an yartadda ilayka ṭarfuk* | "before your glance returns to you" | "before thy gaze returneth unto thee" | "in the blink of an eye" | "before ever thy glance returns to thee" |

The *ʿifrīt* term itself splits four ways — "powerful one," "stalwart," "mighty jinn," "efreet" — none of which fully captures the Arabic category (a hapax in the Quran, a specific class of jinn). The *qawiyyun amīn* credentialing is preserved across all four (strong, trusty/trustworthy).

The Book-knower's credential *ʿilmun min al-kitāb* — "knowledge from the Book" — is preserved in the literal sense, but with one critical loss. The Arabic *min al-kitāb* (definite article *al-* on *kitāb*) signals **a specific Book** (likely the divine inscription, the Lawḥ al-Maḥfūẓ in tradition). English "Scripture" or "Book" carries no such definiteness; the grammatical pointer is lost.

The most consequential loss: **Khattab's idiomatization at 27:40 — "in the blink of an eye"** — collapses the *ṭarf* (gaze) into the *lamḥ* (flicker) family, erasing the 27:40 ↔ 14:43 mirror documented in `concept-Trf.md` and `research-instantaneity-catalog.md` §6. The decisive Arabic structure — *yartadda + ṭarf*, a two-verse exclusive collocation — is invisible to a Khattab-only reader.

### The cumulative pattern

Across all the divergence sites:
- The **comitative grammar** is collapsed to "with."
- The **decomposable-vs-atomic comparator distinction** (lamḥ vs ṭarf) is collapsed to "blink."
- The **definite-article specificity** of *al-kitāb* is collapsed to generic "Scripture."
- The **comparator-asymmetry** between rising-from-seat and gaze-return (the speed-gap encoding the value-inversion) survives literally in three of four translators but is invisible to readers who do not pause on the difference.

**The grammatical theology of divine action is largely invisible in English.** This is consistent with the broader translation-divergence finding: when the Arabic does work via grammar (preposition, definiteness, root-atomicity), English flattens it; when the Arabic does work via lexical content (named entities, named events), English preserves it. Divine-action theology is grammatically encoded — and so it is largely lost in translation.

## 13. Negative space — what the Quran refuses to say

Across the entire teleportation vocabulary, what does not get said?

1. **No description of THE BOOK** the knowledge-from-Book comes from. Definite article *al-kitāb*, but the referent is not specified. Tradition says the Lawḥ al-Maḥfūẓ (Preserved Tablet); the Quran does not name it here.
2. **No description of HOW knowledge enables transport.** The 27:40 verse asserts that knowledge brings the throne; it does not describe the cognitive-causal mechanism. Does the knower visualize the throne? Speak a formula? Touch the divine register? The Quran is silent.
3. **No description of the COMITATIVE moment.** What does it feel like to be *bi-ʿabdihi*? Is the carried party aware of motion? Of accompaniment? Of the divine carrier? The 17:1 verse asserts the carrying; it does not describe the experience.
4. **No description of the trajectory of any teleportation event.** Mecca to Aqṣā has geography; the Quran narrates none of it. Sheba to Jerusalem has geography; the Quran narrates none of it. Earth to *makānan ʿaliyyā* has cosmology; the Quran narrates none of it.
5. **No description of the rest stops** (if any) in the Isrāʾ. Hadith fills in stops; the Quran is silent.
6. **No description of how Idrīs/Jesus traversed the layers.** The seven-heavens cosmology is in the Quran (67:3, 71:15) but is never attached to the rfE-translation events as an itinerary.
7. **No description of the medium** through which any divine action propagates. There is no light, no fire, no air, no earth-shake — for divine actions, no created medium is named.
8. **No description of the agent's interior life.** The Book-knower's psychology, the angel of death's experience, the Spirit's awareness — all unnarrated.
9. **No description of the duration of any trans-cosmic event.** Idrīs's raising — duration unstated. Jesus's translation — duration unstated. Solomon's throne — sub-gaze-return-duration, but not describable below that bound.
10. **No description of return-trajectories.** Muhammad's return from al-Aqṣā is not narrated. Maryam's return from her withdrawal is implied by 19:27 but the journey-back is unnarrated. The Cave Sleepers wake but the world-state during their absence is glimpsed only briefly through the food-at-market scene.

The Quran's apophatic-causation is **the most consistent feature of its teleportation theology**. The catalog of silences is longer than the catalog of described events. If a teleportation event has 4 narrated facts (start, end, speed, carried party), it has roughly 12 unnarrated ones (mechanism, medium, trajectory, intermediate states, agent's interior, recipient's interior, duration of subjective experience, geographical detail, cosmological detail, return-journey, the divine vehicle if any, the principles by which the event was effected).

**The 1-to-3 ratio of narrated-to-unnarrated is itself the doctrine.** The Quran reports what theologically matters; it omits what would compromise the theology.

## 14. Rabbit holes for Phase 3

1. **The Book that the knowledge-from-Book comes from.** A focused study of every *al-kitāb* occurrence in Quranic events of divine-side action would test the hypothesis that *al-kitāb* in 27:40 is the Lawḥ al-Maḥfūẓ (cf. 85:22 *fī lawḥin maḥfūẓ*). Cross-link with Umm al-Kitāb references (3:7, 13:39, 43:4). Is there a single divine-register *al-kitāb* that funds all knowledge-from-Book moments?

2. **The bi-comitative across non-asrā contexts.** Pull every Quranic occurrence of *bi- + person + verb-of-divine-motion*. Does the comitative pattern recur outside the asrā cluster? Test cases: *jiʾnā bika* (We have brought you, addressed to a prophet), *adkhilūhum bi-mā kānū* (admit them with what they were), *baʿathnā bi-bashīr*. If the comitative is a corpus-wide grammar of divine-accompaniment, the Phase-1 finding generalizes.

3. **The mechanism-silence as a corpus-wide doctrine.** Does the silence extend to *every* domain where mechanism would be expected? Examples to test: prophetic revelation (the Quran does not describe how Jibrīl conveys); the Trumpet (the blower is unnamed); creation of Adam (clay is mentioned but assembly process is not); resurrection (bodies reassembled but no description of the process); the splitting of the moon (54:1 — the splitting is reported, the mechanism unnarrated). A corpus-wide audit of *mechanism-silence* would test whether teleportation-vocabulary is one expression of a broader apophatic-grammar.

4. **The anonymity-of-key-figures pattern.** Build an inventory of every Quranic narrative where a function-bearing agent is left unnamed despite tradition supplying a name. Beyond the Book-knower (27:40), al-Khiḍr (18:65), the 100-year sleeper (2:259), the Cave Sleepers (18:9–22), and Gabriel-as-bashar-sawiyy (19:17): does the pattern extend to other figures (the Companion of the Two Gardens at 18:32, the People of the Trench at 85:4)? Hypothesis: the Quran withholds names when the type is more important than the character.

5. **The 27:38–40 inversion as template.** Are there other Quranic passages that stage strength-vs-knowledge competitions and let the comparator-grammar adjudicate? Candidate: 4:113 (*ʿallamaka mā lam takun taʿlam* — taught you what you did not know); 18:65 (Moses asks al-Khiḍr to teach him); 12:55 (Joseph claims *ḥafīẓun ʿalīm*). A study of the Quran's *epistemic-credentialing-clauses* would unpack the broader pattern.

6. **The hierarchy of transport-power across the whole corpus.** This document built the hierarchy from Solomon's court (hoopoe → wind → jinn → Book-knower → divine command → *kun fa-yakūn*). Does the same hierarchy hold for non-Solomon contexts? Test cases: the angel of death (*malaku al-mawt*) — what is his epistemic class? The Spirit at 19:17 — what is its position in the hierarchy? The unnamed *rusulunā* of 6:61 (guardian-extractors) — strength-class or knowledge-class?

7. **The protected vocabulary-space hypothesis.** Time investigation found *abadan* and *xulūd* as God-reserved vocabulary. Teleportation findings here suggest *kun fa-yakūn* and *al-kitāb* (at 27:40) are similarly reserved. Build a corpus-wide list of *protected lexemes* — words the Quran applies almost exclusively to divine reality, never to creature-reality. Hypothesis: the lexicon has a partition between *reserved divine-side vocabulary* and *shared creature-divine vocabulary*, and the partition is the basic structural feature of Quranic theological grammar.

8. **The "carried-companion" grammar in non-asrā events.** The bi-ʿabdihi pattern is well-established for asrā. Does it have analogues in other rescue-events? Noah's Ark (*wa-ḥamalnāhu ʿalā*, 54:13 — accompanies-by-vehicle, not comitative-with-bi); the ʿĀd's destruction (no rescue, so no comitative); Lot's rescue uses both *asrā bi-ahlika* (11:81) and *najjaynāhu* (forms of *njy*, deliver). A study of the *grammar of divine rescue* would distinguish carrying-modes (comitative-direct vs vehicle-mediated vs delivery-extraction).

9. **The decomposable-vs-atomic architecture (from the instantaneity catalog) and the comitative-vs-accusative split (from this document).** Both findings track an *act+substrate vs single-noun* lexical-architectural distinction. Are they the same underlying pattern? Hypothesis: the Quran systematically decomposes divine action into act + substrate (lamḥ + baṣar; rfE + makān; asrā + bi-ʿabdihi) and uses atomic single-nouns for narrative-perceptual events (ṭarf; istaqarra). If so, this is one corpus-wide grammar of divine-vs-creature action, not two separate findings.

10. **The eschaton-as-act-of-Kun fa-yakūn hypothesis.** 6:73 unifies *kun fa-yakūn* with the Trumpet in a single ayah. Is the eschatological event itself an act of divine *kun*? Hypothesis: the Trumpet is not a separate cosmic event; it is the dramatized rendering of a single *kun* uttered for *resurrection-as-creation*. If so, the mechanism-silence about how the Trumpet works is the same mechanism-silence as about *kun fa-yakūn* — there is no creature-mechanism because the event is a closed grammatical loop. Cross-link with `research-trumpet.md` and `research-instantaneity-catalog.md` §8.

11. **What the Book-knower DID, in mechanism-terms.** This is a productive negative-space probe. The Quran says: he uttered *anā ātīka bihi qabla an yartadda ilayka ṭarfuk*; the throne arrived. What is the *act* the Book-knower performed? The Quran does not tell us. Is it a prayer? An invocation? A reading from the Book? An act of intention? **The most agentive-seeming character in the Quran's teleportation corpus is depicted with the most opaque agency-mechanism.** This is the apophatic doctrine at its sharpest: the highest-class created agent acts by means we cannot describe. The investigation could pursue this: catalog every Quranic verse where an agent's action is consequential but the agent's *means* is unnarrated. The Book-knower is the densest case; the angels' arrivals are the most numerous; the Spirit's *tamaththala* (19:17) is the most form-shifting. Together they describe the corpus's reserved agency-vocabulary-space.

12. **Cross-investigation: knowledge-as-medium and time-as-knowledge.** The time investigation found that the Quran's reckoning of the eschaton is bounded by *al-ghayb* (the unseen) — *ʿinda rabbī ʿilmuhā*. The teleportation finding here is that the highest-class transport is bounded by *al-ghayb*-access. **Both investigations converge on knowledge-of-the-unseen as the determining variable.** A cross-investigation synthesis would integrate: the Hour is unknown because it is *al-ghayb*; the Book-knower's transport is fast because he has *ʿilm min al-kitāb* (which approaches *al-ghayb* knowledge); the jinn's failure to detect Solomon's death is because they lack *al-ghayb*-access. The *al-ghayb / al-shahāda* partition is the *master variable* for both time and teleportation.
