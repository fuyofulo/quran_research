# Solomon in the Quran: a story guide

This guide tells the life of Solomon (*Sulaymān*) from the Quran alone. Later stories about him, from
outside the Quran, are listed at the end.

**Where he is.** Solomon is named 17 times, in seven chapters (*surahs*). Most of his story, 30 verses,
is in chapter 27, *The Ant*. The rest is in chapters 38 (*Ṣād*), 21 (*The Prophets*), 34 (*Sabaʾ*) and
2 (*The Cow*). Two chapters take their names from his story: *The Ant*, and *Sabaʾ*, the queen's land.

**Who he is.** He is David's son. God introduces him as a gift to his father: "To David We gave
Solomon. What an excellent servant! He was always turning back to God" (38:30). The Quran lists him
among those who received revelation (4:163), and among those given "the Book, judgment and prophethood"
(6:84-89). No verse calls him "prophet" or "messenger" on his own; his place comes through these lists.

**A note on order.** The Quran gives no timeline for Solomon. It tells his story in pieces, in
different chapters. The order below, from youth to death, is a sensible one, but it is ours, not the
Quran's.

Each episode has three parts: *what happened*, *what it shows*, and *a line to remember*. Our own
interpretations are marked **A reading**. A reference like (27:19) means chapter 27, verse 19.

---

## 1. David's son, and the field case (21:78-79)

**What happened.** David and Solomon judged a case together. A people's sheep had strayed into a field
of crops and grazed it (the word suggests at night). God was "witness to their judgment", and He made
Solomon understand the case, giving each of them judgment and knowledge. The Quran does not say what either of them decided. It turns straight back to David: the mountains and the birds
praised God with him, and he was taught to make armour to protect people (21:79-80).

**What it shows.** The word for "We made him understand" appears only here in the whole Quran. It is
one moment of insight, given by God, about one case. The very next words make sure David is not made
smaller: *each* of them was given judgment and knowledge.

**A reading.** Being right once does not make a son greater than his father. The insight was a gift,
and God was watching both.

**A line to remember.**
> فَفَهَّمۡنَٰهَا سُلَيۡمَٰنَۚ وَكُلًّا ءَاتَيۡنَا حُكۡمࣰا وَعِلۡمࣰاۚ  
> *fa-fahhamnāhā Sulaymān, wa kullan ātaynā ḥukman wa ʿilmā*  
> "So We made Solomon understand it, and to each We gave judgment and knowledge." (21:79)

## 2. Inheriting David, and the speech of birds (27:15-16)

**What happened.** "We gave David and Solomon knowledge, and they said: *Praise be to God, who has
favoured us over many of His believing servants*" (27:15). Then: "Solomon inherited David." He said:
"People, we have been taught the speech of birds, and we have been given something of everything. This
is clear bounty" (27:16).

**What it shows.**
- Father and son speak with one voice, and their first word is praise. They say they are favoured over
  *many* believers, not over all.
- The Quran does not say what Solomon inherited. Beside the word it puts knowledge and God's bounty,
  not treasure.
- This word for "speech" appears only here in the Quran.

**A reading.** "We have been taught" is most likely a king speaking for his house. Either way, he names
the source at once: *bounty*, a gift, not his own skill.

**A line to remember.** "This is clear bounty." (27:16)

## 3. The gifts: wind, copper and jinn (34:12-13, 21:81-82, 38:36-38)

**What happened.**
- **The wind.** God made the wind serve him. Its morning run was a month's journey, and its evening run
  a month (34:12). In one place it blows strongly, by his command, to "the land We had blessed" (21:81);
  in another it runs gently, wherever he wished (38:36).
- **Copper.** God made a spring of molten copper flow for him (34:12).
- **Jinn.** Some jinn worked for him "by the permission of his Lord"; any who turned from God's command
  would taste the fire (34:12). They made whatever he wished: high chambers, statues, basins as big as
  water-tanks, and great cooking-pots fixed in place (34:13).
- **Devils.** Some devils built and dived for him, and others were bound in chains (38:37-38; 21:82).
  God says: "We were guarding them" (21:82).

**What it shows.** Every gift is told as God's doing ("We made it serve him", "by his Lord's
permission"); nothing works by a trick or a magic object. Then comes a task for the household: thanks,
shown in work. The Quran never says he rode the wind. (Chapter 38 tells these gifts after his prayer
for a kingdom; see episode 8.)

**A line to remember.** "Work, family of David, in thanks. And few of My servants are truly grateful."
(34:13)

## 4. The army and the ant (27:17-19)

**What happened.** Solomon's armies were gathered for him: jinn, people and birds, kept in ordered ranks
(27:17). When they came to the valley of the ants, an ant said: "Ants, go into your homes, so that
Solomon and his armies don't crush you without noticing" (27:18). Solomon smiled, laughing at her words,
and prayed: "My Lord, help me to be grateful for Your favour, which You have given me and my parents,
and to do good that pleases You. And by Your mercy, bring me in among Your righteous servants" (27:19).

**What it shows.**
- The ant knows the king's name, and she does not call the army cruel: "without noticing" excuses them.
- The word for Solomon's smile appears only here in the Quran.
- Told that he is a danger to the small, the king answers with a prayer. He does not say "I am
  grateful"; he asks for help to *be* grateful. He includes his parents, and he asks to join the
  righteous by God's mercy, not by his own deeds.

**A reading.** "Kept in ranks" and "help me" come from the same root, which carries the sense of
holding steady. The commander who holds his army in order asks God to hold *him*.

**A line to remember.**
> رَبِّ أَوۡزِعۡنِيٓ أَنۡ أَشۡكُرَ نِعۡمَتَكَ ٱلَّتِيٓ أَنۡعَمۡتَ عَلَيَّ وَعَلَىٰ وَٰلِدَيَّ  
> *rabbi awziʿnī an ashkura niʿmataka llatī anʿamta ʿalayya wa ʿalā wālidayya*  
> "My Lord, help me to be grateful for Your favour, which You have given me and my parents." (27:19)

## 5. The hoopoe (27:20-28)

**What happened.** Solomon inspected the birds and said: "Why do I not see the hoopoe? Or is he among
the absent? I will punish him severely, or slaughter him, unless he brings me a clear reason"
(27:20-21). The hoopoe did not stay away long. He said: "I have learned something you have not, and I
have brought you sure news from Sabaʾ" (27:22). There a woman ruled, given something of everything,
with a great throne. She and her people bowed to the sun instead of God, for Satan had made their ways
look good to them (27:23-24). Why not bow to God, he asks, who brings out what is hidden in the heavens
and the earth, and knows what you hide and show: "no god but Him, Lord of the great Throne"
(27:25-26). Solomon said: "We will see whether you have told the truth or are one of the liars. Take this letter of mine and deliver it to
them. Then step back, and see what they send back" (27:27-28).

**What it shows.**
- The king's first question is about his own eyes ("Why do I not see…?"), and his threat has a way
  out: "a clear reason".
- He said he was given "something of everything"; now a small bird knows what he does not. He neither
  punishes the bird nor simply believes him. He checks.
- The hoopoe calls God the One who "brings out the hidden", having just brought a hidden thing to light.
- "Hoopoe" and "the hidden" each appear only here in the Quran. It never says what became of the bird.

**A line to remember.**
> أَحَطتُ بِمَا لَمۡ تُحِطۡ بِهِۦ  
> *aḥaṭtu bimā lam tuḥiṭ bihi*  
> "I have come to know what you do not know." (27:22)

## 6. The queen (27:29-44)

**What happened.**
- **The letter.** The queen told her chiefs: "A noble letter has been delivered to me. It is from
  Solomon, and it says: *In the name of God, the Most Merciful, the Most Kind. Do not set yourselves
  up against me, but come to me in submission*" (27:29-31).
- **The council.** "Advise me in my affair. I never decide a matter without you" (27:32). They said:
  "We have strength and great might, but the command is yours" (27:33). She said: "When kings enter a
  town, they ruin it and humble its noblest people. That is what they do. I will send them a gift, and
  see what the envoys bring back" (27:34-35).
- **The gift refused.** Solomon said: "Would you add to my wealth? What God has given me is better than
  what He has given you. No: it is you who delight in your gift. Go back to them. We will come with
  armies they cannot face, and drive them out, humbled and brought low" (27:36-37).
- **The throne.** Solomon asked his assembly: "Which of you will bring me her throne before they come to
  me in submission?" A powerful one of the jinn said: "Before you rise from your place. I am strong
  enough, and trustworthy." One who had knowledge of the Book said: "Before your glance returns to you"
  (27:38-40). The Quran does not say how it came. When Solomon saw it settled before him, he said:
  "This is from my Lord's bounty, to test me: will I be grateful or ungrateful?" Thanks, he adds, helps
  only the one who gives it; his Lord needs nothing, and is generous (27:40).
- **The disguise.** "Disguise her throne; let us see whether she is guided." When she came, she was
  asked: "Is your throne like this?" She said: "It is as though it were it." A voice, most likely
  Solomon's side, said: "We were given knowledge before her, and we had submitted." What she had
  worshipped instead of God had held her back; she came from a people who did not believe (27:41-43).
- **The palace.** She was told: "Enter the palace." She thought it was deep water, and uncovered her
  shins. He said: "It is a palace paved smooth with glass." She said: "My Lord, I have wronged myself.
  I submit, with Solomon, to God, the Lord of the worlds" (27:44).

**What it shows.**
- She is wise: she consults, knows what kings tend to do, tests with a gift instead of war, and answers
  carefully about her throne.
- Wealth does not impress him. And the testing runs both ways: he said "we will see" to the hoopoe; she
  says "I will see" to him.
- His letter's opening is the only full "In the name of God, the Most Merciful, the Most Kind" inside
  the running text of any chapter.
- When the throne arrives, he praises neither the one who brought it nor himself. He calls it a test.
- She submits *with* Solomon, not *to* him: both stand as servants of the same Lord. Her words are the
  last in the story, and the threat of war is never carried out.

**A reading.** "It is as though it were it" is honest both ways: she neither claims nor denies what she
cannot be sure of.

**A line to remember.**
> هَٰذَا مِن فَضۡلِ رَبِّي لِيَبۡلُوَنِيٓ ءَأَشۡكُرُ أَمۡ أَكۡفُرُۖ  
> *hādhā min faḍli rabbī li-yabluwanī a-ashkuru am akfur*  
> "This is from my Lord's bounty, to test me: will I be grateful or ungrateful?" (27:40)

No other verse in the Quran says "from my Lord's bounty".

## 7. The horses (38:31-33)

**What happened.** One evening, fine horses, standing poised and swift, were shown to Solomon (38:31).
He said: "I have loved the love of good things, away from the remembrance of my Lord, until they were
hidden behind the screen. Bring them back to me." And he began to stroke their legs and necks
(38:32-33).

**What it shows.** Readers differ on these hard verses. Here is where the words point.
- **"Good things."** The word (*khayr*) almost always means "good" or "wealth"; the Quran has a
  different word for horses. "Love of good things" is the better reading.
- **"Away from the remembrance of my Lord."** All eleven other times the Quran says "away from
  remembrance", it means being distracted from it, as in "let neither your wealth nor your children
  distract you from the remembrance of God" (63:9). Solomon says this about himself.
- **"Until they were hidden."** The verses never mention the sun. The horses are the last thing named,
  and "bring them back" means them, so most likely the horses passed out of sight. Some translators add
  "the sun".
- **"Stroke."** Everywhere else in the Quran this word means wiping or stroking, as in wiping your head
  when washing for prayer (5:6). The Quran uses a different word for striking.
- The Quran does not call this a sin or say God was angry. Solomon reports it himself. The two words for
  the horses appear only here in the Quran.

**A reading.** A wealthy man notices that something good has pulled him away from God, and says so out
loud. That honesty is the point.

**A line to remember.** "I have loved the love of good things, away from the remembrance of my Lord."
(38:32)

## 8. The trial and his prayer (38:34-40)

**What happened.** "We tested Solomon, and We placed a body on his throne. Then he turned back" (38:34).
He said: "My Lord, forgive me, and give me a kingdom not fitting for anyone after me. You are the Giver"
(38:35). So God made the wind serve him, with the builders, the divers and the chained (38:36-38; see
episode 3), and said: "This is Our gift, so give or hold back, without account" (38:39). "And he has
nearness to Us, and a good place of return" (38:40).

**What it shows.**
- **The body is not explained.** The Quran does not say what or whose it was, or whether it was alive. The word appears three other times: twice for the golden calf, a body that lowed, and once
  for the prophets, who were not "bodies that do not eat", that is, living men. It never means a dead
  body.
- **A test, not a punishment.** He turns back, and his first request is forgiveness; only then does he
  ask for a kingdom. No one else in the Quran asks God for a kingdom for himself.
- **The ending.** The Quran never says in so many words that he was forgiven. But it closes with the
  words it used for his father David after David was forgiven: "nearness to Us, and a good place of
  return" (38:25, 38:40). Only these two men receive them.
- **Praise first.** Before the trial, God calls him "an excellent servant; he was always turning
  back" (38:30), words used for only one other man, Job (38:44).

**A reading.** Even a good servant slips. What marks him is how fast he turns back, and what he asks
first.

**A line to remember.**
> رَبِّ ٱغۡفِرۡ لِي وَهَبۡ لِي مُلۡكࣰا لَّا يَنۢبَغِي لِأَحَدࣲ مِّنۢ بَعۡدِيٓۖ  
> *rabbi ghfir lī wa hab lī mulkan lā yanbaghī li-aḥadin min baʿdī*  
> "My Lord, forgive me, and give me a kingdom not fitting for anyone after me." (38:35)

## 9. His death (34:14)

**What happened.** "When We decreed death for him, nothing showed the jinn that he had died except a
creature of the earth eating his staff. When he fell, the jinn saw clearly that if they had known the
unseen, they would not have stayed on in that humiliating torment" (34:14), meaning their hard labour.

**What it shows.** No one noticed the king's death until a small creature ate his staff. The jinn,
who people imagined knew hidden things, did not even know their master was dead. The Quran gives no length of time. The very next verses tell of the people of Sabaʾ, who were
told to be grateful, turned away, and lost their gardens (34:15-19); the Quran does not say whether they
were the queen's people.

**A reading.** Power ends. Neither the king nor his servants could stop death, or even see it come.

**A line to remember.** "If they had known the unseen, they would not have stayed in the humiliating
torment." (34:14)

## 10. Cleared of sorcery (2:102)

**What happened.** Long after Solomon, some people threw God's Book behind their backs (2:101) and
followed instead what the devils used to recite about Solomon's kingdom. The Quran says: "Solomon did
not disbelieve; it was the devils who disbelieved, teaching people sorcery." It speaks of two angels at
Babylon, Hārūt and Mārūt, who taught no one without first warning: "We are only a test, so do not
disbelieve." People learned from them what would separate a man and his wife, yet they could harm no
one except by God's permission (2:102).

**What it shows.**
- The Quran stops to clear Solomon's name. It does not say who accused him, but the words answer a
  charge: that his power came from sorcery. Elsewhere the Quran calls his power a gift
  (34:12, 38:36).
- The words "did not disbelieve" (*wa mā kafara*) appear only here in the Quran.
- One phrase reads two ways: "what was sent down to the two angels", or "nothing was sent down to the
  two angels". Either way, the angels warn first.

**A reading.** When the throne arrived, Solomon asked himself: "Will I be grateful or ungrateful?"
(27:40). In Arabic, "ungrateful" and "disbelieve" share one root. Here God gives the answer.

**A line to remember.**
> وَمَا كَفَرَ سُلَيۡمَٰنُ وَلَٰكِنَّ ٱلشَّيَٰطِينَ كَفَرُواْ  
> *wa mā kafara Sulaymānu wa lākinna l-shayāṭīna kafarū*  
> "Solomon did not disbelieve; it was the devils who disbelieved." (2:102)

## What the Quran does not say

These stories come from later tradition. Beside each is what the Quran gives instead.

- **A magic ring, and a demon who stole it and sat on his throne.** The Quran says only: "We placed a
  body on his throne; then he turned back" (38:34). His power is always God's gift, never an object's.
- **The queen's name, Bilqīs.** The Quran never names her.
- **Solomon marrying the queen.** Her story ends with "I submit, with Solomon, to God" (27:44). No
  marriage, and nothing of her life afterwards.
- **A glass floor built so he could see her legs.** The palace is paved with glass; no reason is given.
- **70 or 100 wives, and a half-formed child laid on his throne.** No wife or child of his appears; the
  "body" is unexplained.
- **Asking God to bring back the sun.** There is no sun in the horse verses.
- **Killing the horses with a sword.** The word means stroking or wiping.
- **Standing dead on his staff for a year.** No length of time is given.
- **Building the Temple in Jerusalem.** The jinn made high chambers, statues, basins and pots (34:13).
  No Temple is named.
- **Hārūt and Mārūt falling for a woman (Zuhra).** Two angels at Babylon warn everyone first. No woman,
  no fall.
- **The verdicts in the field case.** None are given.
- **Solomon's mother, and David and another man's wife.** No mother is named. David's trial in the Quran
  (38:21-25) is a case brought by two men about sheep.
- **The name of the man who brought the throne (Āṣaf), and the hoopoe's reward.** Neither is in the
  Quran.
- **His age, or how long he reigned.** Neither is given.

When the Quran is silent, that silence is part of what it tells us.

## What Solomon's story teaches

These are readings, drawn from the lines above.

1. When something good comes to you, say where it came from and what it is for: "This is from my Lord's
   bounty, to test me" (27:40).
2. Thanks is something you can ask God to help you with, and something you show in your work (27:19,
   34:13).
3. Listen to the small: an ant and a bird saw what the king did not. Check before you judge, and leave a
   way out in every warning.
4. When you slip, say so yourself, turn back quickly, and ask for forgiveness before you ask for more
   (38:32-35).
5. Being given more than others is a test, not proof that you are better. Even David and Solomon said
   they were favoured over *many* believers, not over all (27:15).
6. Truth does not need someone to lose. The queen submitted *with* Solomon, not *to* him (27:44).
7. Power ends. Even Solomon fell, and only a small creature noticed (34:14).
