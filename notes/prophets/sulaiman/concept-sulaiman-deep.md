# Sulaymān (Solomon) in the Quran: synthesis

> Phase 4. Built on `BRIEF.md`, `0-structural/sulaiman-structurals.md`, three narrative files and one
> cross-cutting file in this folder. The evidence is the Arabic text and QAC morphology; translations are
> a secondary layer. Hadith, tafsīr and Isrāʾīliyyāt are outside the evidence base, and appear only by
> name, as tradition. Counts and string checks are **data-grounded**; readings are marked
> **[interpretive]**. The Arabic panels are copied by script from `data/arabic/quran.json`. Every
> CORRECTED item is applied below and listed in `README.md`.

## Headline

**The Quran gives Solomon wind, jinn, molten copper and the speech of birds, and has him answer for every
gift.** He is named 17 times in 7 surahs; his story fills 50 verses in 5 passages, 30 of them in an-Naml. No sentence of his own calls him prophet or messenger; that status comes through the
lists (4:163, 6:84-89). Every gift carries a God-ward line: his own in an-Naml, God's elsewhere. His key sentence, *hādhā min faḍli rabbī li-yabluwanī* (27:40, once in the
Quran), names both the source of power and its purpose: a test of thanks. Seven of the Quran's 75 *shukr*
tokens fall in his verses. He checks every report, and is checked in turn by an ant, a bird, a queen
and a creature that eats his staff (34:14). He reports his one lapse himself (38:32). The ring, the demon, the wives and Bilqīs are not in the text.

## 1. Who he is, and where (data-grounded)

| Passage | Verses | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 27:15-44 | 30 | knowledge to David and Solomon; the inheritance; bird-speech; the ant; the hoopoe; the letter; the queen; the throne; the glass palace |
| 38:30-40 | 11 | *what an excellent servant*; the horses; the trial and the body; the prayer; the gifts; nearness |
| 21:78-82 | 5 | the field case; *We made Solomon understand it*; the wind; the divers |
| 34:12-14 | 3 | wind, copper, jinn; *work in thanks, family of David*; his death |
| 2:102 | 1 | the devils' sorcery; *Solomon did not disbelieve* |

- **Named in 16 ayahs**: 2:102, 4:163, 6:84, 21:78, 21:79, 21:81, 27:15-18, 27:30, 27:36, 27:44,
  34:12, 38:30, 38:34. **David shares 7 of them** (4:163, 6:84, 21:78, 21:79, 27:15, 27:16, 38:30).
- **Family.** He enters as God's gift to his father: *wa-wahabnā li-Dāwūda Sulaymān* (38:30). David is
  the only parent named (27:16, 38:30). In 27:19 he thanks God for favour on *wālidayya*, both his
  parents, who are not named. No mother, wife or child of his appears in any of the 50 verses.
- **Surah names.** Two surahs are named from his story: an-Naml (the ant, 27:18) and Sabaʾ (27:22, 34:15).
- **No timeline.** The text orders only three things: *wa-waritha* places him after David (27:16);
  *thumma anāba … qāla … fa-sakhkharnā* puts the trial, the prayer and the gifts in that order
  (38:34-36); and 34:14 is his death. 2:102 looks back on his reign from later. Any other order is
  an editor's choice.
- **Translation.** The story translates more steadily than the Quran as a whole: its median divergence
  is 0.613, against 0.635 for the corpus. Its hotspots are 38:40 (97th percentile), 38:31 (92nd),
  27:37, 27:43, 27:32, 27:29, 27:17 and 38:32.

## 2. His prophethood (data-grounded)

- **Titles.** Across his 16 ayahs, the roots *nbʾ* and *rsl* give one word only: *al-nabiyyīn* at
  4:163. It belongs to the list's opening (*Noah and the prophets after him*), not to Solomon.
- **The two lists.**
  - *Awḥaynā ilā … wa-Sulaymān* (4:163): revelation went to him.
  - *Wa-min dhurriyyatihi Dāwūda wa-Sulaymān* (6:84), which 6:89 closes with *ulāʾika lladhīna
    ātaynāhumu l-kitāba wa-l-ḥukma wa-l-nubuwwa*, and 6:90 with *fa-bi-hudāhumu qtadih*.
- **Book.** David has the Zabūr (4:163) and stands among *the prophets* in 17:55. Solomon has no book.
- **The series census.** Of 25 named prophets, 18 carry the title in the text. 5 hold it only through
  these lists: Solomon, David, Job, Elisha and Zechariah. Adam and Dhū l-Kifl are never titled.
- **[interpretive]** What the text calls him instead is *ʿabd* and *awwāb* (38:30), and it names his gifts as
  *ḥukm*, *ʿilm* and *mulk*. The Quran draws a servant first and a king second.

## 3. The gifts, and the God-ward answer to each

| Gift | The line that answers it | Speaker |
|---|---|---|
| knowledge, 27:15 | *al-ḥamdu lillāhi lladhī faḍḍalanā* | David and Solomon |
| bird-speech, *min kulli shayʾ*, 27:16 | *inna hādhā la-huwa l-faḍlu l-mubīn* (once in the Quran) | Solomon |
| the army; the ant's warning, 27:17-18 | *rabbi awziʿnī an ashkura niʿmataka* (27:19) | Solomon |
| the queen's gift, 27:35 | *fa-mā ātāniya llāhu khayrun mimmā ātākum* (27:36) | Solomon |
| the throne, 27:40 | *hādhā min faḍli rabbī li-yabluwanī* | Solomon |
| the glass palace, 27:44 | *aslamtu maʿa Sulaymāna lillāh* | the queen |
| the horses, 38:31 | *innī aḥbabtu ḥubba l-khayri ʿan dhikri rabbī* (38:32) | Solomon, confessing |
| wind, builders, the chained, 38:36-38 | *hādhā ʿaṭāʾunā* (38:39, once in the Quran) | God |
| understanding, wind, divers, 21:79-82 | *wa-kunnā fāʿilīn / ʿālimīn / ḥāfiẓīn* | God |
| wind, copper, jinn, 34:12-13 | *iʿmalū āla Dāwūda shukrā* | God |
| his reign, 2:102 | *wa-mā kafara Sulaymān* | God |

**Data-grounded.**
- **No gift stands alone.** In an-Naml, Solomon speaks the God-ward line; in the other passages, God
  speaks it. In 21:78-82 each verse that gives something closes with God naming His own part (*wa-kunnā*
  ×4) or, in 21:80, asking for thanks. There are two near-exceptions: for the palace the queen speaks
  the line, and for the horses it is an admission, not thanks.
- ***Shukr*.** 7 of the Quran's 75 tokens fall in his 50 verses: 27:19, 27:40 ×3, 34:13 ×2 and 21:80.
  The Quran has three first-person *shukr*, and two are his: *an ashkura* (27:19) and *a-ashkuru*
  (27:40). The third is the man at forty (46:15), the only other *awziʿnī*. The root *wzʿ* has 5
  tokens; one is the army *yūzaʿūn*, "held in ranks", two verses before the prayer (27:17).
- **Pronouns.** Solomon never says *anā*: the two in 27:39-40 belong to his helpers. His only *innī*
  is the confession at 38:32.

> فَلَمَّا رَءَاهُ مُسۡتَقِرًّا عِندَهُۥ قَالَ هَٰذَا مِن فَضۡلِ رَبِّي لِيَبۡلُوَنِيٓ ءَأَشۡكُرُ أَمۡ أَكۡفُرُۖ وَمَن شَكَرَ فَإِنَّمَا يَشۡكُرُ لِنَفۡسِهِۦۖ وَمَن كَفَرَ فَإِنَّ رَبِّي غَنِيࣱّ كَرِيمࣱ (27:40)
>
> Saheeh: *And when [Solomon] saw it placed before him, he said, "This is from the favor of my Lord to
> test me whether I will be grateful or ungrateful. And whoever is grateful – his gratitude is only for
> [the benefit of] himself. And whoever is ungrateful – then indeed, my Lord is Free of need and
> Generous."*

- ***Hādhā min faḍli rabbī*** occurs once in the Quran; even *faḍli rabbī* is unique. He names no
  helper, neither the ʿifrīt, who offered with credentials (*qawiyyun amīn*, 27:39), nor the man with
  knowledge of the Book, who offered none.
- ***Li-yabluwanī*.** Only twice in the Quran does a speaker make himself the object of a test verb:
  here, and in *wa-lā taftinnī*, "do not put me to trial" (9:49), a man begging to be excused, of whom the
  text says *into trial they have fallen*. To everyone else the text itself says that wealth, rank and
  plenty are tests (6:165, 8:28, 21:35, 20:131, 72:17).
- **The rule he draws.** Thanks benefits the one who gives it, and ingratitude cannot touch a Lord who
  is *ghaniyy karīm*. The choice is *ashkuru am akfur*, with no third option.
- **Thanks as work (34:13).** *Iʿmalū āla Dāwūda shukrā*: *shukran* reads as purpose ("for thanks"),
  as manner ("thankfully"), or as the thing done ("work thanks"). It is said to the whole family, and
  God adds *wa-qalīlun min ʿibādiya l-shakūr*. David's armour has the same shape: it was made *for
  you*, and it closes with *fa-hal antum shākirūn?*, asked of those who benefit (21:80).
- **38:39.** *Hādhā ʿaṭāʾunā fa-mnun aw amsik bi-ghayri ḥisāb.* *Bi-ghayri ḥisāb* occurs 7 times;
  the other 6 describe God's own giving (2:212, 3:27, 3:37, 24:38, 39:10, 40:40). Ṣād's three other
  *ḥisāb* are all *yawm al-ḥisāb* (38:16, 26, 53).
- **[interpretive]** "Without account" cannot mean "beyond judgment" in a surah so full of the
  Reckoning. It hands him something of God's own freedom to give or hold back, and that freedom is
  itself the test of 27:40.

## 4. Checking and being checked (27:15-44)

**Data-grounded: the shape.**
- **Speech.** The passage has 412 Arabic words; 320 of them are speech. Solomon speaks most: 136
  words in 9 turns, the longest 25 words (27:36-37). The hoopoe has the longest single speech, 60
  words (27:22-26). The queen has the last word (27:44), and it ends *rabbi l-ʿālamīn*. Solomon is the
  only speaker the text names: the ant, the hoopoe, the queen, the ʿifrīt and the man with the Book are
  all left unnamed.
- **The checking verbs.** *Nẓr* has 5 tokens in the passage and *rjʿ* 3, all in the sense "see what
  comes back". **(CORRECTED)** They are shared out among the speakers. Solomon speaks *nẓr* at 27:27,
  27:28 and 27:41, and *rjʿ* at 27:28 and 27:37. The council speaks *nẓr* at 27:33. The queen speaks
  *nẓr* and *rjʿ* at 27:35. The king is not the only one who checks.

**The hoopoe (27:20-28).**
- His first words are a question about his own eyes: *mā liya lā arā l-hudhud?*. Only after that
  comes *or is he among the absent?* (27:20).
- **The threat (27:21).** Three verbs, each with the emphatic *lām* and *nūn*. The third is the way
  out: *aw la-yaʾtiyannī bi-sulṭānin mubīn*. **[interpretive]** He binds himself to accept proof as
  firmly as to punish.
- **The bird's report (27:22).** *Aḥaṭtu bimā lam tuḥiṭ bihi*: a bird tells a king who said *we have been
  given of all things* (27:16) that he knows what the king does not. *Lam tuḥiṭ bihi* occurs
  elsewhere only at 18:68, said to Moses.
- **Two thrones.** *Min kulli shayʾ* with a giving verb is said of three rulers: Solomon (27:16), the
  queen (27:23) and Dhū l-Qarnayn (18:84). Her *ʿarshun ʿaẓīm* (27:23) and God's *al-ʿarshi l-ʿaẓīm*
  (27:26) share an adjective inside one speech.
- **The bird's theology.** God *brings out the hidden*, *al-khabʾ* (27:25), a word that occurs only
  here, from a bird who has just brought a hidden thing out. *Hudhud* also occurs only here.
- **The check (27:27-28).** *Sa-nanẓuru a-ṣadaqta am kunta mina l-kādhibīn*: he neither believes the
  bird nor punishes it; he tests the report with a letter. The text never says what became of the
  hoopoe.

**The ant (27:18-19).**
- She knows the king's name and his army (*Sulaymānu wa-junūduhu*).
- **Her excuse for them.** *Wa-hum lā yashʿurūn* follows that pair directly, so **[interpretive]** it most
  naturally excuses the army: the danger is carelessness, not malice. The phrase occurs 15 times,
  usually of people blind to what is coming, Pharaoh's household among them (28:9, 11).
- ***Tabassama*** occurs only here. He smiles, laughs, and answers being called a danger with a prayer
  to be *made able* to thank, which takes in his parents, and asks to enter among the righteous *by
  Your mercy* (27:19).

**The letter (27:29-31).**
- The letter is 9 Arabic words: the basmala (4) and *allā taʿlū ʿalayya wa-ʾtūnī muslimīn* (5).
- **A script check over all 6,236 ayahs** finds the full *bismi llāhi l-raḥmāni l-raḥīm* in two only:
  1:1 and 27:30. **27:30 is the only full basmala inside a surah's running text.**
- The divergence at 27:29 comes from *ulqiya* (delivered / thrown / cast), not from *karīm*.

**The queen (27:32-44).**
- **Council.** *Mā kuntu qāṭiʿatan amran ḥattā tashhadūn* (27:32). The council offers force and hands
  the decision back to her (27:33).
- **The rule of kings (27:34).** *Inna l-mulūka idhā dakhalū qaryatan afsadūhā … wa-kadhālika
  yafʿalūn.* The last clause is either hers (A) or God's aside (B); no *qāla* separates them.
  - **[interpretive]** A fits the next verse, where she acts on the rule.
  - Solomon's threat then uses her own word, *adhilla* (27:34, 27:37).
- **The gift (27:35).** It is a test, not a bribe: *fa-nāẓiratun bima yarjiʿu l-mursalūn*, which echoes his
  27:28. He refuses it: *a-tumiddūnani bi-māl?* (27:36).
- **The throne.** The ʿifrīt promises it *before you rise from your place*, the man with knowledge of
  the Book *before your glance returns*. The text never says how it came.
- **The disguise (27:41-42).**
  - *Nanẓur a-tahtadī*: another test, and it answers the hoopoe's *fa-hum lā yahtadūn* (27:24).
  - Her answer, *ka-annahu huwa*, says neither yes nor no.
- **27:42b**, *wa-ūtīnā l-ʿilma min qablihā*, is most likely Solomon's side speaking (*qablihā* suits a voice
  outside her). In 27:43, what she worshipped is the grammatical subject of *ṣaddahā*: it was her
  worship that held her back.
- **The palace (27:44).** She takes it for *lujja*, deep water, and uncovers *sāqayhā*, her shins only.
  Then: *rabbi innī ẓalamtu nafsī wa-aslamtu maʿa Sulaymāna lillāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn*. She addresses God,
  not Solomon. *Slm* in the passage has 4 tokens: *muslimīn* ×3 (27:31, 38, 42) and *aslamtu* (27:44).

**[interpretive]**
- **A story about checking.** Under power, the king who could compel tests instead, and the queen who could fight
  tests instead.
- **The end of the contest.** The ending is shared submission: *maʿa*, "with", puts her beside him, and
  *lillāh* names whom both submit to.
- **What the story turns on.** Unequal knowledge: the bird knows what the king does not, the queen half-knows her own
  throne, and the man with the Book knows what the ʿifrīt does not.

## 5. The horses, the trial and the prayer (38:30-40)

> فَقَالَ إِنِّيٓ أَحۡبَبۡتُ حُبَّ ٱلۡخَيۡرِ عَن ذِكۡرِ رَبِّي حَتَّىٰ تَوَارَتۡ بِٱلۡحِجَابِ (38:32)
> رُدُّوهَا عَلَيَّۖ فَطَفِقَ مَسۡحَۢا بِٱلسُّوقِ وَٱلۡأَعۡنَاقِ (38:33)
>
> Saheeh: *"Indeed, I gave preference to the love of good [things] over the remembrance of my Lord until
> the sun disappeared into the curtain." "Return them to me," and set about striking [their] legs and
> necks.* Khattab: *… until they went out of sight … rub down their legs and necks.* Arberry: *… until
> the sun was bidden behind the veil … stroke their shanks and necks.*

***Al-ṣāfinātu l-jiyād* (38:31)** are both words that occur only here (roots *ṣfn*, *jwd*). They are
feminine plurals, read as "standing poised" and "excellent, swift"; the Quran gives nothing else to
test them by. 38:31 sits at the 92nd percentile of divergence.

Four open points:

1. ***Ḥubb al-khayr*: "good things/wealth" or "horses".**
   - **Reading A, good things or wealth.** 100:8 has the identical construct (*li-ḥubbi l-khayri*), and
     *khayr* means wealth at 2:180 (*taraka khayran*).
   - **Reading B, horses.** But the Quran has its own word for horses, *khayl* (3:14, 8:60, 16:8, 17:64,
     59:6), and none of its 196 *khayr* tokens needs the sense "horses".
   - **The text favours A, strongly**, and all four translators render A.
2. ***ʿAn dhikri rabbī*: "away from" or "because of".**
   - Causal *ʿan* exists (9:114, 11:53), and Khattab takes it ("out of remembrance").
   - But *ʿan dhikr* occurs 12 times, and in the other 11 it always means diverted from (5:91, 18:28,
     18:101, 20:124, 21:42, 23:71, 24:37, 43:36, 53:29, 63:9, 72:17). 63:9 has *wealth and children*
     divert you; 24:37 has men *whom neither trade nor sale* diverts.
   - **The text favours "away from", moderately to strongly.**
3. ***Tawārat bi-l-ḥijāb*: the sun or the horses.** The feminine singular verb fits either.
   - **The sun.** No *shms* root appears in 38:28-34, so the sun has to be supplied; Saheeh and
     Arberry supply it.
   - **The horses.** They are the stated antecedent, and *ruddūhā* must be the horses, since the legs
     and necks are theirs. One pronoun running from 38:32 to 38:33 is the simplest chain.
     *Ḥijāb* is a screen or barrier in its other uses (7:46, 17:45, 19:17, 33:53, 41:5, 42:51).
   - **The text favours the horses, moderately.**
4. ***Masḥan bi-l-sūqi wa-l-aʿnāq*: stroking or striking.**
   - *Masḥ* has three other uses (4:43, 5:6 ×2), always wiping, with *bi-* on the thing wiped. The
     Quran's verb for striking is *ḍaraba*, which Ṣād uses six verses later (38:44).
   - **(CORRECTED)** *Sūq* at 48:29 is a plant's stalks. The sense "legs" rests on *sāq* (27:44, 68:42,
     75:29) and on the pairing with *aʿnāq*.
   - **The text favours stroking, moderately to strongly.** Pickthall's "slashing (with his sword)" and Saheeh's
     "striking" are readings the Arabic does not require. The text gives no reason for the stroking.

**What is stated** (data-grounded):
- The text does not call 38:32 a sin and narrates no rebuke.
- Nothing joins 38:33 to 38:34 by cause: 38:34 opens a new sentence with *wa-laqad*.
- The roots of *dhanb*, *khaṭīʾa*, *ithm* and *ʿiṣyān* occur 0 times in his 50 verses, and 0 times in
  David's 38:17-26.
- *ʿAn dhikri rabb-* occurs in three ayahs: 21:42, 72:17 (right after *li-naftinahum fīh*, a test by
  plenty) and 38:32. **[interpretive]** His one lapse is the very failure the Quran ties to tests by abundance.

> وَلَقَدۡ فَتَنَّا سُلَيۡمَٰنَ وَأَلۡقَيۡنَا عَلَىٰ كُرۡسِيِّهِۦ جَسَدࣰا ثُمَّ أَنَابَ (38:34)
> قَالَ رَبِّ ٱغۡفِرۡ لِي وَهَبۡ لِي مُلۡكࣰا لَّا يَنۢبَغِي لِأَحَدࣲ مِّنۢ بَعۡدِيٓۖ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلۡوَهَّابُ (38:35)
>
> Saheeh: *And We certainly tried Solomon and placed on his throne a body; then he returned. He said,
> "My Lord, forgive me and grant me a kingdom such as will not belong to anyone after me. Indeed, You
> are the Bestower."* Khattab adds *˹deformed˺*; Pickthall and Arberry add "mere".

- ***Fatannā*.** **(CORRECTED: 9, not 7)** God says "We tried" in the first person 9 times: 6:53,
  20:40, 20:85, 20:131, 29:3, 38:24, 38:34, 44:17 and 72:17. Two of these (20:131, 72:17) are tests by
  plenty. It is a test, not a punishment. David *ẓanna annamā fatannāhu*, "realised it was a test"
  (38:24); the text never says Solomon did.
- ***Jasad*** has four uses.
  - The golden calf, *a body with a lowing* (7:148, 20:88).
  - Living prophets, who were not made *bodies that do not eat food* (21:8).
  - Here.
  - **It never means a corpse.** It is indefinite and unexplained: whose body it was, living or dead, and what the trial was, the
    text leaves open. *Kursī* occurs only here and at 2:255.
- ***Thumma anāba*** is bare, as at 38:24. **[interpretive]** The prayer that follows is the turning.
- **The order of the prayer.** Forgiveness comes first, then *hab lī*.
  - ***Rabbi ghfir lī*** occurs in 3 ayahs (7:151, 38:35, 71:28), and *hab lī* as a request in 5 (3:38,
    19:5, 37:100, 26:83, 38:35). Only 38:35 joins forgiveness and power.
  - *Innaka anta l-wahhāb* occurs only here and at 3:8.
  - *Lā yanbaghī* is "not fitting" (as at 36:40, the sun and moon): it is a statement of fittingness,
    with no word of envy.
- **Was it granted?** *Fa-sakhkharnā lahu l-rīḥ* (38:36) answers with a gift.
  - Forgiveness is asked, never narrated; David's is (*fa-ghafarnā lahu dhālik*, 38:25).
  - But 38:40 closes with the very words that followed David's forgiveness. **[interpretive]** It is implied,
    not said.
- **The gifts in Ṣād.**
  - The wind runs *rukhāʾan ḥaythu aṣāb*, "gently, wherever he intended" (38:36; *rukhāʾ* occurs only
    here); in 21:81 it is *ʿāṣifa*, forceful, bound for *the land We blessed*; in 34:12 it covers a
    month out and a month back. These are different aspects of one wind, not a contradiction.
  - *Tajrī bi-amrihi* occurs only at 21:81 and 38:36. The text never says he rode the wind.
  - The *shayāṭīn* build and dive (38:37; 21:82); others are *muqarranīna fī l-aṣfād* (38:38, and
    *aṣfād* elsewhere only at 14:49).
- ***Zulfā*** (38:40) has the story's highest divergence (97th percentile). The word is about position
  (67:27, 39:3, 34:37); Pickthall's "favour" and Khattab's "status" add a rank the Arabic does not carry.
  34:37 says wealth and children do not bring anyone *ʿindanā zulfā* "except one who believed and did
  righteousness".

**[interpretive] What the order teaches.**
- Praise comes before the fault: he is *niʿma l-ʿabd … awwāb* (38:30) before the horses are told.
- He speaks the fault himself; it is never called sin, and never punished.
- Then the trial and the return, then forgiveness, then power.
- His seven *shukr* all lie outside Ṣād, and his one plea for forgiveness inside it; the two never
  share a verse.

## 6. Father and son, and the shared formulas

> فَفَهَّمۡنَٰهَا سُلَيۡمَٰنَۚ وَكُلًّا ءَاتَيۡنَا حُكۡمࣰا وَعِلۡمࣰاۚ (21:79)
>
> Saheeh: *And We gave understanding of the case to Solomon, and to each [of them] We gave judgement and
> knowledge.*

**The field case (data-grounded).**
- 21:78 gives two judges (dual *yaḥkumān*), a field (*ḥarth*), a people's sheep that *nafashat* in it
  (strayed and grazed; the word suggests at night), and *We were witness to their judgment*.
- It gives no verdict, no owner and no damages: the familiar details are tafsīr.
- ***Fhm* has one token in the Quran**, *fa-fahhamnāhā* (form II, causative). The text had its usual
  verb, *ʿallama* (21:80 for David; 27:16 in the passive for Solomon), and used a once-only verb for one
  moment instead. **[interpretive]** It describes an act of God about *this* case, not a standing trait.

**David is not made smaller.**
- *Kullan* makes both men the object of *ātaynā*.
- *Ḥukman wa-ʿilmā* is a formula of chosen men, used 4 times: Joseph (12:22), Lot (21:74), Moses (28:14)
  and this verse. Only here do two men share it in one clause.
- God witnessed both judgments with no word of correction, and the next words turn back to David's
  mountains and birds.

**27:15-16.**
- **27:15.** One indefinite *ʿilm* is given to two men, who answer in one dual voice, *wa-qālā
  l-ḥamdu lillāh*, praising God who *faḍḍalanā* over *many* of His *believing* servants.
- **27:16** turns singular: *wa-waritha Sulaymānu Dāwūd*. *Wrth* has 35 tokens, most of them about
  property, or God as heir. Only here is the thing inherited a person. The nearest parallel is Zechariah's
  *yarithunī wa-yarithu min āli Yaʿqūb* (19:6), which concerns a line, not money.
- **Inherited what? [interpretive].** The text does not say. What sits beside the verb is knowledge,
  bird-speech and *faḍl*, not treasure. Kingship is not ruled out by the grammar, but it is not asserted
  either; "he inherited the kingdom" is not the text.
- **Who is "we" in *ʿullimnā*? [interpretive].** Royal "we" is favoured: *yā ayyuhā l-nās* is a king
  addressing his people, and David is already gone (*waritha*). Either way, the next words name the
  source as *faḍl*, not skill.

**David's frame (data-grounded).**
- **2:251.** God gives him *al-mulk wa-l-ḥikma*. Saheeh renders *al-ḥikma* there as "prophethood"; the
  Arabic is "wisdom".
- **34:10-11.** *Yā jibālu awwibī maʿahu*, iron made soft, and the plural *wa-ʿmalū ṣāliḥā* to the
  household.
- **38:17-26.** *Dhā l-ayd, innahu awwāb*; the strengthened kingdom; the two litigants; *fatannāhu*;
  forgiveness; then *yā Dāwūdu innā jaʿalnāka khalīfatan fī l-arḍ* (38:26). The singular *khalīfa*
  occurs twice in the Quran: Adam (2:30) and David.

**The formulas.**

| Formula | Who | Where |
|---|---|---|
| *wa-inna lahu ʿindanā la-zulfā wa-ḥusna maʾāb* | David, Solomon | 38:25, 38:40 only |
| *niʿma l-ʿabdu innahu awwāb* | Solomon, Job | 38:30, 38:44 only |
| *awwāb* (singular) | David, mountains and birds, Solomon, Job, the people of Paradise | 38:17, 19, 30, 44; 50:32 |

- **(CORRECTED)** The *awwāb* count is the singular only. The lemma adds the plural *awwābīn* (17:25),
  and the verb *awwibī* is said to the mountains with David (34:10).
- No phrase is common to all three men.
- **Ṣād gives David and Solomon one frame, step by step:** *awwāb*, *fatannā*, *anāba*, *zulfā*.
- **Job's pattern is different**: no fault, no *fitna*, no forgiveness, only affliction, a call, an
  answer, and *ṣābir* (38:41-44).
- **[interpretive]** Ṣād may be showing ways of turning back: after a ruling (David), after a lapse and a
  trial (Solomon), under suffering (Job). That is a reading of the arrangement, not a statement of the text.

## 7. The defence in 2:102

> وَٱتَّبَعُواْ مَا تَتۡلُواْ ٱلشَّيَٰطِينُ عَلَىٰ مُلۡكِ سُلَيۡمَٰنَۖ وَمَا كَفَرَ سُلَيۡمَٰنُ وَلَٰكِنَّ ٱلشَّيَٰطِينَ كَفَرُواْ (2:102)
>
> Saheeh: *And they followed [instead] what the devils had recited during the reign of Solomon. It was
> not Solomon who disbelieved, but the devils disbelieved.*

- **The context (2:101).** A party given the Book threw it *behind their backs*. 2:102's *wa-ttabaʿū* says what
  they followed instead.
- ***ʿAlā mulki Sulaymān*** reads two ways: (a) "during his reign", since 21:82 and 34:12-13 place
  devils and jinn under him; (b) "against or about his kingdom", a false recitation laid on his reign.
  (b) makes the next clause the answer to a charge.
- **Who disbelieved.** *Wa-mā kafara Sulaymān* is the Quran's only *wa-mā kafara*, and it is the
  verse's own denial. The devils *kafarū*, teaching people *al-siḥr*.
- ***Wa-mā unzila ʿalā l-malakayn*.** The QAC tags this *mā* as relative; the *mā* of *wa-mā kafara*
  and *wa-mā yuʿallimāni* as negative.
  - **The relative reading:** people learned *what was sent down to the two angels* as a second source.
  - **The negative reading:** *nothing was sent down to them*, so sorcery did not come from heaven.
  - Under both, the angels' warning, *innamā naḥnu fitnatun fa-lā takfur*, is their disclaimer: they
    present themselves as a test, not a gift.
- **What charge does it answer? [interpretive].** Denying *kufr* to him by name assumes it had been alleged, and
  *al-siḥr* names what was alleged: that his power over wind, jinn and devils was sorcery. The text says
  neither who said it nor when. Its own account is that every gift ran *bi-idhni rabbihi* (34:12),
  *bi-amrihi* (21:81, 38:36), under *sakhkharnā*.
- **[interpretive]** At 27:40 he asked himself *a-ashkuru am akfur*. 2:102 gives God's answer, in the
  same root: *wa-mā kafara Sulaymān*.
- **Not in the verse:** Zuhra, the angels' fall, the well. These are tradition.

## 8. Against the Quran's other men of power

| Man | His line | Whose he says it is |
|---|---|---|
| Pharaoh | *a-laysa lī mulku Miṣr* (43:51); *anā rabbukumu l-aʿlā* (79:24) | *lī*, *anā* |
| Qārūn | *innamā ūtītuhu ʿalā ʿilmin ʿindī* (28:78) | *ʿindī* |
| the garden-owner | *anā aktharu minka mālan* (18:34) | *anā* |
| Abraham's disputant | *anā uḥyī wa-umīt* (2:258) | *anā* |
| 41:50; 7:131 | *hādhā lī*; *lanā hādhihi* | *lī*, *lanā* |
| Joseph | *rabbi qad ātaytanī mina l-mulk* (12:101) | *You gave me* |
| Dhū l-Qarnayn | *hādhā raḥmatun min rabbī* (18:98) | *rabbī* |
| Solomon | *hādhā min faḍli rabbī li-yabluwanī* (27:40) | *rabbī*, and *to test me* |

**Data-grounded.**
- **The ungrateful man.** 39:49 gives his words again, *innamā ūtītuhu ʿalā ʿilm*, and God replies
  *bal hiya fitna*.
- ***Ātāhu llāhu l-mulk*** occurs twice, seven verses apart: of David (2:251) and of Abraham's disputant
  (2:258).
- **Pharaoh's rivers.** Pharaoh's rivers *tajrī min taḥtī* (43:51) borrow a phrase that, in 36 of its 39 ayahs, describes Paradise.
  Solomon's wind runs *bi-amrihi* under God's *sakhkharnā*.
- **Dhū l-Qarnayn is Solomon's nearest double.**
  - Both are given *min kulli shayʾ* (18:84, 27:16).
  - Both refuse payment because God's gift is better (18:95, 27:36).
  - *Qiṭr*, molten copper, occurs only for the two (18:96, 34:12).
  - Both say *hādhā … min rabbī*.
- **The man of 89:15.** He says *rabbī akraman*, "my Lord has honoured me", when his Lord tested him
  (*ibtalāhu*), and God answers *kallā* (89:17).

**[interpretive] One line, in two halves.**
- **The source.** Those who fall name themselves; those who stand name the Lord.
- **The purpose.** Naming the Lord is not enough: 89:15 does that, and is rebuked for calling a test an honour.
- **What only Solomon does.** He alone says both halves about himself, as the gift arrives. Dhū l-Qarnayn says the first half. The second, the gift as
  a test of oneself, the text says about others (39:49, 89:15) but has no other ruler say.

## 9. The limits of power (34:14)

> فَلَمَّا قَضَيۡنَا عَلَيۡهِ ٱلۡمَوۡتَ مَا دَلَّهُمۡ عَلَىٰ مَوۡتِهِۦٓ إِلَّا دَآبَّةُ ٱلۡأَرۡضِ تَأۡكُلُ مِنسَأَتَهُۥۖ فَلَمَّا خَرَّ تَبَيَّنَتِ ٱلۡجِنُّ أَن لَّوۡ كَانُواْ يَعۡلَمُونَ ٱلۡغَيۡبَ مَا لَبِثُواْ فِي ٱلۡعَذَابِ ٱلۡمُهِينِ (34:14)
>
> Saheeh: *And when We decreed for Solomon death, nothing indicated to the jinn his death except a
> creature of the earth eating his staff. But when he fell, it became clear to the jinn that if they had
> known the unseen, they would not have remained in humiliating punishment.*

**Data-grounded.**
- ***Dābbatu l-arḍ*** in the construct occurs only here. *Kharra*, "he fell", is the verb of David's
  prostration (38:24).
- **The point.** The verse states it itself: the jinn did not know the unseen. *Ghyb* occurs twice in his
  verses: the hoopoe perhaps *absent* (27:20), and the jinn ignorant of *the unseen* (34:14).
- ***Al-ʿadhāb al-muhīn*** with the article occurs twice: the jinn under Solomon (34:14), and Israel under
  Pharaoh (44:30).
- **No duration is given.** The roots *ʿwm*, *snw* and *ḥwl* occur 0 times in the 50 verses.
- **The only other *kursī*** is 2:255, God's, whose guarding never tires Him.
- **Sabaʾ, right after.** A people told *wa-shkurū lahu* (34:15) turn away, are repaid *bimā kafarū*
  (34:17), and *ẓalamū anfusahum* (34:19). The queen had said *ẓalamtu nafsī* (27:44), as a
  confession. The text never ties the two Sabaʾs together.

**[interpretive]**
- **What this power cannot do.** It cannot know everything (27:22), see the absent or the small (27:18,
  27:20), outlive its holder, or make others grateful (34:13, 34:15-17).
- **Its cost is not hidden.** Even God-guarded rule (21:82) was torment to its subjects, who did not know.
- **Dhū l-Qarnayn says the limit aloud:** his wall will be levelled (18:98).
- **Power ends; thanks decides what is kept.**

**[interpretive] What the additions do.**
- **They move his power out of *We subjected* and into objects.** A ring can be stolen, and then the
  gift becomes Pharaoh's *lī*.
- **They turn a trial into a punishment**, and supply a sin where the text has only *fatannā … thumma
  anāba*.
- **They enlarge the marvel**, where the text's marvels point away from him: an ant, a bird, a creature
  of the earth.
- **They turn a conversion into a romance.**
- The retellings make him grander or guiltier; the text makes him answerable.

## 11. Open questions

1. **27:42b.** Is *wa-ūtīnā l-ʿilma min qablihā* Solomon's side speaking, or the queen's? *Qablihā*
   favours an outside voice, but no *qāla* marks it.
2. **27:34b.** Is *wa-kadhālika yafʿalūn* the queen's general rule or God's aside? The grammar permits
   both.
3. **38:34.** Can anything inside the Quran bound the *jasad* further than "an unexplained body, never a
   corpse"? Or is the silence the point?
4. **38:35-40.** Forgiveness is implied by the formula, never narrated. Is the difference from David
   (*fa-ghafarnā lahu*) meaningful, or only a matter of compression?
5. **27:16.** What did Solomon inherit? Nothing in the text decides between standing and knowledge on one side and
   kingship on the other.
6. **34:15.** Is the Sabaʾ of the lost gardens the queen's people? The two passages share the name and
   the theme of *ẓulm* of the self, but are never joined.
7. **38:39.** Does *bi-ghayri ḥisāb* qualify his giving (no account to render), or the size of the gift?
8. **38:31.** The roots of *ṣāfināt* (*ṣfn*) and *jiyād* (*jwd*) occur nowhere else in the Quran. Does the
   scene need more from them than "fine horses", or does its weight rest on 38:32 alone?
9. **The series question.** Five prophets are titled only through the lists: David, Solomon, Job,
   Elisha and Zechariah. Is there a pattern to who is titled directly, and who only by list?
10. **Ṣād's triptych.** Is the David–Solomon–Job sequence a deliberate teaching on kinds of return, or
    an editorial grouping of three *awwāb*?
