# What kind of king: gift, test and return in Solomon (Phase 2)

## Headline finding

Every powerful man in the Quran points at what he has and says whose it is. Pharaoh, Qārūn, the garden-owner and the man of 41:50 answer with themselves (*lī*, *ʿindī*, *anā*). Solomon answers with two things: *from my Lord's bounty* and *to test me* (27:40). Dhū l-Qarnayn shares the first half (18:98). Nobody else says the second half about himself; the text says it of the others (39:49, 89:15). Every gift in Solomon's five passages has a God-ward line attached, spoken by him in an-Naml and by God elsewhere. He reports his own lapse, and asks forgiveness before power.

**Evidence.** Arabic text and QAC morphology, searched by script. Counts are data-grounded; **[interpretive]** marks readings. Hadith, tafsīr and Isrāʾīliyyāt excluded.

## 1. Gift and answer (data-grounded)

| Gift | Line that follows | Speaker |
|---|---|---|
| knowledge, 27:15 | *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi lladhī faḍḍalanā* | David and Solomon |
| bird-speech, *min kulli shayʾ*, 27:16 | *hādhā la-huwa l-faḍlu l-mubīn* (unique) | Solomon |
| the army and the ant, 27:17-18 | *rabbi awziʿnī an ashkura niʿmataka* (19) | Solomon |
| the queen's gift, 27:35 | *mā ātāniya llāhu khayrun mimmā ātākum* (36) | Solomon |
| the throne, 27:40 | *hādhā min faḍli rabbī li-yabluwanī* | Solomon |
| the glass palace, 27:44 | *aslamtu maʿa Sulaymān lillāh* | the queen |
| the horses, 38:31 | *innī aḥbabtu ḥubba l-khayri ʿan dhikri rabbī* (32) | Solomon, confessing |
| wind, builders, the chained, 38:36-38 | *hādhā ʿaṭāʾunā* (39, unique) | God |
| *fahhamnāhā* 21:79; wind 21:81; divers 21:82 | *wa kunnā fāʿilīn* / *ʿālimīn* / *ḥāfiẓīn* | God |
| wind, copper, jinn, 34:12-13 | *iʿmalū āla Dāwūda shukrā* | God |
| his reign, 2:102 | *wa mā kafara Sulaymān* | God |

- **No gift lacks a God-ward line.** In an-Naml Solomon speaks it; elsewhere God does. Every verse of 21:78-82 ends with God naming His part (*wa kunnā* ×4) or asking thanks (21:80).
- **Near-exceptions.** For the palace the queen supplies the line; for the horses it is an admission, not thanks.
- **Pronouns (script, every verse he speaks).** Solomon never says *anā*; the two in 27:39-40 are his helpers'. His only *innī* is the confession (38:32).

## 2. Against the Quran's other men of power

> ثُمَّ إِذَا خَوَّلۡنَٰهُ نِعۡمَةࣰ مِّنَّا قَالَ إِنَّمَآ أُوتِيتُهُۥ عَلَىٰ عِلۡمِۭۚ بَلۡ هِيَ فِتۡنَةࣱ (39:49)
> Saheeh: *then when We bestow on him a favor from Us, he says, "I have only been given it because of [my] knowledge." Rather, it is a trial.*

| Man | His line | Whose it is |
|---|---|---|
| Pharaoh | *a-laysa lī mulku Miṣr* (43:51); *anā rabbukumu l-aʿlā* (79:24) | *lī*, *anā* |
| Qārūn | *innamā ūtītuhu ʿalā ʿilmin ʿindī* (28:78) | *ʿindī* |
| garden-owner | *anā aktharu minka mālan*; *this will never perish* (18:34-35) | *anā* |
| Abraham's disputant | *anā uḥyī wa umīt* (2:258) | *anā* |
| 41:50; 7:131 | *hādhā lī*; *lanā hādhihi* | *lī*, *lanā* |
| Joseph | *rabbi qad ātaytanī mina l-mulk* (12:101) | *You gave me* |
| Dhū l-Qarnayn | *hādhā raḥmatun min rabbī* (18:98) | *rabbī* |
| Solomon | *hādhā min faḍli rabbī li-yabluwanī* (27:40) | *rabbī* + *to test me* |

**Data-grounded.**
- ***Hādhā min faḍli rabbī* occurs once (27:40);** even *faḍli rabbī* is unique. A regex for *this* + owner finds only 27:40, 18:98 and 41:50 as attribution lines.
- ***Ātāhu llāhu l-mulk* occurs twice, seven verses apart:** David (2:251) and Abraham's disputant (2:258).
- **Pharaoh borrows Paradise.** Of 39 ayahs with *tajrī min taḥt-*, 36 are Paradise; the rest are a garden burnt (2:266), nations destroyed (6:6) and Pharaoh's *beneath me* (43:51). Solomon's wind runs *bi-amrihi* (only 21:81, 38:36), under God's *sakhkharnā*.
- **Dhū l-Qarnayn is Solomon's nearest double.**
  1. *Min kulli shayʾ* with a giving verb is said of exactly three rulers: Solomon (27:16), the queen (27:23), Dhū l-Qarnayn (18:84).
  2. Both refuse payment: God's gift is better (18:95; 27:36).
  3. *Qiṭr* occurs only at 18:96 and 34:12.
  4. *Hādhā … min rabbī*.

**One line, in two halves.**
- *Source.* The fallen name themselves; those who stand name the Lord.
- *Purpose.* Naming the Lord is not enough. The man of 89:15 says *rabbī akraman*, "my Lord has honoured me", when his Lord *ibtalāhu*, tested him; God answers *kallā* (89:17). In 39:49 Qārūn's words return, and God adds *bal hiya fitna*.

Solomon says both halves himself, as the gift arrives.

## 3. Power named as a test

> فَلَمَّا رَءَاهُ مُسۡتَقِرًّا عِندَهُۥ قَالَ هَٰذَا مِن فَضۡلِ رَبِّي لِيَبۡلُوَنِيٓ ءَأَشۡكُرُ أَمۡ أَكۡفُرُۖ (27:40)
> Saheeh: *And when [Solomon] saw it placed before him, he said, "This is from the favor of my Lord to test me whether I will be grateful or ungrateful."*

**Data-grounded.**
- **Self as object of a test verb**, twice in the Quran: *li-yabluwanī* (27:40), and *wa lā taftinnī*, "do not put me to trial" (9:49), from a man begging off; the text replies *into trial they have fallen*.
- **To everyone else the text says it:** rank (6:165), wealth and children (8:28), good and evil (21:35), splendour and plenty (*li-naftinahum fīhi*, 20:131, 72:17).
- ***ʿAn dhikri rabb-*** is in three ayahs: 21:42, 72:17 (right after the test by plenty) and 38:32. **[interpretive]** Solomon's one lapse is the failure the Quran ties to tests by abundance.
- **Thanks or kufr.** He asks *a-ashkuru am akfur* (27:40). **[interpretive]** 2:102 answers him: *wa mā kafara Sulaymān*, the Quran's only *wa mā kafara*.
- ***Bi-ghayri ḥisāb* (38:39).** Its six other uses describe God's own giving. Ṣād's three other *ḥisāb* are all *yawm al-ḥisāb* (38:16, 26, 53).

**[interpretive]**
- **The danger is misreading power:** as one's own (Qārūn), as lasting (the garden), as honour (89:15).
- **The text guards its most-gifted king** by having him read his largest gifts rightly, aloud.
- **"Without reckoning"** cannot mean "beyond judgment" in a surah so full of the Reckoning. It gives him God's own freedom to give or withhold; that freedom is the test.

## 4. Lapse and return

| Step | David, 38:17-26 | Solomon, 38:30-40 |
|---|---|---|
| called a returner first | *innahu awwāb* (17) | *niʿma l-ʿabdu innahu awwāb* (30) |
| kingdom | strengthened before the trial (20) | asked for after forgiveness (35) |
| lapse | a ruling; God calls it only *dhālika* (25) | self-reported (32) |
| trial | *ẓanna … annamā fatannāhu* (24) | *fatannā*; a body on his throne (34) |
| return | *istaghfara … wa anāb* | *thumma anāb*; *rabbi ghfir lī* |
| close | forgiven; *la-zulfā wa ḥusna maʾāb* | gifts; the same words |

**Data-grounded.**
- **No sin vocabulary.** The roots of *dhanb*, *khaṭīʾa*, *ithm* and *ʿiṣyān* occur 0 times in his 50 verses, and 0 in 38:17-26.
- ***Rabbi ghfir lī*** is in 3 ayahs (7:151, 38:35, 71:28); ***hab lī*** as a request in 5: a child (3:38, 19:5, 37:100), judgment (26:83), a kingdom (38:35). Only 38:35 joins forgiveness and power.
- His passages' seven *shukr* all lie outside Ṣād; his one *istighfār* lies inside it (the gratitude synthesis: the two never share a verse).

**[interpretive]**
- **Praise comes first:** he is named a returner before the fault is told.
- **The fault is spoken,** by Solomon first, never called sin, never punished.
- **The order is the teaching:** trial, then return; forgiveness, then power.

A good man here still slips, but briefly; he owns it, and turns back to the same nearness as his father.

## 5. The limits of power

> فَلَمَّا قَضَيۡنَا عَلَيۡهِ ٱلۡمَوۡتَ مَا دَلَّهُمۡ عَلَىٰ مَوۡتِهِۦٓ إِلَّا دَآبَّةُ ٱلۡأَرۡضِ تَأۡكُلُ مِنسَأَتَهُۥۖ فَلَمَّا خَرَّ تَبَيَّنَتِ ٱلۡجِنُّ أَن لَّوۡ كَانُواْ يَعۡلَمُونَ ٱلۡغَيۡبَ مَا لَبِثُواْ فِي ٱلۡعَذَابِ ٱلۡمُهِينِ (34:14)
> Saheeh: *nothing indicated to the jinn his death except a creature of the earth eating his staff. But when he fell, it became clear to the jinn that if they had known the unseen, they would not have remained in humiliating punishment.*

**Data-grounded.**
- **Knowing.** After *ūtīnā min kulli shayʾ* (27:16), a bird: *aḥaṭtu bimā lam tuḥiṭ bihi* (27:22). *Lam tuḥiṭ bihi* is otherwise only 18:68, said to Moses.
- **Seeing.** The root *ghyb* occurs twice in his verses: the hoopoe perhaps *absent* (27:20), and the jinn ignorant of *the unseen* (34:14).
- **Noticing.** *Wa hum lā yashʿurūn* (27:18) has 15 uses, usually people blind to what is coming, Pharaoh's household among them (28:9, 11).
- **The cost below.** *Al-ʿadhāb al-muhīn*, with the article, occurs twice: the jinn under Solomon (34:14) and Israel under Pharaoh (44:30).
- **The other throne.** The only other *kursī* is 2:255: none encompass His knowledge, no sleep takes Him, guarding does not tire Him.
- **Sabaʾ.** Right after his death, a people told *wa-shkurū lahu* (34:15) turn away, are repaid *bimā kafarū* (34:17), and *ẓalamū anfusahum* (34:19). Their queen had said *ẓalamtu nafsī* (27:44), confessing.

**[interpretive]**
- **This power cannot** know all, see the absent or the small, outlive its holder, or make others grateful.
- **The cost is not hidden:** even God-guarded rule (21:82) was torment to its subjects while they did not know.
- **Dhū l-Qarnayn says the limit aloud:** his wall will be levelled (18:98).

Power ends; thanks decides what is kept.

## 6. Negative space, consolidated

| Tradition adds | The text | Script, 50 verses |
|---|---|---|
| a ring | no instrument; *bi-amrihi*, *sakhkharnā* | *khtm* 0 |
| a demon on his throne | an indefinite *jasad*, never a corpse | *ṣkhr* 0 |
| Bilqīs; a marriage; 70 or 100 wives | an unnamed queen, *maʿa Sulaymān lillāh* | *nsw* 0; *zwj* only 2:102 |
| the sun brought back | no sun in 38:31-33 | *shms* only 27:24 |
| the horses slaughtered | *masḥ* is wiping in every use | — |
| a year dead on the staff | no duration | *ʿwm*, *snw*, *ḥwl* 0 |
| the Temple | *maḥārīb* (34:13); cf. David's *miḥrāb* (38:21) | *byt* 0 |
| Hārūt, Mārūt and Zuhra | two named angels who warn | no Zuhra |

**[interpretive] What the additions do.**
- **They move his power out of *We subjected* into objects.** A ring can be stolen: the gift becomes Pharaoh's *lī*.
- **They turn a trial into a punishment** and supply a sin, where the text has only *fatannā … thumma anāba*.
- **They enlarge the marvel,** where the text's marvels point away from him: an ant, a bird, a creature of the earth.
- **They turn a conversion into a romance.**

The retellings make him grander or guiltier; the text makes him answerable.

## 7. Corrections to Phase 1

- **(CORRECTED) Ant file, headline and §4.** Of *nẓr* ×5 and *rjʿ* ×3, Solomon speaks *nẓr* at 27:27, 28, 41 and *rjʿ* at 27:28, 37; the council (27:33) and queen (27:35) speak the rest. This supports the file's point that the king is not the only checker.
- **(CORRECTED) Horses file, §3.** God's first-person *ftn* verbs number 9, not 7. Add *li-naftinahum* (20:131, 72:17); both are tests by abundance.
- **(CORRECTED) Horses file, §2.** *Sūq* at 48:29 is a plant's stalks, not legs. The "legs" sense rests on *sāq* (27:44, 68:42, 75:29) and on *aʿnāq*. The stroking reading stands.

## For the story guide

- Proud men say "this is mine". When the throne arrived, Solomon said: *this is from my Lord's bounty, to test me: will I be grateful or not?* (27:40).
- He slipped once and said so himself. He turned back, and asked forgiveness before he asked for a kingdom (38:32-35).
- A bird knew what he did not; an ant saw his army's danger; when he died, only a small creature noticed.
- The ring, the demon, the wives, the queen's name and the Temple are not in the Quran.
