# Solomon: the horses, the trial and the kingdom (38:30-40, 34:12-14, 21:81-82)

## Headline finding

Ṣād does not give three identical trial-and-reward stories. David (38:21-25) and Solomon (38:31-40) share one frame word for word: *fatannā*, *anāba*, then *wa-inna lahu ʿindanā la-zulfā wa-ḥusna maʾāb*. Job (38:41-44) shares only *niʿma l-ʿabd… awwāb* and *wahabnā*; he has no fault, no *fitna*, no forgiveness. The horses are open in three places, and the text leans one way on each: "good things/wealth", horses as subject of *tawārat*, and stroking (*masḥ*, never "strike" elsewhere). *Jasad* is never a corpse. Forgiveness is asked (38:35) but never narrated. Gifts follow, then "without account".

## 1. The shape, beside David and Job (data-grounded)

| Step | David 38:21-25 | Solomon 38:30-40 | Job 38:41-44 |
|---|---|---|---|
| frame | *ʿabdunā… awwāb* (17) | *niʿma l-ʿabd… awwāb* (30) | *ʿabdunā* (41); *niʿma l-ʿabd… awwāb* (44) |
| event | the two litigants | horses (31-33) | *Satan touched me* (41) |
| trial word | *fatannāhu* (24) | *fatannā* (34) | none |
| fault | not named; David *ẓanna* it a test | his own words, 38:32 | none; *We found him patient* |
| turning | *istaghfara… kharra rākiʿan… anāba* | *thumma anāba*, then the prayer | none; he *called* (41) |
| forgiveness | *fa-ghafarnā lahu dhālik* (25) | asked (35), not narrated | none |
| gift | none stated | wind, devils, "Our gift" | *wahabnā lahu ahlahu* (43) |
| close | *la-zulfā wa-ḥusna maʾāb* (25) | the same words (40) | *awwāb* (44) |

- **Shared wording.** *wa-inna lahu ʿindanā la-zulfā* occurs only at 38:25 and 38:40. *Niʿma l-ʿabd* occurs only at 38:30 and 38:44. *Awwāb* (singular) is 5 times in the Quran, 4 in Ṣād (17, 19, 30, 44).
- **Wahaba in Ṣād.** Five tokens: 38:9 (*al-wahhāb*), 38:30 (God *gave* Solomon to David), 38:35 (×2), 38:43 (Job's family).
- **Answer.** Two parallel sequences, not three. David and Solomon match step for step, except that David's forgiveness is narrated and Solomon's is only requested. Job completes a different pattern: affliction, call, answer, *ṣābir*. **[Interpretive]** Ṣād may be teaching three ways a servant "turns back" (by fault, by a trial that follows a lapse, by suffering). That is a reading of the arrangement, not a statement of the text.

## 2. The horses, 38:31-33

> 38:32 فَقَالَ إِنِّيٓ أَحۡبَبۡتُ حُبَّ ٱلۡخَيۡرِ عَن ذِكۡرِ رَبِّي حَتَّىٰ تَوَارَتۡ بِٱلۡحِجَابِ
> Saheeh: *I gave preference to the love of good [things] over the remembrance of my Lord until the sun disappeared into the curtain.* Khattab: *out of remembrance for Allah… until they went out of sight.* Pickthall: *preferred the good things… till they were taken out of sight behind the curtain.* Arberry: *loved the love of good things better than the remembrance… until the sun was bidden behind the veil.*

> 38:33 رُدُّوهَا عَلَيَّۖ فَطَفِقَ مَسۡحَۢا بِٱلسُّوقِ وَٱلۡأَعۡنَاقِ
> Saheeh: *striking [their] legs and necks.* Khattab: *rub down their legs and necks.* Pickthall: *slashing (with his sword).* Arberry: *stroke their shanks and necks.*

- ***al-ṣāfinātu l-jiyād* (31).** *Ṣāfināt* (root *ṣfn*) and *jiyād* (*jwd*) are single occurrences. They are feminine plural, so read as "standing/poised" and "excellent, swift". The Quran cannot test them further. Hapax, hence the 92nd-percentile divergence (0.741).
- ***ḥubb al-khayr.*** *Ḥubb* is an accusative cognate: "I loved a love of the good". **Reading A: "good things/wealth", with the horses as what is in view.** Support: 100:8 (*li-ḥubbi l-khayri*, the identical construct) and *khayr* as wealth at 2:180 (*taraka khayran*) and 2:215, 2:272-273. **Reading B: *khayr* = horses.** The Quran has its own word for horses, *khayl* (3:14, 8:60, 16:8, 17:64, 59:6), and none of its 196 *khayr* tokens needs that sense. 3:14 lists *khayl* among things loved. All four translators already render A. Favoured: A, strongly.
- ***ʿan dhikri rabbī.*** *ʿan* as "away from/over" (Saheeh, Pickthall, Arberry) or "because of" (Khattab, "out of remembrance"). Causal *ʿan* exists (9:114 *ʿan mawʿidatin*, 11:53). But the exact string *ʿan dhikr* occurs 12 times, and in the 11 others it always means turned or diverted away: 5:91, 18:28, 18:101, 20:124, 21:42, 23:71, 24:37, 43:36, 53:29, 63:9 (*wealth and children* divert you), 72:17. Favoured: "away from", moderately strongly. It is Solomon's self-report; God's narration does not repeat it.
- ***tawārat bi-l-ḥijāb*.** The verb's subject is feminine singular, which fits the sun or the horses (a non-rational plural takes a feminine singular). **Sun:** nothing in 38:28-34 mentions it (script: no *shms* root), so it must be supplied; two of four translators supply it. **Horses:** it is the stated antecedent, and *ruddūhā* must mean the horses because the legs and necks (33) are theirs. One pronoun running from 32 to 33 is the simplest chain. *Tawārā* "to hide oneself" is the sense at 16:59 as well. *Ḥijāb* is a barrier or screen in its six other uses (7:46, 17:45, 19:17, 33:53, 41:5, 42:51). Favoured: horses, moderately. Time-setting *bi-l-ʿashiyy* is shared with David's mountains (38:18).
- ***ruddūhā ʿalayya*.** An imperative to unnamed attendants: "return them to me". It shows only that they were gone and he wanted them back.
- ***ṭafiqa masḥan bi-l-sūqi wa-l-aʿnāq*.** *Masḥ* has 3 other uses (4:43, 5:6 ×2), always wiping with *bi-* on the thing wiped (*imsaḥū bi-ruʾūsikum*). The Quran's word for striking is *ḍaraba* (38:44 uses it a few verses on). *Sūq* is "shanks/legs" (27:44, 48:29, 68:42, 75:29). Favoured: stroking/wiping, moderately strongly. The text does not say why (affection, inspection or something else). "Slashing with his sword" is Pickthall's addition. **[Interpretive]** The sacrifice reading needs *masḥ* to mean "cut", which no Quranic use supports.
- **What is stated.** The text does not call 38:32 a sin, does not narrate rebuke and does not link it to 38:34 by a conjunction.

## 3. The trial, 38:34

> 38:34 وَلَقَدۡ فَتَنَّا سُلَيۡمَٰنَ وَأَلۡقَيۡنَا عَلَىٰ كُرۡسِيِّهِۦ جَسَدࣰا ثُمَّ أَنَابَ
> Saheeh: *a body; then he returned.* Khattab: *a ˹deformed˺ body… turned ˹to Allah in repentance˺.* Pickthall: *a (mere) body. Then did he repent.* Arberry: *a mere body; then he repented.*

- ***Fatannā*.** God's own *fatan-* ("We tried") has 7 tokens: Moses (20:40, within a list of rescues, *fatannāka futūnan*), David (38:24), Solomon (38:34), and peoples (6:53, 20:85, 29:3, 44:17). It is a test from God, not a punishment. Unlike David (*ẓanna annamā fatannāhu*), Solomon is never said to have realised it.
- ***Jasad* (4 uses).** The calf of gold (7:148, 20:88: "a body with a lowing") and the prophets (21:8: "We did not make them bodies that do not eat food", living men). Never a corpse. **The text says:** an indefinite body, cast on his throne (*kursī*, only here and 2:255). **It leaves open:** whose body, living or dead, and what the trial was. "Deformed" (Khattab) and "mere" (Pickthall, Arberry) are additions.
- ***Thumma anāba*.** *Anāba* with no object, as in 38:24, is a bare return; *thumma* places it after the trial and before the speech. **[Interpretive]** The prayer of 35 is the turning.

## 4. The prayer, 38:35

> رَبِّ ٱغۡفِرۡ لِي وَهَبۡ لِي مُلۡكࣰا لَّا يَنۢبَغِي لِأَحَدࣲ مِّنۢ بَعۡدِيٓۖ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلۡوَهَّابُ

- **Order.** Forgiveness, then *hab lī*. *Innaka anta l-wahhāb* appears only here and 3:8. *Yanbaghī* is "fitting/proper" (36:40, the sun and moon). So "not fitting for anyone after me" is a statement of fittingness, with no word of envy.
- **Granted?** *Fa-sakhkharnā lahu l-rīḥ* (36) answers by gift. Forgiveness is never stated, but the closing *zulfā* (40) is what followed David's forgiveness. **[Interpretive]** Implied, not said. The text never reports whether anyone later had the like.

## 5. The gifts

- **Wind.** *Rukhāʾan ḥaythu aṣāb* (38:36; *rukhāʾ* occurs once), *ʿāṣifatan* (21:81; *ʿāṣif* is destructive wind at 10:22, 14:18), and a month out and a month back (34:12). Not a contradiction: they are different aspects. *Tajrī bi-amrihi* is the exact phrase in both 21:81 and 38:36 and nowhere else. *Rukhāʾ* gives manner (gentle) and *ḥaythu aṣāb* gives direction (where he intended); *ʿāṣifa* gives force and *ilā l-arḍ allatī bāraknā* a destination; 34:12 gives range. The text never says he rode it.
- **Devils and jinn.** Builders and divers (38:37; diving also 21:82) are called *shayāṭīn*; 34:12 says *min al-jinn* (Khattab renders 21:82 "jinn"). Others are *muqarranīna fī l-aṣfād* (38:38; *aṣfād* only here and 14:49). *Wa-kunnā lahum ḥāfiẓīn* (21:82) means God guarded them. The 34:12 warning (*man yazigh… ʿan amrinā*) shows a command, permission (*bi-idhni rabbihi*) and a penalty.
- **Made:** *maḥārīb, tamāthīl, jifān… wa-qudūr* (34:13), plus the copper spring (*qiṭr*, 34:12; 18:96 is the only other).
- ***Hādhā ʿaṭāʾunā fa-mnun aw amsik bi-ghayri ḥisāb* (39).** *Bi-ghayri ḥisāb* is 7 times, six of God's provision (2:212, 3:27, 3:37, 24:38, 39:10, 40:40). Here it may qualify his giving (no account to be rendered) or the gift. The text calls it *ʿaṭāʾ*, a gift, not a prize.

> 38:40 وَإِنَّ لَهُۥ عِندَنَا لَزُلۡفَىٰ وَحُسۡنَ مَـَٔابࣲ
> Saheeh: *nearness… a good place of return.* Khattab: *a status of closeness… an honourable destination.* Pickthall: *favour with Us, and a happy journey's end.* Arberry: *a near place in Our presence and a fair resort.*

- **Divergence (97th percentile, 0.776).** *Zulfā* is spatial or positional in all uses (67:27 "near", 39:3, 34:37 "nearer in position"). Pickthall's "favour" and Khattab's "status" add a rank the Arabic does not carry. *Ḥusn al-maʾāb* (3:14, 13:29, 38:25, 38:40, 38:49): 3:14 sets it against the love of horses and wealth. **[Interpretive]** 34:37, in this same surah, says wealth and children do not bring one nearer *ʿindanā zulfā* "except one who believed and did righteousness".

## 6. Work in thanks, 34:13

*Iʿmalū āla Dāwūda shukran.* The Arabic reads three ways: *shukran* as purpose ("for thanks"), as manner ("thankfully"), or as the thing done ("work thanks"). *Iʿmalū* is the second plural command, as in 34:11 (*iʿmalū ṣāliḥan*). The command is to the family, not to Solomon alone, and *qalīl min ʿibādiya l-shakūr* is God's remark. **[Interpretive]** Thanks is shown as work with the hands, in the same breath as the jinn's labour.

## 7. His death, 34:14

*Fa-lammā qaḍaynā ʿalayhi l-mawt mā dallahum… illā dābbatu l-arḍ taʾkulu minsaʾatah* (*dābbat al-arḍ* is a unique phrase). *Fa-lammā kharra* uses David's verb (38:24). The verse states the point: *law kānū yaʿlamūn al-ghayb mā labithū fī l-ʿadhāb al-muhīn*. The jinn did not know the unseen. It says nothing of a year or a pose. 34:15-19 turns to Sabaʾ, who were told *wa-shkurū lah* (15), turned away, and were repaid because *kafūr* (17), after asking for longer journeys (19). **[Interpretive]** Solomon's month-long journeys and Sabaʾ's request for farther ones share a theme.

## 8. Negative space (data-grounded absences)

Not in the Quran: the ring and the demon on the throne; the 70 or 100 wives or a half-born child; "bring back the sun" (no sun in 38:31-33); a year dead on his staff; any Temple. These come from tradition. The only "horses" word in the story is hapax *ṣāfināt jiyād*.

## For the story guide

- At evening, fine horses were shown to Solomon; he lost time away from his Lord's remembrance and called them back (38:31-33).
- God tested him, a body lay on his throne, and he turned back, asking forgiveness first (38:34-35).
- Gifts followed: wind, builders and divers, and "give or withhold, without account" (38:36-39).
- The gift is named a gift; thanks is work (34:13).
- When he died and fell, the jinn learned they did not know the unseen (34:14).
- Not in the Quran: the ring, the wives, the sun, the year.
