# Narrative: the ant, the hoopoe and the queen (27:15-44)

## Headline finding

**Sūrat al-Naml tells this as a story of checking and being checked, and the king is not the only one who checks.** Solomon speaks most (136 of the 320 spoken words, 9 turns), but the longest single speech is the hoopoe's (60 words), and the last words of the passage are the queen's (27:44). Solomon answers every gift with a God-ward line (27:15, 16, 19, 40) and every report with a test (*nẓr* ×5, *rjʿ* ×3). The only full basmala inside a surah's running text is the king's letter (27:30). The queen decides by council, then by test, then submits *with* Solomon, not *to* him. The text gives her no name, marriage or end.

## 1. Scene map and speaking turns (data-grounded)

Scenes: gift of knowledge (15-16); army and ant (17-19); roll-call and hoopoe (20-26); the test and letter (27-28); queen and council (29-35); envoy refused (36-37); the throne (38-40); disguise and glass floor (41-44).

- Solomon's longest turn is 25 words (27:36-37); the hoopoe's single turn is 60.
- Unnamed passive *qīla* (27:42, 44): 4 words. The letter's 9 words sit inside the queen's turn.
- **Last word.** Solomon's last line is the floor (27:44a, 5 words). The queen has the final speech (27:44b), and its last words are *Lord of the worlds*. God as narrator speaks after her nowhere.
- **Unnamed voices.** Solomon is the only speaker the text names. The ant, hoopoe, queen, ʿifrīt and the man with the Book are all types.

## 2. Gift → acknowledgement (data-grounded; the reading is [interpretive])

| Gift | His line |
|---|---|
| Knowledge, 27:15 | *al-ḥamdu lillāh* ... *faḍḍalanā ʿalā kathīrin min ʿibādihi l-muʾminīn* (with David) |
| Bird-speech, "of all things", 27:16 | *hādhā la-huwa l-faḍlu l-mubīn* (to the people) |
| The ant's warning, 27:19 | the prayer below |
| The throne arrives, 27:40 | *hādhā min faḍli rabbī li-yabluwanī* |

**27:19, phrase by phrase:**

> فَتَبَسَّمَ ضَاحِكࣰا مِّن قَوۡلِهَا وَقَالَ رَبِّ أَوۡزِعۡنِيٓ أَنۡ أَشۡكُرَ نِعۡمَتَكَ ٱلَّتِيٓ أَنۡعَمۡتَ عَلَيَّ وَعَلَىٰ وَٰلِدَيَّ وَأَنۡ أَعۡمَلَ صَٰلِحࣰا تَرۡضَىٰهُ وَأَدۡخِلۡنِي بِرَحۡمَتِكَ فِي عِبَادِكَ ٱلصَّٰلِحِينَ

- *Awziʿnī an ashkura*: he asks for the ability to thank, not for the thanks. *Wzʿ* occurs 5× in the Quran. Two are *awziʿnī* (here and the man at forty, 46:15). One is the army *yūzaʿūn*, held in ranks, two verses earlier (27:17). **[Interpretive]** the commander of the held asks to be held.
- *Niʿmatika ... ʿalayya wa-ʿalā wālidayya*: the favour includes his parents; thanks reaches back.
- Second request: deeds *you approve*. Third: *admit me by Your mercy among Your righteous servants*. The king asks for *ʿibād* status and ties entry to mercy, not to the deed.

**27:40:**

> فَلَمَّا رَءَاهُ مُسۡتَقِرًّا عِندَهُۥ قَالَ هَٰذَا مِن فَضۡلِ رَبِّي لِيَبۡلُوَنِيٓ ءَأَشۡكُرُ أَمۡ أَكۡفُرُۖ وَمَن شَكَرَ فَإِنَّمَا يَشۡكُرُ لِنَفۡسِهِۦۖ وَمَن كَفَرَ فَإِنَّ رَبِّي غَنِيࣱّ كَرِيمࣱ

- *Mustaqirran ʿindahu*: the throne is "settled by him".
- *Min faḍli rabbī*: the credit goes to the Lord, not to the man who brought it, and not to Solomon. He names no helper.
- *Li-yabluwanī*: the gift is a test; the options are *ashkuru am akfur*, with no third.
- Then the rule he draws: thanks benefits the thanker, ingratitude cannot harm the Lord, who is *ghaniyy karīm*. The first-person *shukr* in 27:19 and 27:40 are two of the Quran's three (the third is 46:15).

**[Interpretive]** the ʿifrīt offers with credentials (*innī ʿalayhi la-qawiyyun amīn*, 27:39); the Book-man offers none (27:40a). Solomon thanks after the second.

## 3. The ant (27:18-19)

- She knows the king's name and his soldiers (*Sulaymān wa-junūduhu*), and gives a command to her own people: *enter your dwellings*. Her reason is *lā yaḥṭimannakum*, "lest they crush you".
- *Wa hum lā yashʿurūn*: **[interpretive]** most naturally a *ḥāl* clause about the army, "while they do not perceive". It excuses them: the danger is carelessness, not malice. Grammatically it could instead describe her own kind, but the plural pronoun follows *Sulaymān wa-junūduhu* directly.
- *Fa-tabassama ḍāḥikan*: *tabassama* is a hapax. Smile, then laughter. **[Interpretive]** a king who hears himself called a hazard answers by asking to be made grateful. The text gives no reply to her.

## 4. The hoopoe (27:20-28)

> أَحَطتُ بِمَا لَمۡ تُحِطۡ بِهِۦ وَجِئۡتُكَ مِن سَبَإِۭ بِنَبَإࣲ يَقِينٍ

- **Inspection (27:20).** His first words are a question about his own eyes: *Why do I not see the hoopoe?* Only then: *or is he among the absent?*
- **Threat and exit (27:21).** Three verbs, each carrying the emphatic *lām-nūn*: *la-uʿadhdhibannahu*, *la-adhbaḥannahu*, *la-yaʾtiyannī bi-sulṭānin mubīn*. The third is sworn with the same force as the first two. **[Interpretive]** he binds himself to accept proof as firmly as to punish. *Sulṭān mubīn* is evidence.
- **27:22.** He stays *not long*. His first words are *aḥaṭtu bimā lam tuḥiṭ bihi*. A bird tells a king he has encompassed what the king has not, though the king said *we were given of all things* (27:16). **[Interpretive]** "all things" leaves room for the gap. The text says nothing of any punishment. Also: *min Sabaʾin bi-nabaʾin yaqīn* is a sound-play, and *Sabaʾ* is named only twice in the Quran (here and 34:15).
- **27:23-26.** Report first (*wajadtu* ×2): a woman who rules, *given of all things* (the same words as Solomon's, 27:16, 27:23), with *a great throne* (*ʿarsh ʿaẓīm*). Then the hoopoe's own theology: they prostrate to the sun, Satan decorated their deeds (27:24), and God is *he who brings out the hidden* (*al-khabʾ*, a hapax, 27:25, from a bird who has just brought a hidden thing out), *Lord of the Great Throne* (*rabb al-ʿarsh al-ʿaẓīm*). The queen's throne and God's carry the same adjective, in one speech. The prostration sign sits at 27:26, the speech's end.
- **27:27-28.** *Sa-nanẓuru a-ṣadaqta am kunta mina l-kādhibīn.* He neither accepts nor rejects. *Nẓr* has 5 tokens in 27:15-44 (27:27, 28, 33, 35, 41), all of them "see what comes back". The checking is done with a letter, and the bird is made the envoy.
- **What it teaches [interpretive].** The threat has an exit, a smaller creature can correct the king, and he still verifies. The queen does the same to him (27:35, echoing 27:28).

## 5. The letter and the basmala (27:29-31)

> إِنَّهُۥ مِن سُلَيۡمَٰنَ وَإِنَّهُۥ بِسۡمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ ﴿٣٠﴾ أَلَّا تَعۡلُواْ عَلَيَّ وَأۡتُونِي مُسۡلِمِينَ

- **Length, data-grounded:** the letter is 9 Arabic words: the basmala (4) and *allā taʿlū ʿalayya wa-ʾtūnī muslimīn* (5). The queen calls it *kitābun karīm* (27:29). The divergence at 27:29 is not mainly *karīm* (all four translators write "noble/honourable") but *ulqiya*: delivered, delivered, thrown, cast.
- **Script check** (diacritics stripped, tanwīn alif rejoined, *ٱ* → *ا*, all 6,236 ayahs): the full phrase *bismi llāhi l-raḥmāni l-raḥīm* occurs in exactly two ayahs of this dataset: **1:1 and 27:30**. No other surah's opening basmala is in the ayah text. So 27:30 is the only full basmala inside a surah's running text. Near-miss: 11:41 (Noah) has *bismi llāhi majrāhā*, not the full formula.
- The letter's request, *come to me as muslims*, uses the same word as 27:38 (the throne, *before they come to me muslimīn*) and 27:42. *Slm* in 27:15-44 has 4 tokens: *muslimīn* ×3 (27:31, 38, 42) and *aslamtu* (27:44).

## 6. The queen (27:29-35, 38-44)

- **Council (27:32).** *Mā kuntu qāṭiʿatan amran ḥattā tashhadūn*: "I was not one who cuts a matter until you witness". *Kuntu* plus a participle suggests habit. Translators differ (witness for me, without you, present with me, bear me witness). The council answers with force (*uli quwwatin wa-ulū baʾsin shadīd*) and returns the decision (*wa-l-amru ilayki*, 27:33). **[Interpretive]** she consults, and the council hands the decision back to her.
- **Judgment of kings (27:34).** *Inna l-mulūka idhā dakhalū qaryatan afsadūhā ... wa-kadhālika yafʿalūn.* Reading A: the last clause is **hers**: a general rule, continuing. Reading B: it is **God's aside**, confirming her. The text has no *qāla* between them. Grammar permits both. **[Interpretive]** A fits the next verse, where she acts on it (27:35). B makes her rule divinely endorsed, yet Solomon's reply in 27:37 uses her very word: *adhilla* (27:34, 27:37).
- **Gift (27:35).** A test, not a bribe: *fa-nāẓira bima yarjiʿu l-mursalūn*, "to see what the envoys return with". Solomon refuses it (27:36): *a-tumiddūnanī bi-mālin? fa-mā ātāniya llāhu khayrun mimmā ātākum*. *Hadiyya* (27:35-36) is the gift. He tells the sender *you rejoice in your gift*. The threat in 27:37 (hotspot, 0.724) is rendered much alike by all four.
- **The throne (27:38-40).** Above (section 2). The ʿifrīt promises *before you rise from your place*; the Book-man, *before your glance returns* (27:40). The text does not say how.
- **Disguise (27:41-42).** *Nakkirū lahā ʿarshahā*, "make her throne unrecognisable". *Nanẓur a-tahtadī*: another *nẓr* test; *lā yahtadūn* was the hoopoe's verdict on her people (27:24). Her answer, *ka-annahu huwa*, "as though it were it": she neither says yes nor no. **[Interpretive]** she is truthful in both directions.
- **27:42b** *wa-ūtīnā l-ʿilma min qablihā wa-kunnā muslimīn*: Solomon's side (the plural matches his *we*, 27:15-16) or the queen. *Qablihā* ("before her") suits an outside voice, so I read it as Solomon's.
- **27:43 (hotspot, 0.723).** *Wa-ṣaddahā mā kānat taʿbudu min dūni llāh*: grammatically *mā* is the subject: what she worshipped hindered her. All four translators follow that. The narrator (God), not Solomon, is the likelier voice, since the sentence explains the queen's inner state.
- **The floor (27:44).** She sees *lujja* and uncovers *sāqayhā* (shins only). Then: *rabbi innī ẓalamtu nafsī wa-aslamtu **maʿa** Sulaymān lillāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn.*

> قَالَتۡ رَبِّ إِنِّي ظَلَمۡتُ نَفۡسِي وَأَسۡلَمۡتُ مَعَ سُلَيۡمَٰنَ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلۡعَٰلَمِينَ

*Maʿa* ("with") puts her beside him, not under him; what she submits to is *lillāh*. She addresses God (*rabbi*), not Solomon. **[Interpretive]** the end of the contest is that nobody wins it: both are muslims before the same Lord. The ʿarsh of 27:23 is hers; the one of 27:26 is God's.

## 7. Negative space (data-grounded absences)

The text never gives: her name; any marriage or betrothal; her body beyond *sāqayhā*; any word of Solomon to her in praise or command after 27:44; what happened to the hoopoe; what happened to Sabaʾ's rule. **Tradition adds:** the name Bilqīs, a marriage, a purpose for the floor (that he wanted to see her legs), the Book-man as Āṣaf, and the hoopoe's reward. These come from outside the text. Sabaʾ appears again at 34:15, with the lost gardens, and the text never ties that people to this queen.

## For the story guide

- An ant warns her people, and the king smiles and asks to be made grateful.
- A king notices an absent bird, threatens it, then listens when it says *I know what you do not*, and checks the report.
- A queen asks her advisers, fears what kings do, tests with a gift, and is not impressed by wealth.
- Two helpers offer the throne; the one who brings it never speaks of himself. Solomon says: *this is a test*.
- She comes, sees glass for water, and says *I wronged myself*: she turns with Solomon to the Lord of the worlds.
- The Quran names no queen, no wedding, no end.
