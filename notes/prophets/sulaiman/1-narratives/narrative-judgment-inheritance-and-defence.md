# Solomon: judgment, inheritance and defence (21:78-80, 27:15-16, 2:101-103, David's frame, the lists)

## Headline finding

Solomon's first act is a gift of understanding, not a win over David: *fa-fahhamnāhā Sulaymān* (21:79), a verb from a root (*fhm*) found nowhere else. The very next clause protects David: *wa-kullan ātaynā ḥukman wa-ʿilmā*. Father and son are a pair (27:15, dual *qālā*), and the text sets "Solomon inherited David" beside a speech about knowledge and bounty, not treasure (27:16). David's frame is praise, work, one judgment gone wrong and forgiven (38:21-26). Solomon shares one formula with David and one with Job. 2:102 clears him of disbelief. No ayah ties *nabī* or *rasūl* to him.

---

## 1. The field case (21:78-80)

**Data-grounded.** 21:78: David and Solomon "judge" (dual *yaḥkumān*) concerning the *ḥarth* (crop-field), when a people's sheep *nafashat* in it (strayed and grazed, by the root's sense at night), and *We were witness to their judgment*. The text gives the field, the sheep, two judges, God as witness. It does not give the verdicts, the owners or the damage. Those details are **tradition** (tafsīr), outside the evidence base. Note *li-ḥukmihim* ("their judgment", plural pronoun) does not say whose: the two judges, or the parties.

> فَفَهَّمۡنَٰهَا سُلَيۡمَٰنَۚ وَكُلًّا ءَاتَيۡنَا حُكۡمࣰا وَعِلۡمࣰاۚ (21:79)
> "So We made Solomon understand it, and to each We gave judgment and knowledge."

- **Hapax.** The root *fhm* has one token in the morphology file: *fa-fahhamnāhā*, form II (causative). Nothing else in the Quran to compare it with.
- **What a once-only verb signals [interpretive].** The text had its ordinary verb *ʿallama* (used for David at 21:80, and passively of Solomon at 27:16). It chose a verb used nowhere else, for one moment: understanding of *this case*, a particular act of God's, not a standing trait. The feminine *-hā* points to the matter, not to anyone's verdict.
- **How David is not demoted.**
  1. *Kullan* (each) is object of *ātaynā*: both receive the same pair.
  2. *Ḥukman wa-ʿilmā* is a formula, found 4 times: Joseph (12:22), Lot (21:74), Moses (28:14), and here. It is the text's gift to a chosen man; here two men share one clause.
  3. God was witness to the judgments (21:78), with no word of correction.
  4. The very next words return to David: mountains and birds glorify (*yusabbiḥna*) with him.
- **David's gifts, 21:80.** *wa-ʿallamnāhu ṣanʿata labūsin lakum li-tuḥṣinakum min baʾsikum; fa-hal antum shākirūn.* The armour is "for you", to protect others, and the closing question, *will you then be grateful?*, goes to them. [interpretive] Thanks is asked of those who benefit from the skill.

## 2. Father and son (27:15-16)

> وَلَقَدۡ ءَاتَيۡنَا دَاوُۥدَ وَسُلَيۡمَٰنَ عِلۡمࣰاۖ وَقَالَا ٱلۡحَمۡدُ لِلَّهِ ٱلَّذِي فَضَّلَنَا عَلَىٰ كَثِيرࣲ مِّنۡ عِبَادِهِ ٱلۡمُؤۡمِنِينَ (27:15)
> وَوَرِثَ سُلَيۡمَٰنُ دَاوُۥدَۖ وَقَالَ يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ عُلِّمۡنَا مَنطِقَ ٱلطَّيۡرِ وَأُوتِينَا مِن كُلِّ شَيۡءٍۖ (27:16)

**Data-grounded.**
- 27:15: one gift, *ʿilm* (indefinite), to two men, and one dual speech, *al-ḥamdu li-llāh*. The verb is *faḍḍalanā*: preferred "over many of His *believing* servants", so the comparison group is believers.
- 27:16 switches to singular *wa-qāla*: Solomon alone, passive *ʿullimnā … ūtīnā*, no teacher named.
- ***Wrth* (inherit).** 35 tokens in the Quran, nearly all about property and law (4:11, 4:12, 4:176) or God as inheritor (19:40). The two prophet cases: Zechariah asks for an heir *yarithunī wa-yarithu min āli Yaʿqūb* (19:6), alongside his fear of the *mawālī* (relatives) after him (19:5); and 27:16, where the object is a **person**, *Dāwūd*, not a thing. Zechariah's request, with its "House of Jacob", is about a line, not money.
- **What follows straight after:** *wa-qāla yā ayyuhā l-nās ʿullimnā manṭiq al-ṭayr wa-ūtīnā min kulli shayʾ … hādhā huwa l-faḍl al-mubīn*. The verse moves from inheriting to knowledge and *faḍl*.
- **Inherited what? [interpretive].** Not stated. The adjacent clause, 27:15's shared *ʿilm*, and David's mountains and birds (21:79, 34:10) all lean to *David's standing, knowledge and gifts*; kingship is not excluded by the grammar but is not asserted either. Do not write "inherited the kingdom" as text.
- **Who is "we" in *ʿullimnā*? [interpretive].** For a royal "we": *yā ayyuhā l-nās* is a king addressing his people, and 27:19 moves between "I" and his parents (*waladayya*). For an inclusive "we": the dual of 27:15, one verse earlier. David is already gone ("inherited"), so inclusion would mean a shared house, not a present David. Context favours **royal "we" spoken by the heir of a house**; either way, the next words name the source as *faḍl*, not skill.

## 3. David's frame and what Solomon receives

**Data-grounded.** David's story, in four shapes:
- **2:251**: kills Goliath; God gives *al-mulk wa-l-ḥikma* and *ʿallamahu mimmā yashāʾ*. (Saheeh renders *al-ḥikma* as "prophethood", and Khattab as "wisdom". The Arabic is "wisdom".)
- **34:10-11**: *faḍl* from God; mountains and birds *awwibī* with him; iron made soft; the command to make full mail, then the plural *waʿmalū ṣāliḥā*, "work righteousness", to the household.
- **38:17-20**: *dhā l-ayd, innahu awwāb*; mountains and birds; strengthened kingdom; *al-ḥikma wa-faṣl al-khiṭāb*.
- **38:21-26**: two litigants; one ewe against ninety-nine; he rules; *ẓanna Dāwūdu annamā fatannāhu*; *fa-staghfara rabbahu wa-kharra rākiʿan wa-anāb*; *fa-ghafarnā lahu dhālik*; *la-zulfā wa-ḥusna maʾāb*; then:

> يَٰدَاوُۥدُ إِنَّا جَعَلۡنَٰكَ خَلِيفَةࣰ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ فَٱحۡكُم بَيۡنَ ٱلنَّاسِ بِٱلۡحَقِّ وَلَا تَتَّبِعِ ٱلۡهَوَىٰ (38:26)

- *Khalīfa* in the singular occurs twice in the Quran: Adam (2:30) and David (38:26).
- **Shared formulas (counted by morphology and normalised string search).**
  - *la-zulfā wa-ḥusna maʾāb*: **38:25 (David) and 38:40 (Solomon)** only. Bare *zulfā* is 34:37 and 39:3.
  - *niʿma l-ʿabd innahu awwāb*: **38:30 (Solomon) and 38:44 (Job)** only. David's 38:17 is *dhā l-ayd, innahu awwāb*, without *niʿma*.
  - So the two shared formulas run David-Solomon and Solomon-Job; no phrase is common to all three. *Awwāb* itself (singular) is 38:17, 38:19, 38:30, 38:44, 50:32.
- **What Solomon receives [interpretive].** (a) A father who was himself *given*: *wa-wahabnā li-Dāwūda Sulaymān* (38:30), with Solomon as God's gift to David. (b) The habit of *awwāb*: David's turning follows a failed judgment (38:24); Solomon's is stated before any trial. (c) The charge of 38:26 (judge by truth, not desire) seen from the other side: in 21:78-79 it is a judgment that goes rightly. (d) The household task *iʿmalū āla Dāwūd shukrā* (34:13) and the praise of 27:15.

## 4. The defence (2:101-103)

> وَٱتَّبَعُواْ مَا تَتۡلُواْ ٱلشَّيَٰطِينُ عَلَىٰ مُلۡكِ سُلَيۡمَٰنَۖ وَمَا كَفَرَ سُلَيۡمَٰنُ وَلَٰكِنَّ ٱلشَّيَٰطِينَ كَفَرُواْ (2:102)

**Data-grounded.**
- 2:101: a party given the Book *threw it behind their backs* when a *rasūl* came confirming what they had. 2:102 begins *wa-ttabaʿū*: the abandoned Book is replaced by something else followed.
- ***ʿalā mulki Sulaymān*: two readings.** **(a) "during his reign"**: the devils recited in his time (21:82, 34:12-13 place devils and jinn under him). **(b) "against/about his kingdom"**: reciting something falsely onto his reign. Both fit; (b) makes the next clause an answer to a charge.
- ***Wa-mā unzila ʿalā l-malakayn*: relative vs negative.** The morphology file tags this *mā* as relative; the *mā* of *wa-mā kafara* and of *wa-mā yuʿallimāni* are negative.
  - **Relative:** "they followed what the devils recited … and what was sent down to the two angels at Babylon". The angels become a second source of what people learn; the sentence has two sources, and the angels' own words (*innamā naḥnu fitna fa-lā takfur*) are a warning attached to theirs.
  - **Negative:** "the devils disbelieved, teaching people sorcery, **and nothing was sent down** to the two angels". The sentence denies that sorcery came from heaven at all. Hārūt and Mārūt remain, as figures who teach only after the warning.
  - Under both, the warning *we are only a trial, so do not disbelieve* is the angels' disclaimer: they present themselves as a test, not a gift.
- **What accusation does it answer? [interpretive, from the text only].** *Wa-mā kafara Sulaymān* denies kufr to him by name, so it presupposes that kufr was alleged; *al-siḥr* names what. The text points to **a charge that Solomon's power over wind, jinn and devils was sorcery**, but says neither who made it nor when. The Quran's own account is that all of it ran *bi-idhni rabbihi* (34:12) and *bi-amrihi* (21:81), never by craft.
- Zuhra, the well and the angels' punishment are tradition. They are not in the verse.

## 5. Prophethood status

**Data-grounded.** Script check on the 16 ayahs where he is named (lemma search in `words.jsonl`): in the roots *nbʾ* and *rsl* the only hit in any of them is *al-nabiyyīn* at 4:163, and that word belongs to the list's opening ("Noah and the prophets after him"). It is not tied to Solomon. In all other ayahs where he is named there is no *nabī*, no *rasūl*.
- **4:163**: *awḥaynā ilā* … *wa-Sulaymān*: he is among those to whom revelation went. David is set apart: *wa-ātaynā Dāwūda zabūrā*.
- **6:84-89**: *wa-min dhurriyyatihi Dāwūd wa-Sulaymān* (Noah's line); *wa-kadhālika najzī l-muḥsinīn* (6:84); *ulāʾika lladhīna ātaynāhum al-kitāb wa-l-ḥukm wa-l-nubuwwa* (6:89); *ulāʾika lladhīna hadā llāh fa-bi-hudāhumu qtadih* (6:90). His title is by membership.
- **17:55** (David): *faḍḍalnā baʿḍ al-nabiyyīn ʿalā baʿḍ wa-ātaynā Dāwūda zabūrā*. David is attached to a book and to *nabiyyīn* by the verse's neighbouring sentence; Solomon has no book.
- Series census: Solomon, David, Job, Elisha and Zechariah are the five titled only through the lists.
- **[interpretive]** What the text prefers for him: *ʿabd* (38:30), *awwāb*, *ḥukm*, *ʿilm*, *mulk*.

## 6. Negative space

| Tradition adds | What the text gives |
|---|---|
| Solomon's **mother**; Bathsheba | No mother named. His only parent is *Dāwūd* (27:16, 38:30); in 27:19 he thanks for favour on *wālidayya* (his parents), unnamed. |
| David's trial in tradition | 38:21-25: two litigants, one ewe, ninety-nine, a ruling, the thought of being tried, forgiveness. No woman, no husband. |
| **The Temple** | None in the passages read. The building words are the jinn's work (in 34:13, outside this file's range). |
| **Age; reign length** | None. No years given. |

## For the story guide

- The first story: a case about sheep in a field. God was present, helped Solomon see it, and says both men were given judgment and knowledge. David is not made smaller.
- Father and son praise together: *al-ḥamdu li-llāh*, 27:15. Solomon then speaks of inheriting David, in the same breath as knowledge and God's bounty.
- David's own pattern: strength, praise, work, one wrong turn, forgiveness, and a charge to judge by truth. Solomon belongs to that house.
- Two phrases link him: "an excellent servant, always turning back" (with Job) and "nearness and a good return" (with David).
- 2:102 clears his name: Solomon did not disbelieve; the devils did. The text does not say who accused him.
- Not in the text: his mother, a Temple, his age, the details of David's fall.
