# Prophets: a series

One investigation per prophet, each in its own folder (`notes/prophets/<name>/`), all built from the
Arabic text alone. The series census is `scripts/build_prophets_census.py` → `data/structural/prophets-census.json`.

## How many prophets does the Quran name?

**25 people traditionally counted as prophets are named in the Quran.** Lists that say 26 or more add
someone the text never calls a prophet. The census tests each name against what the text itself says:

- **18 are given the title in so many words.** The title is *nabī*, *rasūl*, or *among the messengers*:
  - Idrīs (19:56), Noah (7:61), Hūd (26:125), Ṣāliḥ (26:143), Abraham (19:41), Lot (37:133);
  - Ishmael (19:54), Isaac (37:112), Jacob (19:49), Joseph (40:34, implied), Shuʿayb (26:178);
  - Moses (19:51), Aaron (19:53), Elijah (37:123), Jonah (37:139), John (3:39), Jesus (19:30), Muḥammad (33:40).
- **5 hold it only through the lists.** These are 4:163 (*We revealed to …*) and 6:83-86, which 6:89 closes
  with *those are the ones We gave the Book, judgment and prophethood*. The five are Job, David, Solomon,
  Elisha and Zechariah. David also stands in 17:55 among *the prophets*.
- **2 are never titled.** Adam is *chosen* (3:33) and made a *khalīfa* (2:30). Dhū l-Kifl is *among the
  patient* (21:85) and *among the best* (38:48).
- **Often added, never titled.** Luqmān is *given wisdom* (31:12). Dhū l-Qarnayn is in 18:83-98. ʿUzayr is
  named only in a claim the verse rejects (9:30). The guide of Moses in 18:65 is never named.
- **The Quran says the list is not complete.** *Messengers We have told you about, and messengers We
  have not* (4:164, 40:78).
