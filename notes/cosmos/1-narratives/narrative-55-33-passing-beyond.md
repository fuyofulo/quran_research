# 55:33 and the limits of reaching beyond

## Headline finding (100 words)

55:33's verb `tanfudhū` (root `nf*`) means *pass through/penetrate the far side of*, not "travel to" — and this root occurs nowhere else in the Quran (3/3 occurrences, all inside 55:33 itself). The permission clause `illā bi-sulṭān` uses a word that means *warrant/authority/proof* in the clear majority of its 39 Quranic occurrences; none of the four translations checked render it "power" here. The verse sits inside a judgment refrain (55:31 "We shall attend to you") and is followed at 55:35 by fire and smoke sent against the two species. Every other passage on reaching the heavens — 72:8-9, 15:14-18, 37:6-10, 6:35, 17:93 — depicts repulsion or hypothetical failure, never achieved ascent.

## 1. Sūrah 55:33 — the verse itself

| | Arabic | Saheeh | Khattab | Arberry | Pickthall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 55:33 | يَٰمَعۡشَرَ ٱلۡجِنِّ وَٱلۡإِنسِ إِنِ ٱسۡتَطَعۡتُمۡ أَن تَنفُذُواْ مِنۡ أَقۡطَارِ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِ فَٱنفُذُواْ لَا تَنفُذُونَ إِلَّا بِسُلۡطَٰنࣲ | "O company of jinn and mankind, if you are able to pass beyond the regions of the heavens and the earth, then pass. You will not pass except by authority [from Allah]." | "If you can penetrate beyond the realms of the heavens and the earth, then do so. But you cannot do that without Our authority" | "if you are able to pass through the confines of heaven and earth, pass through them! You shall not pass through except with an authority." | "if ye have power to penetrate (all) regions of the heavens and the earth, then penetrate (them)! Ye will never penetrate them save with (Our) sanction." |

**`yā maʿshara al-jinni wa-l-ins`** — the address names both accountable, reasoning species together. This is not a verse about humans alone; it opens onto 55:31's "We shall attend to you, O two burdened ones [al-thaqalān]," a shared reckoning, not a shared research programme.

**`tanfudhū` — root `nf*` [data-grounded].** Grepping the full morphology set (77,429 words): `nf*` occurs exactly **3 times in the whole Quran, and all three are inside 55:33 itself** — `tanfudhū` (word 7), `fa-nfudhū` (word 12), `tanfudhūna` (word 14). Not a root scattered across a "passage" — a root used in this one verse and nowhere else. The sense is *to pierce through and come out the far side* — an arrow through a target, light through glass — presupposing a bounded container with a far edge to break through, not a destination to arrive at. "Passing beyond the confines" is categorically different from "traveling to" a place within them; the Quran never applies this verb to going to the moon, a planet, or a star — only to breaking out of the `aqṭār` (regions/zones/diameters) of the heavens and earth as a totality.

**`illā bi-sulṭān` — the contested word [data-grounded distribution, interpretive framing].** Root `slT`, 39 occurrences (37 as the noun `sulṭān`, 2 as the verb `sallaṭa`, "to give dominion over"). The dominant pattern (~20 of 37) is the recurring formula "God has not sent down/given for this any `sulṭān`" or "bring a clear `sulṭān`" — theological/legal *warrant*, proof a claim rests on, evidence a messenger brings (3:151, 6:81, 7:33, 7:71, 10:68, 12:40, 22:71, 30:35, 40:35, 40:56, 53:23; positively for Moses/messengers at 4:153, 11:96, 14:10, 23:45, 40:23, 44:19, 51:38). A second cluster (14:22, 15:42, 16:99-100, 17:65, 34:21, 37:30) has Satan or misleaders told they hold **no** `sulṭān` over the faithful — closer to "sway/dominion," but bounded influence, not physical force. Only two occurrences plainly denote worldly power/kingship: 28:35 ("supremacy") and 69:29, Pharaoh's "gone from me is my `sultāniyah`" at his death.

**In the four translations of 55:33 itself, none render `sulṭān` as "power."** Saheeh, Khattab, and Arberry all say "authority"; Pickthall says "sanction" — all four cluster on the warrant/permission sense. (Pickthall's "power" in this verse translates a different word, `istaṭaʿtum` — "if you are able" — not `sulṭān`.) **The popular "you'll need rocket power/technology" reading of 55:33 depends on taking `sulṭān` as physical force — the minority sense of the word across the Quran, and a sense none of these four translations apply to this verse.** A lexical observation, not a polemic.

**55:35 — what follows immediately.**

| | Arabic | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| 55:35 | يُرۡسَلُ عَلَيۡكُمَا شُوَاظࣱ مِّن نَّارࣲ وَنُحَاسࣱ فَلَا تَنتَصِرَانِ | "There will be sent upon you a flame of fire and smoke, and you will not defend yourselves." (Saheeh) |

**Reading 55:31-36 as a unit:** 31 opens with a judgment announcement ("We shall attend to you/dispose of you, O two burdened ones") aimed at jinn and humans together. 32 is the refrain ("which of your Lord's favors will you deny"). 33 issues the challenge to pass beyond the confines and denies it's possible without warrant. 34 repeats the refrain. 35 answers with fire and smoke sent against the two species, who cannot defend themselves. 36 repeats the refrain again. **The rhetorical mode of 55:33 is neither a scientific prediction nor a genuine invitation to explore — it is a taunt embedded in a judgment scene**, parallel to the Quran's other "bring a `sulṭān`" challenges, always addressed to those who cannot. The passage does not describe an achievable feat gated behind future technology; it describes an escape from divine reckoning the two species lack standing to attempt, immediately followed by the penalty for those judged.

## 2. The jinn at the gates of heaven — 72:8-9

| | Arabic | Saheeh |
|---|---|---|
| 72:8 | وَأَنَّا لَمَسۡنَا ٱلسَّمَآءَ فَوَجَدۡنَٰهَا مُلِئَتۡ حَرَسࣰا شَدِيدࣰا وَشُهُبࣰا | "And we have sought [to reach] the heaven but found it filled with powerful guards and burning flames." |
| 72:9 | وَأَنَّا كُنَّا نَقۡعُدُ مِنۡهَا مَقَٰعِدَ لِلسَّمۡعِ فَمَن يَسۡتَمِعِ ٱلۡأٓنَ يَجِدۡ لَهُۥ شِهَابࣰا رَّصَدࣰا | "And we used to sit therein in positions for hearing, but whoever listens now will find a burning flame lying in wait for him." |

**[data-grounded]** The Quran's one sustained first-person account of a non-human rational species approaching the heavens is an account of being repelled. The jinn narrator states access *changed*: `kunnā naqʿudu` — "we **used to** sit" (past-habitual), against `fa-man yastamiʿi al-āna` — "whoever listens **now**." Before, stations for listening; now, a flame waiting. 72:10 follows with the jinn admitting they don't know whether this change signals harm or guidance for those on earth — they read the closing-off as an event, not a design feature they'd always known.

The claim recurs three more times, cited not paneled: **15:16-18** (heaven guarded from every expelled devil, except the one who steals a hearing and is pursued by a clear flame), **37:6-10** (the lowest heaven's adornment doubles as protection; devils pelted from every side, driven off, except one who snatches a fragment and is pursued by a piercing flame), **67:5** (stars made into missiles against devils). All four describe the same architecture — a barrier, guards, pursuit-fire for whoever tries to listen in. None describe successful entry.

## 3. 15:14-15 — the strongest anti-triumphalist verse in the set

| | Arabic | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| 15:14 | وَلَوۡ فَتَحۡنَا عَلَيۡهِم بَابࣰا مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ فَظَلُّواْ فِيهِ يَعۡرُجُونَ | "And [even] if We opened to them a gate from the heaven and they continued therein to ascend," (Saheeh) |
| 15:15 | لَقَالُوٓاْ إِنَّمَا سُكِّرَتۡ أَبۡصَٰرُنَا بَلۡ نَحۡنُ قَوۡمࣱ مَّسۡحُورُونَ | "They would say, 'Our eyes have only been dazzled. Rather, we are a people affected by magic.'" |

**[interpretive, but text-driven]** Worth isolating because it cuts against triumphalist and anti-triumphalist readings alike. It is hypothetical (`law fataḥnā`, "if We had opened") and counterfactual, addressed to the Prophet's disbelieving opponents amid their demand for proof. Read plainly: the Quran here *imagines* the very thing the space-travel reading of 55:33 hopes to celebrate — humans physically ascending, unimpeded, through an opened gate — and says the response would not be belief but denial: "our eyes have been dazzled, we are bewitched." The verse doesn't say ascent is impossible — it grants the premise, `fa-ẓallū fīhi yaʿrujūn`, "they kept ascending" — it's a statement about a psychology for which no physical evidence, including passage into the sky, would change the conclusion. Neither a ban on space travel nor a prophecy of it; its subject is disbelief, not astronautics.

## 4. The ladder and the demanded ascent — 6:35, 17:93

| | Arabic | Saheeh |
|---|---|---|
| 17:93 | أَوۡ يَكُونَ لَكَ بَيۡتࣱ مِّن زُخۡرُفٍ أَوۡ تَرۡقَىٰ فِي ٱلسَّمَآءِ ... قُلۡ سُبۡحَٰنَ رَبِّي هَلۡ كُنتُ إِلَّا بَشَرࣰا رَّسُولࣰا | "...or you ascend into the sky. And [even then], we will not believe in your ascension until you bring down to us a book we may read." Say, "Exalted is my Lord! Was I ever but a human messenger?" |

17:93 is one item in a list of impossible demands piled on the Prophet (a spring, a garden, a house of gold) — `tarqā fī al-samāʾ`, "ascend into the sky," among them — and even conceding it, they say they still wouldn't believe without a readable book descending too. The answer isn't about ascent's possibility; it's `hal kuntu illā basharan rasūlan` — "am I anything but a human messenger" — a refusal of the framing that a messenger must perform physical feats. **6:35** repeats the pattern: a tunnel into the earth or `sullaman fī al-samāʾ`, "a ladder into the sky," offered hypothetically as something the Prophet could seek — dismissed, because God could have simply willed universal guidance instead. In both, human ascent is not achieved, forbidden, or predicted — it is a **hypothetical raised by scoffers and answered by redirecting to the Prophet's mortal, non-miracle-working status.** 17:94-95 continues the move: people refused to believe *because* a human was sent, and the reply is that had angels lived on earth, an angel would have been sent instead — the argument is about the category of messenger, not the physics of flight.

## 5. Synthesis — what 55:33 is actually doing

**Does the Quran forbid, permit, predict, or simply not address travelling beyond the earth?** [interpretive] Strictly: none of these as a direct proposition. 55:33 poses a rhetorical challenge inside a judgment scene, not a technical claim about spaceflight; its answer ("not without `sulṭān`") is about warrant before God, not fuel or engineering. Sūrah 55 as a whole is a litany of favors bracketed by the refrain "which of your Lord's favors will you deny" — 55:33 sits in the section addressed to jinn and humans about their inability to escape the reckoning of 55:31, not in any section about the natural world's mechanics (contrast the earlier verses on sun, moon, stars, fruit, rivers, unambiguously in the favors register). **Reading 55:33 as a space-travel prophecy requires lifting it out of a judgment passage and re-reading its `nf*`-verb — "breaking through a boundary" — as "traveling to," and its `sulṭān` — "warrant" — as "technological power,"** against both the verse's own rare verb and the majority sense of its key noun elsewhere. That doesn't make the popular reading foolish — the words are rich enough to carry it, and 72:8-9, 15:16-18, and 37:6-10 do independently establish that the heavens are barred and guarded, thematically adjacent to the reading's interest in cosmic limits. But it is an extrapolation, and should be named as one, not presented as the verse's plain sense.

## What this settles

- `nf*` (55:33's verb) occurs three times in the Quran, all in this one verse — not a general travel-verb; its sense is "break through a boundary," not "journey to a destination."
- `sulṭān`, the gating word, means warrant/authority/proof in the clear majority of its 39 occurrences; none of the four translations checked render it "power" in 55:33.
- The verse's immediate context (55:31, 55:35) is a judgment scene with fire sent against the two species — a taunt inside a reckoning, not an open scientific challenge.
- Every other passage on reaching the heavens (72:8-9, 15:14-18, 37:6-10, 67:5, 6:35, 17:93-95) shows repulsion, hypothetical failure, or a demand dismissed by redirecting to the Prophet's mortal status — never achieved, celebrated ascent.
- The space-travel reading of 55:33 is a coherent extrapolation from rich vocabulary, not the verse's plain sense; the plain sense is about accountability, not aerospace.
