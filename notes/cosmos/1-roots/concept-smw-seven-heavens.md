# `smw` — the heavens, and the seven

## Headline finding (100 words)

Singular `samāʾ` (120 occurrences) and plural `samāwāt` (190 occurrences) are used for
systematically different things: singular co-occurs with rain-descent (`nzl`, 35 shared words) and
water (`mwh`, 22), plural co-occurs with creation/dominion vocabulary (`xlq` 64, `mlk` 35, `sbE` 5,
never `mTr`/`mwh`/`Tyr` more than once). Stars are stated, three times, to be placed only in "the
lowest heaven" (`al-samāʾ al-dunyā`, sing.) — and **no named object is assigned to any *specific*
heaven other than the lowest** (71:16 does place the moon `fīhinna`, `3FP`, in the seven
collectively, without distributing it — correction applied 2026-08-26). The formula "what
is in the heavens and earth" uses `mā` 40 times but `man` (rational-being pronoun) 13 times as a
genuine "whoever" formula, plus 2 more where `man` names God, not inhabitants.

## Root stats (data-grounded)

`smw` — سمو — 381 occurrences, 6 lemmas, 352 ayahs, 81/114 surahs (268 Meccan / 113 Medinan).
Lemma breakdown: `samāʾ` (heaven/sky) 310, `ism` (name) 39, `musammā` (appointed/fixed) 21, `sammā`
(named, verb) 8, `samiyy` (namesake) 2, `tasmiya` (naming) 1. The brief's warning holds: `ism` is a
different sense entirely and is excluded below. `sbE` — سبع — 28 occurrences, 3 lemmas, 24 ayahs,
16/114 surahs (22 Meccan / 6 Medinan).

## 1. Singular vs plural — systematic, not stylistic

Of 310 `samāʾ` occurrences: **120 singular forms, 190 plural forms**, with only 7 ayahs containing
both (2:29, 2:164, 14:32, 27:60, 31:10, 41:12, 57:4 — transition verses, e.g. 41:12's "He determined
them as seven heavens... and adorned **the [singular] nearest heaven** with lamps").

Co-occurring roots in singular-only ayahs (109) vs plural-only ayahs (181), by word count:

| Root | Sense | Singular ayahs | Plural ayahs |
|---|---|---|---|
| `nzl` | send down | 35 | 5 |
| `mwh` | water | 22 | 2 |
| `rzq` | provision | 10 | 3 |
| `xlq` | create | 6 | 64 |
| `sbE` | seven | 0 | 5 |
| `mlk` | dominion | (not top-25) | 35 |
| `kll` | all/every | (not top-25) | 37 |

The pattern is clean: **singular `samāʾ` is the thing rain, water, and provision come down from —
the sky as observed phenomenon.** Rain-descent verses (2:22, 2:164, 14:32, 16:65, 45:5, etc.) use
singular every time; I found zero cases of `nzl al-samāwāt` (plural) for rain. **Plural `samāwāt` is
the thing God *created*, structured into seven, and holds *dominion* (`mulk`) over** — cosmogony and
sovereignty vocabulary, not weather vocabulary. Birds (`Tyr`) appear with singular twice (16:79,
22:31: birds "held" in "the [singular] sky"), never with plural. Stars/lamps verses (below) also
default to singular or explicitly to "the lowest [of the plural] heaven."

**Verdict: yes, the distinction is systematic.** This constrains what "creatures in the heavens"
(question in the shared brief) could mean — if it's about the plural structural heavens, it is not
drawing on the same vocabulary field as birds-in-the-sky or rain-from-the-sky.

## 2. The seven — catalogue

All nine traditionally-cited "seven heavens" verses, with a filter check: **23:17 and 78:12 use
`sabʿ` but never actually contain the word `samāʾ`/`samāwāt`** — they describe "seven paths"
(`ṭarāʾiq`, 23:17) and "seven strong [things] built above you" (78:12) without naming them heavens
directly; the identification is entirely contextual (both follow creation-of-humanity passages and
parallel the other seven-heaven verses in form). The other seven verses use `smw` directly.

| Ref | Vocabulary | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 2:29 | `sawwāhunna sabʿa samāwāt` | leveled/fashioned them into seven heavens |
| 17:44 | `al-samāwātu al-sabʿu` | the seven heavens (+ earth) glorify God |
| 23:17 | `sabʿa ṭarāʾiqa` | seven paths/ways, above you (no `smw`) |
| 23:86 | `rabbu al-samāwāti al-sabʿi` | Lord *of* the seven heavens (+ Throne) |
| 41:12 | `qaḍāhunna sabʿa samāwātin ... ṭibāqan` (67:3, not here) | decreed them as seven heavens in two days |
| 65:12 | `khalaqa sabʿa samāwātin` | created seven heavens, "and of the earth their like" |
| 67:3 | `sabʿa samāwātin **ṭibāqan**` | seven heavens **in layers/tiers** |
| 71:15 | `sabʿa samāwātin **ṭibāqan**` | seven heavens **in layers/tiers** (same phrase) |
| 78:12 | `sabʿan **shidādan**` | seven **strong/mighty** [things], above you (no `smw`) |

**What the vocabulary supports:** `ṭibāqan` (67:3, 71:15) is the load-bearing structural word — it
means stacked/superimposed layers, and both translators' consensus ("layers," "one upon another,"
"in harmony"/"in harmony" for Pickthall is a looser rendering) converges on a tiered stack. `shidādan`
(78:12) describes them as physically robust/firm, consistent with a roof-like structure (`saqf
maḥfūẓ`, "a protected ceiling," appears elsewhere at 21:32 for the sky generally, singular). 23:17's
`ṭarāʾiq` ("paths/ways/tracts") is the most ambiguous term — it can mean layered strata (a "path" as
a level one passes through) but does not by itself specify orbits or roofs. **No verse in this set
uses astronomical orbit-vocabulary; the textual metaphor is architectural (building, layering,
roofing), not orbital.** That is a real constraint on translation choices like "orbits."

**Panel — 67:3**: *alladhī khalaqa sabʿa samāwātin ṭibāqan* — "who created seven heavens in
layers" (Saheeh) / "one above the other" (Khattab) / "one upon another" (Arberry) / "in harmony"
(Pickthall — an outlier, softening the stacking sense).

**Panel — 78:12**: *wa-banaynā fawqakum sabʿan shidādan* — "constructed above you seven strong
[heavens]" — all four translators agree on "strong/mighty"; none reads it as "vast." Firmness, not
size, is what `shidād` conveys.

## 3. 41:12 — stars confined to the lowest heaven, verified

**Panel — 41:12**: `fa-qaḍāhunna sabʿa samāwātin fī yawmayn wa-awḥā fī kulli samāʾin amrahā
wa-zayyannā al-samāʾa al-dunyā bi-maṣābīḥa` — "So He decreed them as seven heavens in two days and
inspired in each heaven its command/affair. And We adorned **the lowest heaven** with lamps." Two
claims are fused: (a) *each* heaven receives its own `amr` — the only content common to all seven;
(b) *only the lowest* receives lamps. Saheeh/Khattab/Pickthall all render `al-samāʾa al-dunyā` as
"the nearest/lowest heaven"; no translator disputes that reading.

Cross-checked against three parallel verses:

- **67:5** — `zayyannā al-samāʾa al-dunyā bi-maṣābīḥa` — identical construction: lamps in the lowest
  heaven, made into missiles against devils.
- **37:6** — `zayyannā al-samāʾa al-dunyā bi-zīnatin al-kawākib` — "adorned the nearest heaven with
  an adornment of stars/planets" — again explicitly the lowest heaven, and here the word is
  `kawākib` (stars/planets) directly.
- **15:16** — `jaʿalnā fī al-samāʾi burūjan` — uses plain **singular** `al-samāʾ` (not "lowest," but
  singular, which per §1 is the sky-as-observed sense) for constellations.

**Verified: every verse that places stars, constellations, or lamps does so in either the singular
sky or explicitly "the lowest heaven" of the seven.**

**Correction (2026-08-26).** An earlier version of this file said no visible celestial object is
placed in heavens 2 through 7. That is too strong. **71:16 — `wa-jaʿala al-qamara fīhinna nūran` —
places the moon `fīhinna`, `3FP` (verified), pointing back to 71:15's seven heavens: in them,
collectively, with no distribution to any one of them.** The defensible negative is narrower and
survives checking: **no verse assigns any named object to a *specific* heaven other than the
lowest.** The consequence is unchanged — the text describes no located content for six of the seven
heavens beyond the bare fact that each received "its command" (41:12). Whatever
is meant by the other six, the Quran gives no inventory of their contents.

## 4. Inventory — what IS said to be in the heavens

- **Angels** — widely; 41 of 191 `mlk`-root ayahs co-occur with plural `samāwāt` (e.g. 2:30-34,
  35:1, 16:2, 97:4). The dominant explicit heavenly population.
- **God's dominion (`mulk`)** — "to Him belongs the dominion of the heavens and earth" recurs as a
  formula (2:107, and dozens more) — sovereignty language, not an inhabitant claim.
- **The `amr`** — descends "between them" (65:12), assigned individually "in each heaven" (41:12).
  Never described in content beyond the word itself.
- **`Rizq` (provision)** — 51:22: `wa-fī al-samāʾi rizqukum wa-mā tūʿadūn`, "and in **the [singular]**
  heaven is your provision." Singular form, consistent with §1: provision-from-the-sky is the
  rain/sustenance sense, not a plural-structural claim.
- **Lamps/stars/constellations** — lowest heaven only (§3).
- **`dābba` (creatures)** — 42:29 alone: `wa-mā baththa fīhimā min dābba`, "what He scattered in
  them both [the dual — heavens AND earth] of creatures," closing with "He is able to gather them."
  This is the one and only place `dābba` is grammatically placed in the heavens (dual pronoun), and
  it is the load-bearing verse for the sibling `dbb` file — flagged here, not re-derived.
- **"The guarded tablet" — NOT found.** `lwH` (tablet) and `Hfz` (guard/preserve) roots **never
  co-occur with `smw` in any ayah** (0 hits each). The lawḥ maḥfūẓ (85:22) exists in the Quran but
  is never textually located "in the heaven(s)" — that association is tafsīr, not co-location.

**What is NOT listed:** no plants, no seas, no mountains (all earth-bound), no humans, no jinn by
name, and critically — nothing at all is specified for six of the seven heavens beyond "its `amr`."

## 5. `mā` vs `man` — the formula audit (done exhaustively)

I traced every `[mā/man] fī al-samāwāt(i)` construction in the corpus by walking the word sequence
before each `samāʾ`/`samāwāt` token.

**Result: 40 instances of `mā` (all tagged REL, "what," across 39 ayahs) vs 15 raw instances of
`man`.** But the 15 `man` hits split into two grammatically distinct constructions:

- **13 genuine "whoever is in the heavens [and earth]" instances**, using rational-being `man`:
  3:83, 10:66, 13:15, 17:55, 19:93, 21:19, 22:18, 24:41, 27:65, 27:87, 30:26, 39:68, 55:29. These
  appear as the subject of prostration (`sajada`), glorification (`sabbaḥa`), belonging (`la-hu`),
  or terror-at-the-Trumpet formulas — always predicated of beings capable of the verb in question.
- **2 instances that are NOT the "whoever is in X" formula at all** — 67:16, 67:17: `a-amintum man
  fī al-samāʾ`, "do you feel secure [against] He who is in the heaven" — `man` here names **God**
  (by exegetical and grammatical consensus of the sentence structure — the referent is the one who
  "causes the earth to swallow you," an act of divine power), not a class of heavenly inhabitants.
  These must not be counted toward the "rational beings in heaven" tally.

So the true split for the collective formula is **`mā`: 39 ayahs / `man`: 13 ayahs**, with `man`
appearing both as a single pronoun covering both realms ("whoever is in the heavens and the earth,"
9 of 13) and doubled ("whoever is in the heavens **and** whoever is in the earth," 4 of 13: 10:66,
22:18, 27:87, 39:68).

**Yes, the Quran does say "whoever is in the heavens" using `man`, 13 times**, independent of the
22:18 and 13:15 examples the brief flagged. Grammatically `man` is reserved for rational beings —
its use here is not accidental variation from `mā`; both forms occur in the identical rhetorical
slot across different surahs (`mā` at 2:255, 3:29; `man` at 13:15, 30:26), which suggests the Quran
grammatically treats the heavens' population as capable of housing rational beings, without naming
who beyond angels. **22:18 needs a caveat**: it lists `man fī al-samāwāti wa-man fī al-arḍ` and then,
as *separate* grammatical subjects, "the sun, the moon, the stars, the mountains, the trees, the
moving creatures (`dawābb`), and many of the people" — the `dawābb` sit alongside earth-bound
sun/moon/mountains/trees, distinct from "whoever is in the heavens," which cuts against reading this
verse as heavenly-creature evidence.

**Panel — 22:18**: *a-lam tara anna Allāha yasjudu lahu man fī al-samāwāti wa-man fī al-arḍi
wa-al-shamsu wa-al-qamaru wa-al-nujūmu wa-al-jibālu wa-al-shajaru wa-al-dawābbu wa-kathīrun mina
al-nās* — "Do you not see that to Allah prostrates whoever is in the heavens and whoever is on the
earth, and the sun, the moon, the stars, the mountains, the trees, the moving creatures, and many of
the people." All four translators render this near-identically; the divergence is not translation
but what counts as *part of* "man fī al-samāwāt" versus what is listed separately.

## 6. 51:47 — the `mūsiʿūn` divergence (report only, no adjudication)

**Panel — 51:47**: `wa-al-samāʾa banaynāhā bi-aydin wa-innā la-mūsiʿūn`.
- Saheeh: "the heaven We constructed with strength, and indeed, We are [its] **expander**."
- Khattab: "We built the universe with great might, and We are certainly **expanding it**."
- Arberry: "heaven — We built it with might, and We **extend it wide**."
- Pickthall: "We have built the heaven with might, and We it is Who make the **vast extent** thereof."

`mūsiʿūn` (root `wsE`, "to make wide/spacious/capacious") is an active-participle plural. The four
translators split across three readings: an *ongoing process* ("expanding" — Khattab), a *completed
attribute* ("its expander" / "vast extent" — Saheeh, Pickthall, closer to "possessor of capacity"
than active expansion), and a *stative spatial verb* ("extend it wide" — Arberry). **This project
does not adjudicate toward a cosmological-expansion reading.** The participle is as compatible with
"We have vast power/capacity" (a divine epithet, paralleling other `-ūn` constructions) as with
"actively enlarging it." The verse cannot bear a determinate astrophysical reading from morphology
alone — four professional translators landing in three places is the data point: real, unresolved
lexical range, not carelessness.

## Negative space

- No orbit-vocabulary anywhere in the seven-heavens set — architecture/layering language only.
- No named object assigned to any *specific* heaven but the lowest; no located content for the
  other six beyond "its `amr`" (71:16's moon is `fīhinna`, collective, undistributed)."
- No co-occurrence of "guarded tablet" vocabulary with `smw` — that association is external to the
  text.
- `dābba` is placed in the heavens exactly once (42:29, dual pronoun) — see sibling `dbb` file.
- 23:17 and 78:12, half the "seven heavens" set by common citation, never actually use the word
  `samāʾ`/`samāwāt`.

## What this settles

Singular/plural `samāʾ` split is real and systematic (atmospheric vs. structural), confirmed by
independent co-occurrence evidence, not just intuition. The seven heavens are described
architecturally (layered, strong) with zero orbital vocabulary. Stars/lamps are textually confined
to the lowest heaven in every relevant verse (41:12, 67:5, 37:6), and **no named object is assigned
to any *specific* heaven other than the lowest** — 71:16 puts the moon `fīhinna` (`3FP`) in the seven
collectively, without distributing it, so the broader claim that nothing visible appears in heavens
2–7 does not stand. The other six have no located contents beyond a private `amr` each. The `mā`/`man` split is real: 13 ayahs use the
rational-being pronoun `man` for "whoever is in the heavens," independent of the two examples flagged
in the brief — but two further `man`-instances (67:16-17) are a different construction (naming God)
and must not be miscounted toward that total. 51:47's `mūsiʿūn` is genuinely underdetermined; no
astrophysical reading is textually forced.
