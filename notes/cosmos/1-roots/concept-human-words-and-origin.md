# The human words — insān, bashar, nās — and where the Quran puts a human being

## Headline finding (100 words)

The three human-words divide labor cleanly: `bashar` (37/123 root-occurrences are the human sense) names the biological mortal — "I am only a bashar like you"; `insān` (90/97) names the human as moral/psychological subject — hasty, ungrateful, disputatious; `nās` (241, one lemma) names people as collective/social body — "O mankind," "most of the people." The pre-cut's 31 human+heaven ayahs are **39% false positives** (the `smw` root also means "name," the `b$r` root also means "glad tidings"). Of the 19 genuine co-occurrences, **zero** locate a human being in the heavens. Human origin is uniformly terrestrial; `arḍ` never occurs in the plural.

## 1. Three words, not synonyms — the data-grounded division of labor

**`bashar`** — root `b$r`, 123 occurrences / 119 ayahs, but only **one of its 11 lemmas is the "human" sense**: `ba$ar` (37 occurrences, 37 ayahs). The other 10 lemmas (86 occurrences: `bu$~ira` 38, `bu$oraY` 15, `ba$iyr` 9, `muba$~ir` 9, `yasotabo$iru` 7, `bu$or` 3, `ba`$iru` 2, `muba$~irat` 1, `>abo$iru` 1, `m~usotabo$irap` 1) are all "glad tidings" (bushrā) — a distinct semantic cluster sharing the root but not the sense. So the root's raw ayah-count (119) overstates the human-word by roughly 3x; the true `bashar`-as-human-being footprint is **37 ayahs**.

Checking those 37 ayahs: the dominant formula is a mortal disclaiming divine or supernatural status — "in anā illā bashar" / "hal hādhā illā basharun mithlukum" (a mere mortal like you) — said by prophets of themselves [14:10, 14:11, 17:93, 18:110, 21:34, 38:71] and by doubters of prophets [14:10, 23:24, 23:33, 23:34, 25:54, 64:6]. The rest are creation-from-matter statements: clay [none directly, see §3], dust-then-bashar [30:20], water-into-bashar [25:54]. `bashar` is the word for **the human considered as a biological, mortal, created body** — never the word for moral failing, never paired with jinn.

**`insān`** — root `Ans`, 97 occurrences / 93 ayahs, 6 lemmas. The human-being senses (`<insa`n` 71, `<ins` 18, `<insiy~` 1 = 90) dominate; the remaining 7 (`'aAnasa` 5, `tasota>onisu` 1, `musota_#onisiyn` 1) are an unrelated derivation of the same root meaning "to perceive/notice" or "seek familiarity" (Moses "perceiving" a fire, 20:10; asking permission before entering a house, 24:27) — not the human noun at all.

Sampling `insān`'s predicate slots shows a consistent moral/psychological charge sheet: hasty [17:11, 21:37], anxious [70:19], ungrateful [100:6, 14:34, 17:67], transgressive [96:6], disputatious [18:54], stingy [17:100], forgetful of his own origin [19:67]. `<ins` (18 occ.) is the collective form almost always paired with `jinn` ("jinn and ins," e.g. 55:33, 51:56) — humanity as a species-category set against another species, not an individual under moral scrutiny. `insān` is **the human as a psychological/moral subject being addressed or indicted**, overwhelmingly in singular, generic ("the human," "man").

**`nās`** — root `nws`, 241 occurrences / 225 ayahs, exactly **one lemma** (`n~aAs`). No polysemy, no verb forms, no other sense. Co-occurrence is dominated by `Alh` (God, 57% of ayahs) and formulaic address (`yā ayyuhā al-nās`, "O mankind") and generalization (`akthar al-nās`, "most of the people," `kvr` root, 16%). `nās` is **people as a collective, social, addressable body** — the audience of revelation, the mass whose majority disbelieves, the crowd God is kind to [22:65] or checks by means of each other [22:40]. It never carries individual moral indictment the way `insān` does, and it is grammatically defective — no true singular; it functions as `insān`'s suppletive plural in ordinary usage while remaining etymologically its own lemma.

**Summary**: `bashar` = body/mortality, `insān` = moral psychology of the individual human, `nās` = the social collective. Distinct registers, confirmed from the lemma tables, not asserted.

## 2. THE CENTRAL AUDIT — 31 human+heaven ayahs, verified word by word

The pre-cut (`cosmos-cooccurrence.json`) lists 31 ayahs where a human-root and `smw` co-occur. **Checking each against `data/morphology/words.jsonl` at the word level (not root level) found that 12 of the 31 are false positives** — the exact trap the brief warned about:

- `smw` also produces `ism` (name) and `musammā` (specified/named) — not `samāʾ` (heaven) at all. False heaven-matches: **3:45, 12:40, 16:61, 19:7, 22:5, 22:34, 22:40, 22:78, 35:45, 61:6.**
- `b$r` also produces `bushira`/`yastabshiru`/`mubashshir` (announce/rejoice at glad tidings) — not `bashar` (human being). False human-matches: **3:45, 19:7, 22:34, 25:48, 30:48, 61:6**.

(3:45, 19:7, 22:34, 61:6 are false on *both* counts — "he named him," "give her/him glad tidings" — no heaven-word, no human-noun, both hits are homonyms of the real target roots.)

**19 ayahs are genuine** — a real `bashar`/`insān`/`nās` and a real `samāʾ`/`samāwāt` in the same verse: 2:164, 4:170, 5:18, 5:97, 7:158, 7:187, 10:24, 13:17, 14:10, 17:93, 22:18, 22:65, 24:35, 30:8, 31:20, 33:72, 35:3, 40:57, 55:33.

Classifying all 19 by relationship:

| Relationship | Ayahs | Count |
|---|---|---|
| **(d) Heavens+earth merism** — God's dominion/creation/knowledge spans both; human word appears as addressee or contrast, never as heaven's content | 4:170, 5:18, 5:97, 7:158, 7:187, 14:10, 22:18, 24:35, 30:8, 31:20, 33:72, 40:57 (+2:164) | 13 |
| **(b) Provision/rain descends from heaven** to reach humans on earth | 10:24, 13:17, 35:3 (+2:164) | 4 |
| **(c) Ascent to heaven is a challenge, hypothetical, or impossible** | 17:93, 55:33 | 2 |
| **(a) Heaven is a vault overhead**, held up/restrained for human benefit | 22:65 | 1 |

(2:164 is double-counted: it is both a merism and a rain-descent verse.)

**Result: in all 19 genuine ayahs, not one places a human being in the heavens.** This confirms the commissioner's hypothesis exhaustively — there is no counter-example. Three ayahs deserve direct quotation because they are the sharpest data points:

**17:93** — the Prophet is challenged to ascend, and answers by naming himself `bashar`:
> أَوۡ تَرۡقَىٰ فِي ٱلسَّمَآءِ وَلَن نُّؤۡمِنَ لِرُقِيِّكَ حَتَّىٰ تُنَزِّلَ عَلَيۡنَا كِتَٰبࣰ ا نَّقۡرَؤُهُۥۗ قُلۡ سُبۡحَانَ رَبِّي هَلۡ كُنتُ إِلَّا بَشَرࣰ ا رَّسُولࣰ ا
> "...or you ascend into the sky. And [even then], we will not believe in your ascension until you bring down to us a book we may read. Say, 'Exalted is my Lord! Was I ever but a human messenger?'" — `bashar` is invoked *precisely to explain why ascent is not on offer*.

**55:33** — `ins` (paired with jinn) is dared to exit the heavens-and-earth system entirely:
> يَٰمَعۡشَرَ ٱلۡجِنِّ وَٱلۡإِنسِ إِنِ ٱسۡتَطَعۡتُمۡ أَن تَنفُذُواْ مِنۡ أَقۡطَارِ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِ فَٱنفُذُواْۚ لَا تَنفُذُونَ إِلَّا بِسُلۡطَٰنࣲ‏
> "O company of jinn and mankind, if you are able to pass beyond the regions of the heavens and the earth, then pass. You will not pass except by authority [from Allah]." Framed as a rhetorical impossibility, not an itinerary.

**33:72** — `insān` is the one thing that accepted a burden the heavens declined:
> إِنَّا عَرَضۡنَا ٱلۡأَمَانَةَ عَلَى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِ وَٱلۡجِبَالِ فَأَبَيۡنَ أَن يَحۡمِلۡنَهَا وَأَشۡفَقۡنَ مِنۡهَا وَحَمَلَهَا ٱلۡإِنسَٰنُۖ
> "We offered the Trust to the heavens and the earth and the mountains, and they declined to bear it... but man bore it." The heavens are explicitly *not* insān's category — a structural separation, not a location.

## 3. Human origin — uniformly terrestrial

Every origin-material verse points to earth, never sky: clay [`Tiyn`: 6:2 "created you from clay," 7:12, 32:7 "began the creation of man from clay"]; dried/altered clay [`SalSal`: 15:26, "from clay from an altered black mud"]; dust [`turAb`: 3:59 "created him [Adam] from dust," 22:5, 30:20 "created you from dust; then, suddenly you were bashar dispersing," 35:11]; sperm-drop [`nuTfa`: 16:4, 76:2]; water [25:54, "created from water a bashar"].

Two verses go further than "made from earthly matter" — they make the earth the *whole life-cycle*:

**71:17** — humans grew *from* the earth like vegetation:
> وَٱللَّهُ أَنۢبَتَكُم مِّنَ ٱلۡأَرۡضِ نَبَاتࣰ ا
> "And Allah has caused you to grow from the earth, a growth" — the verb `anbata` (to sprout/germinate) is agricultural; humans are placed in the same growth-vocabulary as plants, not manufactured elsewhere and installed.

**20:55** — the complete formula, origin/burial/resurrection in one clause:
> مِنۡهَا خَلَقۡنَٰكُمۡ وَفِيهَا نُعِيدُكُمۡ وَمِنۡهَا نُخۡرِجُكُمۡ تَارَةً أُخۡرَىٰ
> "From it [the earth] We created you, and into it We will return you, and from it We will extract you another time." Three verbs — `khalaqnākum` (created), `nuʿīdukum` (return), `nukhrijukum` (bring out) — all governed by `minhā`/`fīhā`, all pointing at the same referent: the earth. There is no rival location anywhere in this pattern. **The earth is origin, grave, and resurrection-site in a single sentence** — this is the strongest structural argument available in the human-word data, because it isn't one verse's silence about the sky, it's an explicit closed loop that starts and ends on the ground.

## 4. The dwelling verses — earth as *the* place, no second location assigned

`mustaqarr` (dwelling/settling-place) appears twice in near-identical language: 2:36 and 7:24 — `wa-lakum fī al-arḍi mustaqarrun wa-matāʿun ilā ḥīn`, "for you on the earth is a dwelling place and provision until a time" — spoken to Adam and Eve at the point of descent, i.e. this is the founding placement statement for the human species. 7:10 reinforces with `makkannākum fī al-arḍ` ("We established you on the earth"). 2:29 has God create "all that is in the earth" *for* humans, then turn to fashion the seven heavens as a separate act. 67:15 makes the earth `dhalūlan` (tamed/subdued) for walking and eating. In no case is a second `mustaqarr`, a second `tamkīn`, or a second provision-clause attached to the heavens. **The dwelling vocabulary is single-valued: it names earth and nothing else.**

## 5. The ascent challenge — impossible, hypothetical, disorienting, no exceptions

Beyond the two already inside the audit (17:93, 55:33), the pattern is corroborated by verses outside the human-word set: **6:35** offers a tunnel into the earth *or* a ladder into the sky as equally fantastical ways to force belief; **15:14–15** — even if a literal gate of heaven opened and the disbelievers kept climbing through it, they would call it an illusion (`sukkirat abṣārunā` — "our eyes have been dazzled"), i.e. ascent wouldn't even produce belief; **52:38** asks rhetorically whether they have a `sullam` (ladder) to eavesdrop on heaven; **6:125** uses climbing into the sky as a *simile for suffocating spiritual distress* (`ka-annamā yaṣṣaʿʿadu fī al-samāʾ`), not a real journey. Every instance of human-directed sky-ascent vocabulary in the Quran is negated, conditional, mocked, or metaphorical. No verse describes a human successfully, literally ascending into and residing in the heavens as a plain narrative fact.

## 6. Negative space — checked, not assumed

- **Does `arḍ` ever occur in the plural?** Checked against `data/morphology/roots.json`: the root `ArD` has **exactly one lemma**, `>aroD`, count 461, POS uniformly noun. There is no plural lemma. **`arḍ` never appears in a plural form anywhere in the Quran.** This is decisive: the grammatical machinery for "multiple earths" (a plural noun, the way `samāwāt` is the plural of `samāʾ`) simply does not exist in the text. Where the Quran wants multiplicity of heavens it inflects the word (`samāwāt`, 190 occurrences); it never does the parallel operation on `arḍ`.
- **Does the Quran say humans exist elsewhere, or that earth is one of several inhabited places?** Not in the 19 genuine human+heaven ayahs, not in the origin verses, not in the dwelling verses, not in the ascent verses. No verse states or implies a second inhabited world.
- **Second location assigned to humans?** None found in this word-set — see §4.

## What this settles

The three human-words are functionally distinct (body/mortality, moral psychology, social collective), not stylistic variants — confirmed from lemma counts, not assumed. Of 31 candidate human+heaven co-occurrences, 12 are root-collision artifacts (`ism`/`musammā` vs. `samāʾ`; `bushrā` vs. `bashar`); of the 19 real ones, **zero** place a human being in the heavens — the relationship is always merism, provision, or blocked ascent. Human origin material is uniformly terrestrial, culminating in 20:55's closed loop (earth as origin, grave, and resurrection-site) and 71:17's plant-growth verb. `arḍ` has no plural form in the Quranic text. On this word-set, the Quran locates the human being on earth, exclusively, and states this positively rather than merely failing to mention anywhere else.
