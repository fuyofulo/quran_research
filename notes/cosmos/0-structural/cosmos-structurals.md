# Structural pre-cut — life beyond earth

> Deterministic layer. Everything counted, not interpreted, unless flagged **[interpretive]**.
> Built by `scripts/build_cosmos_versepack.py` and `scripts/build_cosmos_cooccurrence.py` →
> `data/structural/cosmos-versepack.json` (157 verses), `data/structural/cosmos-cooccurrence.json`.

## Headline finding

The Quran does affirm rational beings in the heavens — **thirteen verses use `man`, the relative
pronoun reserved for rational beings, for "whoever is in the heavens."** They prostrate, they ask
Him, they will be terrified at the Trumpet, and they will fall dead at the blast. But **not one
verse locates a *human* in the heavens**, the word `arḍ` **never occurs in the plural anywhere in
the Quran**, and 65:12 — the verse behind the "seven earths" claim — contains no word for people,
life, or inhabitants. The Quran's answer to "is there life out there" is yes. Its answer to "are
there humans out there" is silence.

---

## 1. The pronoun audit — `mā` versus `man` in the heavens

Arabic distinguishes `mā` (what — things) from `man` (who — rational beings). I searched every
occurrence of both before `fī al-samāwāt`:

| Construction | Count |
|---|---|
| `mā fī al-samāwāt` — *what* is in the heavens | **39** |
| **`man fī al-samāwāt`** — ***who*** **is in the heavens** | **13** |

The thirteen: **3:83, 10:66, 13:15, 17:55, 19:93, 21:19, 22:18, 24:41, 27:65, 27:87, 30:26, 39:68,
55:29.**

What these thirteen predicate of those beings — all data-grounded, straight from the verses:

- **13:15** they prostrate, willingly or by compulsion
- **19:93** *"There is no one in the heavens and earth but that he comes to the Most Merciful as a
  servant"*
- **21:19** those near Him are not too arrogant to worship, nor do they tire
- **24:41** they glorify Him
- **27:65** none of them knows the unseen except God
- **27:87** at the Trumpet they will be **terrified**
- **30:26** all are devoutly obedient to Him
- **39:68** at the blast they will **fall dead**, except whom God wills
- **55:29** *"Whoever is in the heavens and earth asks Him"*

**[interpretive]** Beings that ask, obey, fear, and die are rational and contingent. The Quran is not
neutral on whether there is intelligent life beyond the earth — it repeatedly asserts there is. What
it does not do is tell you what kind, beyond naming angels. The conventional reading takes `man fī
al-samāwāt` to mean the angels; the grammar permits that and does not require it.

## 2. The dispersal verses — one dual against three singulars

The Quran has four parallel verses using `baththa` (to scatter) with `dābba` (moving creature).
Pronoun checked against the morphology segments:

| Ref | Clause | Pronoun tag | Scattered where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **42:29** | `wa-mā baththa **fīhimā** min dābba` | **`3D` — dual** | **the heavens AND the earth** |
| 31:10 | `wa-baththa **fīhā** min kulli dābba` | `3FS` | the earth |
| 2:164 | `wa-baththa **fīhā** min kulli dābba` | `3FS` | the earth |
| 45:4 | `wa-mā **yabuththu** min dābba` | none | unspecified |

**42:29 is the only verse in the Quran that places moving creatures in the heavens.** Its closing
clause — `wa-huwa ʿalā **jamʿihim** idhā yashāʾu qadīr`, He is able to gather **them** when He wills
— uses `-him`, the masculine-plural pronoun Arabic reserves for rational/personal referents.

## 3. Which beings the Quran puts near the heavens

**Corrected 2026-08-26 — the first version of this table was computed at ROOT level and was wrong.**
The root `smw` also yields `ism` (name) and `musammā` (specified); `b$r` also yields the "glad
tidings" cluster; `mlk` also yields `mulk` (dominion) and `malik` (king), which naturally co-occur
with "the heavens and the earth" without saying anything about angels. Root-matching inflated every
row. The table below is recomputed at **lemma** level — `samāʾ`/`samāwāt` only, and each being by its
own lemma set. Denominator: 297 ayahs contain `samāʾ`/`samāwāt`.

| Being (lemma-filtered) | With heavens / total | Share |
|---|---|---|
| **`dābba` — moving creature** | **5 / 18** | **27.8%** |
| `malak` — angel | 7 / 86 | 8.1% |
| `bashar` — mortal human | 3 / 37 | 8.1% |
| `nās` — people | 14 / 225 | 6.2% |
| `jinn` | 1 / 38 | 2.6% |
| `insān` / `ins` — human | 2 / 87 | 2.3% |

The correction **strengthens** the finding rather than weakening it. At root level `dābba` led angels
by 1.8×; at lemma level it leads by **3.4×**, and it leads the human-word by twelve. The creature-word
is, by a wide margin, the Quran's most heaven-attached word for a living thing.

**And the human audit came back empty.** Of the ayahs where a genuine human-word and a genuine
heaven-word co-occur, **19 are real** (12 of the root-level 31 were false positives on the same
`ism`/`bushrā` trap). Classified: 13 are heavens-and-earth merisms where the human is the addressee,
4 are rain or provision descending *to* humans on earth, 2 are ascent-impossibility verses (17:93,
55:33), 1 is heaven-as-overhead-vault (22:65). **Zero place a human being in the heavens.**

## 4. `arḍ` is never plural — the decisive lexical fact

`ArD` has **461 occurrences and exactly one lemma: `arḍ`, singular.** There is no `araḍūn`, no plural
of "earth", anywhere in the Quran.

**So "seven earths" is not a Quranic phrase.** It is an inference from 65:12's `wa-mina al-arḍi
mithlahunna` — "and of the earth, the like of them" — where `mithlahunna` and `baynahunna` both carry
`3FP`, pointing back to the seven heavens. The verse does not repeat "seven," does not say "planets,"
and mentions no inhabitant of any kind. Its own stated purpose is `li-taʿlamū anna-llāha ʿalā kulli
shayʾin qadīr` — that you may know God has power over everything.

The three verses that would carry a claim about extraterrestrial life — 42:29, 45:4, 51:47 — are all
in the top five for translator disagreement. **[interpretive]** That is exactly what you would expect
of verses whose referent the tradition never settled.

## What this settles

- **Rational beings in the heavens: affirmed.** Thirteen verses, using the rational-being pronoun.
- **Non-human creatures beyond the earth: one verse, 42:29, and it uses the dual deliberately.**
- **Humans elsewhere: nothing.** No verse. And no plural of "earth" to hang one on.
- **"Seven earths with people on them": not in the text.** The phrase is not Quranic; the verse it
  comes from is about God's power and says nothing about inhabitants.
