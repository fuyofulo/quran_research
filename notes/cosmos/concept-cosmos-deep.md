# Life beyond earth in the Quran — the synthesis

> Phase-4 capstone over the nine files of this investigation. **Hadith, tafsīr and modern
> "scientific miracle" literature are outside this project's evidence base** — cited below as
> *provenance*, never as evidence. Claims are marked **[data]** (counts, morphology tags, verified
> negatives) or **[interp]** (framing, inference).

---

## 1. Headline finding

**The question splits in two, and the halves get opposite answers.**

**Life beyond earth: affirmed.** Thirteen verses use `man` — Arabic's pronoun for rational beings —
for whoever is in the heavens: they prostrate, glorify, obey, petition, do not know the unseen, are
terrified at the Trumpet, and fall dead at the blast. 42:29 scatters `dābba`, creatures, through
heavens **and** earth on a dual pronoun verified in the morphology. Not an apologetic reading — what
the grammar says. **[data]**

**Humans beyond earth: nothing.** Not one verse. `Arḍ` occurs 461 times and is never pluralised, so
there is no grammatical vehicle for "other earths." Of the 19 genuine human-word + heaven-word ayahs,
zero place a human in the heavens. Human origin, dwelling, death and resurrection are uniformly
terrestrial. **[data]**

The scholar's seven or eight inhabited earth-like planets are not in the text. **[data]**

---

## 2. The question as asked, and why it splits

The commissioner asked: *does the Quran talk about life outside the human, outside the earth — and
are there humans elsewhere, or is it just on earth?* He had heard a scholar say there are **seven or
eight earth-like planets where humans reside**, could not place the source, and wanted it checked:
"it's not plainly written in the Quran" ([`BRIEF.md`](BRIEF.md)).

The instinct was right, and it forces three questions: **is there non-human life beyond the earth?**
(yes, grammatically and repeatedly); **are there *humans* beyond the earth?** (separate, and flatly
negative); **where did "seven earths with people on them" come from?** (a three-layer stack, no layer
of which is the Quranic text). **The commonest error here is answering the first and reporting it as
an answer to the second.** **[interp]**

## 3. What the Quran affirms about life beyond earth

**The pronoun audit. [data]** Arabic distinguishes `mā` (what — things) from `man` (who — rational
beings). Walking every occurrence of both before `fī al-samāwāt` gives **39 ayahs with `mā`, 13 with
`man`**: 3:83, 10:66, 13:15, 17:55, 19:93, 21:19, 22:18, 24:41, 27:65, 27:87, 30:26, 39:68,
55:29 ([`0-structural/cosmos-structurals.md`](0-structural/cosmos-structurals.md)). They predicate
prostration, glorification, belonging to God, service, obedience, petition, ignorance of the unseen,
terror at the Trumpet, and death at the blast — rational, contingent, accountable and mortal, not
scenery. **[interp]**

Two exclusions matter. **67:16–17 do not belong on that list** — `a-amintum man fī al-samāʾ` names
**God**; counting them is the standard mistake. And **all thirteen are heavens-*and*-earth merisms**,
none naming heaven-dwellers alone: eight distribute to individual heaven-dwellers (doubled subject or
universal quantifier, 39:68 included), five are collective only
([`2-cross-cutting/research-is-there-life-beyond-earth.md`](2-cross-cutting/research-is-there-life-beyond-earth.md)).
**[data]**

**42:29 — the irreducible verse. [data]**
> **٤٢:٢٩** وَمِنۡ ءَايَٰتِهِۦ خَلۡقُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِ وَمَا بَثَّ **فِيهِمَا** مِن دَآبَّةࣲ وَهُوَ عَلَىٰ **جَمۡعِهِمۡ** إِذَا يَشَآءُ قَدِيرٌ
> *"And of His signs is the creation of the heavens and the earth, and what He has scattered
> throughout **them both** of creatures — and He is able to gather **them** whenever He wills."*

`fīhimā` is tagged **`PRON: 3D`** — dual. The three parallel dispersal verses decline that pronoun
(2:164 and 31:10 use `fīhā`, `3FS`, the earth; 45:4 has none), so **42:29 is the only verse in the
Quran placing moving creatures in the heavens**. Its closing pronoun `jamʿihim` is `3MP` — the
agreement Arabic reserves for rational or personal referents
([`1-roots/concept-dbb-dabba.md`](1-roots/concept-dbb-dabba.md)).

**16:49 blocks the deflection. [data]** The Quran never calls an angel a `dābba`, and 16:49
coordinates the two as separate items: `…min dābbatin **wa-l-malāʾikatu**` — "…of creatures, **and
the angels**." Saheeh, Pickthall and Khattab read `min dābbatin` as covering both the heavens- and
earth-clauses, a second verse tending 42:29's way; **Arberry restricts the creatures to the earth**.
A genuine 3–1 split, unadjudicated. What is *not* split is the coordination.

**The numbers agree with the grammar. [data]** Share of each being-word's ayahs also carrying
`samāʾ`/`samāwāt`, at lemma level: **`dābba` 5/18 = 27.8%**, angels 8.1%, `bashar` 8.1%, `nās` 6.2%,
jinn 2.6%, `insān` 2.3% — the corpus's most heaven-attached being-word, by 3.4× over angels.

**What 42:29 will not bear: any description. [data]** Across all 18 `dābba` occurrences no verse says
what a heavenly creature looks like, does, or whether it reasons. One clause of location, one of
fate.

## 4. The stress test, honestly reported

The conventional reading takes `man fī al-samāwāt` to be the angels. **This investigation tested that
rather than assuming it**: of each predicate, does the Quran say this of angels *elsewhere*?

**Twelve of thirteen are absorbed, each with a closing verse. [data]**

| Predicate | Closing verse(s) |
|---|---|
| Ignorant of the unseen (27:65) | **2:32**, the angels' own words |
| Terrified at the Trumpet (27:87) | **21:28** `mushfiqūn`; 13:13, 16:50, 34:23 |
| Ask Him (55:29) | **40:7**, Throne-bearers petition; 42:5, 37:164–166 |
| Prostrate, glorify, obey, serve, do not tire (13:15, 19:93, 21:19, 24:41, 30:26) | 16:49, 21:19–20, 66:6 — the angelic profile itself |
| Submit willingly or by compulsion (3:83, 13:15) | Collective merism; the earth-half supplies the reluctance |


> **٢:٣٢** قَالُواْ سُبۡحَٰنَكَ لَا عِلۡمَ لَنَآ إِلَّا مَا عَلَّمۡتَنَآ
> *"They said: Exalted are You; we have no knowledge except what You have taught us."* — this single
> verse closes 27:65.

**One is not — 39:68, the residue. [data]**

> **٣٩:٦٨** وَنُفِخَ فِي ٱلصُّورِ فَصَعِقَ مَن فِي ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَن فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ إِلَّا مَن شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ
> *"And the Horn will be blown, and whoever is in the heavens and whoever is on the earth will fall
> dead, except whom Allah wills."*

A **doubled** construction: heaven-dwellers are a grammatically independent subject of `ṣaʿiqa`, so
the predicate distributes to them. And **no verse in the Quran says an angel dies.** Across every ayah
where an angel-lemma and the root `mwt` co-occur — **six: 2:161, 6:93, 6:111, 10:31, 25:3, 32:11** —
the angel is death's **agent or witness**, never its patient (32:11: *"the angel of death will take
you"*). No verse says an angel is immortal either, and the universal perishing verses do not settle
it: 55:26's `kullu man ʿalayhā fān` is scoped to the earth by `ʿalayhā`, and 28:88 is too total to
decide anything about a species. **[data]**

**Verdict: underdetermination, not contradiction. [interp]** Nothing in the thirteen is
*incompatible* with the angels-only reading — anyone claiming these verses *require* a third
population is overstating. But that reading does not *absorb* 39:68; it *extends* to cover it,
teaching what no other verse teaches. 39:68 is silent between "angels die too" and "the heavens hold
more than angels": a reader may hold the second, but cannot say the verse states it. **The residue is
real and exactly one verse wide** — cutting against the enthusiast in the main, the dismisser in one
place.

## 5. The other two rational species

**Exactly two, both real. [data]**
([`1-roots/concept-jinn-angels-other-beings.md`](1-roots/concept-jinn-angels-other-beings.md))
**Jinn** — 39 genuine occurrences once the root is disambiguated (147 of 201 `jnn` hits are `janna`,
garden): fire-made (55:15), in communities and generations (7:38), morally split (72:11),
judged (6:128), addressed by messengers "from among you" (6:130), and given a whole sūrah of
first-person testimony (72). **Angels** — 88 genuine occurrences once
`mulk`/`malik`/`malakūt` are stripped: winged (35:1), incapable of disobedience (66:6), tireless,
heaven-resident.

**No third species survives checking. [data]** `Al-rūḥ` (97:4) is coordinated separately from the
angels but never given plurality, community or moral agency, and 17:85 withholds its nature;
Throne-bearers are a role inside the angelic category; `al-malaʾ al-aʿlā` names a venue; Hārūt and
Mārūt are `al-malakayn` by their own verse (2:102); Iblīs is `mina al-jinn` (18:50).

**And the jinn are barred from the heavens, not resident in them. [data]** Four passages describe
attempted access and repulsion — 72:8–9 (guards and flames; `kunnā naqʿudu`, "we *used to* sit,"
against "whoever listens *now*"), 15:16–18, 37:6–10, 67:5 — none describing successful entry. Jinn
have the lowest heaven-overlap of any being-word, 2.6%. **The Quran's second rational species is
bound to this world.** **[interp]**

## 6. Why there are no humans out there in this text

**The audit. [data]** ([`1-roots/concept-human-words-and-origin.md`](1-roots/concept-human-words-and-origin.md))
Of 31 ayahs where a human-root and `smw` co-occur, **12 are false positives** (`smw` also yields
`ism`; `b$r` also yields `bushrā`). **Of the 19 genuine ayahs, zero place a human being in the
heavens**: 13 merisms with the human as addressee, 4 provision descending *to* humans, 2
blocked-ascent verses (17:93, 55:33), 1 heaven as overhead vault (22:65).

**The origin material is uniformly terrestrial. [data]** Clay, altered mud, dust, sperm-drop, water
(6:2, 15:26, 3:59, 30:20, 16:4, 25:54); 71:17 adds an agricultural verb — God *sprouted* humanity
from the ground. 20:55 closes the loop:

> **٢٠:٥٥** مِنۡهَا خَلَقۡنَٰكُمۡ وَفِيهَا نُعِيدُكُمۡ وَمِنۡهَا نُخۡرِجُكُمۡ تَارَةً أُخۡرَىٰ
> *"From it We created you, and into it We will return you, and from it We will extract you another
> time."*

Three verbs, one referent: earth as origin, grave and resurrection-site in one sentence. The dwelling
vocabulary is single-valued too — `fī al-arḍi mustaqarr` (2:36, 7:24), `makkannākum fī al-arḍ`
(7:10), no second dwelling-clause anywhere. **[data]**

**The inference, marked as one. [interp]** A second human population would need its own 20:55: a
different ground to be made from, buried in, and raised out of. This anthropology is not a set of
facts about where people live; it is a cycle bound to one substrate. **Weight: moderate, no more.**
20:55 addresses a specific "you," and an addressee-scoped statement is not automatically a law of
humanity. Someone could hold that God made another humanity elsewhere and did not mention it in a
book addressed to this one; nothing refutes that. The pattern establishes only that such a belief
gets **no support** here and runs against the grain — a reason to doubt, not a proof.

**The lexical fact that closes the phrase. [data]** `ArD` occurs **461 times, one lemma, singular** —
no `araḍūn` anywhere. Where the Quran wants multiple heavens it inflects the word (`samāwāt`, 190×);
it never does that to `arḍ`.

## 7. The seven-earths claim, settled

> **٦٥:١٢** ٱللَّهُ ٱلَّذِي خَلَقَ سَبۡعَ سَمَٰوَٰتࣲ وَمِنَ ٱلۡأَرۡضِ **مِثۡلَهُنَّ** يَتَنَزَّلُ ٱلۡأَمۡرُ **بَيۡنَهُنَّ** لِتَعۡلَمُوٓاْ أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيۡءࣲ قَدِيرٌ
> *"God is He who created seven heavens, and of the earth the like of them. The command descends
> between them, that you may know that God is over all things competent…"*

Word by word ([`1-narratives/narrative-65-12-seven-earths-claim.md`](1-narratives/narrative-65-12-seven-earths-claim.md)), all **[data]**:

- `mithlahunna` and `baynahunna` both carry **`3FP`**, verified — pointing back to the seven heavens.
- **"Seven" is not repeated.** It governs the heavens; the earth gets only `mithlahunna`, and `mithl`
  never specifies the respect of likeness — number, layering, bare createdness are all open.
- **No word for planet. No word for life, people, or inhabitant.** Fourteen words, all accounted for.
- **None of the four translators inserts "seven" a second time.** Had it been plainly there, one of
  four independent traditions would have written "seven earths."
- The purpose clause — `li-taʿlamū anna-llāha ʿalā kulli shayʾin qadīr` — is divine power, not an
  inventory.
- **65:12 is the only verse putting "seven" and "the earth" in one clause at all** — a one-off, not a
  formula restated elsewhere.

**Provenance — three layers, all outside this evidence base**, cited as origin, not proof:

1. **Tafsīr.** Classical commentary is genuinely split and richer than the folk version: al-Alūsī
   reports roughly six positions — seven distinct earths with inhabitants, seven geological strata,
   seven continents, seven climate zones — and is explicitly agnostic between them. A modern academic
   reading (Decharneux) notes one may grammatically pair the seven heavens with **one** earth.
2. **A weak report.** *Populated* earths each with their own prophet comes from a report attributed
   to Ibn ʿAbbās, flagged even by sympathetic sources as not rigorously authenticated. A separate,
   well-transmitted hadith uses "seven earths" for *depth* in a land-theft image, mentioning no
   inhabitants — the two get conflated.
3. **TRAPPIST-1, retrofitted.** February 2017: seven roughly Earth-sized planets around an ultracool
   dwarf 40 light-years away — almost certainly what the commissioner half-remembers. No mainstream
   exegetical body was found making that identification.

**The distinction that has to be kept. [interp]** "Not in the text" and "false" are different claims.
A Muslim may hold the seven-inhabited-earths picture **as speculation** without contradicting the
Quran — the text neither asserts nor forbids it — but may not represent it as what 65:12 says. **The
scholar who said seven or eight earth-like planets hold humans was not reading the Quran**; he was
reading a respectable tafsīr tradition on top of a weak report on top of a news cycle — and the first
of the three is serious scholarship on a genuinely ambiguous verse.

## 8. The negative space

([`2-cross-cutting/research-cosmology-negative-space.md`](2-cross-cutting/research-cosmology-negative-space.md)) — all **[data]** unless marked.

- **No astronomy.** No distance, size, mass, age or star-count. The only number ever attached to a
  celestial object is **eleven**, at 12:4 — Joseph reporting a dream.
- **No word for planet, universe or galaxy.** All five `kawkab` occurrences denote a *light seen from
  the ground*, never a place (24:35 proves it — a simile works only on a visual property). `Falak`
  ("orbit") occurs **twice**; the root's other 23 occurrences mean **ship**.
- **No reconciling chronology.** Seven verses say `fī sittati ayyām`; 41:9–12 counts 2 + 4 + 2 = 8,
  and the harmonisation rests on a reading of `sawāʾan` **three of four translators reject**. 2:29
  and 79:27–30 give opposite orders. "Day" is never defined.
- **No described contents for six of the seven heavens** — correcting a claim the sibling root file
  overstated: **no verse assigns any named object to a *specific* heaven other than
  the lowest.** Stars and lamps go to `al-samāʾ al-dunyā` every time (41:12, 67:5, 37:6). **71:16 is
  the partial exception — `wa-jaʿala al-qamara fīhinna nūran` puts the moon `fīhinna` (`3FP`,
  verified, pointing back to 71:15's seven): in them collectively, undistributed.** The narrower
  negative survives; [`1-roots/concept-smw-seven-heavens.md`](1-roots/concept-smw-seven-heavens.md)
  is corrected.
- **No mechanism.** The end-of-cosmos verses say what it would look like, never how it would work:
  81:1's verb for the sun's undoing (`kuwwirat`) is 39:5's verb for **ordinary nightfall**, and
  21:104 rolls the sky in an explicit simile.
- **Geocentrism: vantage, not doctrine.** No verse says the earth is central, that it moves, or that
  it is fixed. The nearest thing to a position claim is `an tamīda bikum` (16:15, 21:31, 31:10), and
  `māda` means *lurch, sway* — stability underfoot. What *is* unmistakably geocentric is the
  standpoint: the sky a roof (`saqf`, the word for house-roofs at 16:26, 43:33) held from falling on
  you; the earth a bed, a cradle, a carpet. **[interp, and the distinction matters]** A vantage is not
  a doctrine — "the sun rises" commits an English speaker to nothing. **The Quran never converts its
  standpoint into a proposition, which is why it has no geocentrism to defend and no heliocentrism to
  be credited with.** Both camps supply a claim the text does not contain.

The same holds for the "scientific miracle" verses, stated without contempt. **[data]** `Mūsiʿūn`
(51:47) has divergence 0.714, and its only other Quranic use, 2:236, means "a man of **ample
means**." `Ratq` and `fatq` are **both hapax legomena inside 21:30** — the same ambiguity that
permits a Big Bang reading permitted a rain-and-vegetation reading for a millennium. **None is
refuted by the Arabic; all are underdetermined by it** — and a reading a text underdetermines is a
reading the reader brings. **[interp]**

## 9. 55:33 and the limits of reaching out

([`1-narratives/narrative-55-33-passing-beyond.md`](1-narratives/narrative-55-33-passing-beyond.md))

> **٥٥:٣٣** يَٰمَعۡشَرَ ٱلۡجِنِّ وَٱلۡإِنسِ إِنِ ٱسۡتَطَعۡتُمۡ أَن **تَنفُذُواْ** مِنۡ أَقۡطَارِ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِ فَٱنفُذُواْ لَا تَنفُذُونَ إِلَّا **بِسُلۡطَٰنࣲ**
> *"O company of jinn and mankind, if you are able to pass beyond the regions of the heavens and the
> earth, then pass. You will not pass except by authority."*

**[data]** The root `nf*` occurs **exactly three times in the Quran, all inside this one verse.** Its
sense is *pierce through and come out the far side* — a bounded container with an edge to break, not
a destination to arrive at. Categorically different from "travelling to."

**[data]** `Sulṭān` (`slT`, 39 occurrences) means **warrant / authority / proof** in the clear
majority — some 20 of 37 noun uses are the "God has sent down no `sulṭān` for this" formula. **None
of the four translators renders it "power" here**: three say "authority," Pickthall "sanction."

**[interp]** And the setting is a judgment scene, not a research programme: 55:31 announces reckoning
on the two burdened species, 55:33 issues the challenge, **55:35 answers with fire and smoke they
cannot defend against.** Every other passage on reaching the heavens shows repulsion or hypothetical
failure — 72:8–9, 15:14–18, 37:6–10, 6:35, 17:93–95 — never achieved ascent; 15:14–15 is sharpest,
where a gate opened and them *still ascending* draws only "our eyes have been dazzled."

**The space-travel reading is a coherent extrapolation from rich vocabulary, not the verse's plain
sense** — it reads a boundary-verb as "travel to" and a warrant-word as "technological power." The
plain sense is accountability, not aerospace. **[interp]**

## 10. The three tiers

Reproduced from
[`2-cross-cutting/research-is-there-life-beyond-earth.md`](2-cross-cutting/research-is-there-life-beyond-earth.md)
— the practical payload of the investigation.

**Tier 1 — What the text asserts. [data]**
- Rational beings exist in the heavens: 13 verses, `man` — they prostrate, glorify, obey, ask, are
  ignorant of the unseen, are terrified, and die.
- Creatures — `dābba` — were scattered through heavens and earth and will be gathered: 42:29, dual
  pronoun verified.
- Two non-human rational species exist: jinn (fire-made, accountable, given messengers) and angels
  (heaven-resident, unfailingly obedient).
- Humanity is from the earth, dwells on the earth, returns to the earth, and is raised from the
  earth.

**Tier 2 — What the text permits but does not assert. [interp]**
- That the heavens hold rational beings *other than* angels. 39:68 leaves room; nothing closes it.
- That 42:29's heavenly `dābba` are living organisms in a biological sense. The word is defined by
  locomotion (24:45); the verse describes nothing either way.
- That other worlds exist. 65:12's `mithlahunna` is genuinely underdetermined — "the like of the
  heavens" has borne readings from strata to continents to climate zones.
- That God created humans elsewhere and did not mention it. Unsupported, not excluded.

**Tier 3 — What the text neither asserts nor forbids. [data] on the silence.**
- That any of those beings are human, humanoid, or human-like. No verse says so or denies it.
- That any other world is inhabited, has prophets, or has scripture.
- That there are seven earths, or seven anythings with people on them. **Not a Quranic phrase —
  `arḍ` has no plural.**
- That the heavens' population is or is not exhausted by angels.

**The line, stated once so it can be held honestly. [interp]** A believer may think there is life
elsewhere and stand on 42:29 and the `man` verses — that is a supported reading. He may *speculate*
that there are humans elsewhere, but he is speculating from silence and against the grain of the
Quran's terrestrial anthropology, and he must not say the Quran teaches it.

## 11. Open questions

1. **Does 39:68 teach that angels die, or that the heavens hold more than angels?** No verse decides
   it — the single unresolved seam here.
2. **What is a heavenly `dābba`?** 42:29 gives location and fate only; `dābba`'s definition (24:45)
   is locomotion, not biology.
3. **Does `dābba` include humans?** A real 2-vs-2 split across 18 occurrences (8:22, 8:55 vs 22:18,
   35:28), 13 neutral.
4. **What is the `amr` inspired into each heaven (41:12)?** Named, never described.
5. **In what respect is the earth `mithl` the heavens (65:12)?** Number, layering, createdness — all
   open; classical scholarship never converged.
6. **Is the jinn/human pair exhaustive of accountable creation?** The Quran names the binary whenever
   it enumerates; it never says it is complete.

---

## 12. What this settles

1. **Life beyond earth: yes.** Thirteen `man` verses and 42:29's dual pronoun — what the grammar
   says, not a stretch.
2. **Humans beyond earth: no verse. Zero.** And no plural of "earth" to hang one on.
3. **The angels-only reading absorbs twelve of the thirteen** — angels elsewhere lack knowledge
   (2:32), fear (21:28), petition (40:7).
4. **It does not absorb 39:68.** No verse says an angel dies. The residue is real, one verse wide —
   and underdetermination, not evidence.
5. **42:29 survives everything** — `dābba` is never applied to angels — but yields location and fate
   only.
6. **"Seven earths with people on them" is not in the Quran.** Ambiguous pronoun + weak report +
   TRAPPIST-1. Unsupported, not false: speculation or nothing.
7. **The Quran does no cosmology.** It says what the stars are *for*, never what they *are*.
8. **The asymmetry is the answer.** Expansive about non-human life, silent about non-terrestrial
   humanity — and that silence sits inside an anthropology (20:55) that would have to be rebuilt to
   accommodate a second humanity anywhere else.
