# Life beyond earth — investigation index

The project's first **cosmological** investigation, and the first commissioned to check a specific
claim the commissioner had heard and could not place: *does the Quran talk about life outside the
human, outside the earth — are there humans elsewhere in the universe, or is it just on earth?* He
added that a scholar he respects had said there are **seven or eight earth-like planets where humans
reside**, and wanted it checked properly, "because it's not something that is plainly written in the
Quran."

So the brief had two deliverables from the start: what the Quran actually says about life beyond the
earth, and an honest verdict on the claim he was told. They turned out to be different findings with
opposite answers, and the discipline of the whole investigation is keeping them apart.

**If you read one file, read [`concept-cosmos-deep.md`](concept-cosmos-deep.md)** — then
[`2-cross-cutting/research-is-there-life-beyond-earth.md`](2-cross-cutting/research-is-there-life-beyond-earth.md).

---

## The headline

**The question splits in two. Life beyond earth: affirmed. Humans beyond earth: nothing.**

Arabic distinguishes `mā` (what — things) from `man` (who — rational beings). Before `fī
al-samāwāt`, the corpus gives 39 ayahs with `mā` and **13 with `man`** — "whoever is in the heavens."
Those beings prostrate, glorify, obey, petition, do not know the unseen, are terrified at the
Trumpet, and **fall dead at the blast**. And **42:29** scatters `dābba` — creatures — through the
heavens *and* the earth on a **dual pronoun verified in the morphology** (`fīhimā`, `3D`), the only
verse in the Quran that does so; the three parallel dispersal verses all decline that pronoun.
`Dābba` is the corpus's most heaven-attached being-word, 27.8% against angels' 8.1%.

The angels-only reading of the thirteen was **stress-tested rather than assumed**, and it absorbs
twelve of them cleanly — the Quran elsewhere says angels lack knowledge (2:32), fear (21:28),
petition (40:7). **It does not absorb 39:68.** Across all six ayahs where an angel-word and the root
`mwt` co-occur, the angel is death's agent or witness, never its patient: **no verse in the Quran
says an angel dies.** The residue is real, one verse wide, and it is *underdetermination, not
evidence*.

The other half is flat. **`Arḍ` occurs 461 times and is never pluralised** — there is no grammatical
vehicle for "other earths." Of the 19 genuine human-word + heaven-word ayahs, **zero place a human in
the heavens.** Human origin, dwelling, death and resurrection are uniformly terrestrial, closing in
20:55's single sentence: from it We created you, into it We return you, from it We bring you out
again.

**65:12 does not say "seven earths."** It says seven heavens and, of the earth, `mithlahunna` — "the
like of them" — with no repeated number, no word for planet, and no living thing mentioned anywhere
in its fourteen words. None of four independent translators inserts "seven" a second time.

## Scope and limits

This project reads the Quranic text directly and treats **hadith, classical tafsīr, and modern
"scientific miracle" literature as outside its evidence base.** That boundary matters more here than
anywhere else in the project, because **almost every popular claim in this area lives on the other
side of it** — the seven inhabited earths, the prophets on other worlds, the Big Bang in 21:30, the
expanding universe in 51:47, the egg-shaped earth in 79:30. Where those sources appear in these
files, they are traced as *provenance* for a claim, never cited as evidence for or against it.

That is a statement about where the authority for a claim comes from, not a verdict on the tradition,
and this investigation does not adjudicate it. The classical commentary on 65:12 is genuinely split
across six or more positions and is explicitly agnostic between them; that is serious scholarship on
an ambiguous verse, and the file says so.

Two further limits. **"Not in the text" and "false" are different claims**, kept apart deliberately:
a Muslim may hold the seven-inhabited-earths picture as speculation without contradicting the Quran —
what he may not do is say the Quran teaches it. And **39:68 does not resolve**: it is silent between
"angels die too" and "the heavens hold more than angels," and no other verse decides it.

Nothing here is a ruling.
