# The negative space of Quranic cosmology — what the text does not say

## Headline finding (100 words)

The Quran contains **no astronomy**. It gives no distance, no size, no count of stars, no description
of any planet, and no mechanism of celestial motion. The word usually translated "orbit" (`falak`)
occurs **twice**; the same root's other 23 occurrences all mean *ship*. It has no word for planet,
universe, or galaxy. Its six-day creation account does not reconcile with 41:9-12, and it never
defines "day." Its end-of-cosmos verses are phenomenological, not mechanistic. Every celestial object
it names is given a *use* — timekeeping, navigation, beauty, defence — and every use is a use to a
human standing on the ground.

**Verification method.** Every negative was checked by extracting **all** occurrences of the relevant
root from `data/morphology/words.jsonl` and reading each in context. Where a negative could not be
cleanly established I weaken the claim; one sibling finding needed correction (§3).

## 1. No astronomy — verified

**`flk`: 25 occurrences, 2 lemmas.** `falak` ("orbit/course") occurs **twice** — 21:33 and 36:40. The
other **23 are `fulk`, meaning *ship***, in the ordinary maritime sense (2:164, 10:22, 16:14, 29:65,
43:12, 45:12 …). The Quran's entire celestial-motion vocabulary is one word used twice, from a root
whose overwhelming use is boats.

**Panel — 36:40** (divergence 0.529; 21:33 is 0.430):

> لَا ٱلشَّمۡسُ يَنۢبَغِي لَهَآ أَن تُدۡرِكَ ٱلۡقَمَرَ وَلَا ٱلَّيۡلُ سَابِقُ ٱلنَّهَارِۚ وَكُلࣱّ فِي فَلَكࣲ يَسۡبَحُونَ
>
> Saheeh: "each, in an orbit, is swimming." — Arberry: "each swimming in **a sky**." — Pickthall:
> "They float each in an orbit."

**What `kullun fī falakin yasbaḥūn` specifies:** each of the four named things — sun, moon, night, day
— has its **own** course (`falakin` is indefinite singular, not shared); they move continuously; none
overtakes another. **What it does not specify:** what a `falak` is (Arberry renders it "sky"), its
shape, centre, radius, period, speed, direction, how sun and moon relate, or why they move. And `yasbaḥūn` carries the **`3MP` tag** — the plural Arabic reserves for rational beings —
verified for both verses. **[interpretive]** The grammar treats sun, moon, night and day as servants
performing, not bodies obeying a law.

Sun and moon are elsewhere "subjected to service," each running to a **named term** never numbered
(13:2, 16:12, 31:29, 39:5); 36:38's `mustaqarr` is undefined; 36:39's lunar `manāzil` are naked-eye
phases — the nearest thing to an astronomical datum in the book.

**Counts and measurements — full inventory.** The only number ever attached to a celestial object is
**eleven**, at 12:4, and that is Joseph reporting a *dream*. The only other cosmic count is "seven"
for the heavens; 9:36's twelve months is a calendar. No distance, size, mass, age or stellar census
anywhere. Verified across `njm` (13), `kwkb` (5), `brj` (4 relevant), `$ms` (33), `qmr` (27).

## 2. No creation timeline that resolves

Seven verses state `fī sittati ayyām` in the identical phrase: 7:54, 10:3, 11:7, 25:59, 32:4, 50:38,
57:4.

41:9-12 counts differently: earth in **two** days (41:9), mountains and sustenance `fī arbaʿati
ayyām` (41:10), heavens finished as seven `fī yawmayn` (41:12). 2 + 4 + 2 = 8. The standard
harmonisation reads the four as *inclusive* of the first two, resting on `sawāʾan` — and **divergence
at 41:10 is 0.636, with the translators splitting exactly there:** only Khattab ("totaling four Days
exactly") produces the arithmetic; Saheeh has "without distinction," Arberry "equal to those who ask,"
Pickthall "alike for (all) who ask." **The reconciliation depends on a reading three of four
translators do not adopt.**

**The order does not settle either.** 2:29 creates what is in the earth *first*, then turns to the
heaven and makes them seven. 79:27-30 builds the heaven, raises its `samk`, darkens its night — **"and
the earth, after that (`baʿda dhālika`), He spread out."** Opposite sequences, neither glossing the
other.

**And "day" is not a unit.** 32:5 measures a day at a thousand years, 70:4 at fifty thousand, 22:47 at
a thousand — but **none of the three is about the six days of creation** (32:5 and 70:4 describe the
*ascent* of the command and of the angels; 22:47, delayed punishment). Importing them to redefine the
creation days is a move the text does not make.

**Verdict [data-grounded]: no coherent chronology.** No start point, no age, no duration in any
earth-unit, no stable ordering, no definition of the unit — and no claim to give one: every six-days
verse ends in a theological clause, never a further cosmological detail.

## 3. No description of the other six heavens — the strongest absence

Three verses place visible objects in a heaven, and all three name **the lowest**: 41:12 (`zayyannā
al-samāʾa al-dunyā bi-maṣābīḥa`), 67:5 (identical construction), 37:6 (`bi-zīnatin al-kawākib`). 15:16
and 25:61 use plain singular `al-samāʾ` — the sky-as-seen. What all seven receive is one unspecified
thing: 41:12's `wa-awḥā fī kulli samāʾin amrahā`, "He inspired in each heaven its `amr`." The `amr` is
never described. It is a placeholder with its contents withheld.

**Necessary correction to the sibling file.** `concept-smw-seven-heavens.md` states that no visible
object is placed in heavens 2-7. **71:16 is a partial exception.** After 71:15's "seven heavens in
layers," 71:16 reads `wa-jaʿala al-qamara **fīhinna** nūran` — the pronoun **3FP**, verified, pointing
back to the seven. The moon is placed *in them*, collectively; the verse neither says which nor
distributes. So the defensible negative is narrower: **no verse assigns any named object to any
*specific* heaven other than the lowest.** That survives checking; the broader version does not.

The consequence stands either way. Six of seven tiers are named, counted, called layered (`ṭibāqan`,
67:3, 71:15) and strong (`shidādan`, 78:12) — and left **empty of description**. A text producing
cosmology would fill them. This one counts them and stops.

## 4. No word for planet, universe, or galaxy — verified

**`kawkab`, all 5 occurrences, each checked:**

| Ref | Context | What it denotes |
|---|---|---|
| 6:76 | Abraham at nightfall sees *a* `kawkab`, calls it lord, rejects it when it **sets** | a light that rises and sets |
| 12:4 | Joseph's dream: eleven `kawkab` + sun + moon prostrate to him | dream-symbols for people |
| 24:35 | the lamp-glass is "as if it were a `kawkab durriyy`" | a simile for **brightness** |
| 37:6 | lowest heaven adorned with an adornment of `kawākib` | decorative lights |
| 82:2 | when the `kawākib` are scattered | lights, undone |

**In none of the five is a `kawkab` a place, a body or a world.** In every case it is a *light seen
from the ground* — and 24:35 proves it, since a simile works only on the visual property. Pickthall
alone translates "planets" (12:4, 82:2); the other three say "stars" throughout. "Planet" is an
importation.

**"Universe."** No such noun. The nearest, `ʿālamīn`, occurs **73 times and only ever in the plural** —
no singular `ʿālam` exists in the corpus — and is a possessive formula (`rabb al-ʿālamīn`) naming *all
beings/worlds*, not a physical whole. For "everything that exists" the Quran uses the merism **"the
heavens and the earth"**: a pair, not a totality-noun. Khattab's "We built the universe" at 51:47
renders singular `al-samāʾ`. **"Galaxy": no candidate root exists.**

## 5. No physically described end-state

**Panel — 81:1-3:** إِذَا ٱلشَّمۡسُ كُوِّرَتۡ / وَإِذَا ٱلنُّجُومُ ٱنكَدَرَتۡ / وَإِذَا ٱلۡجِبَالُ سُيِّرَتۡ

Saheeh "wrapped up"; Khattab "put out"; Arberry "darkened"; Pickthall "overthrown" (divergence 0.631).
**Decisive fact: `kwr` occurs three times in the Quran** — 81:1, and *twice in 39:5*, where God "wraps
(`yukawwiru`) the night over the day and the day over the night." The verb for the sun's end is the
verb for ordinary nightfall: an **appearance**, not a stellar process.

The pattern holds. 21:104 rolls the sky `ka-ṭayyi al-sijilli lil-kutub` — *like a scribe rolling a
scroll*, an explicit simile; 82:1 `infaṭarat`; 84:1 `inshaqqat`; 84:3-4 the earth stretched and
emptied; 39:67 the earth a handful in His grip. **Not one states a cause, a sequence, a duration or a
mechanism.** Each says what it would look like — and 84:2 and 84:5 close not with physics but with
obedience: "it gave ear to its Lord, as it must."

## 6. Geocentric in vantage, never in doctrine

**The Quran contains no assertion that the earth is central, and no assertion that it is not.** Both
were checked directly.

*No centrality claim.* No verse says sun, moon or stars go around the earth. The `sakhkhara` verses
subject them **to God's command and to human benefit** (13:2, 16:12, 31:29, 39:5) — service, not
geometry. `Kullun fī falakin` gives each body its own course and names no centre. There is no word for
centre, axis, or revolution-about-something.

*No motion or rest claim for the earth either.* The earth never appears in the `falak` / `yasbaḥūn` /
`yajrī` verses; the movers are always sun, moon, night, day. Nor is it called fixed. The nearest
approach is `an tamīda bikum` — mountains cast down "lest it **sway** with you" — occurring exactly
**three times** (16:15, 21:31, 31:10; root `myd`, verified). `Māda` is *to lurch, reel, sway*: what a
ship or a camel does. It is a claim about **stability under its inhabitants**, not position or orbit.
Against it stands 27:88, where the mountains "pass like the passing of clouds" and the translators
split on tense (Saheeh/Pickthall future, Khattab/Arberry present; divergence 0.708). The text does not
resolve its own mountains.

*What is unmistakably geocentric is the vantage.* Every cosmic description is framed from a person on
the ground looking up. The sky is a **roof**, `saqfan maḥfūẓan` (21:32) — and `saqf` occurs 4 times,
twice for the roofs of ordinary houses (16:26, 43:33), the same word. It is **raised without pillars
you see** (13:2, 31:10), `ʿamad` being the word for the columns of Iram (89:7) and of Hellfire
(104:9). It is **held from falling on you** (22:65). The earth is furnished for occupancy — a **bed**
(2:22), a **cradle** (20:53, 78:6), a **spread carpet** (71:19), **made tame** (67:15). Dhū al-Qarnayn
reaches "the setting of the sun" and "the rising of the sun" (18:86, 18:90), all four translators
keeping the observational framing. And 6:76-78 has Abraham reason from star, moon and sun to God —
the whole argument running on *setting*, i.e. on appearances.

**[interpretive, and the distinction matters]** A vantage is not a doctrine. "The sun rises" commits a
speaker to nothing about celestial mechanics; English speakers say it daily while knowing better. The
Quran speaks consistently from an observer's standpoint on the ground and **never converts that
standpoint into a cosmological claim**. It has no geocentrism to defend and no heliocentrism to be
credited with: it declines the question. Readers who want it condemned for the first and readers who
want it credited with the second are both supplying a proposition the text does not contain.

## 7. The "scientific miracle" verses, stated precisely

These readings matter to sincere people, and nothing here calls them impious. It states what the
Arabic supports.

**51:47 — `mūsiʿūn`.** Divergence **0.714**, among the highest in the corpus; four translators, three
readings: "expanding it" (Khattab); "its expander" / "the vast extent thereof" (Saheeh, Pickthall);
"extend it wide" (Arberry). **The decisive datum: the lemma `mūsiʿ` occurs exactly twice in the
Quran** — here, and at **2:236**, `ʿalā al-mūsiʿi qadaruhu`, "the man of **ample means** according to
his capacity," on divorce compensation. Verified. Its only other Quranic use concerns financial
capacity. That does not refute the cosmological reading; it shows the word's range is wide enough that
the reading is a choice.

**Panel — 51:47:** وَٱلسَّمَآءَ بَنَيۡنَٰهَا بِأَيۡيْدࣲ وَإِنَّا لَمُوسِعُونَ

**21:30 — `ratq`/`fatq`.** **Both are hapax legomena: `rtq` once, `ftq` once, both inside 21:30** —
no second occurrence anywhere to disambiguate them, the strongest possible underdetermination.
Divergence is only 0.514, the four agreeing on "joined mass, then split," but agreement on the
*rendering* is not agreement on the *referent*. Pre-modern readers took the `ratq` as sky withholding
rain and earth withholding vegetation, split open by rain and growth — which fits the verse's own next
clause, "and We made from water every living thing," at least as naturally. **The same ambiguity that
permits a Big Bang reading permitted a rainfall reading for a millennium.**

**Panel — 21:30:** أَوَلَمۡ يَرَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوٓاْ أَنَّ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضَ كَانَتَا رَتۡقࣰ ا فَفَتَقۡنَٰهُمَا

**79:30 — `daḥāhā`.** Also **hapax: `dHw` occurs once.** Divergence is **low (0.427)** and all four
translators say **"spread it out."** The "egg-shaped" reading rests on the later-lexicographic noun
`udḥiyya` (an ostrich's nesting-hollow) — **outside this project's evidence base**, and a sense no
translator applies here. Meanwhile the neighbouring 79:28, `rafaʿa samkahā`, carries divergence
**0.739**, the highest in this study: the four cannot agree whether `samk` is a ceiling, a vault, a
height or a thickness. The real ambiguity is not where the miracle-literature looks. (55:33 is covered
in `1-narratives/narrative-55-33-passing-beyond.md`.)

**The fair summary.** None of these verses is *refuted* by the Arabic; all are *underdetermined* by it.
A reading a text underdetermines is a reading the reader brings — not an accusation, a description of
what interpretation is. The cost of forgetting it: a claim staked on current science changes when the
science does. The verse was never staking anything.

## The shape of the silence

Assemble every celestial object the Quran names, with its stated purpose:

| Object | Stated purpose | Verses |
|---|---|---|
| stars | navigation in the dark of land and sea | 6:97, 16:16 |
| stars/lamps | adornment of the lowest heaven | 15:16, 25:61, 37:6, 67:5 |
| stars/lamps | missiles against eavesdropping devils | 67:5, 37:7-10 |
| sun & moon | reckoning — `ḥusbān` | 6:96, 55:5 |
| moon's phases | "that you may know the number of years and the reckoning" | 10:5 |
| sun & moon | signs; do not prostrate to them | 41:37 |
| sky | protected roof; held from falling on you | 21:32, 22:65 |
| earth | bed, cradle, carpet, made tame — walk on it, eat from it | 2:22, 20:53, 67:15, 71:19 |

**Every entry is a use-value to a human being on the ground. Not one is a fact about the object in
itself.** The Quran says what the stars are *for* — finding your way, telling the time, being
beautiful, guarding the heaven — never what they *are*. **[interpretive]** The brief's thesis holds:
this is a **sign-system addressed to an observer**, not a map. A map answers *where and how big*; a
sign-system answers *what does it tell you*. The Quran's own word for these objects is `āyāt`, signs,
and the register is consistent — 21:33's bodies take the rational-being verb ending, 22:18 and 55:6
have them prostrate, 41:11 has heaven and earth *answer* God, "we come willingly."

The silence is not a gap in a cosmology; it is what a text looks like when it is not doing cosmology
at all. The space where popular religious cosmology builds its seven inhabited earths, its expanding
universe and its Big Bang is **empty in the text** — and empty space attracts furniture.

## What this settles

1. **No astronomy.** No distance, size, star-count, planetary description or motion mechanism.
   `Falak` occurs twice; its root's other 23 occurrences mean "ship."
2. **`Kullun fī falakin yasbaḥūn` says each body has its own course and none overtakes another — and
   nothing else.**
3. **No coherent creation chronology.** Six-days (7 verses) vs 41:9-12's 2+4+2, reconciled only on a
   reading of `sawāʾan` that 3 of 4 translators reject; 2:29 and 79:27-30 give opposite orders; "day"
   is never defined, and 32:5 / 70:4 / 22:47 are not about creation.
4. **Six of seven heavens have no described contents.** Precisely: no named object is assigned to any
   *specific* heaven but the lowest. 71:16 places the moon `fīhinna` (3FP, verified) — collectively,
   without distributing. The sibling file needs this edit.
5. **No word for planet, universe or galaxy.** All 5 `kawkab` occurrences denote a *light*, never a
   world; `ʿālamīn` is plural-only, 73×, and names beings, not a cosmos.
6. **The end of the cosmos is described as it would look, never as it would work** — 81:1's verb is
   39:5's verb for nightfall.
7. **Geocentric in vantage, silent on geocentrism as doctrine.** Never says the earth is central,
   never says it moves, never says it is still; `an tamīda bikum` (3×) is about not lurching underfoot.
   Reading either geocentrism or heliocentrism in means adding a proposition.
8. **The "scientific miracle" verses are underdetermined, not refuted.** `Ratq` and `fatq` both hapax;
   `daḥā` hapax with all four translators saying "spread out"; `mūsiʿ`'s only other use (2:236) means
   "a man of ample means." The same ambiguity that permits today's reading permitted different
   pre-modern ones.
