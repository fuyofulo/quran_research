# Is there life beyond earth in the Quran?

> Cross-cutting synthesis over `0-structural/`, `1-roots/` (4 files) and `1-narratives/` (2 files),
> plus direct verification against `data/arabic/quran.json`, `data/morphology/words.jsonl`, and the
> four-translator layer. **Hadith, tafsīr, and "scientific miracle" literature are outside this
> project's evidence base.** Every claim is marked **[data]** or **[interp]**.

## Headline finding (100 words)

The two questions have different answers. **Life beyond earth: yes, affirmed.** Thirteen verses use
`man` — Arabic's rational-being pronoun — for whoever is in the heavens; 42:29 scatters `dābba`,
creatures, through heavens and earth on a verified dual pronoun. **Humans beyond earth: nothing.**
Zero verses, no plural of "earth," no inhabited world described. The stress test is decisive in one
direction only: the Quran elsewhere says angels lack knowledge, fear, and petition — so the
angels-only reading absorbs twelve of the thirteen. It never says an angel dies. 39:68 and 42:29 are
the residue: real, thin, and not enough to build a second humanity on.

---

## 1. The two questions, answered plainly

**Q1 — Does the Quran talk about life outside the human, outside the earth?**
**Yes, and not marginally.** It names two non-human rational species (jinn, angels); it uses `man`,
the pronoun reserved for rational beings, for the population of the heavens thirteen times; and it
states once, on a verified dual pronoun, that God scattered *creatures* through the heavens and the
earth. Those beings prostrate, glorify, obey, ask Him, do not know the unseen, will be terrified at
the Trumpet, and will fall dead at the blast. **[data]**

**Q2 — Are there humans elsewhere in the universe, or is it just on earth?**
**The Quran does not say there are, anywhere, in any verse.** Not one of the 19 genuine
human-word + heaven-word ayahs places a human in the heavens. `Arḍ` occurs 461 times and is **never
pluralized** — there is no grammatical vehicle for "other earths." No verse describes an inhabited
world other than this one. The human material is exclusively terrestrial in origin, dwelling, death,
and resurrection. **[data]**

The honest one-line version: **the Quran is expansive about non-human life and completely silent
about non-terrestrial humanity.** Those are not the same finding and should never be reported as one.

---

## 2. The positive case, assembled

Arabic distinguishes `mā` (what — things) from `man` (who — rational beings). Before `fī al-samāwāt`
the corpus gives **39 ayahs with `mā`** against **13 with `man`**: 3:83, 10:66, 13:15, 17:55, 19:93,
21:19, 22:18, 24:41, 27:65, 27:87, 30:26, 39:68, 55:29. **[data]** (67:16-17 is excluded: there `man
fī al-samāʾ` names **God**, not a class of inhabitants. Counting those two is the commonest error
here.)

What the thirteen predicate: prostration (13:15, 22:18), glorification (24:41), belonging to God
(10:66, 21:19, 30:26), coming as a servant (19:93), obedience (30:26), petition (55:29), ignorance of
the unseen (27:65), terror at the Trumpet (27:87), death at the blast (39:68). **[data]** **[interp]**
A portrait of beings rational, contingent, accountable, and mortal — not scenery.

**One structural fact not previously surfaced, and it matters: all thirteen are heavens-*and*-earth
merisms. Not one names heaven-dwellers alone.** **[data]** Nine use a single `man` covering both
realms; four double it (`man fī al-samāwāti wa-man fī al-arḍ`: 10:66, 22:18, 27:87, 39:68). This
constrains how hard each predicate can be pushed:

- **Distributes to individual heaven-dwellers** (doubled subject, or a universal/negative-universal
  quantifier): 10:66, 22:18, 27:87, 39:68, 19:93 (`in kullu man… illā`), 24:41 (`kullun`), 30:26
  (`kullun`), 27:65 (`lā yaʿlamu man…`). **Eight verses.**
- **Collective only** — satisfiable by the set as a whole: 3:83, 13:15, 17:55, 21:19, 55:29. **Five.**

The distinction is load-bearing at 3:83 and 13:15, where submission is `ṭawʿan wa-karhan`, "willingly
or by compulsion." Angels never disobey (66:6), so compulsion sits badly on them — but both are
collective merisms, and the earth-half supplies all the reluctance needed. **[data]** That apparent
objection dissolves on inspection; reported because it would otherwise look like evidence.

## 3. The stress test — does anything resist the angels-only reading?

The conventional reading takes `man fī al-samāwāt` to be the angels, and the being-census supports
it: the heavens have no other named resident population — jinn are explicitly **barred**, not housed
(72:8-9, 15:16-18, 37:6-10, 67:5). **[data]** The test: do the thirteen say anything of
heaven-dwellers that the Quran does not say of angels elsewhere? Each predicate, checked directly:

| Predicate in the 13 | Said of angels elsewhere? | Absorbed? |
|---|---|---|
| Don't know the unseen (27:65) | **Yes, explicitly.** 2:32 — the angels: *"we have no knowledge except what You have taught us."* 2:30-33 has God correct them. | Yes, decisively |
| Terrified at the Trumpet (27:87) | **Yes for fear.** 21:28 `min khashyatihi mushfiqūn`; 13:13 angels glorify `min khīfatihi`; 16:50 `yakhāfūna rabbahum min fawqihim`. For terror on the Day, 34:23 `ḥattā idhā fuzziʿa ʿan qulūbihim` — same root `fzʿ` — though its subject is contextual, not named. | Yes |
| Ask Him (55:29) | **Yes.** 40:7 Throne-bearers petition for believers; 42:5 angels `yastaghfirūna li-man fī al-arḍ`; 37:164-166 angels speak in first person; 78:38 they speak by permission. | Yes |
| Prostrate, glorify, obey, are servants, do not tire (13:15, 19:93, 21:19, 24:41, 30:26) | **Yes — this is the angelic profile.** 16:49, 21:19-20, 37:165-166, 66:6. | Yes; in fact favours angels |
| Submit willingly or by compulsion (3:83, 13:15) | No — but collective merism; earth-half absorbs it (§2). | Yes, by construction |
| **Fall dead at the blast (39:68)** | **No.** See below. | **No** |

> **٣٩:٦٨** وَنُفِخَ فِي ٱلصُّورِ فَصَعِقَ مَن فِي ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَن فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ إِلَّا مَن شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ
> *"And the Horn will be blown, and whoever is in the heavens and whoever is on the earth will fall
> dead, except whom Allah wills."* — a **doubled** construction: heaven-dwellers are a grammatically
> independent subject of `ṣaʿiqa`.

> **٢:٣٢** قَالُواْ سُبۡحَٰنَكَ لَا عِلۡمَ لَنَآ إِلَّا مَا عَلَّمۡتَنَآ
> *"They said: Exalted are You; we have no knowledge except what You have taught us."* — the angels,
> in their own words. This single verse closes 27:65.

**The death check [data].** Every ayah where an angel-lemma and the root `mwt` co-occur: **six —
2:161, 6:93, 6:111, 10:31, 25:3, 32:11.** In all six the angel is death's **agent or witness**, never
its patient (32:11: *"the angel of death will take you"*). **No verse states that an angel dies.** No
verse states an angel is immortal either, and the universal perishing statements do not settle it:
55:26 `kullu man ʿalayhā fān` is scoped to the earth (`ʿalayhā`); 28:88 `kullu shayʾin hālikun illā
wajhah` is too total to decide anything about a species.

**Verdict, with confidence.**

1. **Nothing in the thirteen is *incompatible* with the angels-only reading. Confidence: high.**
   Twelve of thirteen are positively confirmed elsewhere of angels, often verbatim. Anyone claiming
   the `man` verses *require* a third population is overstating.
2. **One is not *confirmed* by it. Confidence: high.** 39:68 predicates death of heaven-dwellers,
   and the Quran never elsewhere predicates death of an angel. The angels-only reading does not
   *absorb* 39:68; it *extends* to cover it, teaching what no other verse teaches.
3. **The residue is underdetermination, not contradiction. Confidence: high.** 39:68 is silent
   between "angels die too" and "the heavens hold more than angels." **[interp]** A reader who wants
   the second may hold it; he cannot claim the verse states it.

That cuts against the enthusiast in the main, and against the dismisser in one place.

## 4. 42:29 — the irreducible verse

> **٤٢:٢٩** وَمِنۡ ءَايَٰتِهِۦ خَلۡقُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِ وَمَا بَثَّ **فِيهِمَا** مِن دَآبَّةࣲ
> وَهُوَ عَلَىٰ **جَمۡعِهِمۡ** إِذَا يَشَآءُ قَدِيرٌ
> *"And of His signs is the creation of the heavens and the earth, and what He has scattered
> throughout **them both** of creatures — and He is able to gather **them** whenever He wills."*

**[data]** `fīhimā` is tagged `PRON: 3D` — dual: the creatures are in **both**. The three parallel
dispersal verses do not do this (2:164, 31:10 use `fīhā`, `3FS`; 45:4 has no locative pronoun), so
42:29 is the only verse placing `dābba` in the heavens, and all four translators render it that way.
At lemma level `dābba` is the Quran's **most heaven-attached being-word — 5/18 = 27.8%**, against
angels 8.1%, `bashar` 8.1%, `nās` 6.2%, jinn 2.6%, `insān` 2.3%.

**Why this survives even the angels-only reading of the thirteen [data]:** the Quran never calls an
angel a `dābba`. It puts the two side by side as separate coordinated items:

> **١٦:٤٩** وَلِلَّهِۤ يَسۡجُدُۤ مَا فِي ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَا فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ مِن دَآبَّةࣲ **وَٱلۡمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ**
> *"And to Allah prostrates whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth of creatures,
> **and the angels**…"*

Saheeh, Pickthall and Khattab read `min dābbatin` as covering **both** clauses — a second verse
tending 42:29's way; **Arberry restricts the creatures to the earth**. A real 3-1 split, reported
not adjudicated. What is *not* split is the coordination: `dābba` and `al-malāʾika` are two items
joined by `wa-`, not one category containing the other. **[data]**

**What 42:29 will bear:** that God dispersed creatures through the heavens as well as the earth,
using a creature-word never applied to angels, and that they fall under a gathering — `jamʿihim`,
`3MP`, the agreement Arabic uses for rational or personal referents. **[data]**

**What it will not bear:** any description. **[data]** Across all 18 `dābba` occurrences, no verse
says what a heavenly creature looks like, does, eats, whether it reasons, or whether it is
accountable. The `3MP` is not proof of humanness — 6:38 uses the identical `3MP` for earth-fauna and
birds once called "communities" gathered to their Lord, and 6:38 **restricts itself to `fī al-arḍ`**,
so its community-status cannot transfer to 42:29 without inference.

**42:29 is one clause of location and one of fate. That is the entire content.**

## 5. The asymmetry, named precisely

Put the two ledgers side by side. **[data]** throughout:

| The Quran has | The Quran does not have |
|---|---|
| A word for creatures in the heavens — `dābba`, 42:29, dual pronoun | Any plural of `arḍ`. 461 occurrences, one singular lemma |
| A rational-being pronoun for heaven-dwellers — `man`, 13 times | Any verse locating a human in the heavens (0 of 19 genuine co-occurrences) |
| Two named non-human rational species, jinn and angels | A third rational species. Every candidate — `al-rūḥ`, Throne-bearers, `al-malaʾ al-aʿlā`, Hārūt and Mārūt, Iblīs — resolves to angel, jinn, or an individual/venue |
| Seven layered heavens, `ṭibāqan` | Contents for six of the seven. Stars are confined to the lowest heaven (41:12, 67:5, 37:6); heavens two to seven get only an unspecified `amr` |
| A dwelling clause for humanity — `fī al-arḍi mustaqarr` (2:36, 7:24) | A second dwelling clause anywhere |

**The asymmetry is total and runs one way.** The Quran had the vocabulary to say humans live
elsewhere — a plural of "earth," a `mustaqarr` in the heavens, a `man fī al-samāwāt` glossed as
`bashar` — and used none of it, while freely using creature- and rational-being vocabulary off the
earth. **[interp]** Not the profile of a text that never considered the question.

Two verses often cited in support are not support. 65:12 says seven heavens *"and of the earth, the
like of them"* — it does not repeat "seven," does not say "planets," and names no living thing; its
purpose clause is `li-taʿlamū anna-llāha ʿalā kulli shayʾin qadīr`. And 55:33's challenge to pass
beyond the heavens' regions uses `tanfudhū` — pierce through to the far side, a root found only in
that one verse — inside a judgment scene answered at 55:35 by fire. A taunt, not an itinerary.
**[data]** The populated-earths version of 65:12 comes from a weak hadith plus, lately, a retrofit
onto TRAPPIST-1 — both **outside this project's evidence base**.

## 6. Why the silence might be principled — an inference, marked as one

**[interp] — inference throughout; weight assessed at the end.**

The Quran's human material is not merely earth-*located*; it is earth-*constituted*, in a closed
loop:

> **٢٠:٥٥** مِنۡهَا خَلَقۡنَٰكُمۡ وَفِيهَا نُعِيدُكُمۡ وَمِنۡهَا نُخۡرِجُكُمۡ تَارَةً أُخۡرَىٰ
> *"From it We created you, and into it We will return you, and from it We will extract you another
> time."*

Three verbs, one referent: earth as origin, grave, and resurrection-site in a single sentence. 71:17
adds the agricultural verb — God *sprouted* humanity from the ground, `anbatakum mina al-arḍi nabātā`
— and origin-material is uniformly terrestrial (clay, dust, water, sperm-drop). **[data]**

**The inference:** a second human population elsewhere would have to be made from a different ground,
buried in a different ground, and raised from a different ground. It would need its own 20:55. The
Quran's anthropology is not a set of facts about where people happen to live; it is a cycle bound to
one substrate. **[interp]** On this reading the silence about humans elsewhere is not an omission —
it is what the anthropology already implies.

**How much weight this bears: moderate, no more.** It argues from the *shape* of a pattern, and
patterns can have unstated exceptions. 20:55 addresses a specific audience — "you" — and an
addressee-scoped statement is not automatically a universal law of humanity. Someone could hold that
God made another humanity from another earth and did not mention it in a book addressed to this one;
nothing refutes that. What the pattern establishes is that such a belief gets **no support** from the
Quran's human material and runs against its grain. A reason to doubt, not a proof of impossibility.

## 7. What a reader may legitimately conclude — three tiers

**Tier 1 — What the text asserts.** **[data]**
- Rational beings exist in the heavens: 13 verses, `man` — they prostrate, glorify, obey, ask, are
  ignorant of the unseen, are terrified, and die.
- Creatures — `dābba` — were scattered through heavens and earth and will be gathered: 42:29, dual
  pronoun verified.
- Two non-human rational species exist: jinn (fire-made, accountable, given messengers) and angels
  (heaven-resident, unfailingly obedient).
- Humanity is from the earth, dwells on the earth, returns to the earth, and is raised from the earth.

**Tier 2 — What the text permits but does not assert.** **[interp]**
- That the heavens hold rational beings *other than* angels. 39:68 leaves room; nothing closes it.
- That 42:29's heavenly `dābba` are living organisms in a biological sense. The word is defined by
  locomotion (24:45); the verse describes nothing either way.
- That other worlds exist. 65:12's `mithlahunna` is genuinely underdetermined — "the like of the
  heavens" has borne readings from strata to continents to climate zones.
- That God created humans elsewhere and did not mention it. Unsupported, not excluded.

**Tier 3 — What the text neither asserts nor forbids.** **[data]** on the silence:
- That any of those beings are human, humanoid, or human-like. No verse says so or denies it.
- That any other world is inhabited, has prophets, or has scripture.
- That there are seven earths, or seven anythings with people on them. **Not a Quranic phrase —
  `arḍ` has no plural.**
- That the heavens' population is or is not exhausted by angels.

**The line, stated once so it can be held honestly:** a believer may think there is life elsewhere
and stand on 42:29 and the `man` verses — a supported reading. He may *speculate* that there are
humans elsewhere, but he is speculating from silence and against the grain of the Quran's terrestrial
anthropology, and he must not say the Quran teaches it. The scholar who said seven or eight
earth-like planets hold humans was not reading the Quran.

## What this settles

1. **Life beyond earth: yes.** Thirteen `man` verses and 42:29's dual pronoun — not a stretch or an
   apologetic reading, but what the grammar says.
2. **Humans beyond earth: no verse. Zero.** And no plural of "earth" to hang one on.
3. **The angels-only reading of the thirteen absorbs twelve of them cleanly** — the Quran elsewhere
   says angels lack knowledge (2:32), fear (21:28, 13:13, 16:50), and petition (40:7, 42:5).
4. **It does not absorb 39:68.** No verse says an angel dies; in all six angel+death ayahs the angel
   is the agent, never the patient. The residue is real and one verse wide.
5. **The residue is underdetermination, not evidence.** 39:68 does not name a non-angel. Do not
   inflate it into one.
6. **42:29 survives everything**, because `dābba` is never applied to angels (16:49 coordinates them
   separately) — but it yields location and fate only, no description.
7. **"Seven earths with people on them" is not in the Quran.** It is a weak hadith plus an ambiguous
   pronoun plus, lately, TRAPPIST-1 — all three outside this text's evidence base.
8. **The asymmetry answers the question:** the Quran is expansive about non-human life and completely
   silent about non-terrestrial humanity — and that silence sits inside an anthropology (20:55) that
   would have to be rebuilt from scratch to accommodate a second humanity anywhere else.
