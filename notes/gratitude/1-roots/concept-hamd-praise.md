# Ḥamd (praise): *al-ḥamdu li-llāh*, *bi-ḥamdi*, *al-Ḥamīd*, and how it meets *shukr*

## Headline finding

*Ḥamd* is the Quran's spoken form of thanks, but it is not only thanks. Humans say *al-ḥamdu li-llāh* spontaneously after a past act done "for us" (guidance, sons, rescue, grief removed, promise kept). They are also told to say it with no gift in view (27:59, 27:93, 37:182). Six times the exact formula *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn* appears, and five of those close something (a destruction, the Paradise call, a surah, the judgment, an argument). *Ḥamd* has no human imperative of its own; *shukr* does. The two roots share only 31:12 and 35:34, and in both the roots are crossed: the human uses one, God carries the other. **God is *ḥamīd* whether praised or not (*ghanī ḥamīd*, 10×).**

## 1. Stats (data-grounded)

`ḥmd`: 63 tokens, 61 ayahs: *ḥamd* 43, *ḥamīd* 17, plus *yuḥmadū* (3:188, wanting to be praised for what one did not do), *ḥāmidūn* (9:112) and *Aḥmad/maḥmūd* (61:6, 17:79). **Active human verb "to praise" and imperative "praise!": none.** Humans speak ḥamd only as a nominal sentence, or as the object of *sabbiḥ bi-ḥamdi*.

**Counts.** Six are commanded (*qul*/*fa-qul*: 17:111, 23:28, 27:59, 27:93, 29:63, 31:25). Six are spontaneous (the people of Paradise ×4, Abraham, David and Solomon). Five open a surah. The rest are narrated.

**With a reason (11).**
- *Creation* 6:1, 35:1; *the Book* 18:1; *ownership* 34:1.
- *Rescue* 23:28; *sons in old age* 14:39; *knowledge or favour* 27:15; *guidance* 7:43; *grief removed* 35:34; *promise kept* 39:74.
- **Every spontaneous human utterance except 10:10 has a perfect-tense "who did X *to us/me*."** Praise by a person arises from something done *to* them.
- **17:111 is the exception that matters.** Its reason is a set of *negations*: no son, no partner, no weakness. Praise there is for who He is, not for a gift.

**Without a reason (12).**
- 27:59, 27:93, 29:63 and 31:25 are commands. The last two answer the opponents' own admission: *you say God made the heavens and sends the rain; then say: praise to God* (*most do not know*). **[interpretive]** Praise is the conclusion of what they already concede.
- **Praise after judgment on wrongdoers:** 6:45 (*they were cut off. And praise to God*) and 39:75. So *ḥamd* is also said when justice is done, not only when a gift arrives.

**The formula and its closings.** *Al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn* occurs exactly 6×: 1:2 (opens), then 6:45, 10:10, 37:182, 39:75, 40:65. **[interpretive]** The last five are the line that ends a scene.

## 3. *Bi-ḥamdi*: 15 ayahs

- **Everything:** thunder with the angels (13:13); *there is nothing but glorifies by His praise* (17:44, whose ending is *He is Forbearing, Forgiving*); creation at 64:1; the angels (2:30, 39:75, 40:7, 42:5).
- **Believers, when reminded by the verses:** they fall in prostration *and* glorify with praise (32:15). Compare 15:98 (prostrate).
- **Humans on the Day:** they respond with praise (17:52).
- **The Prophet, commanded 7×** (15:98, 20:130, 25:58, 40:55, 50:39, 52:48, 110:3). **Times:**
  - before sunrise and before sunset (20:130, 50:39);
  - hours of the night and the ends of the day (20:130);
  - evening and morning (40:55);
  - on rising (52:48);
  - part of the night, after prostration, at the stars' setting (50:40, 52:49).
- **What comes with it.**
  - Four of the seven follow *be patient over what they say* (20:130, 40:55, 50:39, 52:48), so the command to praise comes under insult, not under gift.
  - 25:58 follows reliance on the Ever-Living.
  - **Istighfār 4×: 40:7** (angels ask forgiveness for believers), **40:55** (ask forgiveness for *your sin*), **42:5** (angels, for those on earth), **110:3** (after victory: *glorify with praise and seek His forgiveness, He is ever Accepting of repentance*).
  - **Order is fixed:** praise first, forgiveness second in 40:55 and 110:3.

## 4. Praise in the hereafter

7:43, 10:10, 35:34, 39:74-75, 34:1 (*to Him belongs praise in the Hereafter*), 28:70 (*in the first and the last*).
- **Paradise speech in 10:10 has three parts:** *subḥānaka llāhumma*, then greeting *salām*, then *the last of their call: al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn*.
- **Data:** every recorded Paradise sentence of the people (7:43, 35:34, 39:74) is a *ḥamd*, each with a reason. Their last call (10:10) has none.
- **[interpretive]** The Quran's Paradise speech is praise, not petition; none is recorded there.
- **Is there *shukr* in Paradise? Only God's.** In the three Paradise-setting shukr ayahs I found, the speaker of thanks is God. He is *Shakūr* in the mouth of the people (35:34) and in 35:30 (*He gives them their wages in full and increases from His bounty*). Their striving is *mashkūr* (76:22). **No person in Paradise says *shakara* or *ashkur*.** What they say is *ḥamd*.

## 5. *Ḥamd* vs *shukr*: tested

**The traditional claim (lexicography, not text):** *ḥamd* is broader: it is praise for who He is and for gifts. *Shukr* is thanks for gifts only.

1. **Does ḥamd appear with no gift in view? Yes.** 17:111 (negations), 2:30 and 13:13 (creatures' praise), 30:18 (*at night and when you are at noon*), 45:36, 64:1 (ownership and dominion), the Prophet's *bi-ḥamdi* under insult (20:130). The claim holds on this side.
2. **Does shukr appear with no gift in view?** In the purpose clauses and commands in the Phase-0 lists, no: each follows a gift, an easing or a rescue. **Edge case:** *Shākir ʿalīm* (2:158) and *mashkūr* (17:19) have God thanking a human *deed*, a gift in the other direction. I did not find *shukr* attached to a bare attribute of God.
3. **Do they share an ayah? Yes, twice, but in a way that matters.**
   - **31:12:** *Be grateful (ishkur) to God… whoever is grateful, is grateful for himself; whoever is ungrateful, God is ghanī **ḥamīd**.* It is the name *Ḥamīd*, not the act of praise.
   - **35:34:** *They will say **al-ḥamdu** li-llāh… our Lord is Forgiving, **Shakūr**.* Here *shakūr* is God's name.
   - **Phase-0 refinement:** there is no ayah in which one human act of *ḥamd* and one human act of *shukr* stand together. In the two shared ayahs the roots are crossed: **people thank (31:12) and God is Praiseworthy; people praise (35:34) and God is Thanker.**

> وَقَالُواْ ٱلۡحَمۡدُ لِلَّهِ ٱلَّذِيٓ أَذۡهَبَ عَنَّا ٱلۡحَزَنَۖ إِنَّ رَبَّنَا لَغَفُورࣱ شَكُورٌ (35:34)
> Saheeh: "Praise to Allah, who has removed from us [all] sorrow. Indeed, our Lord is Forgiving and Appreciative."

> وَمَن يَشۡكُرۡ فَإِنَّمَا يَشۡكُرُ لِنَفۡسِهِۦۖ وَمَن كَفَرَ فَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ غَنِيٌّ حَمِيدࣱ (31:12, part)
> Saheeh: "whoever is grateful is grateful for [the benefit of] himself. And whoever denies [His favor] - then indeed, Allah is Free of need and Praiseworthy."

4. **[interpretive] How is a person meant to say thanks?**
   - The text gives the verb *shukr* with imperatives (7 commands) and with deed (*work in thanks*, 34:13; worship, 2:172), but never a spoken "I thank You."
   - It gives the *words*, *al-ḥamdu li-llāh*, with a "who… to us" on top, but no imperative of the root.
   - So thanks looks like a pair: *shukr* names the stance and the doing; *ḥamd* is what is said. The sentences that name the reason (7:43, 14:39, 35:34, 39:74) are the closest thing the Quran records to a spoken thank-you.
   - The "ḥamd is broader" claim survives; the text adds that *ḥamd* is the form humans *speak*.

## 6. *Al-Ḥamīd*

*Ghanī ḥamīd* 10×:
- **After turning away, disbelief or stinginess (6):** 4:131, 14:8 (*if you and everyone on earth disbelieve*), 31:12, 57:24, 60:6, 64:6.
- **After a command to give (1):** 2:267, *spend from the good things… know that God is ghanī ḥamīd.*
- **After ownership or need (3):** 22:64, 31:26, 35:15 (*you are the ones in need of God; God is the ghanī, the ḥamīd*).
- **Others:** *ʿazīz ḥamīd* 3× (14:1, 34:6, 85:8), always on the path, revelation or persecuted believers. 22:24: the people of Paradise were guided *to the path of the Ḥamīd*. *Walī ḥamīd* 42:28 follows rain after despair. *Ḥakīm ḥamīd* 41:42 follows the Book. *Ḥamīd majīd* 11:73 is spoken to the household of Abraham, whose old-age son parallels Abraham's own praise at 14:39.

**What it implies.** *Ghanī* before *ḥamīd* says God's praiseworthiness does not rest on being praised. In the six turning-away cases, the ayah gives no loss to Him. **[interpretive]** Human praise then adds nothing to God, but 31:12 states the benefit lands on the thanker.

## 7. Al-Fātiḥa (short)

1:2 attaches praise to *rabb al-ʿālamīn*, then 1:3 *al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm*, then 1:4 *mālik yawm al-dīn*: lordship, mercy, the Day. No "who gave us" clause. **[interpretive]** The seven verses move from praise in the third person to speech *to* Him at 1:5, and the praise is the part that is not a request. The petition (1:6, guidance) comes only after it.

## Negative space

- No imperative *iḥmad* and no active "I praise You."
- No *shukr* beside *bi-ḥamdi* or *tasbīḥ* in any ayah.
- No person in Paradise says *shakara*.
- The Quran sets no count for *ḥamd*; it names times (section 3). Counted formulas are hadith, outside this evidence.

## For practice

- When something has just been given, the Quran's sentence is: *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi lladhī…* followed by what He did for you. Say what He did (7:43, 14:39, 35:34, 39:74).
- When there is nothing to name, say the short form anyway (27:93, 37:182). Praise does not wait for a gift.
- In a hard stretch, praise before sunrise, before sunset and in part of the night, with patience (20:130, 50:39-40).
- After you have praised, ask forgiveness. That is the pairing the text gives, in that order (40:55, 110:3).
- Your praise adds nothing to Him (*ghanī ḥamīd*); it is for you (31:12).
- Close a day the way Paradise closes its call: *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn* (10:10).
