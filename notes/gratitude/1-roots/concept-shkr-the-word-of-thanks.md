# shkr: the word of thanks

## Headline finding

*Shukr* is something humans do and God answers. No verb of thanking ever has God as its subject. He is only the Name *Shakūr/Shākir* (6×) and the one for whom effort is *mashkūr* (2×), always beside forgiveness, knowing or forbearance. Humans never say "I thank You" with this root: the only three first-person verbs ask to be *enabled* (27:19, 46:15) or report a *test* (27:40). Thanks takes God as *li-* object or the blessing as direct object, never an event. The six *shkr*/*kfr* ayahs promise **increase** (14:7) and **God's approval** (39:7). Whoever thanks, thanks *for himself*.

## 1. Lemma table (data-grounded: `$kr.json`, 75 tokens / 69 ayahs)

| Lemma | n | Subject | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *shakara* (verb) | 46 | humans only | 20 *tashkurūn*, 9 *yashkurūn*, 5 plural + 2 singular imperatives, 3 first person, 3 *man shakara* (27:40 ×2, 54:35), 4 perfects |
| *shākir* | 14 | 12 humans, **2 God** | humans: *al-shākirīn* (3:144, 3:145, 6:63, 6:53, 7:144, 7:189, 10:22, 39:66), *shākiran* (16:121, 76:3), 7:17, 21:80. God: 2:158, 4:147 |
| *shakūr* | 10 | 6 humans, **4 God** | humans: Noah (17:3), "few" (34:13), *ṣabbār shakūr* ×4 (14:5, 31:31, 34:19, 42:33). God: 35:30, 35:34, 42:23, 64:17 |
| *mashkūr* | 2 | effort of the believer | 17:19, 76:22 |
| *shukūr* | 2 | thanks as due from people | 25:62 (*desires thanks*), 76:9 (*we want from you no reward and no thanks*) |
| *shukr* | 1 | the family of David | 34:13 *iʿmalū … shukran* |

- **Subjects.** Every verb has humans (or *man*, "the one who") as subject. God appears only as the participle/intensive (*Shākir*, *Shakūr*) or as the one for whom effort is *mashkūr*.
- **Group addresses dominate**: 36 of 46 verbs are plural, 29 of them *tashkurūn*/*yashkurūn* in purpose clauses or complaints.
- **Objects of thanks, when stated**:
  - *li-* + God: 2:152 *lī*, 2:172 *li-llāh*, 29:17 and 34:15 *lahu*, 31:12 *li-llāh*, 31:14 *lī wa-li-wālidayka* (6 of the 7 imperatives).
  - Blessing as direct object: 16:114 *wa-shkurū niʿmata llāh*; 27:19, 46:15 *an ashkura niʿmataka*. Also *shākiran li-anʿumih* (16:121).
  - **No *bi-* or *ʿalā* ever.** Thanks goes *to* a person (*li-*), and *for* a thing only as the blessing itself in the accusative. Never "for the rescue" or "for the fruit": those are the occasion, not the object.
  - Most tokens have **no object** (the purpose clauses, few/most).
  - **People**: parents beside God (31:14) are the only human object commanded; 76:9 refuses people's thanks.

## 2. The first person (data-grounded)

Morphology confirms exactly **three** first-person tokens (27:19, 27:40, 46:15), all imperfect singular; no *nashkuru*. The plural vows (6:63, 7:189, 10:22) use *la-nakūnanna mina l-shākirīn*, "we will be among the grateful": a participle, in danger, followed by breaking (6:64, 10:23, 7:190).

**Panel 1: two near-identical requests (27:19, Solomon; 46:15, the man at forty)**

رَبِّ أَوۡزِعۡنِيٓ أَنۡ أَشۡكُرَ نِعۡمَتَكَ ٱلَّتِيٓ أَنۡعَمۡتَ عَلَيَّ وَعَلَىٰ وَٰلِدَيَّ وَأَنۡ أَعۡمَلَ صَٰلِحࣰا تَرۡضَىٰهُ

Saheeh (27:19): *My Lord, enable me to be grateful for Your favor which You have bestowed upon me and upon my parents and to do righteousness of which You approve.*
Khattab (46:15): *Inspire me to always be thankful for Your favours… and to do good deeds that please You.*

The clause is **word-for-word identical** in both ayahs. Beside thanks it asks:
1. **Work You approve** (*ṣāliḥan tarḍāhu*). Thanks is asked for together with a deed, as in 34:13's *work in thanks*.
2. **Parents are inside the blessing**: the favour is on *me and my parents*. It begins before the speaker.
3. They then **diverge**:
   - 27:19: *admit me by Your mercy among Your righteous servants*.
   - 46:15: *make my offspring righteous; I have repented to You (*innī tubtu ilayka*); I am of the Muslims*.

So in 46:15, the one extended first-person request for thanks sits in the same breath as **repentance**. **[interpretive]** The ask is not "I thank" but "make me able"; it is answered as a gift, not a performance.

The third first-person token is different (27:40, Panel 2).

## 3. The divine Thanker (data-grounded, then interpretive)

| Ayah | Name | What God "thanks" | Neighbours |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2:158 | *Shākir ʿalīm* | one who *volunteers good* (*taṭawwaʿa khayran*) | knowing |
| 4:147 | *Shākir ʿalīm* | *you thank and believe* (no punishment) | knowing |
| 35:30 | *Ghafūr shakūr* | reward in full, plus increase from His bounty | forgiving |
| 35:34 | *Ghafūr shakūr* | said by the people of Paradise, after "who removed grief" | forgiving |
| 42:23 | *Ghafūr shakūr* | *whoever earns a good deed, We increase its good* | forgiving |
| 64:17 | *Shakūr ḥalīm* | *a goodly loan*: He multiplies it and forgives | forbearing |
| 17:19, 76:22 | *mashkūr* | **effort** (*saʿy*) for the Hereafter by a believer | none |

- **What is "thanked"** is never a word, always something done: voluntary good, belief with thanks, a good deed, a loan, striving. The recipients of the effort are people (a loan; feeding the poor 76:8-9) as well as God.
- **Names in pairs**: *ghafūr shakūr* ×3, *shakūr ḥalīm* ×1, *shākir ʿalīm* ×2. Every use is paired. **Forgiving or forbearing + Thanking**: in 35:30 and 64:17 the forgiveness is named *with* the multiplying. Thanks and forgiveness are two sides of one response to the same deed.
- **Translators diverge** (2:158 0.717): *appreciative*, *responsive*, *all-thankful*.
- **[interpretive]** Without tafsīr, the text allows this much: the Name appears where a human has done something small, costly or unseen, and what follows is an **answer that exceeds it** (increase, multiplied, in full). It cannot be a debt, since the same God is *ghanī* (31:12). *Shakūr* reads as "God notices and returns more"; 39:7's *He approves it for you* is the nearest paraphrase. Whether it is "grateful" in the human sense, the text does not say.

**Panel 2: 27:40**

قَالَ هَٰذَا مِن فَضۡلِ رَبِّي لِيَبۡلُوَنِيٓ ءَأَشۡكُرُ أَمۡ أَكۡفُرُۖ وَمَن شَكَرَ فَإِنَّمَا يَشۡكُرُ لِنَفۡسِهِۦۖ وَمَن كَفَرَ فَإِنَّ رَبِّي غَنِيࣱّ كَرِيمࣱ

Saheeh: *This is from the favor of my Lord to test me whether I will be grateful or ungrateful. And whoever is grateful, his gratitude is only for himself. And whoever is ungrateful, my Lord is Free of need and Generous.*
Khattab: *…it is only for their own good… Self-Sufficient, Most Generous.*

Solomon first **names the source** (*min faḍli rabbī*), then calls the gift a **test** with two outcomes, and does not claim to pass. Qārūn's sentence (28:78) is the opposite.

## 4. The few and the most (data-grounded)

What precedes each:

| Ayah | Gift just named | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| 7:10 | earth established, livelihoods | creation |
| 23:78, 32:9, 67:23 | hearing, sight, hearts | creation (the body) |
| 34:13 | the family of David: what the jinn built; *work in thanks* | a given ability and labour |
| 2:243 | *those who fled death; God said "die", then revived them* | rescue/life |
| 10:60, 27:73 | God *dhū faḍl ʿalā l-nās* | no specific gift |
| 12:38 | Joseph: *not associating anything with God… is from God's faḍl* | **guidance** |
| 40:61 | night to rest, day to see | creation |
| 7:17 | Iblīs: *You will not find most of them grateful* | the claim itself |

- **All 5 "most do not thank" follow the word *faḍl* (bounty)**: 2:243, 10:60, 12:38, 27:73, 40:61, each time *wa-lākinna akthara… lā yashkurūn*. The gift is named as bounty, and the thanks is absent.
- **The four "little do you thank" are all creation**: land and the senses. It is **not** always creation: 2:243 is a revival, 12:38 is faith itself, and 34:13 is the work given to a household.

## 5. Thanks vs *kfr* (data-grounded, translators split)

Six ayahs contain both: 2:152, 14:7, 27:40, 31:12, 39:7, 76:3.

| Ayah | Saheeh | Khattab | Pickthall | Arberry |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2:152 | *deny Me* | *never be ungrateful* | *reject not Me* | *not ungrateful* |
| 14:7 | *deny* | *ungrateful* | *thankless* | *thankless* |
| 27:40 | ungrateful | ungrateful | ungrateful | ungrateful |
| 31:12 | *denies* | *ungrateful* | *refuseth* | *ungrateful* |
| 39:7 | *disbelieve(ing)* | *disbelieve* | *thankless* | *unthankful* |
| 76:3 | ungrateful | ungrateful | *disbelieving* | *unthankful* |

Only **27:40** has all four translators agreeing, because there *akfur* answers *ashkur* directly in the test. In the other five the translators disagree between *deny/disbelieve* and *be ungrateful*, so Phase 0's warning holds: the root does not decide. Context for ingratitude: *kafara bi-anʿum Allāh* (16:112) and *wa-hal nujāzī illā l-kafūr* (34:17, after Saba' were told to thank in 34:15).

**Outcomes**:
- **Thank**: remembered (2:152), increased (14:7), approved (39:7), the benefit one's own (27:40, 31:12), rewarded (3:144-145).
- **Not thank**: *My punishment is severe* (14:7), but also **God loses nothing**: *ghanī* (39:7, 27:40 *ghanī karīm*, 31:12 *ghanī ḥamīd*). Ingratitude hurts only the ungrateful.
- **76:3 is neutral**: God *guided* him to the way, *grateful or ungrateful*. The choice is left.

**Panel 3: 39:7**

وَلَا يَرۡضَىٰ لِعِبَادِهِ ٱلۡكُفۡرَۖ وَإِن تَشۡكُرُواْ يَرۡضَهُ لَكُمۡ

Saheeh: *He does not approve for His servants disbelief. And if you are grateful, He approves it for you.*
Khattab: *…nor does He approve of disbelief… if you become grateful ˹through faith˺, He will appreciate that.*

God's one explicit approval of thanks. Khattab adds *through faith*, not in the Arabic.

## 6. 14:7, *la-in shakartum la-azīdannakum* (data-grounded)

**Panel 4: 14:6-7**

وَإِذۡ تَأَذَّنَ رَبُّكُمۡ لَئِن شَكَرۡتُمۡ لَأَزِيدَنَّكُمۡۖ وَلَئِن كَفَرۡتُمۡ إِنَّ عَذَابِي لَشَدِيدࣱ

Saheeh: *And [remember] when your Lord proclaimed, "If you are grateful, I will surely increase you [in favor]; but if you deny, indeed, My punishment is severe."*
Khattab: *…I will certainly give you more. But if you are ungrateful…*

- **Who speaks.** Moses, to his people (14:6 *udhkurū niʿmata llāh*, the rescue from Pharaoh), reporting what *your* Lord (*rabbukum*) **proclaimed** (*taʾadhdhana*). God speaks in the first person ("I will increase"). The verb *taʾadhdhana* occurs only twice: here and 7:167, where it announces a punishment.
- **What is increased.** Unstated. The object is "you" (*-kum*). Saheeh adds *in favor*, Khattab *more*. The text names no category (wealth, land, faith).
- **Conditional and oath-marked.** The promise is conditional (*la-in shakartum*). The apodosis carries a *lām* plus the heavy *nūn* (*la-azīdannakum*), the emphatic oath-form, which 7:167 (*la-yabʿathanna*) uses too. The warning half has *la-shadīd* without the *nūn*. **[interpretive]** The positive half is the more emphatic.
- **Is it shown fulfilled?** Not directly. The text never narrates "they thanked, then He increased". Nearest cases:
  - the negative half **is** shown: Saba' told to thank (34:15), then undone (34:16-17);
  - the positive half is stated as a law: *We increase for him good therein* (42:23), *increase them from His bounty* (35:30), *We will reward the grateful* (3:144-145);
  - one narrated reward: Lot's family saved and *thus We reward whoever thanks* (54:34-35).

  

## Divergence and absences
- **Absent**: any thanks verb with God as subject; any first-person *shakartu*/*nashkuru*; any *shkr* + *sbḥ* ayah; any narrated increase after thanks; thanks by *bi-* or *ʿalā*.

## For practice
- When you want to thank God, the Quran's own words are a **request**: *awziʿnī an ashkura niʿmataka* (27:19, 46:15). Ask to be enabled to thank, and to do work He approves.
- Thank **to** God (*li-*) and, where it is natural, name the blessing itself (*niʿmataka*), including what came through your parents.
- Thanks is **work** (34:13), so pair the words with a deed done for Him. Asking for it and repenting can sit together (46:15).
- Thanks is **for yourself** (27:40, 31:12); God is not in need (39:7).
- Do not treat 14:7 as a formula for specific gain: it promises "increase" without saying what.
- Small, unseen good (a loan, a volunteered act: 2:158, 64:17) is what the Name *Shakūr* answers.
