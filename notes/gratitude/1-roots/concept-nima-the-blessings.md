# Niʿma, ālāʾ, faḍl, mann: the blessings

## Headline finding

The Quran's lists of blessings are mostly material (food, water, animals, ships, shelter), but the word *niʿma* itself attaches mostly to guidance, the Book, prophethood and rescue (27 of 49 ayahs; material goods 7). Four verbs are done to a blessing: *remember* (12 commands, almost all recalling a rescue or a choosing), *count* (impossible: 14:34 ends on man's ingratitude, 16:18 on God's forgiveness), *speak of* (93:11), and, only implied, *use it* (28:77). A blessing is lost by the receiver's stance, not God's whim (8:53). *Ālāʾ* is "denied", as signs are. *Faḍl* sits in all five "most do not thank" ayahs. *Mann* is God's alone to confer, and misuse of it by people is condemned.

## Stats (data-grounded)

- `nʿm`: 140 tokens. Blessing sense: *niʿma* noun **50 tokens / 49 ayahs** (3:103 twice) plus *anʿama* 17. Excluded: *anʿām* 33, *naʿīm* 17, *niʿma* "how excellent" 18, *nuʿma* 2.
- `ʾlw`: 34 *ālāʾ* (31 refrain, 7:69, 7:74, 53:55). The 3 verbal tokens are unrelated (oath, shortfall).
- `fḍl`: 104 tokens, 92 ayahs (*faḍl* 84). `mnn`: 27 tokens, 25 ayahs; 3 of these are *manna*.
- **Divergence**: 93:11 0.784 (high), 14:34 0.723, 16:18 0.697, 55:13 0.660, 8:53 0.660. Outside the core set, 74:6 scores 0.796.

## 1. The catalogue

**Table** (hand-classified; one primary category per verse in the listing passages 16:3-18, 16:80-81, 14:32-33, 55:2-12):

| Category | Verses | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Food and water | 6 | 16:10-11, 14:32, 55:10-12 |
| Guidance, Book, balance | 6 | 16:9, 16:16, 55:2, 55:7-9 |
| Animals | 4 | 16:5-8 |
| Night, day, sun, moon | 3 | 16:12, 14:33, 55:5 |
| Sea and ships | 2 | 16:14, 14:32 |
| Shelter, clothing | 2 | 16:80-81 |
| Body, senses, speech | 2 | 55:3-4 (senses: 16:78, outside) |
| Family | 0 here | 16:72 (spouses, children, grandchildren) |
| Security, union | 0 here | named only with *niʿma*: 3:103, 5:11, 33:9, 29:67 |
| Forgiveness | 0 here | ends 16:18; paired with completing at 48:2 |

**Most named in the lists: material provision**, 14 of 25 tagged verses (food and water 6, animals 4, ships 2, shelter 2). Guidance and the Book come next at 6.

**Most named by the word *niʿma* itself** (49 ayahs):
- guidance, Book, prophethood, religion, covenant **16** (2:40, 2:47, 2:122, 2:150, 2:231, 5:3, 5:6, 5:7, 5:20, 5:110, 12:6, 16:121, 48:2, 49:8, 52:29, 68:2);
- rescue, union, security **11**;
- unspecified **13**;
- material **7**;
- between people **2** (26:22, 92:19).

**[interpretive]** The lists point at the world and the word points at what was done for people. The material gifts are mostly called *rizq* and *āya* rather than *niʿma*.

**Is guidance a *niʿma*?** Yes, explicitly at 49:7-8: faith made beloved to you is *faḍlan mina llāh wa-niʿma*. At 5:3 *dīn* is "perfected" and *niʿma* "completed" in parallel (two verbs, possibly one thing). *Completing the niʿma* occurs 6×: 2:150, 5:3, 5:6, 12:6, 16:81, 48:2. In 2:150 and 48:2 it is listed *beside* guidance (and at 48:2 beside forgiveness), so it is not simply guidance. **The same catalogue ends in different purposes:** 16:14 grateful, 16:81 *tuslimūn* ("that you submit"), 16:15 guided, 16:17 reminded. Thanks is one outcome among several.

## 2. The verbs done to a blessing

**Remember (12).** Addressees: Children of Israel and Moses's people 5 (2:40, 2:47, 2:122, 5:20, 14:6); believers 5 (2:231, 3:103, 5:7, 5:11, 33:9); Jesus 1 (5:110); mankind 1 (35:3). What is recalled: election or covenant 5, deliverance 3 (14:6, 5:11, 33:9), union 1 (3:103), revelation 2 (2:231, 5:110), **provision only 1 (35:3)**. Remembering is followed by conduct each time: fulfil the covenant (2:40), fear God (5:7, 5:11, 2:231), do not divide (3:103). In 14:5-7 remembering leads to *la-in shakartum la-azīdannakum*. *Remember the ālāʾ* adds 7:69, 7:74 (to ʿĀd and Thamūd). At 43:13 remembering is a sentence said: *subḥāna lladhī sakhkhara lanā hādhā*.

**Count (14:34 / 16:18).** The verbs are *taʿuddū* (count) and *lā tuḥṣūhā* (cannot enumerate).

> 14:34 — وَإِن تَعُدُّواْ نِعۡمَتَ ٱللَّهِ لَا تُحۡصُوهَآ ۗ **إِنَّ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ لَظَلُومࣱ كَفَّارࣱ**
> Saheeh: "mankind is [generally] most unjust and ungrateful." Khattab: "truly unfair, ˹totally˺ ungrateful." Pickthall: "a wrong-doer, an ingrate."
> 16:18 — وَإِن تَعُدُّواْ نِعۡمَةَ ٱللَّهِ لَا تُحۡصُوهَآ ۗ **إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَغَفُورࣱ رَّحِيمࣱ**
> Saheeh: "Allah is Forgiving and Merciful." Khattab: "All-Forgiving, Most Merciful."

The clause is identical; only the ending differs. 14:34 follows *He gave you all you asked*, 16:18 follows the unanswerable "does He who creates equal one who does not?" Humans fail to enumerate only here and at 73:20 (the night, followed by *fa-tāba ʿalaykum* and *istaghfirū*, then *ghafūrun raḥīm*, the same ending as 16:18). Elsewhere God is the one who enumerates (36:12, 72:28, 78:29). **[interpretive]** The shortfall of thanks is met by forgiveness; the same fact read from the human side is *kaffār*. Forgiveness belongs to the blessing's own arithmetic.

**Speak (93:11).** **وَأَمَّا بِنِعۡمَةِ رَبِّكَ فَحَدِّثۡ**
> Saheeh: "report [it]." Pickthall: "of the bounty of thy Lord be thy discourse." Khattab: "proclaim the blessings." Arberry: "declare it."

*Ḥaddith* is the only imperative of Form II of `ḥdth`; the other Form II tokens are 2:76 (tell) and 99:4 (the earth tells her news). It is *narrating*, with no addressee named. Pickthall turns the verb into a noun; Khattab pluralises and raises the volume to "proclaim"; Saheeh adds "[it]". 93:6-8 (orphan, sheltered; astray, guided; poor, enriched) comes just before. **[interpretive]** The clearest candidate for what one tells is one's own particular rescue. The text names no formula.

**Use it (fourth verb)?** Only partly. 28:77 *aḥsin kamā aḥsana llāhu ilayk* is the sole *aḥsin kamā*, and it uses *aḥsana*, not *niʿma*. Closest *niʿma* cases: Moses, *bimā anʿamta ʿalayya fa-lan akūna ẓahīran lil-mujrimīn* (28:17) and Solomon's thanks joined to *aʿmala ṣāliḥan* (27:19). Spending from provision is commanded in 7 ayahs, always with *rizq*, never *niʿma* (2:254, 4:39, 13:22, 14:31, 35:29, 36:47, 57:7). So a "use it" duty is real, but the Quran files it under spending, not under blessing.

## 3. Blessing turned

- 14:28 *baddalū niʿmata llāhi kufran* (exchanged); 2:211 *yubaddil*.
- 16:83 *yaʿrifūna niʿmata llāh thumma yunkirūnahā*: **know, then reject.** Ingratitude here is not ignorance.
- 16:112: a city secure, provisioned, *kafarat bi-anʿumi llāh*, so it tasted hunger and fear. These two are exactly what Quraysh is told God removed at 106:3-4 (*aṭʿamahum min jūʿ wa-āmanahum min khawf*), where the asked response is *let them worship*.
- 39:8 *nasiya* (forgot whom he called on); 39:49 self-attribution *ʿalā ʿilm*.

> 8:53 — ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَمۡ يَكُ مُغَيِّرࣰ ا نِّعۡمَةً أَنۡعَمَهَا عَلَىٰ قَوۡمٍ حَتَّىٰ يُغَيِّرُواْ مَا بِأَنفُسِهِمۡ
> Saheeh: "until they change what is within themselves." Pickthall: "in their hearts." Khattab: "until they discontinue their faith." Arberry: "what was within themselves."

**Mechanism the text names (data-grounded):** God "does not change" a blessing; the default is continuity, and withdrawal follows a change *in the people*. In 8:52 and 8:54 that change is denying the signs and sins; the verb *yughayyirū mā bi-anfusihim* recurs at 13:11. The Arabic says *anfus* (selves), not hearts or faith: Pickthall and Khattab add a theology. Khattab also flattens 14:28 ("meet favours with ingratitude") and loses the *exchange*.

## 4. Ālāʾ

The refrain *fa-bi-ayyi ālāʾi rabbikumā tukadhdhibān* (55:13...77), 31×, addressed to a dual: humans and jinn.

> Saheeh: "deny." Pickthall: "Which is it, of the favours of your Lord, that ye deny?" Khattab: "favours ... both deny." Arberry: "O which of your Lord's bounties will you and you deny?"

All four use *deny*; none uses "be ungrateful" or "call a lie." *Kadhdhaba* takes *āyāt* as its commonest object (37 of 176 tokens); it is used of *Hell* at 55:43. **[interpretive]** The verb makes each blessing a claim about its Giver, like a sign, and refusing it is a false verdict, not a lapse of manners. The refrain asks "which?", demanding a specific denial. 53:55 changes the verb to *tatamārā* (doubt / dispute: Saheeh vs the rest).

**Between the refrains:** the first refrain comes only at 55:13, after the Quran, man's creation, speech, sun and moon, the balance, the earth and fruit have all been listed with no refrain between them. Then the two seas, pearl and ships (6 refrains: 13-25). Then **9 refrains after judgement material** (28-45): perishing, "We will attend to you", the flame, the split sky, the criminals, Hell. Then 16 refrains in the two pairs of gardens (47-77). **Yes: punishment is followed by the refrain**, 7 times directly (32-45). **[interpretive]** The warning is itself counted among the blessings.

## 5. Faḍl and mann

**Faḍl.** 12 ayahs share a verse with `shkr`: 2:243, 10:60, 12:38, 16:14, 27:40, 27:73, 28:73, 30:46, 35:12, 35:30, 40:61, 45:12. **All five "most do not thank" ayahs (2:243, 10:60, 12:38, 27:73, 40:61) contain *faḍl*, and *tabtaghū min faḍlih* sits in 5 of the 6 service-of-creation purpose clauses.** *Niʿma* co-occurs with `shkr` in 7 ayahs, *mann* with *shākirīn* only at 6:53. **[interpretive]** *Faḍl* is surplus, what exceeds due, and it is what people seek (*tabtaghū*); *niʿma* is what is remembered, counted, exchanged.

**Mann.** God as subject (11): 3:164 the Messenger, 4:94, 12:90, 14:11, 20:37, 28:5, 28:82, 37:114, 49:17, 52:27, 6:53. The recipient's own sentences are *Allāhu ʿalaynā* (12:90), *lawlā an manna llāhu ʿalaynā* (28:82), *fa-manna llāhu ʿalaynā* (52:27). A person as subject is nearly always reproved: 2:262, 2:264 (reminders cancel charity), 26:22 (Pharaoh), 49:17, 74:6 (*lā tamnun tastakthir*, divergence 0.796). 38:39 is the exception (grant or withhold).

**[interpretive]** The receiver's posture: name the favour as the reason for one's condition, as in rescue, survival, Paradise. Never turn it into a claim on the giver. The people at 49:17 count their Islam as a favour *to* the Prophet. The answer reverses it: God has done you the favour by guiding you.

## 6. 102:8

*Thumma la-tusʾalunna yawmaʾidhin ʿani n-naʿīm*. Renderings: "pleasure" (Saheeh, Pickthall); "worldly pleasures" (Khattab); "true bliss" (Arberry).
- *Naʿīm* has 17 tokens; the other 16 are Paradise (e.g. 56:12, 83:24). 102:8 is the only one where it is asked about, which explains the split.
- The Quran asks about: a message sent (7:6), deeds or inventions (15:92-93, 16:56, 16:93, 37:24), the covenant (17:34, 33:15), and **hearing, sight and heart (17:36)**: the same senses given in 16:78 *that you may be grateful*.
- 102:1 frames it as rivalry in increase.

Verdict: the text says one will be asked about the *naʿīm*, and nothing further, so it does not tell us what is asked. **[interpretive]** Blessing and accountability are the same gift seen twice.

## Negative space

- No prescribed form of words of thanks for any blessing, except 43:13.
- No *shukr* verb attached to *ālāʾ*.
- Forgiveness and *niʿma* share an ayah only at 48:2 (forgive, complete, guide).
- No ranking among blessings.

## Phase-0 correction

The structurals give `niʿma` 68. That mixes in 18 tokens of *niʿma* "how excellent" (*niʿma l-ʿabd*...); the blessing noun is **50**.

## For practice

- When recalling a blessing, name a specific rescue or choosing (an enemy turned friend, a danger passed), as the 12 commands do; they seldom stop at food.
- Tell someone, not only God (93:11), without making it a favour you hold over them (2:264, 49:17).
- Do not try to finish the list. Stop where 14:34 and 16:18 stop, and let forgiveness take the remainder.
- Say, after the benefit is in hand, the line 43:13 gives for riding: *glory to Him who subjected this to us*.
- Ask what each gift obliges you to, as in 2:40, 28:17 and 28:77.
- Do not deny a specific blessing; "which of them?" is the Quran's own question.

## Orchestrator addendum (verified 2026-10-22)

- **"No prescribed words of thanks except 43:13" is too narrow.** The Quran prescribes or models words for meeting a gift in at least four places:
  - **43:13-14**: on mounting a ship or animal, *remember your Lord's blessing* and say *subḥāna lladhī sakhkhara lanā hādhā wa mā kunnā lahu muqrinīn, wa innā ilā rabbinā la-munqalibūn*. The commanded words for remembering a blessing are **glorification (tasbīḥ)** and a statement of return to God.
  - **23:28-29**: on boarding the ark, Noah is told to say *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi lladhī najjānā…* and then to ask *rabbi anzilnī munzalan mubārakan*. Praise, then a request.
  - **18:39**: *mā shāʾa llāh, lā quwwata illā bi-llāh*, given as what the garden-owner *should have said*.
  - **27:19 / 46:15**: *rabbi awziʿnī an ashkura niʿmataka*.
  The first two matter for Phase 2: **the Quran's own words for a blessing in hand are tasbīḥ and ḥamd, not a `shkr` sentence.**
- **Phase-0 count corrected**: the blessing noun *niʿma* is 50 tokens / 49 ayahs. The lemma `niEoma` (18) is the praise verb *niʿma*, "how excellent" (e.g., *niʿma l-mawlā*).
