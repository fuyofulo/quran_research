# How the Quran teaches gratitude: the model and three stress tests

## Headline finding

The Quran teaches thanks as a response to God, and to parents beside Him, for ordinary repeated gifts, for rescue and for easing. Its spoken signs are praise, glorification, *takbīr* and *mā shāʾa llāh*. Its substance is using the gift as given and answering it with work and worship. No verse lets thanks be words alone: the only thanks spoken entirely as words are vows, and all of them are broken. Thanks is not kept for ease: the grateful are rewarded for standing firm in defeat (3:144). 14:7's "increase" is addressed to a people and narrated only in its negative half.

## 1. The model (data-grounded unless marked)

| Part | What the text gives |
|---|---|
| **Object** | God, with *li-* (6 of 7 imperatives). The blessing itself is the direct object at 16:114, 27:19 and 46:15. One human object: **parents, in the same verb as God** (31:14). The righteous of 76:9 want no thanks from the people they feed. |
| **Occasions** | Mostly ordinary and repeated: 22 of the 43 gift-naming `shkr` ayahs are food, sea, night and day, or the senses. Then the eased law (2:185, 5:6), rescue when few (3:123, 8:26), one forgiveness (2:52) and one second life (2:56). **Never an affliction.** |
| **Spoken** | **Never "I thank You" with `shkr`.** The words the text gives for a gift in hand are: *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi lladhī…* naming the act (23:28, 14:39, 35:34); *subḥāna lladhī sakhkhara lanā hādhā* (43:13); *li-tukabbirū llāha ʿalā mā hadākum* (2:185, 22:37); *mā shāʾa llāh, lā quwwata illā bi-llāh* (18:39); *hādhā min faḍli rabbī* (27:40). Also the request *rabbi awziʿnī an ashkura niʿmataka* (27:19, 46:15), and *fa-ḥaddith*, "tell of it" (93:11). |
| **Done** | **Use the gift**: *eat… and thank* (2:172, 16:114, 34:15; `ʾkl` is in 7 of the 69 `shkr` ayahs). *Seek His bounty* (5 of 6 creation clauses). *Mention the Name, eat, feed the needy* (22:36). *Take what I have given you* (7:144). **Answer it**: with work (34:13), worship (2:172, 16:114, 29:17, 39:66), fear of God (3:123) and by not turning back (3:144). Outside the root: *do good as God has done good to you* (28:77). |
| **Companions** | Patience (*ṣabbār shakūr* ×4). Remembrance (`dhkr` in 5 `shkr` ayahs: 2:152, 8:26, 14:5, 22:36, 25:62). Faith, named *after* thanks (4:147). *Taqwā* (3:123). The address that commands thanks at 2:152 turns at once to patience at 2:153. |
| **Fruit** | It is *for himself* (27:40, 31:12). God approves it (39:7). He increases (14:7), rewards (3:144-145, 54:35) and remembers (2:152). He loses nothing when it is missing (*ghanī*, 31:12, 39:7). He answers as *Shakūr*, never as the subject of a `shkr` verb. |
| **Rarity** | *Few of My servants* (34:13). *Little do you thank* ×4. *Most do not thank* ×5, each right after the word *faḍl* (bounty). Iblīs predicted it (7:17). After Saba' he *found his assumption true* about them (34:20) **[interpretive link]**. |
| **Opposite** | **An act or a sentence**: <br>• denying or exchanging the gift (14:28, 16:112); <br>• crediting oneself, in five sentences (28:78, 39:49, 41:50, 18:35-36, 11:10); <br>• rescue, then forgetting (9 passages); <br>• *knowing it, then denying it* (16:83); <br>• asking for less (34:19). |

**[interpretive] The model in one line.** Thanks works as a loop, in four steps:
1. The gift is received as *from Him*, named with God as the verb's subject.
2. It is used for what its verse says it is for.
3. It is marked aloud with praise or glorification.
4. It is answered with worship and righteous work.

God then answers the answer, as *Shakūr*. Ingratitude breaks the loop at one of three points:
- **the source is forgotten**, as by those rescued at sea;
- **the source is relocated**: *because of my knowledge*;
- **the gift is refused** (34:19).

## 2. Stress test 1: "Thanks is a deed, not only a word"

### Is thanks ever satisfied by speech alone?

- **Human `shkr` sentences: six.**
  - Three are a request or a test: 27:19, 46:15, 27:40.
  - Three are vows: *la-nakūnanna mina sh-shākirīn* (6:63, 10:22, 7:189). **All three are broken** (6:64, 10:23, 7:190).
  - So the only thanks the Quran shows made entirely of words is the vow, and every vow fails.
- **The nearest approved speech-alone is Paradise.** Its people say *al-ḥamdu li-llāh* (7:43, 10:10, 35:34, 39:74), and nothing more is asked of them.
  - This is never called *shukr*.
  - Twice the reply points back to past work: *for what you used to do* (7:43); *excellent is the reward of the workers* (39:74).
- **Speech comes with acts.** 93:11 (*tell of it*) follows two acts (93:9-10). The spoken *ḥamd* of David and Solomon (27:15) belongs to the household later told *work in thanks* (34:13). **[interpretive]** The text never says the words were not enough. It simply sets a deed after them.
- **Speech is not optional either.**
  - 18:39 rebukes a *missing* sentence: *why did you not say…?*
  - All five self-crediting ingrates fail by a sentence.
  - Words count on both sides.

### Deeds called thanks with no speech

- **34:13**: the work itself.
- **3:144-145**: the grateful are those who do not turn back.
- **54:35**: Lot's family are saved: *thus do We reward whoever thanks*. No word of theirs is recorded.
- **16:121**: Abraham's *shākiran* stands in a list of states: devout, upright, not an associator (16:120).

> **34:13** ٱعۡمَلُوٓاْ ءَالَ دَاوُۥدَ شُكۡرࣰاۚ وَقَلِيلࣱ مِّنۡ عِبَادِيَ ٱلشَّكُورُ
> Saheeh: *Work, O family of David, in gratitude. And few of My servants are grateful.* Arberry: *Labour… in thankfulness.* Khattab: *Work gratefully.* Pickthall: *Give thanks, O House of David!*

### What *shukran* allows

The morphology gives an indefinite accusative noun (QAC `N INDEF ACC`) and assigns it no syntactic role. The four translators split three ways:
- **purpose**: *in gratitude*, *in thankfulness*;
- **state**: *gratefully*;
- **content**: the work *is* the thanks. Pickthall folds *work* into *give thanks*.

**The passage leans one way (data-grounded).** In 34:11-13 the verb *ʿamila* takes the thing done as its accusative three other times:
- *iʿmal sābighātin*, "make coats of mail" (34:11);
- *iʿmalū ṣāliḥan*, "do righteousness" (34:11);
- *yaʿmalūna lahu mā yashāʾ*, "they made for him what he willed" (34:13).

By that local pattern, *shukran* is the thing done: thanks is *done* the way righteousness is *done*. The grammar does not rule out purpose or state.
- Under every reading, thanks is tied to the work. Under none is it speech.
- Under the purpose reading, thanks is the motive and the work is its form.

**Verdict: holds, restated.** *Thanks is a deed and also a word, but never the word alone.* The text treats words as a required sign (18:39), never as the whole. The only thanks made of words alone is the vow, which is the text's standard picture of failure.

## 3. Stress test 2: "Patience is for hardship, thanks for ease"

**For the split (data-grounded).** The occasions for thanks are gifts. The trial-list of 2:155-157 (fear, hunger, loss) asks for patience and *innā li-llāh*, with no `shkr`. The tendency is real.

### Thanks after hardship

- **3:123 and 8:26** are told to people looking back on danger: Badr *while you were few*; *few, oppressed, afraid… He sheltered you*. What they thank for is the rescue.
- **14:5-7** places the law of increase after the *days of God*: Pharaoh's torment, then the rescue (14:6).
- **2:52** follows a sin, the calf, not a hardship.

### Thanks *in* hardship

> **3:144** أَفَإِيْن مَّاتَ أَوۡ قُتِلَ ٱنقَلَبۡتُمۡ عَلَىٰٓ أَعۡقَٰبِكُمۡۚ … وَسَيَجۡزِي ٱللَّهُ ٱلشَّٰكِرِينَ
> Saheeh: *So if he was to die or be killed, would you turn back on your heels?… but Allah will reward the grateful.*

Here the grateful are those who stand firm at the worst news, with no gift in view. Two ayahs later the same holding firm is called patience: *they did not weaken… and God loves the patient* (3:146). In one battle, the same conduct gets both names.

### Patience in ease

> **11:10-11** وَلَئِنۡ أَذَقۡنَٰهُ نَعۡمَآءَ بَعۡدَ ضَرَّآءَ مَسَّتۡهُ لَيَقُولَنَّ ذَهَبَ ٱلسَّيِّـَٔاتُ عَنِّيٓۚ إِنَّهُۥ لَفَرِحࣱ فَخُورٌ ۝ إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ صَبَرُواْ وَعَمِلُواْ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ
> Saheeh: *if We give him a taste of favor after hardship… he is exultant and boastful — except for those who are patient and do righteous deeds.*

- Patience is the exception to *exulting in relief*, not only to despairing in loss (11:9).
- **28:80**: faced with Qārūn's display of wealth, *none are granted it except the patient*.
- **76:8, 76:12**: feeding others *despite love of it* is rewarded *for what they patiently endured*.

### The four *ṣabbār shakūr* contexts

Each one holds ease and danger in a single situation:
- **14:5**: the *days of God*: torment, then rescue.
- **31:31**: ships sail *by the favour of God*, which is ease. The next ayah brings *waves like canopies*.
- **34:19**: a prosperous people undone by asking for distance.
- **42:33**: ships like mountains, which God could becalm or wreck (42:34), *but He pardons much*.

**[interpretive]** The sign is offered to one person who can read a voyage that may turn at any moment. It is not a division between two kinds of people.

**Verdict: fails as a rule, survives as a tendency.**
- Thanks is for the gift. That includes rescue *from* hardship, and standing firm *within* it (3:144).
- Patience is for holding firm, and that includes holding firm *in ease* (11:11).
- *Ṣabbār shakūr* is one person facing one situation that can turn.
- Thanks *for* the hardship itself is never asked.

## 4. Stress test 3: "If you thank, I will increase you"

> **14:7** وَإِذۡ تَأَذَّنَ رَبُّكُمۡ لَئِن شَكَرۡتُمۡ لَأَزِيدَنَّكُمۡۖ وَلَئِن كَفَرۡتُمۡ إِنَّ عَذَابِي لَشَدِيدࣱ
> Saheeh: *If you are grateful, I will surely increase you [in favor]; but if you deny, indeed, My punishment is severe.* Khattab: *I will certainly give you more.*

### A law about peoples (data-grounded)

- Moses speaks it to his people, in the plural, while recalling a rescue of the whole people (14:6).
- Its negative half is narrated only of communities:
  - Saba' (34:15-17);
  - the town (16:112);
  - those who *settled their people in the abode of ruin* (14:28).
- The mechanism is stated of a *qawm*, a people: God does not change a blessing until they change what is in themselves (8:53, 13:11).

### The individual (data-grounded)

- **`zyd` (increase) shares an ayah with `shkr` only three times**: 14:7, 35:30 and 42:23.
- In the other two, **God is the *Shakūr***, and the increase answers a deed done by individuals:
  - **35:29-30**: those who recite, pray and spend *secretly and openly* are paid *their rewards in full, and more from His bounty*, in *a trade that will never perish*;
  - **42:23**: *whoever earns a good deed, We increase for him good therein*. This comes right after the gardens of 42:22.
- **3:144-145** promises the grateful a reward. It sits beside both worlds' rewards, given to whoever wants them.
- **The one narrated reward for thanks is a rescue**: Lot's family, called *a blessing from Us* (54:34-35).
- **Abraham** is called *grateful*, then *chosen, guided, given good in this world* (16:121-122). No particle of cause joins the two.

> **17:19-20** فَأُوْلَٰٓئِكَ كَانَ سَعۡيُهُم مَّشۡكُورࣰا ۝ كُلࣰّا نُّمِدُّ هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ وَهَٰٓؤُلَآءِ مِنۡ عَطَآءِ رَبِّكَ
> Saheeh: *it is those whose effort is ever appreciated. To each [category] We extend — to these and to those — from the gift of your Lord.*

Worldly giving reaches both groups. Ease and narrowing are both tests (89:15-16). The ungrateful can be given respite *so that they increase in sin* (3:178).

**Verdict: both, on different evidence.**
- **For peoples**, 14:7 is a law, shown only in its negative half.
- **For the individual**, the reward of thanks is framed as God's own *shukr*: more than the deed, placed mainly in the hereafter. The text explicitly denies that worldly abundance follows thanks (17:20).
- **What the text does not show**:
  - what is increased;
  - when;
  - anyone who thanked and was then given more worldly goods, with the text marking the thanks as the cause.
- **[interpretive]** Reading 14:7 as a formula for personal prosperity goes beyond the text.

## 5. What the Quran does not teach about thanks

1. **No counted formula (checked).**
   - Tasbīḥ and ḥamd have *times* (20:130, 30:17-18, 50:39-40, 33:42). Remembrance is asked to be *much* (33:41). None has a number.
   - `shkr` has no time-word at all.
   - Each of the three phrases in the after-prayer hadith occurs over a gift, separately and uncounted: tasbīḥ (43:13), ḥamd (23:28), takbīr (2:185, 22:37).
2. **No prostration of thanks (checked).**
   - `sjd` shares no ayah with `shkr` (0 of 69).
   - The nearest are 17:107-109, where those given knowledge fall prostrate saying *subḥāna rabbinā, the promise of our Lord is fulfilled*, and Joseph's family bowing to Joseph at the reunion (12:100).
   - Neither is called thanks.
3. **No "thank people" (checked).**
   - The only human object of `shkr` is parents (31:14).
   - Between people, the text uses other words: repaying (*li-yajziyaka ajra mā saqayta lanā*, 28:25), returning a greeting with a better one (4:86), and *do not forget graciousness between you* (2:237).
4. **No command to feel grateful (checked).** The text regulates the feelings around a gift, but never calls any of them gratitude. `frḥ` (rejoice) has 22 tokens and one command:

> **10:58** قُلۡ بِفَضۡلِ ٱللَّهِ وَبِرَحۡمَتِهِۦ فَبِذَٰلِكَ فَلۡيَفۡرَحُواْ هُوَ خَيۡرࣱ مِّمَّا يَجۡمَعُونَ
> Saheeh: *In the bounty of Allah and in His mercy — in that let them rejoice; it is better than what they accumulate.*

   - Rejoicing is forbidden over possessions (28:76, 57:23).
   - Despair and exultation are the ungrateful person's moods (11:9-10, 17:83, 42:48).
   - Two ayahs after 10:58 comes *most do not thank* (10:60).
   - No `shkr` ayah asks for thanks *of* the heart. The inner life appears once: God *knows what is in the breasts* when He judges thanks and denial (39:7). Otherwise hearts appear as a gift (16:78, 23:78, 32:9, 67:23) or as other people's hearts turned toward Abraham's household (14:37).
5. **No thanks for hardship itself (checked).**
   - None of the 43 gift-naming ayahs names an affliction.
   - What the text gives in hardship:
     - *innā li-llāh* (2:156);
     - praise and glorification *during* distress (15:97-98, 20:130);
     - praise once grief is *removed* (35:34).
6. **Added:**
   - **No `shkr` in Paradise**, where only ḥamd is spoken.
   - **No narrated person thanks after being forgiven.** 2:52 is a purpose clause.
   - **`shkr` + ṣalāt (prayer): 2 ayahs** (5:6, 14:37). **`shkr` + tasbīḥ: 0.**

## For practice

- **Name the source first.** Say what He did, with Him as the subject (*al-ḥamdu li-llāhi lladhī…*). When the gift arrives, say *mā shāʾa llāh* (18:39).
- **Use the gift as its verse says.** Eat, seek His bounty in the day, and feed someone from what you were given (22:36).
- **Ask rather than declare**: *rabbi awziʿnī an ashkura niʿmataka… wa-an aʿmala ṣāliḥan tarḍāh* (27:19, 46:15).
- **After a rescue, the test is the next ordinary day.** Keep the sincerity of the storm once things are calm (10:22-23).
- **In loss, the text asks for patience and *innā li-llāh*, not thanks for the loss.** In ease it asks for patience too (11:11).
- **Rejoice in His bounty and mercy, not in what you own** (10:58, 57:23).
- **Do not measure thanks by what you have in this world.** Both kinds of people are given (17:20).
