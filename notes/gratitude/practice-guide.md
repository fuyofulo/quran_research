# Thanking God: a practice guide from the Quran

This guide comes from a close reading of every verse in the Quran about thanks, praise and blessings. It is for anyone who wants to thank God the way the Quran teaches.

Verse numbers are in brackets. The Arabic is copied exactly from the Quran text. The Quran is the only source here. Where a well-known practice comes from hadith, the reported sayings of the Prophet, the guide says so. That does not make it wrong. It only means it is not in the Quran itself.

## What thanks is in the Quran

In the Quran, thanks is a response to God. It is something you say, and something you do.

No one in the Quran ever says "I thank You, God" in those words. When people speak about thanking, they ask God to *help* them thank (27:19, 46:15). Or they say a gift is a test of whether they will be thankful (27:40).

Instead, the Quran gives words of praise and glory for the moment a gift arrives. *Al-ḥamdu lillāh*, "praise be to God", is what people say. Then it asks you to *use* the gift well: to work, to worship, to feed others, to be good to your parents.

So thanks has two halves: you say it, and you do it. The only thanks in the Quran made of words alone is a promise made in danger, "we will be among the thankful". Every one of those promises is broken (6:63-64, 10:22-23, 7:189-190).

Your thanks is for your own good. "Whoever is thankful is thankful for his own sake" (27:40, 31:12). God does not need it, but He is pleased with it for you (39:7).

## The Quran's own words, moment by moment

Each moment below gives the Quran's words. "Who" is the Quran's own setting. "When" is our suggestion.

### 1. When something good is yours

> مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ لَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِٱللَّهِۚ

*Mā shā'a llāh, lā quwwata illā billāh.*
**Meaning:** What God willed. There is no power except with God.
**Who:** A believing man, to a friend who walked into his rich garden thinking it would never end: why did you not say this? (18:35-39)
**When:** Looking at your home, your work, your family, or anything you feel proud of.

### 2. When a gift arrives

> هَٰذَا مِن فَضۡلِ رَبِّي لِيَبۡلُوَنِيٓ ءَأَشۡكُرُ أَمۡ أَكۡفُرُۖ

*Hādhā min faḍli rabbī, li-yabluwanī a-ashkuru am akfur.*
**Meaning:** This is from my Lord's bounty, to test me: will I be thankful, or ungrateful?
**Who:** Solomon, a king, when a throne was brought to him in the blink of an eye (27:40).
**When:** Something good comes that you could not have made happen yourself. Solomon did not claim to pass the test; he kept the question open.

### 3. When you set off or ride

> سُبۡحَٰنَ ٱلَّذِي سَخَّرَ لَنَا هَٰذَا وَمَا كُنَّا لَهُۥ مُقۡرِنِينَ
> وَإِنَّآ إِلَىٰ رَبِّنَا لَمُنقَلِبُونَ

*Subḥāna lladhī sakhkhara lanā hādhā wa mā kunnā lahu muqrinīn. Wa innā ilā rabbinā la-munqalibūn.*
**Meaning:** Glory be to Him who made this serve us. We could never have managed it ourselves. And to our Lord we will return.
**Who:** The Quran tells people to say this when they settle onto a ship or an animal, after remembering their Lord's blessing (43:12-14).
**When:** Getting into a car, a bus or a plane (using it beyond ships and animals is our extension).

### 4. After you come through something

> ٱلۡحَمۡدُ لِلَّهِ ٱلَّذِي نَجَّىٰنَا مِنَ ٱلۡقَوۡمِ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ
> رَّبِّ أَنزِلۡنِي مُنزَلࣰا مُّبَارَكࣰا وَأَنتَ خَيۡرُ ٱلۡمُنزِلِينَ

*Al-ḥamdu lillāhi lladhī najjānā mina l-qawmi ẓ-ẓālimīn. Rabbi anzilnī munzalan mubārakan wa anta khayru l-munzilīn.*
**Meaning:** Praise be to God, who saved us from the wrongdoing people. My Lord, let me land in a blessed place. You are the best of those who bring people to land.
**Who:** God told Noah to say this when he boarded the ark (23:28-29).
**When:** After a danger passes, an illness ends, or a hard time is over. Notice the shape: first praise, then ask for what comes next.

### 5. When a long hope comes true

> ٱلۡحَمۡدُ لِلَّهِ ٱلَّذِي وَهَبَ لِي عَلَى ٱلۡكِبَرِ إِسۡمَٰعِيلَ وَإِسۡحَٰقَۚ إِنَّ رَبِّي لَسَمِيعُ ٱلدُّعَآءِ

*Al-ḥamdu lillāhi lladhī wahaba lī 'ala l-kibari Ismā'īla wa Isḥāq. Inna rabbī la-samī'u d-du'ā'.*
**Meaning:** Praise be to God, who gave me Ishmael and Isaac in my old age. My Lord truly hears prayer.
**Who:** Abraham (14:39).
**When:** Something you prayed for over a long time finally comes. This is the Quran's habit: praise God "who did" something, and name what He did.

### 6. When a sadness lifts

> ٱلۡحَمۡدُ لِلَّهِ ٱلَّذِيٓ أَذۡهَبَ عَنَّا ٱلۡحَزَنَۖ إِنَّ رَبَّنَا لَغَفُورࣱ شَكُورٌ

*Al-ḥamdu lillāhi lladhī adhhaba 'anna l-ḥazan. Inna rabbanā la-Ghafūrun Shakūr.*
**Meaning:** Praise be to God, who has taken away our grief. Our Lord is truly Forgiving and Thankful.
**Who:** The people of Paradise (35:34).
**When:** A worry ends. These words also hold God's two names that join forgiveness and thanks (see below).

### 7. After a success

> فَسَبِّحۡ بِحَمۡدِ رَبِّكَ وَٱسۡتَغۡفِرۡهُۚ إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ تَوَّابَۢا

*Fa-sabbiḥ bi-ḥamdi rabbika wa-staghfirh. Innahu kāna tawwābā.*
**Meaning:** So glorify your Lord with His praise, and ask His forgiveness. He is always ready to accept those who turn back to Him.
**Who:** God, to the Prophet, when "God's help and the victory" had come (110:1-3).
**When:** After an exam, a finished job, anything that went well. This is an instruction, not a set phrase. You might say *subḥānaka* and *al-ḥamdu lillāh*, then ask forgiveness in your own words.

### 8. For who He is, with no particular reason

> ٱلۡحَمۡدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلۡعَٰلَمِينَ

*Al-ḥamdu lillāhi rabbi l-'ālamīn.* Praise be to God, Lord of all the worlds (1:2).

> ٱلۡحَمۡدُ لِلَّهِ ٱلَّذِي لَمۡ يَتَّخِذۡ وَلَدࣰا وَلَمۡ يَكُن لَّهُۥ شَرِيكࣱ فِي ٱلۡمُلۡكِ وَلَمۡ يَكُن لَّهُۥ وَلِيࣱّ مِّنَ ٱلذُّلِّۖ وَكَبِّرۡهُ تَكۡبِيرَۢا

*Al-ḥamdu lillāhi lladhī lam yattakhidh waladan wa lam yakun lahu sharīkun fi l-mulki wa lam yakun lahu waliyyun mina dh-dhulli, wa kabbirhu takbīrā.*
**Meaning:** Praise be to God, who has no son, no partner in His rule, and no protector out of weakness. And declare His greatness, fully.
**Who:** 1:2 opens the Quran. 17:111 is a command to the Prophet: "Say…"
**When:** Any time. Praise does not have to wait for a gift (27:59, 27:93).

### 9. Looking at the sky, the sea, the world

> رَبَّنَا مَا خَلَقۡتَ هَٰذَا بَٰطِلࣰا سُبۡحَٰنَكَ

*Rabbanā mā khalaqta hādhā bāṭilan, subḥānak.*
**Meaning:** Our Lord, You did not create all this for nothing. Glory be to You.
**Who:** People who remember God standing, sitting and lying down, and reflect on the heavens and the earth (3:190-191).
**When:** On a walk, under the night sky, by the sea.

### 10. For your parents

> رَّبِّ ٱرۡحَمۡهُمَا كَمَا رَبَّيَانِي صَغِيرࣰا

*Rabbi rḥamhumā kamā rabbayānī ṣaghīrā.*
**Meaning:** My Lord, have mercy on them, as they raised me when I was small.
**Who:** The Quran tells each of us to say it, while lowering "the wing of humility" to our parents (17:24).
**When:** Whenever you think of them.

### 11. To ask God to help you be thankful

> رَبِّ أَوۡزِعۡنِيٓ أَنۡ أَشۡكُرَ نِعۡمَتَكَ ٱلَّتِيٓ أَنۡعَمۡتَ عَلَيَّ وَعَلَىٰ وَٰلِدَيَّ وَأَنۡ أَعۡمَلَ صَٰلِحࣰا تَرۡضَىٰهُ

*Rabbi awzi'nī an ashkura ni'mataka llatī an'amta 'alayya wa 'alā wālidayya wa an a'mala ṣāliḥan tarḍāh.*
**Meaning:** My Lord, help me to be thankful for Your blessing, which You gave to me and to my parents, and to do good that pleases You.
**Who:** Solomon the king (27:19). The very same words are said by an ordinary person who has reached forty (46:15).
**When:** Often. This is the nearest the Quran comes to a person saying "thank You". You ask to *be able* to thank, and in the same breath, to *do* good.

### 12. At the end of the day

> دَعۡوَىٰهُمۡ فِيهَا سُبۡحَٰنَكَ ٱللَّهُمَّ وَتَحِيَّتُهُمۡ فِيهَا سَلَٰمࣱۚ وَءَاخِرُ دَعۡوَىٰهُمۡ أَنِ ٱلۡحَمۡدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلۡعَٰلَمِينَ

*Da'wāhum fīhā subḥānaka llāhumma, wa taḥiyyatuhum fīhā salām, wa ākhiru da'wāhum ani l-ḥamdu lillāhi rabbi l-'ālamīn.*
**Meaning:** Their prayer there is "Glory be to You, O God". Their greeting there is "Peace". And the last of their prayer is "Praise be to God, Lord of all the worlds".
**Who:** The people of Paradise (10:10).
**When:** At the end of the day, before sleep.

**One more, with no set words.** At the end of the month of fasting, the Quran asks you to "declare God's greatness for having guided you, and perhaps you will be thankful" (2:185), and the same over the sacrifice (22:37). It names the act but gives no fixed words.

## Thanks as something you do

In the Quran, thanks is mostly action.

- **Work in thanks.** "Work, family of David, in thanks. Few of My servants are truly thankful" (34:13).
- **Use the gift for what it is for.** "Eat of the good things We have given you, and thank God" (2:172). God made the sea and the day "so that you may seek His bounty, and so that you may be thankful" (16:14, 28:73). Earning a living is part of the picture.
- **Do good as God has done good to you** (28:77). His kindness to you is the model for yours to others.
- **Feed others.** Over the sacrifice: mention God's name, eat, and feed the needy and the one who asks, "so that you may be thankful" (22:36). The righteous feed the poor, the orphan and the captive, and say: "We feed you only for God's sake. We want no reward from you, and no thanks" (76:8-9).
- **Thank your parents.** "Be thankful to Me and to your parents" (31:14). They are the only people the Quran tells you to thank. If they push you to worship another besides God, do not obey them in that, but keep their company kindly (31:15).

## The ordinary things

Most of the Quran's reasons for thanks are everyday ones. About half of the thanks verses that name a gift name food, water, the sea, animals, night and day, or the senses (22 of 43).

- **Hearing, sight and heart.** "God brought you out of your mothers' wombs knowing nothing, and gave you hearing, sight and hearts, so that you may be thankful" (16:78). Three more times, the same gifts end with "little do you give thanks" (23:78, 32:9, 67:23).
- **Food, water and fire.** The Quran asks you to look at the seed you plant, the water you drink and the fire you light. Is it you who makes them, or God? "If We willed, We could make the water bitter. So why do you not give thanks?" (56:63-73). Then: "So glorify the name of your Lord, the Most Great" (56:74).
- **Night and day.** God made night and day follow each other "for anyone who wishes to remember, or wishes to give thanks" (25:62).

You do not need a big event to thank God. A glass of water is one of the Quran's own reasons.

## How thanks, praise, glory and asking forgiveness fit together

You may have felt that asking forgiveness and glorifying God go together. The Quran agrees.

Four times, "glorify your Lord with praise" and "ask forgiveness" sit in the same verse (40:7, 40:55, 42:5, 110:3). In three of them, glory and praise come first. In one, asking forgiveness comes first, as a daily pair: "ask forgiveness for your sin, and glorify your Lord with praise, evening and morning" (40:55).

Across many verses there is a pattern (not a rule):

1. **Remember** what God has done.
2. **Acknowledge** Him: glorify, praise, declare His greatness, thank.
3. **Ask**: for what you need, and for forgiveness.
4. **Turn back** to Him.

You can see it in 110:3: glorify with praise, ask forgiveness, and "He is always ready to accept those who turn back". You can also see it in 46:15. The person asks to be able to thank, asks to do good and for good children, and then ends:

> إِنِّي تُبۡتُ إِلَيۡكَ وَإِنِّي مِنَ ٱلۡمُسۡلِمِينَ

*Innī tubtu ilayka wa innī mina l-muslimīn.*
"I have turned back to You, and I am one of those who submit to You" (46:15).

God's answer comes in the next verse: "We accept the best of what they did, and overlook their bad deeds" (46:16).

**Something honest to know.** The Quran's word for thanks and its words for asking forgiveness never appear in the same verse. They do come close together twice. In one prayer, Abraham hopes his family will be thankful, praises God for his sons, and asks forgiveness for himself and his parents (14:37-41). And in the battle passage that promises a reward to the thankful (3:144-145), the steadfast say only: "Our Lord, forgive us our sins" (3:147).

Thanks and forgiveness also meet in two other ways:

- **Through turning back.** "I have turned back to You" (*tubtu ilayk*) appears twice in the Quran, and both times it is next to thanks. One is here (46:15). The other is Moses, who said "Glory be to You, I turn back to You", and was told "be among the thankful" (7:143-144).
- **Through God's names.** God calls Himself *Ghafūr Shakūr*, "Forgiving, Thankful", three times (35:30, 35:34, 42:23). Yes, God thanks too: He gives back more than the good you do (35:30, 64:17). On His side, forgiving your faults and thanking your good deeds go together. On your side, they are two things you do.

Two verses tie this together. "If you tried to count God's blessings, you could never number them." Once, this ends "people are truly unjust and ungrateful" (14:34). Once, it ends "God is truly Forgiving, Merciful" (16:18). You can never thank enough, and the Quran sets His forgiveness right beside that fact. (Joining the two is our reading.)

**Glory keeps thanks honest.** When you say "Glory be to Him who made this serve us; we could not have done it ourselves" (43:13), you refuse to take the credit. That is the cure for the danger described below.

## What the Quran does not ask

- **No count.** The Quran gives times for glorifying and praising God: morning and evening, before sunrise and sunset (40:55, 20:130, 50:39). It asks you to remember God "much" (33:41). It never gives a number. Saying *subḥān Allāh*, *al-ḥamdu lillāh* and *Allāhu akbar* 33 times each after prayer is a traditional practice from hadith.
- **No prostration of thanks.** That comes from hadith.
- **No "feel grateful".** The Quran never tells you how to feel about a gift, with one lovely exception: "In God's bounty and His mercy, in that let them rejoice. It is better than all they gather" (10:58). Rejoice in what God gives, not in what you own (28:76, 57:23).
- **No thanks *for* the hardship itself.** When hard things happen, the Quran asks for patience and the words "We belong to God, and to Him we return" (2:156). It asks you to praise and glorify Him during hard times (15:97-98, 20:130). It never asks you to thank Him *for* the pain.
- **But thanks still counts in defeat.** In a battle passage, the Quran asks: if the Prophet died or was killed, would you turn back? Then it says, "God will reward the thankful" (3:144). The thankful there are the ones who held on.
- **No promise of riches.** "If you are thankful, I will give you more" (14:7) was said by Moses to his whole people. The Quran never shows someone getting richer because they gave thanks. God gives to everyone: "to each We extend from your Lord's gift" (17:20).
- **No general "thank people".** The only people the Quran tells you to thank are your parents (31:14). It does speak well of repaying kindness (28:25) and of returning a greeting with a better one (4:86). The saying "whoever does not thank people does not thank God" comes from hadith.

## The danger to watch for

The Quran's most common picture of ingratitude is not dramatic. It is forgetting.

People at sea are caught in a storm. They call on God sincerely and promise: "If You save us, we will be among the thankful." He saves them, and *at once* they go back to doing wrong (10:22-23). The Quran calls their prayer sincere. The problem is the next calm day. This story of rescue, then forgetting, appears nine times.

The second danger is one sentence: "I was only given this because of what I know" (28:78, 39:49). It takes the credit away from God. The answer is the words you have already met: *mā shā'a llāh* (18:39), "this is from my Lord's bounty" (27:40), "we could not have done it ourselves" (43:13).

## A simple daily practice

*This is our suggestion, built from the verses above. It is not a rule from the Quran.*

1. **Morning.** Say *al-ḥamdu lillāhi rabbi l-'ālamīn* (1:2). Then name one thing God did for you, the Quran's way: "Praise be to God, who…" (14:39, 23:28).
2. **When something good happens.** *Mā shā'a llāh, lā quwwata illā billāh* (18:39).
3. **When you set out.** *Subḥāna lladhī sakhkhara lanā hādhā…* (43:13-14).
4. **Do one thankful thing.** Feed someone, help a parent, or do your work well as thanks (22:36, 31:14, 34:13).
5. **Evening.** Glorify God with praise and ask forgiveness. The Quran pairs them "evening and morning" (40:55). For forgiveness you can use the Quran's own words:

   > رَبَّنَا فَٱغۡفِرۡ لَنَا ذُنُوبَنَا وَكَفِّرۡ عَنَّا سَيِّـَٔاتِنَا

   *Rabbanā fa-ghfir lanā dhunūbanā wa kaffir 'annā sayyi'ātinā.* "Our Lord, forgive us our sins and wipe away our bad deeds" (3:193). Then turn back: *innī tubtu ilayka* (46:15).
6. **Before sleep.** End the way Paradise ends: *subḥānaka llāhumma … al-ḥamdu lillāhi rabbi l-'ālamīn* (10:10).
7. **Now and then.** Pray Solomon's prayer (27:19).

Keep it small. The Quran sets no number, so there is nothing to fall behind on. What it asks is that the gift be noticed, credited to God, and used well.
