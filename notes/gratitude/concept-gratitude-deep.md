# Gratitude (*shukr*) in the Quran: synthesis

> Phase-4 synthesis of the nine investigation files listed in `README.md`. Plain-language companion: `practice-guide.md`.

## Headline

The Quran teaches thanks as a response to God that is said and done, but never only said. No one in it says "I thank You". The three first-person uses of `shkr` ask to be *enabled* to thank (27:19, 46:15) or call a gift a *test* (27:40). Over a gift the text gives words of praise, glorification, takbīr and *mā shāʾa llāh*. As substance it asks that the gift be used, and answered with work and worship (34:13). Thanks never shares a verse with seeking forgiveness. The two meet through turning back (*tawba*, 46:15) and in God's names, *Ghafūr Shakūr*.

## 0. Evidence and marking

The evidence is the Arabic text (Uthmani Ḥafṣ) and the Quranic Arabic Corpus morphology. Translations are secondary and are cited where they split. Hadith, tafsīr and fiqh are outside the evidence, so a well-known practice missing from the text is reported as an absence. Counts and quoted wording are data-grounded. **[interpretive]** marks a reading.

## 1. The vocabulary

The catalogues of blessings (16:3-18, 16:80-81, 14:32-33, 55:2-12) are mostly material: 14 of 25 tagged verses. **[interpretive]** The lists point at the world. The word *niʿma* points at what was done for people.

**Two traditional claims, tested.**
- "*Ḥamd* is broader than *shukr*" **survives.** *Ḥamd* occurs with no gift in view (17:111, 27:59, 30:18, 20:130). *Shukr*'s commands and purposes follow a gift or a rescue. The text adds that *ḥamd* is the form people *speak*.
- "*Shukr* is heart, tongue and limbs": **two parts are attested.** The tongue: 18:39 rebukes a missing sentence. The limbs: *work in thanks* (34:13). No `shkr` ayah asks for thanks *of the heart*. Hearts appear as a gift (16:78) and as what God knows when He judges thanks (39:7).

## 2. The model

**Object.** God, with *li-* (6 of 7 imperatives). The one human object is **parents, in the same verb as God** (31:14). Its limit is 31:15: do not obey them in associating partners, but keep their company kindly. The givers of 76:9 want *no reward and no thanks*.

**Occasions.** Mostly ordinary and repeated. Of the 69 `shkr` ayahs, 43 name a gift, and 22 of those are food, the sea, cattle, night and day, or the senses. The 14 purpose clauses are:
- creation put at your service, 6;
- the law eased, 2 (the fast, 2:185; ablution, 5:6), and made clear, 1 (5:89);
- forgiveness, 1 (2:52), and a second life, 1 (2:56);
- rescue when few, 2 (3:123, 8:26);
- the senses, 1 (16:78).

**None is an affliction.** In 11 of the 14, the same ayah names another purpose or command beside thanks: *seek His bounty* (5×), *magnify God* (2:185), *fear God* (3:123), *remember* (8:26), *mention the Name, eat, feed* (22:36).

**Spoken signs.** No `shkr` sentence is spoken as thanks. The words given over a gift are:
- *subḥāna lladhī sakhkhara lanā hādhā* (43:13-14), on mounting;
- *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi lladhī najjānā*, then a request for *a blessed landing* (23:28-29);
- *mā shāʾa llāh, lā quwwata illā bi-llāh* (18:39), as what the garden-owner should have said;
- *hādhā min faḍli rabbī* (27:40);
- takbīr *for having guided you* (2:185, 22:37), an act with no formula;
- the request *rabbi awziʿnī an ashkura niʿmataka* (27:19, 46:15);
- *fa-ḥaddith*, "tell of it" (93:11).

Unprompted praise names what He did, with God as the subject of the verb: *who saved us* (23:28), *who gave me Ishmael and Isaac* (14:39), *who removed grief* (35:34), *who guided us* (7:43).

**Substance: the deed.**
- Use the gift as its verse says: *eat … and thank* (2:172, 16:114, 34:15).
- Seek His bounty (16:14, 28:73, 30:46, 35:12, 45:12).
- *Mention the Name, eat, feed the needy and the beggar* (22:36).
- Answer the gift with work (34:13), worship (2:172, 29:17, 39:66), *taqwā* (3:123) and steadfastness (3:144).
- Outside the root: *do good as God has done good to you* (28:77).

> **34:13** ٱعۡمَلُوٓاْ ءَالَ دَاوُۥدَ شُكۡرࣰاۚ وَقَلِيلࣱ مِّنۡ عِبَادِيَ ٱلشَّكُورُ
> Saheeh: *Work, O family of David, in gratitude. And few of My servants are grateful.* Khattab: *Work gratefully.* Pickthall: *Give thanks.*

**Companions.**
- Patience: *ṣabbār shakūr* 4× (14:5, 31:31, 34:19, 42:33). The command to thank at 2:152 turns at once to patience at 2:153.
- Remembrance: the `dhkr` root is in 5 `shkr` ayahs.
- Faith, named after thanks (4:147).
- *Taqwā* (3:123).

**Fruit.**
- Thanks is *for himself* (27:40, 31:12).
- God approves it (39:7), remembers the thanker (2:152) and rewards (3:144-145, 54:35).
- For peoples, He increases (14:7).
- He loses nothing when thanks is missing, since He is *ghanī* (31:12, 39:7).

**Rarity.**
- *Few of My servants* (34:13).
- *Little do you thank* 4×. Three of these follow hearing, sight and hearts.
- *Most do not thank* 5×, each with *faḍl* in the ayah.
- Iblīs predicted it (7:17).

**Opposite.** An act **or a sentence** (§5).

**[interpretive] The model as a loop.**
1. The gift is received as *from Him*.
2. It is used for what its verse says.
3. It is marked aloud with praise or glorification.
4. It is answered with worship and work.

Then God answers the answer, as *Shakūr*.

## 3. Three verdicts

**1. "Thanks is a deed, not only a word": holds, restated as *a deed and a word, never the word alone*.**
- There are six human `shkr` sentences:
  - three are a request or a test (27:19, 46:15, 27:40);
  - three are vows, *la-nakūnanna mina sh-shākirīn* (6:63, 10:22, 7:189), and **all three are broken** (6:64, 10:23, 7:190).
- So the only thanks made of words alone is the vow, the text's standard picture of failure.
- Words are still required. 18:39 rebukes a missing sentence, and the five self-crediting ingrates fail by a sentence.
- Some deeds are called thanks with no words: 34:13, 3:144, 54:35.
- *Shukran* in 34:13 can be purpose, manner, or the thing done. The passage leans to the last: *ʿamila* takes the thing done as its object three other times in 34:11-13.

**2. "Patience is for hardship, thanks for ease": fails as a rule, survives as a tendency.** The occasions of thanks are gifts, and the trial list of 2:155-157 has no `shkr`. But:
- **3:144 names the grateful in defeat.** They are those who do not *turn back on their heels* at the news that the Prophet might die or be killed. Two ayahs later, the same holding firm is called patience (3:146).
- **Patience is asked in ease.** The exception to the *exultant and boastful* in relief is *those who are patient* (11:10-11).
- **Each *ṣabbār shakūr* context holds ease and danger together**: a voyage that can turn (31:31-32, 42:32-34), torment then rescue (14:5-6), prosperity undone (34:19). **[interpretive]** The phrase describes one person in a situation that can turn, not two kinds of people.
- **Thanks *for* the hardship itself is never asked.**

**3. "If you thank, I will increase you": a law about peoples, not a prosperity formula.**

> **14:7** وَإِذۡ تَأَذَّنَ رَبُّكُمۡ لَئِن شَكَرۡتُمۡ لَأَزِيدَنَّكُمۡۖ وَلَئِن كَفَرۡتُمۡ إِنَّ عَذَابِي لَشَدِيدࣱ
> Saheeh: *If you are grateful, I will surely increase you [in favor]; but if you deny, indeed, My punishment is severe.* Khattab: *I will certainly give you more.*

- **Who speaks.** Moses, to his people, in the plural, recalling a rescue of the whole people (14:6). What is increased is not named.
- **The negative half is narrated, always of communities**: Saba' (34:15-17), the town (16:112) and 14:28. The mechanism is stated of a people too: God does not change a blessing until they change what is in themselves (8:53, 13:11).
- **The positive half is never narrated as increase.** `zyd` shares an ayah with `shkr` 3× (14:7, 35:30, 42:23). In the other two, the *Shakūr* is God, and the increase rewards prayer, spending and good deeds.
- **One reward for thanks is narrated**: Lot's family's rescue (54:34-35). Abraham is called grateful and then chosen (16:121-122), with no particle of cause between the two.
- **Worldly giving reaches both kinds of people** (17:20). Ease and narrowing are both tests (89:15-16). The ungrateful may get respite *so that they increase in sin* (3:178).
- **[interpretive]** Reading 14:7 as a personal prosperity formula goes beyond the text.

## 4. God the Thanker

God is never the subject of a `shkr` verb. He is the Name, always in a pair:
- **Shākir ʿalīm**: after *whoever volunteers good* (2:158); and *what would God do with your punishment if you thank and believe?* (4:147).
- **Ghafūr shakūr**, with *ghafūr* always first (35:30, 35:34, 42:23): wages paid in full and increased; Paradise's words; a good deed whose good is increased.
- **Shakūr ḥalīm** (64:17): *a goodly loan* multiplied, and forgiveness.
- **The believer's effort** is *mashkūr*, "thanked" (17:19, 76:22).

What He "thanks" is always something done, never a word. His answer exceeds it: in full, increased, multiplied. Translators split (2:158: *appreciative*, *responsive*, *all-thankful*).

The two roots meet only crossed. **People thank, and God is *Ḥamīd*** (31:12). **People praise, and God is *Shakūr*** (35:34). *Ghanī ḥamīd* follows turning away in 6 of 10 uses: His praiseworthiness does not rest on being praised.

**[interpretive]** The Name appears where someone has done something small, costly or unseen, and the answer is larger than the deed. It cannot be a debt, since He is *ghanī*. "He notices and returns more" is the nearest paraphrase. *He approves it for you* (39:7) is the text's own.

## 5. The grateful and the ungrateful

**The grateful: twelve portraits.** They are Noah, Abraham, David's family, Solomon, Luqmān, the man at forty, Moses, Joseph, the Prophet, the righteous of 76, Lot's family and the grateful of 3:144.
- **Thanks beside an act.** In 9 of 12, thanks sits beside work, worship, prayer, feeding or steadfastness.
- **Speech.** It appears in 7 portraits, never alone.
- **The same prayer.** 27:19 and 46:15 write the deed into the prayer, in identical words, for a king and for an ordinary person at forty.
- **No one says *I am grateful*.** The nearest is a name God gives: Noah *ʿabdan shakūrā* (17:3), Abraham *shākiran li-anʿumih* (16:121).
- **The shape of grateful speech** (12:100-101, 14:39-41): name the gift with God as the subject of the verb, close with one of His Names, then ask.
- **The gift as a test.** Solomon calls the gift a test and does not claim to pass (27:40). 89:15-16 reads the same test the opposite way.

**The ungrateful, counted by passage.**

| Form | Passages |
|---|---|
| Rescue, then forget (3 with a vow: 6:63 and 10:22 at sea; 7:189 over a child) | **9** |
| Denying the giver or exchanging the gift (net of 4 overlaps with the row above) | 7 |
| Crediting oneself, always a sentence: *only because of knowledge I have* (28:78, 39:49); 41:50, 18:35-36, 11:10 | 5 |
| Using the gift to transgress (10:23, 28:76-77, 14:28) | 3 |
| Despair or exultation as the gift moves (11:9-10, 17:83, 42:48) | 3 |
| Asking for less (34:19) | 1 |

- **The danger-prayer is called sincere** (*mukhliṣīna lahu d-dīn*: 10:22, 29:65, 31:32), never fake. The fault is the relapse, *at once* (10:23).
- 31:32 leaves a remnant.
- The commonest relapse verb is *yushrik-* (5 of 9).
- Ingratitude *knows*, then rejects (16:83).
- Loss is not always visible: *enjoy yourselves* (16:55, 30:34); respite (3:178).

**[interpretive]** Ingratitude breaks the loop at one of three points:
- the source is forgotten (the rescued at sea);
- the source is relocated (*because of my knowledge*);
- the gift is refused (34:19).

## 6. Thanks with praise, glorification, remembrance, takbīr and seeking forgiveness

The reader's starting intuition is that seeking forgiveness and glorifying God go hand in hand. **The text agrees, and thanks stands just outside that pair.**

### The map (data-grounded, `words.jsonl`)

| Shares an ayah with `shkr` | Ayahs |
|---|---|
| `sbḥ` glorify | **0** |
| istighfār | **0**, and none within ±5 ayahs |
| `ḥmd` praise | 2 (31:12, 35:34, crossed) |
| `dhkr` remember (core lemmas) | 3 (2:152, 8:26, 22:36); 5 with the whole root |
| `kbr` takbīr | 1 (2:185) |
| `twb` turning back | 1 (46:15) |

**Among the others:**
- `sbḥ` × `ḥmd`: 16 ayahs;
- istighfār × `twb`: 9;
- *sabbiḥ bi-ḥamdi* × istighfār: 4 (40:7, 40:55, 42:5, 110:3);
- `ḥmd` × `dhkr`: 0.

**Around thanks instead:**
- the `gfr` root is in 5 `shkr` ayahs, always as God's forgiveness;
- human turning comes within two ayahs of thanks in 7 passages.

**Narrowing made at synthesis.** The *request* "forgive us" (*ighfir*) comes within five ayahs of thanks twice:
- 3:144-147: the grateful are rewarded, and the steadfast say only *our Lord, forgive us our sins*;
- 14:37-41: Abraham prays that his family *may be grateful*, praises God for his sons (14:39), and ends *forgive me and my parents*.

Thanks and asking forgiveness share passages, never a sentence.

> **110:3** فَسَبِّحۡ بِحَمۡدِ رَبِّكَ وَٱسۡتَغۡفِرۡهُۚ إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ تَوَّابَۢا
> Saheeh: *Then exalt [Him] with praise of your Lord and ask forgiveness of Him. Indeed, He is ever Accepting of repentance.*

The response commanded when *God's help and the victory* come (110:1) has no `shkr` word. It is the reader's pair.

### The order

- **Remembrance first** in 8 of the 9 ayahs `dhkr` shares with the others. The exception, 25:18, is the reminder *forgotten* through enjoyment.
- **Glorify-with-praise before istighfār in 3 of 4.** 40:55 reverses it: *seek forgiveness for your sin and glorify with praise, evening and morning*.
- **Acknowledgement before asking** in 110:3, 40:7, 42:5, 23:28-29, 3:191-193, 7:143 and the Fātiḥa (1:2 before 1:6).
- **Thanks last** when it is a purpose (2:52, 2:152, 2:185, 8:26, 22:36).
- **Turning or returning closes** 43:14, 46:15, 7:143, 110:3 and 40:7.
- **Rites end differently**:
  - the fast in *magnify, thank* (2:185);
  - the sacrifice in *thank, magnify* (22:36-37);
  - the night prayer (73:20) and the departure from ʿArafāt (2:199) in *seek forgiveness*;
  - the victory in *glorify with praise, seek forgiveness* (110:3).

**[interpretive]** A recurring order: ***remember → acknowledge (glorify, praise, magnify, thank) → ask → turn back.*** The occasion seems to choose what leads. When a verse names a gift, acknowledgement leads (2:185, 22:36, 110:1). When it names a shortfall, istighfār leads or stands alone (2:198-199, 73:20, 3:135, 40:55).

### How thanks reaches forgiveness

**(a) Through turning back.**

> **46:15** رَبِّ أَوۡزِعۡنِيٓ أَنۡ أَشۡكُرَ نِعۡمَتَكَ … إِنِّي تُبۡتُ إِلَيۡكَ وَإِنِّي مِنَ ٱلۡمُسۡلِمِينَ
> Saheeh: *My Lord, enable me to be grateful for Your favor … Indeed, I have repented to You, and indeed, I am of the Muslims.*

- *Tubtu ilayka* occurs exactly twice, 46:15 and 7:143. Both are beside a `shkr` word.
- Moses says *subḥānaka, tubtu ilayka*. God answers *be among the grateful* (7:143-144).
- 46:16 answers the prayer: *We accept the best of what they did and overlook their misdeeds*.
- At 4:146-147, *those who turn and set right* is followed by *if you thank and believe*.

**(b) Through God's names.** *Ghafūr shakūr* (3×) joins, on God's side, what people keep as two acts. Compare 34:15 (*a good land and a forgiving Lord*) and 64:17. **[interpretive]** He forgives the bad and thanks the good as one response. People ask forgiveness for faults and thank Him for gifts, in separate sentences.

**(c) Forgiveness as an occasion of thanks, once** (2:52). No narrated person thanks after being forgiven.

**(d) The uncountable gift.** *If you counted God's blessing you could not number it* occurs twice, with two endings: *man is most unjust, most ungrateful* (14:34), and *God is Forgiving, Merciful* (16:18). 73:20 counts the night vigil, not the blessing. **[interpretive]** The text never says "seek forgiveness for thanking too little". Joining the uncountable gift to forgiveness is ours.

### Glorification, takbīr and remembrance

> **43:13-14** وَتَقُولُواْ سُبۡحَٰنَ ٱلَّذِي سَخَّرَ لَنَا هَٰذَا وَمَا كُنَّا لَهُۥ مُقۡرِنِينَ ۝ وَإِنَّآ إِلَىٰ رَبِّنَا لَمُنقَلِبُونَ
> Saheeh: *and say, "Exalted is He who has subjected this to us, and we could not have [otherwise] subdued it. And indeed we, to our Lord, will [surely] return."*

- **The words that remember a blessing.** The verse opens *then remember the favor of your Lord*. Its words glorify the giver, **disown one's own power** and name the return (`qlb`, not `twb`).
- **The disavowal.** The other *mā kunnā* disavowal is Paradise's praise: *we would never have been guided had God not guided us* (7:43). Both invert *because of knowledge I have* (28:78).
- **Glorification near thanks.** `sbḥ` touches `shkr` only within two ayahs, in 5 places. At least 15 of the 41 *subḥān* ayahs end *above what they associate*, and *yushrik-* is the commonest relapse after rescue.
- **Takbīr.** *Li-tukabbirū llāha ʿalā mā hadākum* occurs exactly twice, both beside thanks (2:185, 22:37). The formula *Allāhu akbar* is absent.
- **Remembrance** starts the sequence (*remember Me … thank Me*, 2:152). It also stands as a choice beside thanks (*whoever wishes to remember or wishes to thank*, 25:62).

**[interpretive] A division of labour.**
- *Shukr* names the stance and the deed.
- *Ḥamd* is what is said, naming what He did.
- *Tasbīḥ* keeps the gift from being credited to anyone else, oneself included. **Glorification is not thanks. It is what keeps thanks from going wrong.**

### The hereafter's last words

> **10:10** دَعۡوَىٰهُمۡ فِيهَا سُبۡحَٰنَكَ ٱللَّهُمَّ وَتَحِيَّتُهُمۡ فِيهَا سَلَٰمࣱۚ وَءَاخِرُ دَعۡوَىٰهُمۡ أَنِ ٱلۡحَمۡدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلۡعَٰلَمِينَ
> Saheeh: *Their call therein will be, "Exalted are You, O Allah," and their greeting therein will be, "Peace." And the last of their call will be, "Praise to Allah, Lord of the worlds!"*

- **What is spoken in Paradise.**
  - Glorification, peace, and praise last (10:10).
  - Praise *who fulfilled His promise* (39:74).
  - Praise *who removed grief*, then *our Lord is ghafūr shakūr* (35:34).
  - Once, God's favour (*fa-manna*) recalled with no *ḥamd* (52:26-28).
- **No istighfār inside Paradise.** The last recorded request is on the Day: *perfect our light for us and forgive us* (66:8).
- **No human `shkr` there.** *Shakūr* is God's, in their mouths (35:34).

**[interpretive]** Glorification survives, and praise gets the last word. Seeking forgiveness survives only as the Name *Ghafūr*, spoken in praise. Human thanks is absorbed into praise, and the Thanker is now God. The final speech has no request in it.

## 7. Negative space (each checked)

- **No count.**
  - Glorification and praise have *times* (20:130, 30:17-18, 40:55, 50:39-40), and remembrance is to be *much* (33:41). None has a number.
  - `shkr` has no time-word.
  - The after-prayer ×33 is hadith. Its three phrases each occur in the Quran over a gift, uncounted (43:13, 23:28, 2:185).
- **No prostration of thanks.** `sjd` shares no ayah with `shkr`.
- **No "thank people"**, except parents (31:14).
  - Between people the text has repaying (28:25), a better greeting (4:86) and *do not forget graciousness* (2:237).
  - Givers decline thanks (76:9).
- **No commanded feeling, except *rejoice* in His bounty and mercy (10:58).**
  - Rejoicing in possessions is forbidden (28:76, 57:23).
  - The named feelings are the ungrateful's: despair (11:9) and exultation (11:10). No feeling of gratitude is named.
- **No thanks for hardship itself.** In hardship the text gives *innā li-llāh* (2:156), patience, and praise and glorification during distress (15:97-98).
- **Also absent:** human `shkr` in Paradise; anyone who thanks after being forgiven; "I thank You"; any ranking of blessings.

## 8. Corrections the investigation made to itself

The full log is in `README.md`. These are the corrections that change the picture:
- `ḥmd` and `shkr` are crossed, not paired.
- Only two vows are at sea.
- *Niʿma* is 50 tokens.
- The prescribed words over a gift go well beyond 43:13.
- 5:89 is law made clear, not eased. 2:56 is a second life. Forgiveness leading to thanks rests on 2:52 alone.
- A companion purpose or command appears in 11 of 14 clauses, not all.
- Ingratitude is an act *or a sentence*.
- 35:30 and 42:23 are not 14:7's positive half.
- 3:144 names the grateful in defeat.
- **40:55 is istighfār first.** Praise-before-istighfār holds in 3 of 4, which also corrected the istighfār synthesis.
- 52:26-28 is Paradise speech without *ḥamd*.
- At synthesis: *ighfir* comes near thanks twice (3:147, 14:41).

## Open questions

1. What does 14:7 increase? Is anyone shown increased *because* of thanks? The text is silent.
2. Is *shukran* (34:13) purpose, manner, or the thing done?
3. Why does the istighfār verb never come within five ayahs of thanks? Is it deliberate, or a result of where each occurs (thanks in purpose clauses, istighfār in commands and prayers)?
4. What made Noah *ʿabdan shakūrā* (17:3)?
5. How many spoken acknowledgements have no *ḥamd*, like Joseph's *aḥsana bī* (12:100)?

## For practice

- Name the gift with God as the one who did it: *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi lladhī …* (23:28, 14:39).
- When a gift arrives, glorify Him and disown your own power (43:13), or say *mā shāʾa llāh* (18:39).
- Ask to be enabled to thank *and* to do the deed (27:19, 46:15). Then use the gift (34:13, 22:36).
- After a gift, glorify with praise and ask forgiveness (110:3). Close by turning back (46:15).
- In loss, the text asks for patience, not thanks for the loss. Thanks there means not turning back (3:144).
