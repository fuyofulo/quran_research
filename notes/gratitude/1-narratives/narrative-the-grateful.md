# The grateful: twelve portraits

## Headline finding

Across twelve portraits, gratitude is never speech alone. Most carry a `shkr` word (named or told), yet Joseph, and the righteous of 76 who refuse thanks, thank or give without one. In nine portraits thanks sits next to an act: work, worship, feeding, prayer, steadfastness. Only 27:19 and 46:15 write the act into the prayer itself: *enable me to thank, **and** to do righteousness You approve*. The gift is called the *test* (27:40; cf. 89:15). The prayers end in *mercy* (27:19) or *repentance and submission* (46:15). Human-to-human thanks is owed to parents (31:14) and waived by givers (76:9).

## 2. Speech, deed or state? (data-grounded count, then [interpretive])

Count thanks tied to an act in the text beside it:
- **In the thanks-sentence itself (3):** 34:13 (*work... in thanks*), 27:19, 46:15 (*and to do righteousness You approve*).
- **In the same passage (6 more):** Abraham (obedient, prayer 14:37, 14:40), Luqmān (prayer, patience, 31:17), Moses (take the tablets with strength, 7:145), the Prophet (worship, 39:66; orphan and petitioner, 93:9-10), the righteous of 76 (feeding), 3:144 (not turning back).
- **No act stated (3):** Noah, Joseph (apart from raising his parents), Lot's family. The latter two are rescue stories told in a line.

So **nine of twelve**. Speech appears in seven portraits (*al-ḥamdu li-llāh* at 27:15, 14:39, 23:28; the prayers at 27:19, 46:15; Joseph's address). In none is it the only thing.

**[interpretive]** The portraits do not make a "state". No one says *I am grateful*. The nearest is a name given by God (*shakūr*, *shākir*). The text shows thanks as **recited gift, then asked-for action**, not a feeling reported.

## 3. Solomon at 27:40: the gift as the test

> فَلَمَّا رَءَاهُ مُسۡتَقِرًّا عِندَهُۥ قَالَ هَٰذَا مِن فَضۡلِ رَبِّي لِيَبۡلُوَنِيٓ ءَأَشۡكُرُ أَمۡ أَكۡفُرُۖ وَمَن شَكَرَ فَإِنَّمَا يَشۡكُرُ لِنَفۡسِهِۦۖ وَمَن كَفَرَ فَإِنَّ رَبِّي غَنِيࣱّ كَرِيمࣱ
> "...he said: *This is from my Lord's favour, to test me whether I will be grateful or ungrateful.*"

Solomon names the throne *faḍl* and in the same breath *ibtilāʾ* (`bly`). He does not say *I thank*. He says *whether*. The question is kept open on his own account.

**Compare 89:15-16.** Man, *when his Lord tests him (ibtalāhu), honours him and gives him ease*, says *my Lord has honoured me*. When He tests by tightening provision, says *my Lord has humiliated me*. The same verb covers both. The human error in 89 is reading the gift as proof of standing. Solomon reads the gift as the exam. He then adds the thing 89 lacks: the benefit of thanks returns to the thanker, and God is *ghanī karīm*, so nothing is owed to Him.

**[interpretive]** The test is not hidden in loss. It is sharpest at the peak, for the king who says *we have been given from all things* (27:16).

## 4. Two prayers, word for word (27:19 and 46:15)

> رَبِّ أَوۡزِعۡنِيٓ أَنۡ أَشۡكُرَ نِعۡمَتَكَ ٱلَّتِيٓ أَنۡعَمۡتَ عَلَيَّ وَعَلَىٰ وَٰلِدَيَّ وَأَنۡ أَعۡمَلَ صَٰلِحࣰا تَرۡضَىٰهُ ... (27:19, continuing *wa-adkhilnī bi-raḥmatika fī ʿibādika ṣ-ṣāliḥīn*)
>
> رَبِّ أَوۡزِعۡنِيٓ أَنۡ أَشۡكُرَ نِعۡمَتَكَ ٱلَّتِيٓ أَنۡعَمۡتَ عَلَيَّ وَعَلَىٰ وَٰلِدَيَّ وَأَنۡ أَعۡمَلَ صَٰلِحࣰا تَرۡضَىٰهُ وَأَصۡلِحۡ لِي فِي ذُرِّيَّتِيٓۖ إِنِّي تُبۡتُ إِلَيۡكَ وَإِنِّي مِنَ ٱلۡمُسۡلِمِينَ (46:15)

| Slot | 27:19 and 46:15 (identical) | Only 27:19 | Only 46:15 |
|---|---|---|---|
| address | *rabbi* | | |
| ask | *awziʿnī* ("enable/hold me") | | |
| purpose 1 | *an ashkura niʿmataka llatī anʿamta ʿalayya wa-ʿalā wālidayya* | | |
| purpose 2 | *wa-an aʿmala ṣāliḥan tarḍāhu* | | |
| then | | *wa-adkhilnī bi-raḥmatika fī ʿibādika ṣ-ṣāliḥīn* | *wa-aṣliḥ lī fī dhurriyyatī; innī tubtu ilayka wa-innī mina l-muslimīn* |

**Data-grounded.**
- The verb *awziʿ* (`wzʿ`, form IV imperative) occurs only in these two verses. The same root's passive describes Solomon's armies *marshalled in ranks* two verses earlier (27:17), and the wrongdoers gathered (27:83, 41:19).
- Both prayers are about *the favour on me **and on my parents***. Parents appear in this portrait gallery at 27:19, 46:15, 14:41, 31:14 and 12:100.
- 46:15 is introduced by the pregnancy and weaning clause, *until he reaches maturity and forty years*, and the speaker is the generic *insān*. 27:19 is said by a king who commands jinn, men and birds. The same words are put on both lips.
- Only 46:15 is followed by an explicit reply (46:16): **best deeds accepted, misdeeds overlooked**. The reply to 27:19 is the story continuing.

**[interpretive]** The king's version asks for entry into the company of the righteous *by mercy* (27:19). The ordinary man's version closes with *tubtu* and *mina l-muslimīn*, and asks for his children. Thanks, for both, is a petition about the future, not a receipt.

## 5. Thanks without the word

**Joseph (12:100-101).**
- Gift: *qad jaʿalahā rabbī ḥaqqan* (the dream made true), *qad ātaytanī mina l-mulk*, *ʿallamtanī min taʾwīl al-aḥādīth*.
- Goodness: *wa-qad **aḥsana bī*** (good to me) *idh akhrajanī mina s-sijn*. He says prison, not the pit; and the break with his brothers is Satan's *nazagh* (12:100).
- God: *inna rabbī **laṭīfun** li-mā yashāʾ*, *al-ʿAlīm al-Ḥakīm*.
- Then the ask: *tawaffanī musliman wa-alḥiqnī bi-ṣ-ṣāliḥīn* (12:101).
- Same shape as the two prayers: recite what was given, then ask.

**Abraham (14:39).** *Al-ḥamdu li-llāhi lladhī wahaba lī ʿalā l-kibar Ismāʿīl wa-Isḥāq; inna rabbī la-samīʿu d-duʿāʾ.* The gift is named (sons in old age), and the closing phrase names God as the one who *hears asking*. The praise leads straight into more asking (14:40-41, including forgiveness for himself and his parents).

**Shared vocabulary.** Name the gift; say what God did with a verb (*wahaba*, *aḥsana*, *ātā*); close with a Name (*Samīʿ*, *Laṭīf*, *ʿAlīm*). Then ask.

## 6. Thanking people

- **31:14** *ani shkur lī wa-li-wālidayk*: one verb, two objects, with God first. Preceded by the mother's *weakness upon weakness* and weaning.
- **31:15 is the limit.** If they strive to make you associate, *do not obey them, **yet keep their company kindly*** (*ṣāḥibhumā fi d-dunyā maʿrūfan*), *and follow the way of those who turn back to Me*. Thanks to parents covers kindness, not obedience in shirk.
- **46:15** puts the parents inside the thanks to God: the favour is *upon me and upon my parents*. **17:23-24** names the deeds (*no "uff"*, *a noble word*, *lower the wing*, *my Lord, have mercy on them as they raised me*). Neither uses `shkr`. 46:17 shows the opposite: *uff to you* from the one who refuses the call.
- **76:8-9** the givers refuse it: *we want from you no reward and no thanks (shukūr)*, and the text approves (76:11-12).

**Shape (data-grounded).** In 12 portraits, human-to-human `shkr` occurs **once as a command, to the child toward the parent**, and **once as a refusal, by givers toward the needy**. The text never commands thanking benefactors in general. Its deed-vocabulary for parents is kindness, humility, mercy and prayer. **Hadith's "whoever does not thank people..." is outside the evidence base and is not in these verses.**

**[interpretive]** Debt flows up to parents and God; the giver downward asks nothing.

## 7. What God says of the grateful

- **Chosen and guided:** Abraham, 16:121 (*ijtabāhu wa-hadāhu*), then *good in this world* (16:122).
- **Rewarded:** *sa-najzī sh-shākirīn* (3:144-145), *kadhālika najzī man shakar* (54:35, right after the rescue as *niʿma*).
- **Named servant:** Noah, *ʿabdan shakūrā* (17:3).
- **Few:** 34:13, said of the family of David themselves.

**Is Noah's gratitude explained?** No. 17:3 gives a name and no cause. The closest text is 23:28-29, where Noah is **told** to say *al-ḥamdu li-llāh* on boarding, and to ask for a landing. **[interpretive]** The Quran gives his descendants the title as an argument: *you are the offspring of those carried; he was shakūr*. What made him so is not stated.

## For practice

1. Name the gift exactly, with God as the verb's subject: "You gave me... You were good to me when...". Joseph and Abraham model this.
2. Make thanks an ask: *enable me to thank and to do what You approve.* Thanks that stops at words has no portrait in the text.
3. When something comes easily, ask whether it is a gift or a test (27:40). The king did.
4. Include your parents in the thanks (27:19, 46:15), and keep kindness even where you cannot obey (31:15).
5. End with return: *tubtu ilayka* and *mina l-muslimīn* (46:15), or *by Your mercy* (27:19).
6. When you give, you need not seek thanks (76:9).
