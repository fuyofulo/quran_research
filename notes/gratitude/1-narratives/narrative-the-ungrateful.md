# The ungrateful: Saba', the town, the gardens, Qārūn, the broken vows

## Headline finding

The Quran's ingratitude is mostly an **act** with a fixed shape: a gift, a command to meet it (*eat and thank*, 34:15; *say mā shāʾa llāh*, 18:39; *seek the hereafter with it*, 28:77), a different response, and a loss. The commonest form is denying the giver or crediting oneself, but the best-attested sequence is **rescue, then forget**: nine passages, three with a vow to "be among the grateful" (6:63, 10:22, 7:189), all broken. Loss is not always visible: some are left with the gift (3:178), a worse sign.

## 1. Anatomy (data-grounded counts, passage level)

Unit = a passage from the `the-ungrateful` pack (116 verses). A passage may carry more than one form. `kfr` judged by context: *deny* vs *be ungrateful* is often **both at once** (34:17, 16:55 *li-yakfurū bi-mā ātaynāhum*: "so as to deny/be ungrateful for what We gave"). Expiation senses excluded (none arise in these verses).

| Form | Passages | Count |
|---|---|---|
| (a) denying the giver / giving his gift to another | 16:112, 14:28, 34:17, 18:42, 16:55, 29:66, 30:34, 7:190, 43:15, 17:89, 25:50 | **11** |
| (b) crediting oneself (*knowledge*, *mine*, *it will never perish*) | 28:78, 39:49, 41:50, 18:35-36, 11:10 (*bad times left me*) | **5** |
| (c) forgetting after rescue (vow or call, then relapse) | 10:21-23, 17:66-69, 29:65-67, 31:32, 30:33-34, 6:63-64, 7:189-190, 16:53-55 | **9** (3 with vow) |
| (d) using the gift for transgression | 10:23 (*yabghūn*), 28:76-77 (tyranny, corruption), 14:28 | **3** |
| (e) asking for the gift's removal | 34:19 | **1** |
| (f) despair or boasting when it moves | 11:9-10, 17:83, 42:48 | **3** |
| unspecified portrait (*kanūd*, *kafūr*, *mā akfarah*) | 100:6, 22:66, 80:17 | 3 |

**Most frequent: (a), 11.** But four of those eleven (16:55, 29:66, 30:34, 7:190) are the closing verses of the (c) passages. Without the overlap, (a) is 7 and **(c) is the largest at 9**. **[interpretive]** Denial is usually where forgetting ends, not a separate sin.

Relapse verbs: *yushrik-* 5, *aʿraḍa* 1, *yabghūn* 1, *makr* 1, *jaḥada* 1 (31:32). Only 7:189 gives the gift itself to partners.

**Is the sincerity real by the text's own account?** The text calls it sincerity: *mukhliṣīn lahu d-dīn* (10:22, 29:65, 31:32), and 17:67 says the false hopes are *lost* (*ḍalla*). It never calls it fake and never praises it. It holds the cry as true and the relapse as the fault. 31:32 also leaves a remnant: *minhum muqtaṣid*. **[interpretive]** The sincerity is real **and** short: danger removes the competing claimants, so the cry is pure. Gratitude is the same sincerity carried into safety, where the competitors return (*at once*, *idhā*, 10:23, 30:33). The vow is *to be among the grateful*, a future state; thanks has to outlast the storm.

## 3. Saba' and the town

Panel 1. **34:15** لَقَدۡ كَانَ لِسَبَإࣲ فِي مَسۡكَنِهِمۡ ءَايَةࣱۖ جَنَّتَانِ عَن يَمِينࣲ وَشِمَالࣲۖ كُلُواْ مِن رِّزۡقِ رَبِّكُمۡ وَٱشۡكُرُواْ لَهُۥۚ بَلۡدَةࣱ طَيِّبَةࣱ وَرَبٌّ غَفُورࣱ
Saheeh: "Eat from the provisions of your Lord and be grateful to Him. A good land [have you], and a forgiving Lord." Khattab: "Eat from the provision of your Lord, and be grateful to Him. Yours is a good land and a forgiving Lord."

| | Saba' (34:15-19) | The town (16:112-113) |
|---|---|---|
| Blessing | two gardens; safe journeys between visible cities (34:18) | *āminatan muṭmaʾinnah*, provision *raghadan* from everywhere |
| Command | *kulū… wa-shkurū lahu* (34:15) | none stated in the parable; a Messenger arrives (16:113) |
| What they did | *aʿraḍū* (turned away, 34:16); then *rabbanā bāʿid bayna asfārinā* (34:19) | *fa-kafarat bi-anʿumi llāh* |
| What was taken | flood of the dam; gardens replaced by bitter fruit, tamarisk, sparse lote; scattered | *libās al-jūʿ wa-l-khawf* |

**The mechanism, in the text's own words:** 34:17 *dhālika jazaynāhum bi-mā kafarū*: "that We repaid them *because* they were ungrateful/denied" (bi-mā = cause). 16:112 ends *bi-mā kānū yaṣnaʿūn* (for what they used to *do*), the second place the text puts it as deed. 16:112 is a reversal of the gift's own words: *safe and at ease* becomes *hunger and fear*. 34:16 does not say who sent the flood: "We sent upon them." 34:17 states it as law: *wa-hal nujāzī illā l-kafūr*. **[interpretive]** Compare 8:53 and 13:11, both stated in the abstract: God does not change a blessing until a people change what is in themselves. The narratives are the examples.

34:19 is the strangest: the people ask for **distance**, hardship dressed as boldness. The verse closes *inna fī dhālika la-āyātin li-kulli ṣabbārin shakūr*: the sign is offered to the *very patient, very grateful* (also 14:5, 31:31, 42:33), not to the ruined.

## 4. What the grateful party says

The text supplies the words of meeting a blessing; it does so by rebuking those who did not use them.

Panel 2. **18:39** وَلَوۡلَآ إِذۡ دَخَلۡتَ جَنَّتَكَ قُلۡتَ مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ لَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِٱللَّهِ
Saheeh: "And why did you, when you entered your garden, not say, 'What Allah willed [has occurred]; there is no power except in Allah'?" Khattab: "If only you had said, upon entering your property, 'This is what Allah has willed! There is no power except with Allah!'"

Panel 3. **28:77-78** وَٱبۡتَغِ فِيمَآ ءَاتَىٰكَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلدَّارَ ٱلۡأٓخِرَةَۖ وَلَا تَنسَ نَصِيبَكَ مِنَ ٱلدُّنۡيَاۖ وَأَحۡسِن كَمَآ أَحۡسَنَ ٱللَّهُ إِلَيۡكَۖ … قَالَ إِنَّمَآ أُوتِيتُهُۥ عَلَىٰ عِلۡمٍ عِندِيٓ
Saheeh: "seek, through that which Allah has given you, the home of the Hereafter; and do not forget your share of the world. And do good as Allah has done good to you… He said, 'I was only given it because of knowledge I have.'" Khattab: "seek the reward of the Hereafter by means of what Allah has granted you, without forgetting your share of this world. And be good to others as Allah has been good to you… 'I have been granted all this because of some knowledge I have.'"

The text gives one phrase and two acts, none using the word *shukr*:
- **A phrase on entering**: *mā shāʾa llāh, lā quwwata illā bi-llāh* (18:39). It locates the power: not in the owner.
- **No exulting** (*lā tafraḥ*, 28:76): God "does not love the exultant." (Contrast 10:22, where they *rejoice* in the wind before the storm.)
- **Spend it toward the hereafter, keep your worldly share, do good as God did good to you** (28:77). The model of *iḥsān* is the gift itself: he is asked to give back in kind.
- These are advice to the proud, not a recorded prayer. Absent: any "thank You" from either party.

Qārūn's answer, *ʿalā ʿilmin ʿindī*, is not refuted by God arguing; it is answered by an example (28:78: generations stronger than him destroyed), then the earth swallowing him with *no company to aid him other than God* (28:81). The garden-owner's end uses the same phrase (18:43) and a late regret: *I wish I had not associated anyone with my Lord* (18:42). **[interpretive]** Both ends return to the one party he had left out.

## 5. Feeling or act?

Panel 4. **100:6-8** إِنَّ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ لِرَبِّهِۦ لَكَنُودࣱ ۝ وَإِنَّهُۥ عَلَىٰ ذَٰلِكَ لَشَهِيدࣱ ۝ وَإِنَّهُۥ لِحُبِّ ٱلۡخَيۡرِ لَشَدِيدٌ
Saheeh: "Indeed mankind, to his Lord, is ungrateful. And indeed, he is to that a witness. And indeed he is, in love of wealth, intense." Khattab: "…humankind is ungrateful to their Lord, and they certainly attest to this, and they are truly extreme in their love of worldly gains."

Data: the text gives two traits next to *kanūd*: **witness** (*shahīd*, to it, either he testifies against himself or his own conduct testifies; the pronoun is open) and **intensity of love of *khayr*** (wealth/goods). Neither is a feeling of ungratefulness; both are a posture toward goods. Every other portrait is behaviour: turning away (17:67, 17:83), speech (28:78, 39:49, 41:50, 11:10), choice (*abā… illā kufūrā*, 17:89, 25:50), assigning a share (43:15). Even 80:17 (*qutila l-insān mā akfarah*) ends on what he has *not done*: *lammā yaqḍi mā amarah* (80:23). Between them stands creation-in-stages (80:18-22). **[interpretive]** The text defines ingratitude by what the person does with the gift and who gets the credit; an inner feeling is never named. One may feel warm and still be *kanūd* if wealth is loved with intensity and the giver goes unspoken.

## 6. What happens to the ungrateful in this life

Not always loss, by the pack. Losses: Saba', the town, the garden, Qārūn. But:
- 16:55, 29:66, 30:34 end *fa-tamattaʿū* ("enjoy yourselves"): an invitation, not a punishment, then *fa-sawfa taʿlamūn*.
- 39:50 (*ʿan mā kānū yaksibūn*): earlier ones said the same and their earnings did not avail them, a loss deferred or hidden.
- 3:178, outside the pack: respite given so that they *increase in sin*. **[interpretive]** A gift kept after ingratitude is the quiet form of the warning.
- 11:9 and 17:83 are the despair half: take it away and he is *ya'ūs kafūr*.

**What 14:7's warning half looks like narrated:** *inna ʿadhābī la-shadīd*. It is narrated as reversals (gardens to tamarisk, safety to fear, vast wealth to no helper), each beginning with a *loss of what the gift made possible*, never as the removal of nothing. The increase half (*la-azīdannakum*) is stated as law and **never narrated** among the grateful: this file shows the stick in detail, not the carrot. 14:28 pins the exchange itself: *baddalū niʿmata llāhi kufrā*, exchanged the blessing for ingratitude.

## Phase-0 corrections

1. The vow to "be among the grateful" is attested **three** times only (6:63, 10:22, 7:189), and **only two are at sea**; 7:189 is a child. Of the nine rescue-then-relapse passages, **sincerity wording** (*mukhliṣīn*) appears only in 10:22, 29:65, 31:32, and *munībīn* in 30:33.
2. 31:32 does not condemn everyone: *fa-minhum muqtaṣid*.
3. 80:17 *mā akfarah* is ambiguous between *deny* and *be ungrateful*; the pack's Saheeh says "disbelieving".

## For practice

- When a gift arrives, say whose it is **before** it feels like yours: *mā shāʾa llāh, lā quwwata illā bi-llāh* (18:39).
- The test is not the emergency prayer; it is the next ordinary day. Notice what you stop saying once the danger is gone.
- Do not credit your own knowledge or effort alone (28:78, 39:49): the text calls the gift a *test*, not a verdict.
- Turn the gift outward: seek the hereafter with it, keep your share, do good as you were done good to (28:77).
- Do not ask for less of what you were given (34:19), and do not collapse when it is withdrawn (11:9).
