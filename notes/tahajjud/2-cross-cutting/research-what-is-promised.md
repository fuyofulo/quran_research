# What is promised to the one who prays at night

> Cross-cutting. Audits every reward-claim in the 184-verse night corpus against the Quran's own
> standard promise-formula. **Data-grounded** = counts, grammar, verse inventories. **[Interpretive]**
> = what it means. The later tradition has an enormous amount to say about `maqām maḥmūd`; none of it
> is used here. Hadith and tafsīr are outside this evidence base by design.

## Headline finding (100 words)

The night corpus contains **no unconditional, second-person-plural, content-specified promise for
night-prayer.** Twenty-odd reward-clauses were audited; every one fails at least one of those three
tests, and most fail two. The single unhedged plural promise in the whole corpus (73:20, *"whatever
good you send forward you will find with God, better and greater in reward"*) is grammatically
attached to almsgiving — in the very sentence that **cancels** the vigil obligation. Meanwhile the
Quran addresses "you" (plural) about the night six times, and every one of the six says the night was
made for you to **rest** in. Not once to stand in it.

## 1. The audit

Every clause in the corpus that could be read as a reward for night-worship. Columns: what is
promised, to whom (person/number), certainty-marker, whether content is specified, and what the
promise is actually *attached* to grammatically.

| Ref | Promised | To whom | Marker | Content | Attached to |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **17:79** | `maqāman maḥmūdā` | 2MS — the Prophet | **`ʿasā`** (perhaps) | **named, never explained** (hapax) | the vigil — the only case |
| 73:5 | `qawlan thaqīlā`, a heavy word | 2MS | **`innā sa-`** (strongest in corpus) | specified | the vigil — but it is a *burden*, not a reward |

| 39:9 | *nothing* | — | — | — | the night-stander himself only `yarjū` — **hopes** |
| 52:48 · 26:218–219 | `bi-aʿyuninā` / `yarāka ḥīna taqūm` | 2MS | `inna` / declarative | being **seen**, not rewarded | standing |

| 50:40 · 52:49 · 76:26 · 73:2–4 | *nothing* | 2MS | — | — | — |
| 6:96 · 10:67 · 27:86 · 28:72 · 28:73 · 40:61 | the night, **for you to rest in** | **2MP** ×6 | declarative; 28:73 adds `laʿallakum tashkurūn` | specified | the night itself |

**Verdict on the expectation: confirmed.** No row satisfies all three of (a) plural addressee,
(b) unhedged, (c) content specified, *and* is attached to night-worship. The near-misses are the
proof:

- **17:79** is the only promise attached to the vigil itself — 2MS, hedged with `ʿasā`, its content a
  word occurring nowhere else, its verb (`yabʿathaka`, root `bʿv`) placing delivery after resurrection.
- **73:20** is the only unhedged 2MP promise — and its five plural imperatives are `iqraʾū` (what is
  easy), `aqīmū al-ṣalāt`, `ātū al-zakāt`, `aqriḍū`, `istaghfirū`. **The only plural imperatives in the
  entire night corpus are the ones that replace the vigil.** The reward-clause follows the giving.
- **2:274** is the only complete standard-formula promise in any verse containing `layl` — and it is
  for **charity**.

**[Interpretive]** Read down the "attached to" column and a pattern emerges that no single verse
states: whenever the Quran moves from describing night-worship to specifying a reward, the
grammatical subject changes underneath it. 25:64 stands at night; 25:75 is rewarded `bimā ṣabarū`.
32:16 leaves the bed; 32:19 pays out to `alladhīna āmanū wa-ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt`. 51:17 sleeps little;
51:16 explains the garden by `kānū muḥsinīn`. The vigil is never the *stated* ground of payment.

## 2. Three kinds of not-telling

The fasting investigation established a total silence: no reward for fasting anywhere. The night is
not one silence but three, and they are different instruments.

**(a) Named and unexplained — 17:79.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Arabic | وَمِنَ ٱلَّيۡلِ فَتَهَجَّدۡ بِهِۦ نَافِلَةࣰ لَّكَ عَسَىٰٓ أَن يَبۡعَثَكَ رَبُّكَ مَقَامࣰا مَّحۡمُودࣰا |
| Saheeh | And from [part of] the night, pray with it as additional [worship] for you; it is expected that your Lord will resurrect you to a praised station. |

`maqām` occurs 14× (plus `muqām` 3×): `maqām Ibrāhīm` (2:125), `maqāma rabbihi` — the standing before
God that one fears (14:14, 55:46, 79:40), the angels' `maqāmun maʿlūm` (37:164), the `maqām amīn` of
the God-conscious (44:51), the `muqām` of paradise (25:76). Only here is a `maqām` qualified `maḥmūd`,
and `maḥmūd` occurs nowhere else. **Data-grounded.** **[Interpretive]** This is not withholding — it is
naming without defining, which does something withholding does not: it hands the reader a term to
carry and no way to cash it. A reader can *want* a maqām maḥmūd without knowing what he wants.

Note also `Hmd`: the night-glorification commands are `sabbiḥ **bi-ḥamdi** rabbika` (20:130, 50:39,
52:48) — glorify *with the praise of* your Lord — and the one thing promised is that the praiser
becomes `maḥmūd`, praised. **[Interpretive]** The direction of praise reverses; that is the whole
content the text supplies.

**(b) Promised and explicitly withheld — 32:17.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Arabic | فَلَا تَعۡلَمُ نَفۡسࣱ مَّآ أُخۡفِيَ لَهُم مِّن قُرَّةِ أَعۡيُنࣲ جَزَآءَۢ بِمَا كَانُواْ يَعۡمَلُونَ |
| Saheeh | And no soul knows what has been hidden for them of comfort for eyes as reward for what they used to do. |

Here the reward is asserted to exist, assigned a category (`qurrat aʿyun`), and its content sealed by
a verb — `ukhfiya`, passive, "has been hidden." **Data-grounded.** The withholding is an act performed
by the text, not an omission. **[Interpretive]** This does the opposite of (a): it forecloses
imagination rather than seeding it. You are told there is nothing here for you to picture.

**(c) Total silence — the imperatives themselves.** 50:40, 52:49, 76:26 and 73:2–4 issue night-worship
commands with **no consequence-clause of any kind**. **Data-grounded.** This is the fasting pattern
exactly, reproduced inside the night corpus.

**[Interpretive] What each does to a reader.** (a) gives him a destination he cannot describe; (b)
tells him the destination exists and refuses him the description; (c) tells him nothing. Together they
produce a practice that cannot be motivated by anticipation — which, on the evidence of 39:9, is the
point: the night-stander there does not *know* he is rewarded, he `yarjū raḥmata rabbih`. The Quran
gives the night-worshipper the same epistemic position it gives its reader.

## 3. `qurrat aʿyun` — the vocabulary of the withheld reward

The idiom "coolness of the eye" occurs **7 times**: noun `qurrat` at 25:74, 28:9, 32:17; verb
`taqarra ʿayn` at 19:26, 20:40, 28:13, 33:51. **Data-grounded** (root `qrr`, `data/morphology/words.jsonl`).

| Ref | Who / what | Register |
|---|---|---|
| 19:26 | Maryam, told to `qarrī ʿaynan` over the newborn — inside the fast-of-silence verse | newborn child |
| 20:40 | Moses returned to his mother, `kay taqarra ʿaynuhā **wa-lā taḥzan**` | child restored |
| 28:13 | same event, near-identical wording, `**wa-lā taḥzan**` | child restored |
| 28:9 | Pharaoh's wife on the infant Moses: `qurratu ʿaynin lī wa-laka` — plus **`ʿasā an`** | adopted child |
| 25:74 | ʿibād al-Raḥmān pray for spouses and offspring as `qurrata aʿyun` | spouse + children |
| 33:51 | the Prophet's wives, `an taqarra aʿyunuhunna **wa-lā yaḥzanna**` | spouses consoled |
| **32:17** | the hidden reward | **the only eschatological use** |

**Six of seven are domestic**, and every one of the six turns on the presence of a specific person —
a child, a spouse. **Three of them pair the idiom with "and not grieve"** (`wa-lā taḥzan(na)`:
20:40, 28:13, 33:51). **Data-grounded.** The phrase appears in **no** standard paradise-description
anywhere in the Quran; the garden vocabulary is `jannāt`, `anhār`, `azwāj muṭahhara`, `ʿuyūn`,
`ghurfa`, `nuzul` — never this.

**[Interpretive]** So the one reward the Quran attaches to the night-risers is described in the only
word-family it otherwise reserves for a mother getting her baby back — not a place, not a rank, but
the specific relief of grief when someone you were separated from is returned to you. For a practice
performed alone, in the dark, unwitnessed, that is a pointed choice of register.

It also closes a loop. **25:74 — `rabbanā hab lanā min azwājinā wa-dhurriyyātinā qurrata aʿyun`** —
is the closing prayer of the same twelve-item list whose third item (25:64) is spending the night
prostrating and standing. The night-standers *ask* for coolness of the eyes in their families; the
other night-portrait (32:16–17) is told a coolness of the eyes has been hidden for them.
**Data-grounded** that both use the same 3-occurrence noun. **[Interpretive]** The reward is answered
in the vocabulary of the request.

## 4. The baseline: how the Quran normally promises

`alladhīna āmanū wa-ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt` occurs in **50 verses**; `jannātin tajrī min taḥtihā al-anhār`
in **34**; `lā khawfun ʿalayhim wa-lā hum yaḥzanūn` in **12**. **Data-grounded** (whole-corpus string
search, diacritic-stripped). The register is fixed: **third-person plural generic category** (believers-and-doers-of-good, never a
named rite); **no hedge** — `bashshiri… anna lahum` (2:25), `jazāʾuhum ʿinda rabbihim` (98:8),
`fa-lahum jannātu al-maʾwā` (32:19), not one using `ʿasā` or `laʿalla`; **content maximally
specified** — rivers, fruit, spouses, permanence, `riḍwān`, and at 98:8 even mutual satisfaction
(`raḍiya-llāhu ʿanhum wa-raḍū ʿanh`). Compare 3:195 for the full force of the language when it
commits: `lā uḍīʿu ʿamala ʿāmilin minkum` + double emphatic `la-ukaffiranna` / `la-udkhilannahum`.

**The night-verses depart on every axis at once**: singular not plural, addressed to one man not a
category, hedged not flat, hapax or withheld not specified. **Data-grounded.**

The cleanest demonstration is internal to Sūrat al-Sajdah. 32:16 describes the night-risers; 32:17
withholds their reward; 32:18 pivots (`a-fa-man kāna muʾminan ka-man kāna fāsiqā`); and **32:19
switches the subject to the standard formula and pays out in full** — `alladhīna āmanū wa-ʿamilū
al-ṣāliḥāti fa-lahum jannātu al-maʾwā`. **Data-grounded.** Two verses apart, both registers: the
night-risers get the sealed envelope, the generic believers the itemised garden.

## 5. Does any felt state attach to the *vigil*?

The fasting investigation's claim was that every experiential state in the Quran attaches to the Book,
to remembrance, or to the night vigil. **The last clause does not survive testing.**

- **`ṭmʾn` (settling of the heart) occurs 13 times**: 2:260, 3:126, 4:103, 5:113, 8:10, 10:7, 13:28
  ×2, 16:106, 16:112, 17:95, 22:11, 89:27. **Not one is night-attached.** 13:28 attaches it to
  **dhikr** with no time-word. 4:103 uses it as a *precondition* for prayer (`fa-idhā aṭmaʾnantum
  fa-aqīmū al-ṣalāt`), not a product of it. **Data-grounded.**
- **`shifāʾ wa-raḥma`** (17:82) sits inside the tahajjud passage — attached to `nunazzilu min
  al-qurʾān`, the sending down of the Book. **Data-grounded.**
- **32:16 `khawfan wa-ṭamaʿan` and 39:9 `yaḥdharu… wa-yarjū`** are the *mode* in which the night-riser
  calls — dispositions he brings, not states he receives. **Data-grounded.**
- **73:6 `ashaddu waṭʾan wa-aqwamu qīlā`** is a property of the night-rising itself — impression and
  precision of speech — not a feeling in the person (see `3-rabbit-holes/rabbit-nashiat-al-layl.md`).

**The one genuine exception, and it is thin:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Arabic | فَٱصۡبِرۡ عَلَىٰ مَا يَقُولُونَ وَسَبِّحۡ بِحَمۡدِ رَبِّكَ قَبۡلَ طُلُوعِ ٱلشَّمۡسِ وَقَبۡلَ غُرُوبِهَاۖ وَمِنۡ ءَانَآيِٕ ٱلَّيۡلِ فَسَبِّحۡ وَأَطۡرَافَ ٱلنَّهَارِ **لَعَلَّكَ تَرۡضَىٰ** |
| Saheeh (20:130) | So be patient over what they say and exalt [Allah] with praise of your Lord before the rising of the sun and before its setting; and during periods of the night, and at the ends of the day, that you may be satisfied. |

`laʿallaka tarḍā` is the **only** interior state the Quran attaches to a command that includes night
worship. **Data-grounded.** But note precisely what it is: 2MS to the Prophet; hedged with `laʿalla`;
and attached to a tasbīḥ command whose time-list runs **dawn, sunset, night-hours, and the ends of the
day** — the night is one item in four. The state is promised to the glorifying, not to the night.

**Answer: no felt state attaches to the vigil as such.** The association runs entirely through
recitation (17:82), remembrance (13:28), and glorification (20:130) — practices that *happen* at night
among other times. **[Interpretive]** The fasting file's third category should be narrowed: the states
attach to the Book, to dhikr, and to tasbīḥ. The vigil is a container for those, not a source of its
own.

The inverse is stated repeatedly and in the plural. Six verses tell "you" (2MP) that the night was
made **for rest**: `jaʿala al-layla sakanan` (6:96), `li-taskunū fīhi` (10:67, 27:86, 28:73, 40:61),
`taskunūna fīh` (28:72) — 28:73 adding `wa-min raḥmatihi`, out of His mercy. **Data-grounded.** And
8:11 gives the one clear interior state the Quran attaches to night: `yughashshīkumu al-nuʿāsa
amanatan minhu` — drowsiness sent as *security* — given to people **sleeping**.

## 6. The honest bottom line

What a person who prays at night can legitimately expect, on the Quran's own terms:

1. **A named station — hedged, post-mortem, addressed to someone else.** 17:79 is real and is the only
   promise attached to the vigil. It is `ʿasā`, 2MS, and `maḥmūd` is never defined. Anyone claiming to
   know its content is drawing on material outside this text.
2. **Inclusion in the standard promise, on the standard terms.** The night-standers of 25:64, 32:16,
   51:17, 3:113 are all inside groups the Quran rewards fully — but as believers-who-do-good, patient,
   God-conscious. The vigil is evidence of the category, never the stated ground of the payment.
3. **A reward in the language of family reunion, explicitly sealed.** 32:17 is a real promise whose
   content is refused you by name.
4. **Nothing felt, promised, at the time.** No settled heart, no light, no peace is offered for the
   standing itself. What is offered — 20:130's `laʿallaka tarḍā` — is hedged and belongs to tasbīḥ.
5. **How this differs from fasting: less than it first appears, but not nothing.** Fasting has total
   silence. The night has a named-but-unexplained station and a promised-but-withheld reward — a real
   difference in *kind*, since the night has content and fasting has none, but not the difference
   between silence and payment. It is the difference between an empty page and a sealed envelope with
   one word written on the outside.

## For the night

- Do not go looking for the feeling. The Quran never promises one for standing at night, and the
  verse people reach for (13:28) is about dhikr and names no hour.
- If a state comes, the text credits the recitation and the glorifying — not the hour, not the
  tiredness. Recite; the night is where recitation lands hardest (73:6), not a merit of its own.
- Expect nothing you can inspect. 39:9's night-stander does not know he is rewarded; he hopes. That is
  the position the text puts you in, deliberately, and it is not a defect in your practice.
- Notice what the corpus keeps doing: 51:19, 32:16, 25:67, 73:20 all end the night on **giving**. Every
  time the Quran turns from the vigil to a plural addressee and an unhedged promise, it is talking
  about wealth. 2:274 pays out in full for spending — by night.
- The one reward named for the night-risers is `qurrat aʿyun`, the phrase used when a mother gets her
  child back. Whatever it is, it is not described as grandeur.
- The night is six times called a place made for you to rest. Vigil is a portion taken from it
  (`min al-layl`), never the whole, and the one time the Quran found people over-doing it, it
  relaxed the command (73:20) and pointed them at zakāt.
