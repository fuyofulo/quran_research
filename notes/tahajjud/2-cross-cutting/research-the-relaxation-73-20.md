# The relaxation — 73:20, the one devotional obligation the Quran walks back

## Headline finding (100 words)

The Quran reduces a stated quantity exactly twice: the battle-ratio at **8:66** and the night-vigil
at **73:20**. Only 73:20 is devotional — the class of explicit devotional walk-backs has one member.
And the two are relaxed on opposite grounds. 8:66 names a defect: `ʿalima anna fīkum ḍaʿfā`, He knew
there is weakness among you. 73:20 names no defect at all: `ʿalima an lan tuḥṣūhu` — He knew you
would not *keep count* of it. The grounds are metrological, not moral. 17:12 confirms the reading:
the night's sign was **erased** and the day made visible *so that you could count*. The vigil is
scaled back because night is not a countable surface.

**[data]** = counted or quoted. **[interp]** = a reading built on it. Marked throughout.

## 1. Is 73:20 unique? — the test and the verdict

**[data]** The test I applied: does the passage (a) state a devotional requirement with a magnitude,
(b) subsequently *reduce that magnitude*, and (c) mark the reduction as a change? All three, or it is
something else.

| Candidate | What it actually does | Reduction? |
|---|---|---|
| **8:65 → 8:66** | 1:10 ratio (20 beat 200) restated as 1:2 (100 beat 200), marked `al-āna` "now" | **Yes** — but military, not devotional |
| **73:20** | half/third/two-thirds of the night replaced by `mā tayassara` | **Yes** — devotional |
| 2:187 | Lifts a *restriction* (relations on the fast-night). Days of fasting unchanged | No — boundary, not amount |
| 2:184–185 | Sick/traveller **defer** the days: `ʿiddatun min ayyāmin ukhar`, an equal number later | No — deferral, quantity preserved |
| 4:101–103 | `laysa ʿalaykum junāḥun an taqṣurū` — a conditional permission, and **4:103 restores it**: `fa-idhā iṭmaʾnantum fa-aqīmū al-ṣalāh`, prayer is `kitāban mawqūtā`, a timed prescription | No — suspension with a stated restore clause |
| 5:6 | Tayammum — a *substitute procedure* when water is absent | No — substitution |
| 2:286, 22:78, 24:61, 48:17, 9:91 | General principles (`wusʿahā`, `min ḥaraj`) or blanket exemptions for the blind, lame, ill, poor | No — no prior magnitude to reduce |
| 2:178, 4:28, 33:50 | `takhfīf` on retribution; lightening in marriage law; a Prophet-specific *expansion* | Not devotional quantities |

**Verdict [data]:** 73:20 is **the only devotional obligation in the Quran whose stated magnitude is
explicitly reduced.** It belongs to a class of two reductions overall; the other member is a combat
ratio. Everything else the corpus offers is exemption, deferral, substitution, suspension, or
general principle — mechanisms that leave the requirement standing for those who can meet it.

Two absences make the point sharper. **[data]** 73:20 contains **no makeup clause** — no `ʿiddatun
min ayyāmin ukhar`, no equivalent of fasting's carried-forward days. And it contains **no restore
clause** — nothing like 4:103's "when you are secure, re-establish it." The fast is deferred and the
shortened prayer is resumed. The vigil is simply reduced, permanently, for everyone.

## 2. 8:66 — the class of two, and where the two diverge

**[8:66]**

| | |
|---|---|
| Arabic | ٱلۡـَٰٔنَ خَفَّفَ ٱللَّهُ عَنكُمۡ وَعَلِمَ أَنَّ فِيكُمۡ ضَعۡفࣰ ا |
| Saheeh | Now, Allah has lightened [the hardship] for you, and He knows that among you is weakness |

**[data]** Same skeleton as 73:20: God knows something about you, and the demand comes down. But the
vocabulary does not overlap at a single point. `xff` (17 occurrences) — the Quran's actual verb for
*lightening* — **never appears in Sūrah 73**, and the two places where `khaffafa` addresses a
community both name `ḍaʿf`/`ḍaʿīf` as the cause (8:66; 4:28 `wa-khuliqa al-insānu ḍaʿīfā`). 73:20
reaches instead for `twb` (turning) and `ysr` (ease), and names no weakness anywhere.

**[interp]** The Quran has a standing idiom for "you are too weak, so here is less." It declined to
use it on the vigil. That is a choice, and it is the whole difference between the two members of
this class: the fighters are told they are weak; the night-standers are told they cannot count.

## 3. `lan tuḥṣūhu` — the best finding in this file

**[73:20, core clause]**

> وَٱللَّهُ يُقَدِّرُ ٱلَّيۡلَ وَٱلنَّهَارَۚ عَلِمَ أَن لَّن تُحۡصُوهُ فَتَابَ عَلَيۡكُمۡ
> — *"And God measures the night and the day. He knew that you would not enumerate it, so He turned
> toward you."*

**[data]** Root `HSy` — 11 occurrences, essentially one lemma (`aḥṣā`). The subject distribution is
the finding:

| Subject | Refs | n |
|---|---|---|
| **God (or His record) enumerates** | 18:49, 19:94, 36:12, 58:6, 72:28, 78:29 | **6** |
| **Humans cannot enumerate** | 14:34, 16:18, **73:20** | **3** |
| Humans try to count elapsed time — and are corrected | 18:12 | 1 |
| Humans commanded to count — a legal waiting-period | 65:1 `wa-aḥṣū al-ʿidda` | 1 |

`aḥṣā` is a divine-competence verb. **[data]** When God is subject it takes a totality: `kulla
shayʾin aḥṣaynāhu` (36:12, 78:29), `aḥṣā kulla shayʾin ʿadadā` (72:28), `aḥṣāhu Allāhu wa-nasūhu` —
God counted it while they forgot (58:6). When humans are subject it is negated twice over the
identical clause:

**[14:34 / 16:18]** وَإِن تَعُدُّواْ نِعۡمَتَ ٱللَّهِ لَا تُحۡصُوهَا — *"If you should count God's
favour, you will not enumerate it."*

**[data]** So the exact grammatical shape of "you cannot count it" appears three times in the Quran:
twice of God's favours, once of the night. **[interp]** The night is put in the same bucket as
divine grace — a thing whose quantity exceeds human accounting. That is not an insult.

The one narrative test case confirms it. **[data]** 18:12 asks *which of the two parties better
counted* how long the sleepers stayed; at 18:19 they answer "a day, or part of a day," at 18:25 the
text says three hundred years and nine, and 18:26 closes it: `quli Allāhu aʿlamu bimā labithū`. **The
only story in which humans attempt to count elapsed time, they are off by three centuries.** And the
sole positive human imperative from the root, 65:1's `wa-aḥṣū al-ʿidda`, counts a divorce waiting-
period — a social, daylight, calendar quantity, not a devotional one.

**The cosmological clincher [data].** 17:12: `fa-maḥawnā āyata al-layl wa-jaʿalnā āyata al-nahāri
mubṣiratan **li-tabtaghū faḍlan min rabbikum wa-li-taʿlamū ʿadada al-sinīna wa-l-ḥisāb**` — "We
erased the sign of the night and made the sign of the day visible, that you may seek bounty from
your Lord and know the number of the years and the reckoning."

**[interp]** Counting and seeking bounty are assigned to the *day* by design. The night's sign was
erased. 73:20 then says the night cannot be enumerated and excuses the bounty-seeker — both of its
grounds are the daytime functions 17:12 already named. The inability at 73:20 is not a lapse in the
worshipper; it is the specification of the night. And the clause immediately before it —
`wa-Allāhu yuqaddiru al-layla wa-l-nahār` — states who *does* hold the measure. **God measures; you
cannot count. The relaxation follows from a difference in faculty, not in resolve.**

## 4. What replaces the quota

**[data]** 73:20 is where Sūrah 73 switches from singular to plural. Six 2MP imperatives, five
distinct verbs: `fa-qraʾū` (×2), `wa-aqīmū al-ṣalāh`, `wa-ātū al-zakāh`, `wa-aqriḍū Allāha qarḍan
ḥasanā`, `wa-staghfirū Allāh`. Prayer, zakāt, and the goodly loan appear nowhere else in the sūrah.
`qarḍan ḥasanan` occurs 6× in the Quran (2:245, 5:12, 57:11, 57:18, 64:17, 73:20).

**Two of the five distinct imperatives are money.** The measured night is replaced by (i) recitation
without a quota, (ii) the ordinary five-times prayer, (iii) obligatory almsgiving, (iv) a voluntary
loan to God, (v) forgiveness-seeking.

**[interp]** The sūrah that opens on the most private image in the Quran — one man wrapped in a
cloak in the dark — closes on a loan. What is removed is a *quantity of night*; what is added is a
*relationship maintained in daylight, with other people's needs inside it.* Nothing here reduces the
demand overall. It redistributes it from measurable solitude into unmeasurable obligation.

**The fasting parallel [data].** The fasting synthesis found `Swm`'s strongest content co-roots to be
`TEm` (feed), `skn` (the needy), `wjd` (afford), `Sdq` — and feeding-praise outnumbering
fasting-praise roughly 3:1 across five surahs. **Both of the Quran's two great personal austerities
have giving bolted to them at the point where the austerity is scaled back.** 2:184 offers `fidyatun
ṭaʿāmu miskīn` in place of the fast; 73:20 offers zakāt and a loan in place of the vigil. **[interp]**
The Quran's substitute for a private discipline you cannot sustain is, in both cases, money moved
toward someone else. Compare also 22:78, which pairs `mā jaʿala ʿalaykum fī al-dīni min ḥaraj` with
the identical couplet `fa-aqīmū al-ṣalāta wa-ātū al-zakāh`.

## 5. The three exemptions — and what they rank above the vigil

**[data]** `marḍā` (sick) · `yaḍribūna fī al-arḍi yabtaghūna min faḍli-llāh` (travelling the earth
seeking God's bounty) · `yuqātilūna fī sabīli-llāh` (fighting in God's way).

Illness and travel are exactly the pair the fasting passage names **twice** (2:184, 2:185
`marīḍan aw ʿalā safar`) and 5:6 names for tayammum. The third is new to this list.

**The middle one is the one to notice.** `yaḍribūna fī al-arḍ` is the Quran's phrase for travel, and
it is here qualified: *seeking God's bounty*. **[data]** `ibtighāʾ min faḍl Allāh` is commercial
vocabulary elsewhere without exception in the relevant cases — 62:10 (`when the prayer is concluded,
disperse in the land and seek God's bounty`), 2:198 (no blame on trading during Ḥajj), 17:12 (the
daylight sign given for seeking bounty), 28:73 (night for rest, day for seeking bounty), 45:12 and
35:12 (sea trade).

**So: earning a living is a stated, sufficient reason not to keep the night vigil.** Not a grudging
allowance — it sits in the same list as illness and combat, introduced by the same `ʿalima`.

**[interp]** Read the exemption-set as a ranking. Three things outrank the vigil: your body, your
livelihood, and the defence of the community. The vigil is not the top of a devotional hierarchy —
it is a practice the text is willing to subordinate to a caravan. Compare 25:63–74, where among
twelve traits of the servants of the Merciful, night-standing sits second and is the only private
ritual on a list otherwise dominated by conduct toward others.

## 6. `fa-tāba ʿalaykum` — turning toward people who did nothing wrong

**[data]** The phrase `fa-tāba ʿalaykum` attaches to a relaxed obligation in exactly two places.

**[2:187]** عَلِمَ ٱللَّهُ أَنَّكُمۡ كُنتُمۡ تَخۡتَانُونَ أَنفُسَكُمۡ فَتَابَ عَلَيۡكُمۡ وَعَفَا
عَنكُمۡ — *"God knew you were betraying yourselves, so He turned toward you and pardoned you."*

The two differ at exactly one point, and it is the decisive one. **2:187 names a wrong**
(`takhtānūna anfusakum`) and pairs the turning with pardon (`ʿafā ʿankum`). **73:20 names nothing**
— no `khiyāna`, no `ʿafā`, no `dhanb`, no `ḍaʿf`. The clause between `ʿalima` and `fa-tāba` is
`an lan tuḥṣūhu` and nothing else.

**[interp]** So the Quran performs its characteristic gesture of mercy-after-fault on a case where
no fault is stated. God turns toward people whose only condition is that they could not keep count.
`twb` is overwhelmingly a sin-vocabulary root — 87 occurrences, clustered by the corpus's own
community detection into "Sin–Mercy Economy," co-occurring with `gfr` in 25 ayahs. **[interp]** Its
use here, with the sin slot empty, is the strongest single signal in the passage: the relaxation is
not forgiveness for slacking. It is God moving toward a limit He built.

## 7. What it means that this is the practice that gets lightened

**[interp]** The honest reading is not "so the vigil doesn't matter." Sūrah 73 spends eight verses
building its case — a heavy word is coming (73:5), impression and speech are steadiest at night
(73:6), the day is already full (73:7) — and 73:20 revokes none of it. Recitation survives the
relaxation; it is the one thing commanded twice in the verse. What is deleted is the *number*.

Three things follow.

**The Quran removed the metric, not the practice.** A quota is the part of a discipline that can be
failed, tracked, and turned into a scoreboard. `Mā tayassara` cannot be failed. **[interp]** It also
cannot be bragged about, and this is a sūrah that has just said, at 73:19, `fa-man shāʾa-ttakhadha
ilā rabbihi sabīlā` — whoever wills may take a way to his Lord. Voluntarism immediately precedes the
un-quantifying.

**The relaxation is preventative, not remedial.** `ʿalima an sayakūnu minkum marḍā` — *there will
be* sick among you. Future tense. The exemptions are written before the failures occur.
**[interp]** The text does not wait for burnout to legislate against it.

**Intensity was real and was not sustainable, and the Quran says so without embarrassment.** 73:20
opens by *reporting* that the Prophet and a group with him actually kept two-thirds, half, a third —
this happened — and then ends it. **[interp]** The pattern is not "aim lower." It is: an unmeasurable
practice held at unmeasurable intensity converts into a measured, ordinary, socially-anchored set
of obligations that runs indefinitely. Zakāt, ṣalāh, a loan, istighfār — none of them require you to
be awake at 3am, and all of them are still standing tomorrow. The seven-day version of a practice is
the version the Quran itself declined to institutionalise.

## For the night

- The Quran ends your quota for you. There is no target hours, no streak, and losing count is the
  *stated reason* the target was removed — not evidence you failed.
- `Mā tayassara` — what comes easy — is the instruction, not the compromise. Recite the amount you
  can recite well.
- Sickness, earning a living, and defending people all outrank the vigil in the text's own list. If
  you have work tomorrow, that is a Quranic exemption, not an excuse.
- If you cut the night short, cut it *into* something: 73:20's replacements are prayer, zakāt, a
  loan, and asking forgiveness. Two of the five are money.
- Nothing in 73:20 says you did wrong. `Fa-tāba ʿalaykum` arrives with the sin-slot empty.
- What survives the relaxation is recitation. Commanded twice, unquantified both times. If you keep
  one thing from Sūrah 73, keep the reciting, not the clock.
- The intensity was real — the Prophet and a group did keep two-thirds of the night. The Quran
  reports it, and then stops requiring it. Both halves of that are the instruction.

For the night.
