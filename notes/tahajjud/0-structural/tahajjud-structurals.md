# Structural pre-cut — tahajjud and night-worship

> Deterministic layer. Everything here is counted, not interpreted, unless flagged **[interpretive]**.
> Generated by `scripts/build_tahajjud_versepack.py` and `scripts/build_tahajjud_cooccurrence.py`
> → `data/structural/tahajjud-versepack.json` (184 verses), `data/structural/tahajjud-cooccurrence.json`.

## Headline finding

The named practice is a hapax; the described practice is everywhere. **`hjd` occurs exactly once in
the Quran** (17:79) — the entire institution of "tahajjud" rests on one word in one verse. Meanwhile
the surrounding vocabulary is enormous (`*kr` 292, `dEw` 212, `Slw` 99, `lyl` 92, `sbH` 92, `sjd`
92). **This is the exact inverse of the fasting corpus**, where the name (`Swm`, 14×) is small but
the legislation is dense and concentrated in one passage. Fasting is a named institution with thin
content. Night-worship is an unnamed practice with thick content.

---

## 1. The `wa-min al-layli fa-` template — verified exhaustively

The phrase *"and from [a portion of] the night, then —"* is a fixed construction. I searched the
whole corpus for `min al-layl` (diacritic-stripped). **Nine verses contain it. Four are imperatives
built on the identical template**, and they are the closest thing the Quran has to a night-vigil
command set:

| Ref | The command | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| **17:79** | `wa-mina al-layli **fa-tahajjad** bihi nāfilatan lak` | keep vigil |
| **50:40** | `wa-mina al-layli **fa-sabbiḥhu** wa-adbāra al-sujūd` | glorify |
| **52:49** | `wa-mina al-layli **fa-sabbiḥhu** wa-idbāra al-nujūm` | glorify |
| **76:26** | `wa-mina al-layli **fa-sjud** lahu wa-sabbiḥhu laylan ṭawīlā` | prostrate + glorify |

(20:130 belongs with these but uses `wa-min ānāʾi al-layli fa-sabbiḥ` — the hours of the night, a
different time-word, so it falls outside the literal string search.)

**Three of the four say *glorify*. One says *keep vigil*.** The hapax `tahajjad` is slotted into a
template otherwise occupied by `sabbiḥ`. **[interpretive]** Tahajjud is not a distinct institution
with its own vocabulary — it is one filling of a slot whose usual filler is glorification.

### And the same construction commands flight

The other partitive-night verses are not devotional at all:

| Ref | |
|---|---|
| 11:81 | `fa-asri bi-ahlika **bi-qiṭʿin mina al-layl**` — Lot, set out with your family in a portion of the night |
| 15:65 | `fa-asri bi-ahlika **bi-qiṭʿin mina al-layl**` — near-identical wording |
| 11:114 | `wa-**zulafan mina al-layl**` — prayer at the approaches of the night |
| 51:17 | `qalīlan **mina al-layli** mā yahjaʿūn` — they slept little of it |
| 10:27 | `qiṭaʿan **mina al-layli** muẓlimā` — faces covered as by patches of dark night |

**A portion of the night is commanded for exactly two things in the Quran: worship, and escape.**
Same partitive construction, same imperative mood. **[interpretive]** That is a suggestive adjacency
and the narrative file develops it.

## 2. Which worship-verb actually belongs to the night

For every worship-root, the share of its ayahs that also contain `lyl`:

| Root | Sense | With night / total | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| `nfl` | supererogatory | 1 / 3 | 33.3% |
| **`sbH`** | **glorify** | **9 / 87** | **10.3%** |
| `sjd` | prostrate | 7 / 81 | 8.6% |
| `qnt` | stand devoutly | 1 / 12 | 8.3% |
| `qwm` | stand | 22 / 597 | 3.7% |
| **`Slw`** | **pray (ṣalāh)** | **3 / 90** | **3.3%** |
| `tlw` | recite | 1 / 61 | 1.6% |
| `*kr` | remember | 4 / 264 | 1.5% |
| `dEw` | supplicate | 2 / 182 | 1.1% |
| `gfr` | forgive | 2 / 202 | 1.0% |
| `rtl` | recite measuredly | 0 / 2 | 0% |

**Glorification is three times more night-attached than prayer.** And the three practices most
associated with night in popular devotion — remembrance, supplication, and seeking forgiveness — are
the *least* night-attached in the Quran's actual wording, at 1.5%, 1.1% and 1.0%.

The `Slw`-with-night verses are only 11:114, 17:78, 73:20. Worth noting that **none of them calls
the night vigil `ṣalāh`** — 11:114 and 17:78 are prayer-timetables, and 73:20 mentions establishing
prayer as a separate clause after the vigil discussion.

**`nhr` (day) appears in 42 of the 81 night-ayahs — more than half.** The Quran's dominant register
for the night is **astronomical, not liturgical**: night paired with day, alternating, subjected,
measured, a sign. Sun, moon, sky and earth all outrank every worship-verb except glorification.

**[interpretive]** Any claim about "the Quran's night" has to survive this table. The devotional
night is a minority register inside a mostly cosmological one.

## 5. Hapax density

**54 lemmas in the night vocabulary occur exactly once.** Concentrated in the load-bearing verses:

- **17:78–79** — `dulūk` (the sun's decline), `tahajjad`, `maḥmūd` (in `maqāman maḥmūdā`). *Three
  unique words in two verses.*
- **73:6** — `nāshiʾa` (the night-rising), `waṭʾ` (tread/impression). *Two in one verse.*
- **51:17** — `yahjaʿūn` (they sleep).

**[interpretive]** The Quran describes the night vigil in words it uses nowhere else. Whatever this
practice is, the text did not have a settled vocabulary for it — or deliberately declined to build one.

## 6. Corpus comparison — fasting vs the night, at a glance

| | Fasting | Night-worship |
|---|---|---|
| The name | `Swm`, 14 occurrences | `hjd`, **1** occurrence |
| Legislation | one dense passage (2:183–187) | scattered; no single passage |
| Quantity specified | a month, 3 days, 60 days, 10 days | **nothing** — 73:20 states fractions then dissolves them |
| Exemptions | illness, travel (stated twice) | illness, travel, struggle (73:20) |
| Explicitly eased | 2:185, root `ysr` | 73:20, root `ysr` — `mā tayassara` |
| Reward promised | **none anywhere** | 17:79 `maqāman maḥmūdā`; 32:17 a reward explicitly withheld from description |
| Outcome particle | `laʿalla` (perhaps) | `ʿasā` (perhaps) |

Both practices are eased using the same root. Both hedge their outcome with a perhaps-particle. The
detailed comparison is `2-cross-cutting/research-tahajjud-vs-fasting.md`.

## For the night

- The Quran commands *a portion* of the night, never the whole of it, and never a number. Every
  quantity it mentions (73:20's two-thirds, half, third) it immediately withdraws.
- If you are going to fill a portion of the night, the text's own most frequent instruction for that
  slot is **glorify** — not petition, not ask. Three of the four template-verses say `sabbiḥ`.
- The night vigil is never called `ṣalāh` in the Quran. Whatever form you give it is yours to choose.
- The Quran's night is mostly a sign to be *thought about* (42 of 81 ayahs pair it with the day as a
  wonder). Being awake in it to notice that is closer to the text's centre than most vigil framings.
