# Tahajjud and the Quranic night — synthesis

> Phase-4. Reads every file in `notes/tahajjud/`. **[data]** = counted, quoted, or verified against
> the morphology. **[interp]** = a reading built on it. Hadith and classical tafsīr are **outside
> this evidence base**, and for tahajjud that boundary bites harder than almost anywhere else.

---

## 1. Headline finding

The Quran never told you to pray at night. It told one man — six times, always second-person
masculine singular — and it never once put a night-standing command in the plural. [data] Fasting it
addressed to you directly: plural, decreed, counted, with a month attached. So the ranking you
arrived with is inverted. **The practice you completed and felt nothing from is the obligation. The
practice you suspect is harder is the one the text never obliged you to** — it called it `nāfila`,
surplus, beyond what was owed. [data] The fast suspends intake, and the Quran never once praises
hunger. The vigil suspends sleep, which the Quran calls a sign, a mercy, and something God made for
you, and never once condemns. [data] The three reasons it gives for the night are practical, not
mystical. And the one time it quantified the vigil, it dissolved its own numbers in the same verse.

---

## 2. The corpus in one paragraph

`hjd` occurs **exactly once** in the Quran — `fa-tahajjad` at 17:79 — and that hapax is the entire
lexical basis of the named practice. [data] Around it sits an enormous vocabulary: `lyl` 92, `sbH`
92, `sjd` 92, `Slw` 99, `dEw` 212, `*kr` 292, `gfr` 234, `qwm` 660.
([tahajjud-structurals.md](0-structural/tahajjud-structurals.md);
[concept-hjd-the-one-verse.md](1-roots/concept-hjd-the-one-verse.md)) **This is the exact inverse of
the fasting corpus**, where the name (`Swm`, 14×) is small but the legislation is dense and
concentrated in one passage. Fasting is a named institution with thin content; night-worship is an
unnamed practice with thick content. [data] And the hapax is not even syntactically distinctive:
`wa-min al-layli fa-` + imperative is a fixed template with four instances, and **three of the four
say `sabbiḥ`, glorify — only 17:79 says `tahajjad`**. [data] Tahajjud is one filling of a slot whose
usual filler is glorification. [interp]

## 3. What the Quran commands, and to whom

This is the centre of the investigation, and it is a grammatical fact before it is anything else.

**Every night-worship imperative in the Quran is `IMPV 2MS`, and all six sit in passages addressing
the Prophet by singular pronoun.** [data]
([narrative-two-portraits-51-32.md](1-narratives/narrative-two-portraits-51-32.md) §5;
[research-tahajjud-vs-fasting.md](2-cross-cutting/research-tahajjud-vs-fasting.md) §1)

| Ref | Imperative | Singular-addressee marker in context |
|---|---|---|
| 73:2 | `qumi` — stand | `yā ayyuhā al-muzzammil` (73:1) |
| 17:79 | `fa-tahajjad` | `nāfilatan **laka**`, same clause |
| 20:130 | `fa-sabbiḥ` | `fa-ṣbir ʿalā mā yaqūlūn`, same verse |
| 50:40 · 52:49 | `fa-sabbiḥhu` | `iṣbir` (50:39) · `iṣbir li-ḥukmi rabbik` (52:48) |
| 76:26 | `fa-sjud` + `wa-sabbiḥhu` | `ʿalayka` (76:23) |

> **73:2** — قُمِ ٱلَّيۡلَ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا — *"Stand the night, except a little."*

The form-I imperative `qum` occurs **twice in the whole corpus** — 73:2 and 74:2 (`qum fa-andhir`) —
both singular, both to the same wrapped figure. The plural `qūmū` occurs **once**, at 2:238 (`wa-qūmū
li-llāhi qānitīn`), inside a verse about guarding the **prescribed** prayers; it has nothing to do
with the night. [data] The plural exists in the Quran's vocabulary of standing. It is simply never
pointed at the night. Contrast the fast, imposed on a plural in a passive perfect:

> **2:183** — يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ كُتِبَ عَلَيۡكُمُ ٱلصِّيَامُ … لَعَلَّكُمۡ تَتَّقُونَ
> `kutiba` PERF PASS · `ʿalaykum` 2MP · `tattaqūna` IMPF 2MP. [data]

**73:20 is the one verse addressing a plural about the night — and it changes the content in the same
breath.** Six 2MP imperative tokens, five distinct verbs: `fa-qraʾū` (×2), `wa-aqīmū al-ṣalāh`,
`wa-ātū al-zakāh`, `wa-aqriḍū Allāha qarḍan ḥasanā`, `wa-staghfirū Allāh`. [data] Not one is a verb
of standing at night. `aqīmū` is form IV of `qwm`, and across all 54 of its occurrences it never once
describes a body rising to its feet — 40 of the 54 are the fixed "establish prayer" formula. [data]
([concept-postures-sjd-qwm-qnt.md](1-roots/concept-postures-sjd-qwm-qnt.md) §1) **[interp] — the
central claim.** When the Quran addresses a group about the night, it does not hand them the vigil in
a smaller size. It hands them something else: recitation scaled to capacity, the ordinary
obligations, and two giving-clauses.

The three portraits are not commands at all. 25:64 (`yabītūna`), 51:17 (`kānū… yahjaʿūn`), 32:16
(`tatajāfā junūbuhum`) are third-person habitual reports of a type. 51:15–19 narrates people
**already in the garden** and explains backwards what they used to be like; 32:15–17 offers the
behaviour as evidence of belief, not its precondition. [data] Neither unit ever shifts to imperative.
One qualification: singular address is the Quran's ordinary mode for instructions later read as
general, and 51:17 / 32:16 show non-prophets doing this and being rewarded. Generalising the
*practice* is defensible; generalising the *reward clause* is a step the text does not take.
([rabbit-maqam-mahmud.md](3-rabbit-holes/rabbit-maqam-mahmud.md) §5)

## 4. What the Quran says the night is

Classifying all 81 `lyl` ayahs by primary register
([concept-lyl-the-night.md](1-roots/concept-lyl-the-night.md) §2): **cosmological/sign 35 (43.2%),
devotional 15 (18.5%), narrative 13 (16.0%), oaths and merism 18 (22.2%)**; night's commonest
companion is `nhr` (day) at 42 of 81. [data] **The Quran's night is astronomical before it is
liturgical, by more than two to one.** Any claim about "the Quran's night" has to survive that.

**Eight ayahs state that the night's God-given purpose is rest** — `libās` and `subāt` (78:9–11,
25:47), `sakan` (6:96), `li-taskunū fīhi` (10:67, 27:86, 28:72, 28:73, 40:61). [data] None is
qualified; none is paired with a counter-clause about vigil.

> **28:73** — وَمِن رَّحۡمَتِهِۦ جَعَلَ لَكُمُ ٱلَّيۡلَ وَٱلنَّهَارَ لِتَسۡكُنُواْ فِيهِ
> *"And out of His mercy He made for you the night and the day, that you may rest in it."*

Night-rest is filed under `raḥma`, and the sleep vocabulary agrees. It is tiny — about 15 words
across `nwm`, `DjE`, `rqd`, `hjE` — and uniformly positive: sleep is `min āyātihi`, among His signs
(30:23), beside marriage and the diversity of languages; it is `subāt`, repose God made; it is a
battlefield mercy, `amanatan minhu` (8:11). **Scanning all 15: the Quran never once condemns sleep**
— no lemma for laziness, no verse pairing sleep with heedlessness, no instruction to resist it.
[data] ([concept-sleep-vocabulary.md](1-roots/concept-sleep-vocabulary.md) §6) Sharper still,
`tawaffā` — "to take in full," the Quran's standard verb for **dying** — is applied to sleep with no
modification (39:42), and 6:60 has God `yatawaffākum bi-l-layl` then `yabʿathukum fīhi`, the
resurrection root on the waking. Sleep is not compared to death; it is described in death's own
vocabulary, differentiated only by outcome. **[interp]** Staying awake is therefore not opting out of
something neutral — it is declining a nightly small death.

Rest-vocabulary and vigil-vocabulary sit inside the same 81 ayahs and **never address each other**.
No verse says "though night is for rest, stand anyway." [data] The text does not harmonise it.

Narratively, the night is the hour of transition. Revelation descends at night (97:1, 44:3, anchored
at 2:185). The root `sry` has 7 verbal occurrences and **all 7 are night-journeys**; 6 are divine
commands to depart — Lot twice, Moses three times, the Prophet once. The rescued leave at `saḥar`
(54:34); the doomed are seized at dawn (11:81, 15:66); and the plotters use the same hour — `bayyata`
occurs exactly 3 times and is always scheming (4:81, 4:108, 27:49), while ruin arrives at 68:17–19
`wa-hum nāʾimūn`. [data]
([narrative-night-as-divine-timing.md](1-narratives/narrative-night-as-divine-timing.md)) **[interp]**
Night in the Quran is not sacred; it is **decisive**. The same darkness carries Lot out and carries
Ṣāliḥ's attackers in. It confers nothing. What varies is who is awake in it.

## 5. Why night — the stated reasons, and the ones readers supply

Exactly three, all in one passage, none spiritual.
([research-why-night.md](2-cross-cutting/research-why-night.md) §2) **Purpose — 73:5**, `innā
sanulqī ʿalayka qawlan thaqīlā`, "We will cast upon you a heavy word": future tense, so the reason
precedes what it justifies, and the vigil is preparation. **Mechanism — 73:6**, the night-rising
presses harder and speech comes out straighter. **Constraint — 73:7**, `inna laka fī al-nahāri
sabḥan ṭawīlā`, the day is already full — no translator reads this as praise of the day; all four
read it as *crowded*. [data]

> **73:6** — إِنَّ نَاشِئَةَ ٱلَّيۡلِ هِيَ أَشَدُّ وَطۡـࣰٔا وَأَقۡوَمُ قِيلًا
> *Arberry:* "heavier in tread, more upright in speech." · *Saheeh:* "more effective for concurrence
> [of heart and tongue] and more suitable for words." (Divergence 0.829; two hapaxes in six words.)

`aqwam` occurs four times and is never aesthetic in the other three: 2:282 uses it of written
testimony being **more resistant to error**, 4:46 of undistorted speech, 17:9 of the straightest way.
[data] ([rabbit-nashiat-al-layl.md](3-rabbit-holes/rabbit-nashiat-al-layl.md) §3) **73:6's claim is
accuracy — not intensity, beauty, or feeling.** [interp, well grounded] No `taqwā`, no `ikhlāṣ`, no
`khushūʿ` vocabulary appears anywhere in the passage.

**The verified negatives — each scanned across all 81 `lyl` ayahs, not asserted.** [data]
([research-why-night.md](2-cross-cutting/research-why-night.md) §5)

- **The night is never called blessed as a time of day.** `brk` appears in 4 of the 81, and blesses
  God (7:54), the *precincts* (17:1), the *towns* (34:18), and **one specific night** (44:3). Zero
  verses bless night-as-such.
- **God is never said to be nearer at night.** The four nearness statements — 2:186, 50:16, 11:61,
  34:50 — contain no night vocabulary at all. The idea is entirely extra-Quranic.
- **No verse ranks night prayer above prayer at another hour.** Every elative in the 81 ayahs was
  extracted; exactly two are evaluative and both sit in 73:6 — and that comparative is **elliptical,
  with no `min`-complement**. Stronger and straighter than *what* is never stated. The corpus's only
  explicit `khayrun min` is 97:3, comparing one night to a span of months. Meanwhile 17:78 gives the
  timetable's one distinguishing property to the **dawn** recitation: `mashhūdā`, witnessed.

Everything else — quiet, sincerity, fewer witnesses, subdued ego, "it's harder so it's worth more" —
is reader-supplied, and most of it comes from the later tradition, which is outside this evidence
base and not weighed here. The point is only that the gap should be visible. On the last item, 73:20
moves the opposite way: `mā tayassara`, what is easy.

## 6. What is actually promised

Twenty-odd reward-clauses in the corpus were audited against three tests — plural addressee,
unhedged, content specified. **Not one clause satisfies all three and is attached to night-worship.**
[data] ([research-what-is-promised.md](2-cross-cutting/research-what-is-promised.md))

> **17:79** — وَمِنَ ٱلَّيۡلِ فَتَهَجَّدۡ بِهِۦ نَافِلَةࣰ لَّكَ عَسَىٰٓ أَن يَبۡعَثَكَ رَبُّكَ مَقَامࣰا مَّحۡمُودࣰا

**17:79 is the only promise attached to the vigil itself**, and every component is qualified.
`maḥmūd` is a hapax — a **passive** participle inside a root (`Hmd`, 63×) whose active form `ḥamīd`
appears 17 times and is exclusively divine. A passive participle names something done *to* it:
praised, by someone. **The text never names the agent.** `maqām` is polysemous across its 14
occurrences, and 17:79 does not use the `maqāma rabbih` construction anchoring its three
judgment-scene uses. `yabʿathaka` comes from `bEv`, a **resurrection verb in 37 of 67 occurrences
(55%)** against 15 for messenger-sending. And `ʿasā` is a hope-particle — the same grade of hedge the
fast gets with `laʿalla`. [data] **[interp, well supported]** This is an eschatological station, not
a state you will notice after tonight.

The second promise is stated and then sealed:

> **32:17** — فَلَا تَعۡلَمُ نَفۡسࣱ مَّآ أُخۡفِيَ لَهُم مِّن قُرَّةِ أَعۡيُنࣲ جَزَآءَۢ بِمَا كَانُواْ يَعۡمَلُونَ
> *"No soul knows what has been hidden for them of comfort of eyes, as reward for what they used to do."*

`ukhfiya` is passive and deliberate — the withholding is an act the text performs. And the idiom
matters: `qurrat aʿyun` occurs 7 times, **six of them domestic** (a child restored, a spouse, a
newborn), three paired with "and not grieve," and it appears in no standard paradise-description
anywhere in the Quran. [data] **[interp]** The one reward named for the night-risers is described in
the vocabulary reserved for a mother getting her child back.

Two findings set the limit on what a reader can claim. First, **the vigil is never the stated ground
of the payment**: 25:64 stands at night but 25:75 is rewarded `bimā ṣabarū`; 32:16 leaves the bed but
32:19 pays out to `alladhīna āmanū wa-ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt`; 51:17 sleeps little but 51:16 explains the
garden by `kānū muḥsinīn`. [data] Two verses apart, Sūrat al-Sajdah runs both registers — the
night-risers get the sealed envelope, the generic believers the itemised garden. Second, **no felt
state attaches to the vigil.** `ṭmʾn` occurs 13 times and **not one is night-attached**; 13:28
attaches it to dhikr with no time-word. The corpus's single interior state, `laʿallaka tarḍā`
(20:130), is 2MS, hedged, and belongs to a tasbīḥ command whose time-list runs dawn, sunset,
night-hours and the ends of the day — the night is one item in four. [data]

**The honest limit:** a reader can claim inclusion in the standard promise on the standard terms — as
a believer who does good, is patient, is God-conscious. He cannot claim the `maqām maḥmūd` from the
grammar, cannot describe the 32:17 reward, and cannot expect anything at the time. 39:9's
night-stander does not know he is rewarded; he `yarjū raḥmata rabbih`, hopes.

## 7. The relaxation

**73:20 is the only devotional obligation in the Quran whose stated magnitude is explicitly reduced.**
The test — states a magnitude, reduces it, marks the reduction — was run against every candidate:
2:184–185 is deferral, 2:187 lifts a boundary not an amount, 4:101–103 is suspension with an explicit
restore clause, 5:6 is substitution, 2:286 / 22:78 are general principles with no prior magnitude.
Two passages pass, and **the other is a combat ratio** (8:66). [data]
([research-the-relaxation-73-20.md](2-cross-cutting/research-the-relaxation-73-20.md)) 73:20 has no
makeup clause and no restore clause. The vigil is reduced, permanently, for everyone.

> **73:20** (core) — وَٱللَّهُ يُقَدِّرُ ٱلَّيۡلَ وَٱلنَّهَارَۚ عَلِمَ أَن لَّن تُحۡصُوهُ فَتَابَ عَلَيۡكُمۡ
> *"And God measures the night and the day. He knew you would not enumerate it, so He turned toward you."*

Three things in that clause do the work.

**`lan tuḥṣūhu`.** Root `HSy`, 11 occurrences. When God is the subject (6 of 11) it takes a totality.
When humans are the subject it is negated — and **the exact clause-shape appears three times in the
Quran: twice of God's favours (`wa-in taʿuddū niʿmata Allāhi lā tuḥṣūhā`, 14:34, 16:18) and once of
the night.** [data] **[interp]** The night is put in the same bucket as divine grace, a thing whose
quantity exceeds human accounting. That is not an insult. 17:12 clinches it: the sign of the night
was **erased** and the day made visible `li-taʿlamū ʿadada al-sinīna wa-l-ḥisāb` — counting is
assigned to the day by design. The inability at 73:20 is the specification of the night, not a lapse
in the worshipper.

**`khaffafa` is deliberately not used.** `xff` — the Quran's actual verb for lightening, 17
occurrences — **never appears in Sūrah 73**, and both times it addresses a community it names `ḍaʿf`
as the cause (8:66, 4:28). [data] The Quran has a standing idiom for "you are too weak, here is
less," and declined to use it. The fighters are told they are weak; the night-standers are told they
cannot count.

**`fa-tāba ʿalaykum` arrives with the sin-slot empty.** The string occurs three times — 2:54 (the
golden calf), 2:187 (fasting), 73:20 (the vigil) — the two worship-relaxations being the only two.
But 2:187 names a wrong (`takhtānūna anfusakum`) and pairs the turning with pardon (`ʿafā ʿankum`);
**73:20 names nothing — no `khiyāna`, no `ʿafā`, no `dhanb`, no `ḍaʿf`.** [data] `twb` is
overwhelmingly a sin-vocabulary root (87 occurrences, co-occurring with `gfr` in 25 ayahs).
**[interp]** Its use here with the fault-slot empty is the passage's strongest signal: this is not
forgiveness for slacking. It is God moving toward a limit He built.

What replaces the quota: prayer, zakāt, a goodly loan, istighfār, and recitation without a number —
**two of the five distinct imperatives are money.** [data] What is removed is a quantity of night;
what is added is a set of obligations maintained in daylight with other people's needs inside them.
And the three exemptions read as a ranking: **your body, your livelihood, and the defence of the
community all outrank the vigil in the text's own list.** `ibtighāʾ min faḍl Allāh` is commercial
vocabulary everywhere else it appears (2:198, 62:10, 35:12, 45:12). Earning a living is a stated,
sufficient reason not to keep the vigil. [data]

## 8. Tahajjud versus fasting

This is what the investigation was commissioned for.
([research-tahajjud-vs-fasting.md](2-cross-cutting/research-tahajjud-vs-fasting.md))

| Dimension | Fasting | Night vigil |
|---|---|---|
| The name | `Swm`, **14** occurrences | `hjd`, **1** (17:79) |
| Grammatical address | **plural** — `kutiba ʿalaykum` | **singular** — six `IMPV 2MS`, Prophet-directed |
| Legislation | one dense passage, 2:183–187 | scattered; no single passage |
| Quantity | a month; 3 / 10 / 60 days | **none** — 73:20 states fractions, then dissolves them |
| Time-unit | a **month** (`shahr Ramaḍān`) | a **portion** (`min al-layl`, partitive) |
| Exemptions | illness, travel — stated twice | illness, travel, **and fighting** (73:20) |
| Pardon-formula before easing | 2:187 `fa-tāba ʿalaykum` (fault named) | 73:20 `fa-tāba ʿalaykum` (**no fault named**) |
| What is promised | **nothing, anywhere** | 17:79 `maqām maḥmūd`; 32:17 stated then sealed |
| Outcome particle | `laʿallakum tattaqūn` | `ʿasā an yabʿathaka` |
| Stated mechanism | 22:37 — what does **not** reach God | 73:6 — what the hour's **conditions** are |
| What it suspends | **intake** | **sleep** |
| Standing of what is given up | hunger: 5 occurrences, **all negative** | sleep: sign, repose, mercy |
| What it adds | nothing specified | recitation in `tartīl` (73:4) |
| Ever called ṣalāh? | `Swm` and `Slw` share **zero** ayahs | never — all 3 `Slw`+`lyl` ayahs mean the prescribed prayer |
| Position in 33:35 | `al-ṣāʾimīn`, **8th** of ten pairs | `al-qānitīn`, **3rd** of ten pairs |

(Every row is **[data]**.)

**The person is the deepest difference.** The Quran addresses you about fasting, and you are inside
the plural. It names a month, counts days, prescribes make-ups, issues a plural imperative to complete
it, and states two exemptions twice. It has never addressed you in the imperative about the night at
all. No quantity is specified, and the only passage that quantifies it dissolves its own numbers. The
one word naming the practice calls it `nāfila` — a lemma whose only other occurrence (21:72) is a
grandson given as a *bonus* beyond what was asked. [data] The founding command builds its exemption in
from the first utterance: `qumi al-layla **illā qalīlā**`.

**The second difference is what each practice gives up, and it cuts against the folk ranking.** `jwE`
(hunger) occurs 5 times: a trial (2:155), a punishment worn as a *garment* (16:112), something you
will not suffer in the garden (20:118), hellfire's food (88:7), and a favour — He fed them *against*
it (106:4). Every occurrence is negative, twice the **removal** is the blessing, and none is in a
fasting verse. Hunger is never praised anywhere in the Quran under any root. [data] Sleep is a sign, a
repose, a mercy, and is never condemned. **The two practices suspend things with opposite standing in
the text.** Three consequences. The vigil cannot be framed as victory over a vice. It explains why the
vigil is never pluralised — a general obligation to withhold a general mercy would contradict eight
rest-verses. And most usefully: **the fast asks you to stop taking something in; the vigil asks you to
decline, for a portion of the night, a good thing God is offering. The first is abstention. The second
is a trade.** [interp]

**The mechanism-verses run in opposite directions.** 22:37 (`lan yanāla Allāha luḥūmuhā wa-lā
dimāʾuhā wa-lākin yanāluhu al-taqwā minkum`) is negative-then-adversative: it names what does *not*
arrive, subtracting a candidate. 73:6 is a bare comparative predication with no negation and no
transfer, making a positive empirical claim about the hour. [data] **[interp]** The fast is justified
by ruling out the wrong account of itself; the vigil by describing what the hour is like. This matters
for you specifically. **If you fast and feel nothing, 22:37 already said the feeling was never the
cargo. If you keep vigil and feel nothing, 73:6 has made a claim you can check** — which makes the
night the more falsifiable practice, and the one where "nothing happened" carries weight.

**Where they rhyme, they rhyme on procedure, and they hedge toward different objects.** Both are
eased with `ysr`; both use `fa-tāba ʿalaykum`, and in both the turning comes *before* the lightening
— so the lighter rule is not a lower standard the failure earned, it is what the rule becomes once
God has already absorbed the failure. [data] **Neither practice, in the Quran's own handling, is a
test you can fail by finding it hard.** But the hedges point elsewhere: `taqwā` is a disposition
(`wqy` 258×, 166 of them the transitive act of shielding), while `maqām maḥmūd` is a station on a
resurrection verb whose content is never supplied. [data] **The fast hopes for a change in you now;
the night hopes for a placement later, and declines to describe it.** [interp]

**So which is heavier? Fasting. Not close.** [interp, on data throughout] Two honest qualifications.
Uncommanded is not unimportant — the Quran describes night-worshippers with more warmth than it ever
spends on fasters. And difficulty and obligation are different axes: the vigil may well be
experientially harder *because* nothing carries it. A fast has a start time, a calendar, and other
people doing it; a vigil at 3 a.m. has none of those. The text does not contradict that. It declines
to convert it into a duty.

**And here the evidence boundary bites hardest.** Almost everything that makes tahajjud feel like a
heavy named institution — a rakʿah count, the last third of the night, its rank among voluntary
prayers, its place in a nightly schedule — comes from the hadith corpus and the later tradition, not
from this text. The Quran supplies no count, no hour, no frequency, and never calls the vigil `ṣalāh`.
[data] That is a statement about where the authority for the practice's *form* comes from, not a
criticism of the tradition, and this investigation cannot adjudicate it. But it explains the mismatch
you are feeling: **you are comparing a Quranic obligation against a largely post-Quranic institution,
and finding the second heavier.**

## 9. What travels with it

**Giving, 4 out of 4.** Among the four genuine personal-practice virtue-lists containing a
night-worship item, **every one names spending in the same breath**: 25:67; 3:17 (`al-munfiqīn`, same
verse); 51:19 (a `ḥaqq` in their wealth for the asker and the deprived); 32:16 (`wa-mimmā razaqnāhum
yunfiqūn`, same verse). [data] The two misses in the six-list survey are a rhetorical question (39:9)
and a creed-statement (3:113–115), not lists that omit giving.
([narrative-ibad-al-rahman-25.md](1-narratives/narrative-ibad-al-rahman-25.md) §6) And the two
independent portraits — different surahs, no shared vocabulary — land on the **same three-part
sequence in the same order: leave the bed, address God, give money.** [data] 73:20 adds two
giving-clauses where the singular command had none. **[interp]** The Quran does not treat
night-worship as a self-contained transaction between a person and God. It terminates outward.

**Night-worship is never the centrepiece.** Among the twelve traits of the servants of the Merciful
(25:63–74), night-standing is **1 of 12** and the *only* private-ritual item; five are social
conduct, three restraint from grave sins, two formulaic prayers, one receptivity. [data] It sits
second, bracketed by saying "peace" to a provocateur and by dread of Hell — one `wa-lladhīna` clause
among twelve, carrying exactly the weight of "they do not bear false witness."

**Tasbīḥ is the night's characteristic verb.** Share of each worship-root's ayahs also containing
`lyl`: `sbH` **10.3%** (9/87), `sjd` 8.6%, `qwm` 3.7%, **`Slw` 3.3%**, `tlw` 1.6%, `*kr` 1.5%, `dEw`
1.1%, `gfr` 1.0%. [data] Glorification is three times more night-attached than prayer — and the three
practices popular devotion most associates with the night are the *least* night-attached in the
Quran's own wording. It holds under pressure: `dEw` has **zero** genuine night-supplication hits
(35:13 is idol-calling, 71:5 is Nūḥ's preaching campaign), and two of `*kr`'s four night-ayahs argue
night and day are **interchangeable** occasions (25:62's `khilfatan`). [data]
([concept-dhikr-dua-istighfar-night.md](1-roots/concept-dhikr-dua-istighfar-night.md)) The exception
is `istighfār` at `al-asḥār` (51:18, 3:17) — the only devotional act the Quran pins to a named hour.
And the clearest statement that God *answers* a call (2:186) sits inside the **fasting** legislation.

## 10. Open questions

1. **Does the singular address generalise?** The investigation's strongest finding is also its
   biggest interpretive fork. Singular address is the Quran's ordinary mode for commands later read
   as general — but the *reason* given at 73:5, a heavy word arriving, cannot generalise, because no
   one else is receiving revelation. The portraits at 51:17 and 32:16 show non-prophets doing it and
   being rewarded, which supports extension. **The text does not perform the extension for you.**
   Confidence for the practice: moderate-high. For the reward clause: low.
2. **Is the `saḥar` resonance design or coincidence?** `saḥar` names exactly three things: two
   forgiveness-seekings (3:17, 51:18) and one rescue (54:34). That the hour of asking-to-be-covered
   is the hour of deliverance is defensible, not provable, at n=3.
3. **What is `bihi`'s antecedent at 17:79?** Unresolved between the Qurʾān (17:78's doubled `qurʾān
   al-fajr`) and `al-layl` (the nearest noun); translators split, Arberry resolving to "night." If
   the Qurʾān reading is right, tahajjud is not generic night-prayer but staying up *with the
   recitation specifically* — a change to the practice's content, not just its frame. ~60/40 at best.
4. **Minor, flagged not used.** Who does the praising in `maqāman maḥmūdā` (the passive leaves the
   agent open); whether `waṭʾ` means impression or correspondence; and 73:20's compositional seam —
   Sūrah 73 is classed Meccan but 73:20 names zakāt and fighting, which this dataset cannot settle.
5. **What hadith adds — explicitly out of scope.** The rakʿah counts, the last third of the night,
   the divine-descent narrations, the ranking among voluntary prayers: none of it is in this text and
   none of it is weighed here. This investigation makes no claim about whether it should be. It
   claims only that the Quran does not supply it — and that a reader who cannot tell the two apart
   will keep mistaking the second for the first.

## 11. What this changes

- You did not fail at the easier practice and then face the harder one. You completed the **only one
  of the two the Quran obliged you to**; the other is `nāfila` — surplus, never counted, with the
  exemption built into its founding verse.
- Stop ranking them. One is a plural obligation with numbers, the other a singular invitation with
  none. Holding the vigil as a duty you are failing is a category error the text does not support.
- Do not fight your sleep. The Quran calls it a sign, a repose and a mercy, and never condemns it.
  The vigil is a trade, not a conquest.
- Expect nothing you can inspect. No felt state is promised for standing at night; 39:9's
  night-stander does not know he is rewarded, he hopes. That is deliberate.
- The one claim the Quran makes about the night is checkable: impression lands harder, speech comes
  out straighter (73:6). Test it rather than believe it. **Fasting never offered you an experiential
  claim at all — which is why feeling nothing there was never evidence of anything.**
- Both practices end on someone else. Four of four night-worship lists name giving in the same
  breath; the relaxation itself replaces the night with a zakāt and a loan.
