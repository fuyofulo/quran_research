# `sbH` (س ب ح) — tasbīḥ, glorification

## Headline finding (100 words)

`sbH` splits into two unequal jobs: `sabbaḥa` (42×, verb — command or report of glorifying) and
`subḥān` (41×, noun/exclamation — "Glory be to..."). The root's base sense is physical: swimming,
gliding, moving freely through a medium (21:33, 36:40, 79:3, and critically 73:7's `sabḥan ṭawīlā`,
"long business/motion," the corpus's second-highest-divergence verse). Of 9 night-`sbH` ayahs, 4 are
cosmological reports (angels, orbiting bodies), 4 are commands to Muhammad, 1 (17:1) is a theophanic
exclamation. The commands share 17:79's exact syntax: `wa-min(a) al-layl + fa- + imperative`. `subḥān`
is corrective, not celebratory — it follows false claims about God almost every time it appears.

---

## 1. The lemma table is the instrument (data-grounded)

92 occurrences, 87 ayahs, 49 surahs, POS split N48/V44 — almost exactly even, which already signals
two different grammatical lives for one root.

| Lemma | Arabic | Count | POS | Function |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sabbaḥa` | سَبَّحَ | 42 | V | verb — command or report of glorifying |
| `subḥān` | سُبْحَٰن | 41 | N | exclamation — "Glory be to..." |
| `tasbīḥ` | تَسْبِيح | 2 | N | the act/noun of glorifying |
| `yasbaḥu` | يَسْبَحُ | 2 | V | to swim/glide (cosmic bodies) |
| `musabbiḥūn` | مُسَبِّحُون | 2 | N | those who glorify (37:143, 166) |
| `sabḥ` | سَبْح | 2 | N | swimming/gliding/motion (73:7, 79:3) |
| `sābiḥāt` | سَّٰبِحَٰت | 1 | N | "the swimmers/gliders" (79:3) |

The two largest lemmas do completely different grammatical work. `sabbaḥa` (verb, imperative or 3rd
person) is an *action* — something someone does or is told to do: "glorify," "he glorifies," "they
glorify." `subḥān` never inflects as a verb; it is a fixed exclamatory noun, almost always followed by
a possessive suffix or genitive (`subḥānaka`, `subḥānahu`, `subḥāna Allāh`) — grammatically closer to
an interjection than a predicate. One is a *practice*; the other is a *declaration*. That distinction
turns out to matter for §5.

## 2. Base sense: swimming, gliding (data-grounded + interpretive)

Two lemmas (`yasbaḥu`, `sābiḥāt`) and one occurrence of `sabḥ` are unambiguously physical motion:
**21:33 / 36:40**, `kullun fī falakin yasbaḥūn` — each [heavenly body] swimming in an orbit — and
**79:3**, `wa-l-sābiḥāti sabḥā`, the swearing-oath "swimmers/gliders, swimming."

The load-bearing case is **73:7**:

> إِنَّ لَكَ فِي ٱلنَّهَارِ سَبۡحًا طَوِيلًا
> **Saheeh:** "Indeed, for you by day is prolonged occupation."
> **Khattab:** "For during the day you are over-occupied ˹with worldly duties˺"
> **Arberry:** "surely in the day thou hast long business."
> **Pickthall:** "Lo! thou hast by day a chain of business."

Divergence 0.917 — second-highest in the entire night corpus (after 73:1's `al-muzzammil`, 0.933). No
translator renders `sabḥ` here as anything physical — "occupation," "business," "chain of business" —
because in English "swimming" cannot carry the metaphorical weight of *being taken up and carried
along by the day's demands*. But the Arabic word is identical in root to the word used 42 times for
glorifying God. **Interpretive, held carefully:** the semantic core of `sbH` is not praise-language at
all — it is *unimpeded movement through a medium*. Swimming, orbiting, being occupied/swept along —
all "sabḥ." Tasbīḥ, then, may not be "saying words about God's transcendence" so much as *moving with
God the way a swimmer moves with water or a star moves in its orbit* — an act of alignment, not
utterance. This reading is attractive precisely because it explains why the root can describe planets,
birds, business, and prayer with no sense of strain in the text. It should not be overstated: nowhere
does the Quran gloss `sabbaḥa` as "swim" explicitly: the metaphor is reconstructed from lemma-family
proximity, not stated. Confidence: moderate.

## 3. Night-attachment: the cosmological/command split (data-grounded)

The 9 night-`sbH` ayahs, sorted by character:

**Cosmological reports (4)** — third person, describing entities that already glorify:
- **21:20** — angels: `yusabbiḥūna al-layla wa-l-nahāra lā yafturūn`, "they glorify night and day,
  never flagging."
- **21:33** and **36:40** — celestial bodies: `kullun fī falakin yasbaḥūn`, each swimming in an orbit
  (night/day/sun/moon as the antecedent set).
- **41:38** — those near the Lord (commonly read as angels): `yusabbiḥūna lahu bi-l-layli wa-l-nahāri
  wa-hum lā yasʾamūn`, "they glorify Him night and day, untiring."

**Commands to a human (4)** — second person imperative, addressed to Muhammad:
- **20:130** — `wa-min ānāʾi al-layli fa-sabbiḥ wa-aṭrāfa al-nahār`, "and in the watches of the night,
  glorify, and at the ends of the day."
- **50:40** — `wa-mina al-layli fa-sabbiḥhu wa-adbāra al-sujūd`, "and [in part] of the night glorify
  Him, and after prostration."
- **52:49** — `wa-mina al-layli fa-sabbiḥhu wa-idbāra al-nujūm`, "and in a part of the night glorify
  Him, and at the setting of the stars."
- **76:26** — `wa-mina al-layli fa-sjud lahu wa-sabbiḥhu laylan ṭawīlā`, "and part of the night
  prostrate to Him and glorify Him a long night."

**One special case (1)** — **17:1**, `subḥāna alladhī asrā bi-ʿabdihi laylan...` — not a report of
routine glorification and not a command, but the exclamatory `subḥān` marking a singular miraculous
event (the night journey). It belongs to the exclamation-lemma, not the verb-lemma, and functions as
a theophanic frame, not an act of worship being performed by anyone in the narrative.

**The structural parallel (data-grounded):** three of the four commands (50:40, 52:49, 20:130) and the
sujūd-paired one (76:26) all use the identical skeleton **`wa-min(a) al-layli fa- + [imperative]`** —
"and from the night, then [verb]." This is precisely 17:79's construction: `wa-mina al-layli fa-
tahajjad bihi nāfilatan lak`. Same partitive `min al-layl` ("a portion of the night, not all of it"),
same `fa-` consequence marker, same second-person imperative. Tahajjud's hapax is not syntactically
unique — it is one instance of a recurring night-command template whose other instances all take
`sabbiḥ`. **This is the precise structural sense in which glorifying and keeping vigil are the same
grammatical act aimed at different verbs.**

## 4. The cosmic-participation finding (data-grounded, interpretive reading tested)

The Quran states repeatedly, as settled fact, that everything already glorifies God, independent of
human observance: 13:13 (thunder), 24:41 (birds), 57:1, 59:1/24, 61:1, 62:1, 64:1 (all opening with
`sabbaḥa/yusabbiḥu li-llāhi mā fī al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ`). The sharpest statement is **17:44**:

> تُسَبِّحُ لَهُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتُ ٱلسَّبۡعُ وَٱلۡأَرۡضُ وَمَن فِيهِنَّۚ وَإِن مِّن شَيۡءٍ إِلَّا يُسَبِّحُ بِحَمۡدِهِۦ وَلَٰكِن لَّا تَفۡقَهُونَ تَسۡبِيحَهُمۡ
> **Saheeh:** "...there is not a thing except that it exalts [Allah] by His praise, but you do not
> understand their [way of] exalting."
> **Khattab:** "...there is not a single thing that does not glorify His praises—but you ˹simply˺
> cannot comprehend their glorification."
> **Arberry:** "...nothing is, that does not proclaim His praise, but you do not understand their
> extolling."
> **Pickthall:** "...there is not a thing but hymneth His praise; but ye understand not their praise."

24:41 makes the same claim specifically of birds "in ranks," each "knowing its prayer and its
glorification" (`ṣalātahu wa-tasbīḥahu`) — instinctive, not learned. 21:79 adds mountains, subjected
to glorify alongside David.

**Reading, tested:** if `sabbaḥa` names an activity every created thing is already doing (17:44's claim
is total — "not a thing except"), then the human commanded to glorify at night (17:79's neighbor
passage, same surah) is not originating an act unique to worship — they are **joining a condition that
is already universal and constant**, made audible/intentional in a being capable of choosing it. Fasting
and formal prayer are institutions the Quran legislates from nothing; tasbīḥ is an institution the Quran
says already governs the cosmos, and simply invites the human into. This is the single most confident
interpretive claim in this file — it rests on an explicit, repeated, unqualified textual statement (not
an inference from imagery), and 17:44 sitting in the same sūrah as 17:79 is unlikely to be coincidence.
Confidence: high, marked interpretive only insofar as "joining" vs. "originating" is my framing, not
the text's own word.

## 5. `subḥāna` as correction, not celebration (data-grounded)

Checking the contexts of `subḥān Allāh` / `subḥānahu` / `subḥānaka`: 2:116, 4:171, 6:100, 19:35, 23:91,
37:159 all appear **immediately after** an attribution to God of a son, partner, or limitation —
"Allah has taken a son — exalted is He!" (2:116); "the Messiah... was but a messenger — exalted is He
above having a son" (4:171); "they have fabricated for Him sons and daughters — exalted is He and high
above what they describe" (6:100); "it is not [befitting] for Allah to take a son — exalted is He!"
(19:35); identical pattern at 23:91, 37:159. This is not one or two coincidences; it is the dominant
usage pattern of the exclamation across the sample.

**Data-grounded conclusion:** `subḥāna` functions as a **corrective disavowal** — it declares God free
of a specific false claim just made, structurally closer to "no — God is above that" than to generic
praise. This distinguishes tasbīḥ sharply from `ḥamd` (praise/thanks for what God is or does — additive)
and `shukr` (gratitude for a benefit received — reciprocal). Tasbīḥ, in its exclamatory form, is
*subtractive*: it removes deficiency from the concept of God rather than adding attributes to it. If
the verb-form `sabbaḥa` names an ongoing state of unimpeded alignment (§2) and the noun-form `subḥān`
names the act of stripping away false attribution, the two lemmas cohere: to glorify is to keep God
free of every distortion, continuously, the way a swimmer stays clear of obstruction.

## 6. `sbH` vs `Ḥmd` and `shukr` — the compound formula (data-grounded)

`sabbiḥ bi-ḥamdi rabbik` — "glorify with the praise of your Lord" — recurs at 15:98, 40:55, 50:39,
52:48, 110:3, and as the daytime clause of 20:130. **But checking which of the 9 night-specific
clauses use this compound**, the pattern inverts the expected: the *daytime/general* commands (50:39,
52:48, 20:130's first clause) carry `bi-ḥamdi rabbik`; the *night-specific* clauses that follow them
(50:40, 52:49) drop it and say only `fa-sabbiḥhu` — bare glorification, paired instead with `adbāra
al-sujūd` (50:40) or `idbāra al-nujūm` (52:49). 76:26 pairs the night command with `sujūd`, not `ḥamd`,
directly: "prostrate to Him and glorify Him." 20:130's night clause (`fa-sabbiḥ`) is elliptical,
carrying the earlier `bi-ḥamdi` by continuation rather than repeating it. **So the compound formula
is a daytime/framing device; the night clause itself typically strips tasbīḥ down to the bare verb**,
often re-paired with posture (`sujūd`) rather than with `ḥamd`. Interpretively: pairing `sabbiḥ` with
`ḥamd` in the day-verses anchors glorification (subtractive, corrective) to praise (additive,
descriptive) — a fuller devotional statement for daylight address; the night, when it appears alone,
seems to ask for the more stripped, corrective act itself, alongside bodily posture rather than spoken
content.

## For the night

- The night-commands are not asking for eloquence. `wa-min al-layli fa-sabbiḥhu` is bare and short —
  three or four words. The formula that needs `bi-ḥamdika` belongs to the day-verses.
- Tasbīḥ at night is grammatically identical in shape to tahajjad at night (`wa-min al-layl + fa- +
  imperative`) — treat them as the same kind of instruction if not the same content.
- 17:44's claim is total: everything already glorifies. Standing at night to glorify is not starting
  something; it's the one moment a human joins a chorus already running, awake to it rather than deaf.
- When `subḥāna` is on your tongue, its native job is correction, not decoration — it is doing the
  work of stripping away a wrong thought about God, not adding a compliment.
- 76:26 pairs glorifying with prostrating, not with speaking praise-content — the posture may be doing
  as much work as the words.
