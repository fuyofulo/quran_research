# `hjd` (هجد) — the one verse that names tahajjud

## Headline finding (data-grounded + interpretive, 100 words)

`hjd` occurs once in the Quran: `fa-tahajjad` at 17:79, form V, imperative. Everything the Quran
"says" about tahajjud is compressed into nine words. Word-by-word: the object is explicitly partitive
(`min al-layl`, not the whole night); `bihi`'s antecedent is genuinely ambiguous between "the Qurʾān"
and "the night" — translators split on it, and I lean Qurʾān (~60%) on redundancy and discourse
grounds, not certainty; `nāfilatan laka` may restrict the command to the Prophet; the outcome is
hedged with `ʿasā` (perhaps), mirroring fasting's `laʿalla`; `maqāman maḥmūdā` is a hapax the Quran
never explains. No quantity, timing, or frequency is specified anywhere.

## 1. The verse, word by word

**17:79** — وَمِنَ ٱلَّيۡلِ فَتَهَجَّدۡ بِهِۦ نَافِلَةࣰ لَّكَ عَسَىٰٓ أَن يَبۡعَثَكَ رَبُّكَ مَقَامࣰ ا مَّحۡمُودࣰ ا

| Saheeh | Pickthall | Khattab | Arberry |
|---|---|---|---|
| And from [part of] the night, pray with it as additional [worship] for you; it is expected that your Lord will resurrect you to a praised station. | And some part of the night awake for it, a largess for thee. It may be that thy Lord will raise thee to a praised estate. | And rise at ˹the last˺ part of the night, offering additional prayers, so your Lord may raise you to a station of praise | And as for the night, keep vigil a part of it, as a work of supererogation for thee; it may be that thy Lord will raise thee up to a laudable station. |

**`mina al-layl`** — partitive `min`, "from/of the night," not the accusative-of-duration `al-layla`
that 73:2 uses (`qumi al-layla illā qalīlā`, "stand THE night, except a little" — object, whole night
minus a remainder). 17:79 never puts the night in the accusative as a quantity to be spent; it treats
it as a source to draw a portion *from*. Even before `tahajjad` is defined, its container is already
built as a fraction, not a duration. **Data-grounded**: this is a real grammatical distinction between
the two most load-bearing night-vigil verses in the corpus, not a nuance imported from outside.

**`bihi`** — "with/by it." Its antecedent is not stated and the two live candidates are `al-layl`
(the immediately preceding noun, same ayah) and `qurʾāna al-fajri` (17:78, repeated twice for
emphasis in the sentence right before this one: `wa-qurʾāna al-fajri inna qurʾāna al-fajri kāna
mashhūdā`). **Confidence: moderate, leaning Qurʾān (~60%), not resolved.** Two arguments for Qurʾān:
(1) redundancy — the night is already supplied as the partitive frame by `mina al-layl`; a second
pronoun glossing "it" as "the night" again is a strange doubling, whereas `tahajjad bihi` as
instrumental ("keep vigil BY MEANS OF it," i.e., by reciting) gives the pronoun independent work; (2)
discourse architecture — 17:78 closes on a doubled, emphatic mention of `qurʾān al-fajr` immediately
before 79 opens, and 17:82 closes the whole unit by naming the Qurʾān again as `shifāʾ wa-raḥma`. The
passage brackets itself around the Qurʾān. Argument for `al-layl`: it is the literally nearest noun,
matching gender and number, and this is the grammatical default absent a strong reason to reach
further back — Arberry's "keep vigil **a part of it**" reads `bihi` this way, folding it back into the
partitive night-phrase rather than treating it as an instrument. The translator split (saheeh/pickthall
render an instrumental "with/for it" that is compatible with either noun; Arberry explicitly resolves
to "night") is itself data: **the ambiguity is real, not an artifact of my reading.** If Qurʾān is
right, tahajjud is not generic night-prayer — it is staying up *with the recitation specifically*.

**`nāfilatan laka`** — root `nfl`, 4 occurrences total, 2 lemmas (`>anfāl`, "spoils," 8:1 — unrelated
sense; `nāfila`, "an addition/extra," 17:79 and 21:72, where it describes Jacob as a "bonus" grandson
beyond what was asked). Both `nāfila` occurrences mark something given *beyond* what was owed or
requested. Here: tahajjud is framed as *surplus* worship, not baseline obligation. The `laka` ("for
you," second-person singular) is grammatically addressed to the Prophet, consistent with the whole of
17:73–80 which is second-person-singular throughout. **Whether `laka` narrows the nāfila-status (and
by extension the command) to Muhammad specifically, or is simply the ordinary singular address the
whole surah uses for him without restricting the practice, the Quran's own text does not disambiguate.**
This is a genuine interpretive fork — I report it, I do not resolve it.

**`ʿasā an yabʿathaka rabbuka`** — "perhaps your Lord will raise/resurrect you." `ʿasā` is a
hope/expectation particle, grammatically weaker than a promise. **This directly parallels the fasting
corpus's finding at 2:183, which conditions the reward of fasting on `laʿallakum tattaqūn` ("so that
you might attain taqwā") — also a hope-particle, not a guarantee.** Two of the Quran's signature
personal disciplines — the fast and the night-vigil — are each anchored to their stated outcome by a
"perhaps," not a "will." That is a shared grammatical fact across both practices, not a coincidence
worth glossing over.

**`maqāman maḥmūdā`** — root `Hmd` (63 occurrences, 61 ayahs, 40 surahs — one of the Quran's most
frequent roots, mostly `al-ḥamd` and `ḥamīd` as divine praise). Within that huge root, the passive
participle `maḥmūd` ("praised, praiseworthy") occurs exactly once, here. **The Quran names the
destination and gives it no content.** No description of what the "praised station" consists of, who
praises the one who attains it, or when. This sits inside a root otherwise saturated with praise-of-God
language, which makes the silence pointed: the Quran reaches for `Hmd` sixty-two other times to talk
about praising Allah, and the one time it uses the root's passive form for a human destination, it
says nothing more about it.

## 2. `hjd`: a root that reportedly names its own opposite

Within the Quran, `hjd` supplies exactly one data point: the form-V imperative `tahajjad`, unanimously
rendered by all four translations as a waking/rising sense (pray / awake / offering prayers / keep
vigil) — never as "sleep." **On Quranic evidence alone, tahajjud is unambiguously the wakeful act.**
The claim that the root's *base* sense in Arabic covers both sleeping and shedding sleep (i.e., an
addad word, one carrying contradictory senses) is a fact from Arabic lexicography (e.g., Lisān
al-ʿArab), not something derivable from a single Quranic occurrence — I flag it here explicitly as
**outside the Quranic evidence base.** What the Quran does confirm is the direction of the form-V
derivation: away from sleep, toward vigil. That much is internal.

## 3. Hapax density in 17:78–79 — tested, not confirmed as exceptional

17:78–79 carries three hapaxes from the night-vocabulary corpus: `dulūk` (17:78), `tahajjad` and
`maḥmūd` (17:79) — 3 in 2 verses, against a corpus-wide rate of 54 hapaxes across ~184 verses (≈0.29
per verse). That is elevated. But it is **not unique**: the same dataset shows 33:35 (the "muslimīn
wa-muslimāt..." list verse) packing **3 hapaxes into a single verse** (`ṣāʾimīn`, `ṣāʾimāt`, `dhākirāt`
— denser than 17:78–79's spread), and 73:6 matching 17:79's own rate with 2 hapaxes in one verse
(`nāshiʾa`, `waṭʾ`). **Verdict: 17:78–79 is on the high end of hapax density but not an outlier in this
corpus — comparable clustering happens at least twice more (33:35, 73:6).** The honest reading is that
the Quran reaches for rare vocabulary when naming precise, technical, or enumerated things generally
(a list of virtues, a physiological description, a named practice, a sun-position term), and 17:78–79
is one instance of that pattern, not evidence of something singular happening to tahajjud specifically.

## 4. The passage as a unit — 17:76–82

76–77 close the preceding narrative (the plot to expel the Prophet, framed as the fate of all
messengers). 78 opens a prayer-timetable: `duluk al-shams` (sun's decline, hapax) through `ghasaq
al-layl` (night's darkening) to `qurʾān al-fajr` (dawn recitation) — the ordinary, obligatory prayer
schedule, closed with `inna qurʾāna al-fajri kāna mashhūdā`, "the dawn recitation is witnessed."
79 adds the night vigil explicitly as `nāfila` — surplus, beyond 78's timetable. 80 is a dua for
sound entry/exit and supporting authority. 81 declares truth arrived, falsehood vanished. 82 states the
Qurʾān is `shifāʾun wa-raḥmatun li-l-muʾminīn` — healing and mercy for believers.

**If `bihi` in 79 points at the Qurʾān**, the unit coheres tightly: obligatory prayer bookended by
Qurʾān recitation (78) → an *extra* vigil done by means of that same Qurʾān (79) → dua for right
passage (80) → the arrival of truth over falsehood (81) → the Qurʾān named explicitly as healing and
mercy (82). The whole six-verse run would then be organized around one object — the Qurʾān — moving
from timetable, to surplus vigil with it, to its effect (truth displacing falsehood, healing, mercy).
That is a strong, legible reading, but it depends on the `bihi` resolution I have already flagged as
~60% confidence, not settled. **Interpretive**, built on a **data-grounded** ambiguity.

## 5. Negative space — verified, not assumed

Checked directly against the versepack and 73:20, the sole quantified night-vigil passage in the
corpus:

- **Rakʿāt count**: not specified anywhere for tahajjud. Zero.
- **Which part of the night**: 17:79 says only `min al-layl` — "some of the night," no further
  specification (not "the last third," not "before dawn"; that is a post-Quranic gloss some
  translations import, e.g. Khattab's "˹the last˺ part," bracketed as an addition).
- **How often**: not specified. No daily/weekly frequency word appears in 17:79.
- **Obligatory for anyone besides the addressee**: 17:79's imperative is second-person singular,
  addressed in context to the Prophet. **73:20 — the only place the Quran quantifies a night vigil at
  all (two-thirds, half, a third of the night) — uses `taqūmu` (root `qwm`), not `hjd`, is addressed
  to the Prophet and "a group of those with you," and then explicitly relaxes the requirement**:
  "He knew you would not be able to enumerate it (`lan tuḥṣūhu`), so He has turned to you [in ease] —
  recite what is manageable of the Qurʾān." The one place the Quran comes closest to a number, it
  immediately dissolves that number into "whatever is easy." **Flatly stated: the Quran specifies no
  rakʿāt, no fixed portion of the night, no frequency, and no explicit universal obligation for
  tahajjud.** What quantification exists (73:20) belongs to a different verb, a different addressee
  set, and cancels its own precision in the same breath.

## For the night

- The only word the Quran gives this practice, `tahajjad`, comes with no rulebook attached — no count,
  no clock, no minimum. Whatever structure a person now uses (a number of rakʿāt, a specific hour) is
  built on top of the text, not read out of it.
- Take `min al-layl` literally: a portion, not the whole night and not a specific fraction. 73:20's
  own arc — from two-thirds/half/third down to "whatever is easy" — is the Quran's own move away from
  quantification, not toward it.
- If the recitation reading of `bihi` is right, the content of the vigil matters more than its length:
  time spent with the Qurʾān itself, not a duration to be logged.
- The hoped-for outcome (`ʿasā`, "perhaps") is not owed. That is the same grammar the Quran uses for
  fasting's taqwā — worth carrying into the fasting comparison, not treating as tahajjud's peculiarity.
