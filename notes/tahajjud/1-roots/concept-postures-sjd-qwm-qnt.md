# Posture vocabulary: `qwm` (stand), `sjd` (prostrate), `qnt` (stand devoutly), `jnb` (sides), `DjE` (beds), `Slw` (prayer)

## Headline finding (100 words)

`qwm` (660×) is 58% "a people" (`qawm`, 383) and 11% Resurrection (`qiyāma`, 70) — the literal
bodily-standing sense (`qāma`, `qā'im`, `maqām`) is only **69 occurrences (10.5%)**. `aqāma` (54) is
separate again: 74% of its occurrences are the fixed formula "establish prayer," never physical
standing. Night-standing and prostration are a fixed couplet (5+ verses), never named alone in a
vigil context. 39:9 stacks four dispositions then pivots to knowledge. 32:16's "sides withdraw from
beds" is an image of leaving sleep, not of liturgy. The Quran never once calls the night vigil ṣalāh.

**Real standing-sense total: 69 occurrences (`qāma` + `qā'im` + `qā'imah` + `maqām`) — 10.5% of 660.**
Add the 4 rarer `muqām`/`muqāmah` "station" nouns and it reaches 73; either way, roughly one in ten.

`>aqaAma` (54) is its own category, and the brief's suspicion holds exactly: of its 54 occurrences,
**40 (74%) are in the same ayah as `al-Salah`** — the "establish prayer" formula. The other 14 are
"establish" applied to the Torah and Injil (5:66, 5:68), one's face toward religion (7:29, 10:105,
30:30, 30:43), the balance/scale (55:9), a wall (18:77), testimony (65:2) — never a body. `aqāma` is
**causative institution, not posture**. Zero of its 54 occurrences describe someone physically rising
to their feet. Data-grounded: this is a clean semantic split, not overlap.

## 2. The night-standing couplet

Every catalogued night-standing verse pairs prostration and standing — neither appears alone in a
vigil context in this dataset:

- **39:9** — `sājidan wa-qā'imā` (prostrating and standing)
- **3:113** — `yatlūna āyāti-llāhi ānā'a al-layli wa-hum yasjudūn` (recite by night, prostrating) — paired
  with the preceding `ummatun qā'imah` (a standing/upright community) in the same verse
- **25:64** — `sujjadan wa-qiyāmā` (prostrating and standing)
- **3:191 / 4:103** (day-time dhikr, not night, but same triad) — `qiyāman wa-quʿūdan wa-ʿalā junūbihim`
  (standing, sitting, on their sides) — a *three*-posture list, the only place the Quran names a full
  bodily range for remembrance

**Interpretive**: the couplet reads less like a prayer-cycle instruction (rakʿāt) and more like a
merism — "standing and prostrating" stands for the whole of night-devotion the way "night and day"
stands for all time. No verse describes moving *between* the two in sequence; they're named as two
poles of the same activity, not steps 1 and 2. The 3:191/4:103 triad (standing/sitting/on-the-side)
suggests the Quran's real interest is "in whatever posture" — remembrance regardless of position —
not a specified physical form.

## 3. 39:9 — the panel

> أَمَّنۡ هُوَ قَٰنِتٌ ءَانَآءَ ٱلَّيۡلِ سَاجِدࣰ ا وَقَآئِمࣰ ا يَحۡذَرُ ٱلۡأٓخِرَةَ وَيَرۡجُواْ رَحۡمَةَ رَبِّهِۦۗ قُلۡ هَلۡ يَسۡتَوِي ٱلَّذِينَ يَعۡلَمُونَ وَٱلَّذِينَ لَا يَعۡلَمُونَ
> *Saheeh*: Is one who is devoutly obedient during periods of the night, prostrating and standing,
> fearing the Hereafter and hoping for the mercy of his Lord [like one who does not]? Say, "Are those
> who know equal to those who do not know?"

Four things stacked in one clause: `qānitun` (devoutly obedient — the disposition), `ānā'a al-layl`
(the hours of the night — the time), `sājidan wa-qā'iman` (prostrating and standing — the posture),
then `yaḥdharu al-ākhirata wa-yarjū raḥmata rabbih` (fearing the Hereafter, hoping for mercy — the
emotional stance). The verse builds from outer form (posture) to inner state (fear/hope), then
detonates into a question about knowledge that has nothing grammatically to do with prayer.

**The move** (interpretive): the verse doesn't end "such a person is rewarded" — it ends by asking
whether knowers and non-knowers are equal, then attributes remembrance (`yatadhakkaru`) to "people of
understanding" (`ulū al-albāb`). Night-standing is being redescribed as an act of *knowing* — the
vigil is evidence that its performer understands something (the reality of the Hereafter, the nearness
of mercy) that the non-vigilant does not. Posture is downstream of comprehension, not a substitute for
it. This is a data-grounded structural fact (the clause sequence is right there); the theological
weight assigned to it is interpretive.

## 4. The fear/hope pair — checked against `xwf`/`TmE`

39:9 uses `yaḥdharu … yarjū` (root `H*r` / `rjw`). 32:16 uses `khawfan wa-ṭamaʿan` (root `xwf` / `TmE`).
**These are different root-pairs for the same emotional structure** — the Quran does not have one fixed
vocabulary for fear-and-hope; it restates the pairing with different words in each portrait.

Checking `TmE` (12 occurrences total): it co-occurs with `xwf` in exactly **4 ayahs (33%)** — 7:56,
13:12, 30:24, 32:16 — always as the fixed collocation `khawfan wa-ṭamaʿan`. But three of the four
(7:56, 13:12, 30:24) are about **lightning and rain as a sign of Allah's power**, not worship: "He
shows you the lightning, [causing] fear and aspiration" (13:12, 30:24). Only **32:16** applies the
formula to a person. **The night-vigil borrows a weather-sign formula; it isn't the formula's home
turf.** This is the checkable claim resolved: the pair is not distinctively liturgical — the Quran
reuses one emotional structure (dread and hope, held together) across both nature-signs and
worship-portraits, but with two different vocabularies, and `xwf`/`TmE` is the *minority* application.

## 5. 32:16 — beds, not liturgy

> تَتَجَافَىٰ جُنُوبُهُمۡ عَنِ ٱلۡمَضَاجِعِ يَدۡعُونَ رَبَّهُمۡ خَوۡفࣰ ا وَطَمَعࣰ ا
> *Saheeh*: They arise from [their] beds; they supplicate their Lord in fear and aspiration.
> *Arberry*: Their sides shun their couches...

`tatajāfā` (root `jfw`, 1 occurrence in the whole Quran) and `maḍājiʿ` (root `DjE`, 3 occurrences) are
both hapax-adjacent. `DjE`'s three occurrences:

- **3:154** — `la-baraza alladhīna kutiba ʿalayhimu al-qatlu ilā maḍājiʿihim` — those fated to be killed
  would have gone out "to their maḍājiʿ" — here the word means **the place where one falls dead**, a
  fated resting place, not a bed for sleep.
- **4:34** — `wa-uhjurūhunna fī al-maḍājiʿ` — "forsake them in bed[s]" — a step in marital discipline;
  the bed as the site of withheld intimacy.
- **32:16** — the bed one voluntarily leaves for the sake of prayer.

Three occurrences, three registers: death, estrangement, worship — but in all three, `maḍājiʿ` is
where something is *interrupted or withheld*, never simply "where one sleeps peacefully." 32:16's verb
`tatajāfā` sharpens this: it is not "they got up" (a neutral act, which would use `qwm`) but "their
sides recoiled/shrank away" — an image of the body pulling back from comfort. **Data-grounded**: no
liturgical vocabulary appears in 32:16 at all — no `Slw`, no `sjd`, no `rkE`. The verse describes a
body leaving a bed and then calling on God; the *prayer performed after* is not described. The image
is compositionally about departure from rest, not about the devotional act that follows it.

`jnb`'s "physical side of the body" sense (`janb`, 8 occurrences: 3:191, 4:36, 4:103, 9:35, 10:12,
22:36, 32:16, 39:56) elsewhere describes a man in distress calling out "lying on his side" (10:12) —
the *opposite* posture to 32:16's active withdrawal. The vigil is framed as the inverse of collapse.

## 6. `qnt` — the full set (13 occurrences, 3 lemmas)

`qānit`/`qānitāt` (11) and `yaqnut` (2, verbal). Two-thirds of the semantic field is generic submission
to God ("all in the heavens and earth are `qānitūn`," 2:116, 30:26) or to a spouse/community (4:34's
`qānitāt` describing wives; 3:43's command to Mary; 16:120's Abraham). Only **39:9** ties `qānit`
explicitly to night hours. **33:35**'s ten-quality list places `wa-l-qānitīna wa-l-qānitāt` third,
immediately before the fasting men and fasting women (`al-ṣā'imīna wa-l-ṣā'imāt`) — a direct textual
bridge between this investigation's vocabulary and the fasting corpus, noted here for the comparison
agent, not developed further.

## 7. Negative space

No verse specifies a number of rakʿāt, a sequence of postures, or a count of prostrations for the
night prayer. The only numeric content in the entire posture set is **temporal**, not postural: 73:20
measures night-standing in **fractions of the night** (two-thirds, half, a third) — duration, never
repetitions. The closest thing to a posture *sequence* is 3:43 (`uqnutī … wa-sjudī wa-rkaʿī` — Mary
told to be devout, prostrate, and bow), but it names no order beyond the imperative list, no count, and
isn't set at night. **This is a genuine absence, verified against the full `qwm`/`sjd`/`qnt`/`Slw`/`DjE`
vocabulary examined here** — rakʿah-counting is post-Quranic.

**Is the night vigil ever called `ṣalāh`?** Checked all three `Slw`+`lyl` ayahs:

- **11:114** — "establish prayer at the two ends of the day and at the approach of night" — the
  ordinary five daily prayers extending toward nightfall, not a vigil.
- **17:78–79** — v.78 commands `aqim al-ṣalāh` (the standard prayer times, sun's decline to
  nightfall, plus dawn); v.79 then separately commands `fa-tahajjad` ("and from the night, keep vigil")
  as `nāfilatan laka` — explicitly *additional*, and named with a different root entirely. The text
  itself distinguishes ṣalāh from tahajjud in adjacent verses.
- **73:20** — the night-standing is described with `taqūmu` (you stand), not `Salli`; `aqīmū al-ṣalāh`
  appears later in the same ayah as a separate, general clause alongside zakāh — read most naturally as
  the standard prayer obligation, not a redescription of the night-standing just mentioned.

**No. In every ayah where night and ṣalāh share a verse, ṣalāh denotes the ordinary prescribed
prayer, and the night-specific act is denoted by `qwm` (`taqūmu`, `qumi`) or the hapax `tahajjad`.**
This is a decisive, data-grounded negative: the Quran's own vocabulary keeps the night vigil
terminologically separate from ṣalāh, even while placing them in the same verses.

## For the night

- The command is durational, not choreographic: 73:20 measures the vigil in thirds and halves of the
  night, never in units or repetitions. There is no Quranic rakʿah count to fulfil.
- Standing and prostrating are named as a pair, not a sequence — don't treat them as fixed step 1/step
  2; the text treats them as two poles of one devotion.
- 32:16's image is leaving the bed, not performing a rite — the withdrawal from comfort is itself
  named as the act. The prayer that follows is real but the text's attention is on the departure.
- 39:9 makes the vigil a matter of *knowing*, not of merit alone — it ends on a question about
  understanding, not a promise of reward.
- Don't call it ṣalāh in your own head and expect Quranic warrant for a fixed liturgical form — the
  word the text reaches for is `qiyām al-layl`, standing in the night, deliberately kept apart from the
  five-times vocabulary.
