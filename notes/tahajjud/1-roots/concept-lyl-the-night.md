# `lyl` (ليل) — the night itself

## Headline finding (100 words)

Classifying all 81 `lyl` ayahs by register: **43% (35) are cosmological/sign, 18.5% (15) are
devotional, 16% (13) are narrative, and 22.2% (18) are oaths/similes/other.** Worship is a minority
register of the Quran's night — smaller even than the merism/oath category. The dominant register is
night as a physical fact: alternating with day, subjected, made *for rest* (`li-taskunū fīhi`), not
made for vigil. The Quran's own bridge from sign to worship is 3:190–191. Five distinct oath-verbs
(`yaghshā`, `sajā`, `yasrī`, `adbara`, `wasaq`) swear by the night. Every departure-command and every
revelatory descent is anchored to night vocabulary — verified, not merely two of them.

## 1. Stats (data-grounded)

92 occurrences, 2 lemmas (`layl` 84×, `laylah` 8×), 81 distinct ayahs, 49 surahs, POS: 89 noun / 3
temporal-adverb. Top co-occurring roots (from `data/concepts/lyl.json`): `nhr` (day) 42 ayahs (51.9%
of all `lyl` ayahs), `qwm` 22, `Ayy`/`Alh` 17 each, `jEl` 16, `rbb` 15, `$ms` 12, `smw`/`qmr` 10 each,
`sbH` 9, `sxr` 8, `sry` 5.

## 2. Classification by register — the main deliverable

**Rule:** each of the 81 ayahs gets exactly one primary bucket, assigned by its main clausal content,
not by incidental presence of a word.
- **(a) Cosmological/sign** — the alternation/subjection formulas, night-day-sun-moon statements, and
  the "made the night for X" purpose-clauses that sit inside the same creation-report register (even
  when X is rest) — because these describe cosmic order, not human practice or a plotted event.
- **(b) Devotional** — a human (or angel) is depicted standing, glorifying, prostrating, or praying
  *at* night, or a night-prayer instruction is issued.
- **(c) Narrative** — night marks a specific, dated event: a command to travel, an appointment kept, a
  revelation sent down, a punishment's duration.
- **(d) Other** — oaths (5), similes/eschatological images (`covered with pieces of the night`,
  10:27), and the merism `bi-l-layli wa-l-nahār` / `laylan aw nahāran` used as an idiom for "always" or
  "at any time" rather than as a cosmic-order claim (2:274, 10:24, 13:10, 21:42, 34:18, 34:33, 37:138,
  71:5), plus 2:187's legislative use of "the night" as fasting's temporal boundary.

Borderline calls, stated so the rule is falsifiable: **25:62** ("He made the night and day in
succession for whoever desires to remember or gratitude") is scored (a) because its main clause is a
creation-act, but its purpose-clause is devotional — it is a miniature 3:190–191. **6:60** (God "takes
your souls by night") is scored (a), not narrative, since it states a recurring law (sleep-as-death),
not a dated event.

**The headline number: only 15 of 81 night-verses (18.5%) depict or command night-worship.** Almost

co-occurrence figure from Phase 0 (42/81, 51.9%) and this classification point the same direction: the
Quran's characteristic night-statement is cosmological, and devotion is grafted onto that base, not the
other way around.

Devotional ayahs (15): 3:113, 11:114, 17:78, 17:79, 20:130, 21:20, 39:9, 41:38, 50:40, 51:17, 52:49,

69:7, 97:1–3.

## 3. The alternation formulas — verb choice is checkable

Four distinct verbs describe the night/day transition, and none of them are neutral:

- **`kawwara`** (39:5, `yukawwiru al-layla ʿalā al-nahār... wa-yukawwiru al-nahāra ʿalā al-layl`) — "to
  wind/coil," the verb for wrapping a turban. Night and day don't alternate like a switch; they are
  wound over each other like cloth around a head.
- **`walaja`** (22:61, 31:29, 35:13, 57:6, `yūliju al-layla fī al-nahār`) — "to make enter/penetrate."
  One is inserted into the other's body.
- **`ikhtilāf`** (2:164, 3:190, 10:6, 23:80, 45:5) — plain "difference/alternation," the abstract-noun
  register, consistently paired with `la-āyātin` (signs) and an epistemic tag (`li-qawmin
  yaʿqilūn/yatafakkarūn/li-ūlī al-albāb`).
- **`salakha`** (36:37, `naslakhu minhu al-nahār`) — "to skin/strip off," a butcher's verb. Day is
  peeled off the night like a hide, leaving darkness (`fa-idhā hum muẓlimūn`).

```
39:5   يُكَوِّرُ ٱلَّيۡلَ عَلَى ٱلنَّهَارِ وَيُكَوِّرُ ٱلنَّهَارَ عَلَى ٱلَّيۡلِ
       Saheeh: He wraps the night over the day and wraps the day over the night
       Khattab: He wraps the day over the night and wraps the night over the day
       Arberry: He wraps the day about the night
       Pickthall: He maketh night to succeed day

36:37  وَءَايَةࣱ لَّهُمُ ٱلَّيۡلُ نَسۡلَخُ مِنۡهُ ٱلنَّهَارَ فَإِذَا هُم مُّظۡلِمُونَ
       Saheeh: a sign for them is the night. We remove from it [the light of] day...
       Khattab: We strip from it daylight, leaving them in darkness
```

**Interpretive:** these are not one image repeated four ways. Coiling, insertion, and skinning are
three different physical actions performed on two things that resist merging; only `ikhtilāf` names
the *result* (difference) rather than the *act*. The verb variety builds a night that is actively
produced each cycle, not a default state day recedes into.

## 4. The bridge: 3:190–191

The single clearest place the Quran moves from night-as-sign to night-as-worship in one breath.

```
3:190  إِنَّ فِي خَلۡقِ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِ وَٱخۡتِلَٰفِ ٱلَّيۡلِ وَٱلنَّهَارِ لَأٓيَٰتࣲ لِّأُوْلِي ٱلۡأَلۡبَٰبِ
       Indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth and the alternation of the night and the
       day are signs for those of understanding.
3:191  ٱلَّذِينَ يَذۡكُرُونَ ٱللَّهَ قِيَٰمࣰا وَقُعُودࣰا وَعَلَىٰ جُنُوبِهِمۡ وَيَتَفَكَّرُونَ فِي خَلۡقِ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِ
       Those who remember Allah standing, sitting, and on their sides, and give thought to the
       creation of the heavens and the earth...
```

**Data-grounded:** 190 ends with `li-ūlī al-albāb` (people of understanding); 191 opens
`alladhīna yadhkurūna` — a relative clause that *defines* who those people are. There is no verb of
motion or new subject between them; 191 is syntactically an appositive gloss on 190's closing phrase.

**Interpretive:** the mechanism the Quran gives for turning a sign into worship is not command but
*noticing*. The alternation is observed (190), and the observing itself, sustained through every bodily
posture (standing/sitting/reclining — not "at night" specifically), becomes remembrance (191). Night is
not singled out as the worship-time here; postures are. This is the Quran's most explicit sign→worship
hinge, but it does not say "and therefore pray at night" — that inference is imported, not stated.

## 5. What night is FOR — and the unresolved tension

```
78:9   وَجَعَلۡنَا نَوۡمَكُمۡ سُبَاتࣰا           We made your sleep [a means for] rest
78:10  وَجَعَلۡنَا ٱلَّيۡلَ لِبَاسࣰا              We made the night as clothing
78:11  وَجَعَلۡنَا ٱلنَّهَارَ مَعَاشࣰا            We made the day for livelihood
```

Repeated near-verbatim at 25:47 (`jaʿala lakumu al-layla libāsan wa-l-nawma subātā`). Add 10:67, 27:86,
28:73, 40:61 — all `li-taskunū fīhi`, "that you may rest in it," and 6:96 (`jaʿala al-layla sakanā`).
That is **eight separate ayahs stating night's God-given purpose is rest**, none of them qualified,
none paired with a counter-clause about vigil.

**This is a real tension, not a rhetorical one, and it does not resolve inside the `lyl` data.** The
devotional ayahs (18.5% of the corpus) never reframe rest as the *wrong* use of night; they simply
describe a minority who take *part* of it (`min al-layl`, 17:79; `qalīlan min al-layl`, 51:17) for
prayer instead. The Quran's rest-vocabulary and its vigil-vocabulary occupy the same 81 ayahs without
ever addressing each other. Whoever prays tahajjud is not following an instruction to overturn night's
stated purpose — they are carving a portion out of a night the text elsewhere insists is *for* rest,
and the carving is always partial (a third, a half, two-thirds — 73:20 — never the whole night).

## 6. The five oaths — one verb each

| Ayah | Participle | Gloss | Rarity check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 92:1 | `idhā yaghshā` | when it covers/overwhelms | root `g$w`, 29 occurrences elsewhere — common |
| 93:2 | `idhā sajā` | when it grows still/settles | root `sjw`, **1 occurrence in the whole Quran** — this ayah only |
| 89:4 | `idhā yasr` | when it journeys/passes | root `sry` — same root as `asrā` (the Isrāʾ verb); only other `sry` form besides `>asoraY` |
| 74:33 | `idh adbar` | when it retreats | root `dbr`, 44 occurrences elsewhere — common |
| 84:17 | `wa-mā wasaq` | and what it gathers/enfolds | root `wsq`, **2 occurrences total** — the only other is `ittasaqa` (84:18, the moon "become full") |

**Data-grounded rarity:** `sajā` and `wasaqa` are as rare as the brief predicted — hapax and
near-hapax. **Interpretive but checkable:** the five verbs trace a single night's arc rather than
five images of the same thing — arrival (`yaghshā`, engulfing), settling (`sajā`, stillness),
motion (`yasrī`, night itself as a traveler — pairing this oath-verb with the Prophet's own
night-journey verb is unlikely to be accidental), retreat (`adbara`), and gathering
(`wasaqa`, of scattered things into one, paired with the moon reaching fullness next verse). God swears
by the night at every phase of its passage, not by "the night" as a static noun.

## 7. Night as the time God acts — verified, not asserted

Checked against the `lyl` occurrence list directly (not assumed):

- **Departures using the explicit noun `layl(an)`:** Lot (11:81, 15:65 — `bi-qiṭʿin min al-layl`), the
  Prophet's night journey (17:1 — `asrā bi-ʿabdihi laylan`), Moses and the Israelites (44:23 —
  `fa-asri bi-ʿibādī laylan`). Three for three.
- **Two commands that do NOT contain the word `layl`:** 20:77 and 26:52 (`asri bi-ʿibādī`, no
  `laylan`) — checked directly against `data/concepts/lyl.json`, and neither surah 20 nor 26 appears in
  its occurrence list except 20:130. The "night" in these two is carried entirely by the verb `asrā`
  itself (root `sry`, "to journey by night") — not a separate noun. So the claim "every departure
  happens at night" is true of the *content* of all five verses, but only three of the five say so with
  the word "night"; the other two encode it in the verb. This distinction matters and is easy to miss.
- **Descents, explicit `laylah`:** the Qur'an sent down `fī laylatin mubārakah` (44:3) and `fī laylati
  al-qadr` (97:1, three times in three ayahs). Moses' appointment (`mīʿād`, 2:51, 7:142) is 40 *nights*
  (`layālī`), not a departure but a duration — the Torah's reception is also night-denominated.

**Verified claim:** every discrete divine command to leave a place, and every named act of revelatory
descent, is anchored in night vocabulary — either the noun `layl(ah)` or the cognate verb `asrā/yasrī`.
No departure or descent in this corpus is dated to day.

## Translation divergence (data-grounded)

Two `lyl` ayahs sit in the corpus's top-15 divergence scores: **97:1** (0.775, translators split on
rendering `laylat al-qadr` as a name vs. a description) and **93:2** (0.745, `sajā` — "grows still,"
"is still," "darkens," "cometh still" — four different verb choices for a hapax with no comparandum).
The rest of the high-divergence list (73:1, 73:7, 26:219, 73:6) belongs to the devotional/`sbH` layer,
not to `lyl` itself — night's own vocabulary is comparatively easy to translate; it's what people *do*
at night that resists English.

## Negative space / for synthesis

- No `lyl` ayah ever states a *duty* to pray at night in unconditional terms; every devotional ayah is
  either descriptive (of a righteous minority) or explicitly partial/optional (`nāfilatan`, 17:79;
  "except a little," 73:2; the relaxation at 73:20).
- The rest-vocabulary (8 ayahs) and the vigil-vocabulary (15 ayahs) never cross-reference each other —
  no ayah says "though night is for rest, stand anyway." The tension is structural, not resolved.
- Cosmological register (43%) dwarfs devotional (18.5%) by more than 2:1. Any downstream file treating
  "the Quran's night" as primarily liturgical should cite this split.
- Flag for `research-tahajjud-vs-fasting.md`: 2:187 uses night as fasting's own boundary marker — the
  fast's law and the vigil's practice share the same temporal unit but pull in opposite directions (the
  night is when the fast *lifts*; it is also when the vigil is *added*).

## For the night

Night is a sign before it is a summons. The Quran describes it, wraps it, strips it, swears by it, and
tells you eight times it is for rest — before it ever tells anyone to stand in it. If you pray at
night, know what you are doing against the text's own grain: taking a portion, not the whole, of a
time explicitly built for sleep. The oaths' five verbs — arrival, stillness, motion, retreat, gathering
— are a better map of an actual night than any single image. Start reading it, per 3:191, in whatever
posture you're already in.
