# Dhikr, Duʿāʾ, Istighfār at night — `*kr`, `dEw`, `gfr`, `tlw`/`rtl`, and the pre-dawn sense of `sHr`

## Headline finding (100 words)

The three practices popular devotion most associates with night — remembrance, supplication,
forgiveness-seeking — are barely night-attached in the Quran's wording, and the shortfall is **not**
rescued by alternate time-vocabulary. `*kr` (292 occ.) shares a *layl*-ayah only 4/264 times; two of
those four use the night/day pair to argue remembrance is available in **either** period, not that it
belongs to night. `dEw` (212 occ.) shares one 2/182, and both hits are non-devotional (cosmology,
prophetic preaching). `gfr` gains real ground only through `al-asḥār` (3:17, 51:18, 73:20) — genuine,
but three verses out of 234. Where dhikr *does* get timed, the time-word is `bukratan wa-aṣīlā`
(morning-and-evening), not night. The dominant night-timed verb elsewhere in this corpus is `sabbiḥ`,
confirmed again here.

**Data-grounded** throughout unless marked interpretive.

## 1. The surprise — weaker than assumed, and not rescued by other time-words

Direct check of every ayah where `*kr`, `dEw`, or `gfr` shares a verse with `lyl`:

- **`*kr` + `lyl`: 11:114, 21:42, 25:62, 39:9.** Only two are substantively about remembrance-as-night-practice (11:114, 39:9). **25:62** is a counter-finding, not a confirmation: *"He made the night and day **in succession** [khilfatan] for whoever desires to remember [yadhakkara] or desires gratitude"* — the verse's point is that night and day **substitute for each other**, so remembrance is available regardless of which one a person has. **21:42** pairs night+day only as a cosmological merism ("who protects you by night and day") before pivoting to "yet they turn from the remembrance of their Lord" — night is not the object of the turning-away, God is.
- **`dEw` + `lyl`: 35:13, 71:5.** Neither is devotional supplication. 35:13 uses `tadEuwna` for **idol-calling** ("those you call besides Him") inside a cosmological night/day verse. 71:5 is Nūḥ's *daʿwah* — his prophetic **preaching campaign** to his people, "night and day," not prayer to God. **Zero genuine du'a-at-night hits via the word `layl`.**
- **`gfr` + `lyl`: 39:5, 73:20.** 39:5 is coincidental: the night/day-wrapping cosmological verse closes with the divine epithet *al-Ghaffār* — a name, not an act of forgiveness-seeking. **73:20 is genuine**: after describing the night-vigil, it commands *"wastaghfirū Allāh, inna Allāha ghafūrun raḥīm"* — the one clean case of istighfār actually embedded in night-prayer legislation.

**Now test the alternate time-words the assignment named.** `ānāʾ al-layl` and `zulafan mina al-layl`
both contain the literal word `layl` (they are genitive constructions — "the hours **of the night**",
"an approach **of the night**") — they are not escape routes around the word `layl`; every one of
their occurrences (3:113, 20:130, 39:9; 11:114) is already counted above. The only genuinely
*layl*-free formula is `bukratan wa-aṣīlā` / `bi-l-ghuduwwi wa-l-āṣāl` ("morning and evening"). Checking
where `*kr` pairs with it: **3:41** and **24:36** keep `*akara`/`yudhkara` **untimed** ("remember your
Lord much") while the *adjacent verb `sabbiḥ` takes the time-word* in the same ayah — the clock
attaches to glorification, not remembrance, even standing side by side. **76:25** and **7:205** do time
the dhikr-verb to this formula — but `bukratan wa-aṣīlā` names the day's two **bookends**, not the
night's interior. It is a different time-concept from `layl` altogether, and it isn't nocturnal.

**Verdict: the association is genuinely weaker than assumed, not merely relabeled.** Two of `*kr`'s
four "night" ayahs argue night is interchangeable with day; `dEw` has no genuine night-supplication
hookup by any test; only `gfr` gains real (if still small) ground, and it does so through `al-asḥār`,
not through any of the day-bracket formulas. Where a time-word is reliably attached to a verb in this
corpus, that verb is `sabbiḥ` (see sibling file), not `*kr`/`dEw`/`gfr`.

## 2. Al-Asḥār — the pre-dawn hour, and a correction to the brief

**Disambiguation result, by lemma, not root.** `sHr` (63 occ.) splits: `siHor` (28, sorcery-noun),
`sa`Hir` (22, sorcerer), `m~asoHuwr` (4, bewitched), `musaH~ariyn` (2), `saH~aAr` (1) — all magic-sense,
55 of 63. The pre-dawn sense belongs to exactly **one lemma: `saHar` (N), 3 occurrences: 3:17, 51:18,
54:34.** The brief's working assumption that `saHaru` (3 occ.) is a second pre-dawn lemma does **not**
hold up: `saHaru` is tagged **V** in the morphology (7:116 "they bewitched the people's eyes," 7:132
"whatever sign you bring to bewitch us," 23:89 "you are men bewitched") — sorcery, not time. **Only
3:17, 51:18, and 54:34 carry the asḥār sense**, and only two of those (3:17, 51:18) attach it to
forgiveness-seeking; 54:34 is the hour Lot's family was rescued, unrelated to istighfār.

**51:18** is a standalone declarative: *wa-bi-l-asḥāri hum yastaghfirūn* — "and in the hours before dawn
they would seek forgiveness." Nothing else is said about their night; this is the whole sentence.

**3:17** is a five-item list: *al-ṣābirīna wa-l-ṣādiqīna wa-l-qānitīna wa-l-munfiqīna
wa-l-mustaghfirīna bi-l-asḥār* — the patient, the truthful, the devoutly obedient, those who spend, and
the forgiveness-seekers *at dawn*. **Only the fifth and last item carries a time-marker; the other four
are given no temporal qualifier at all.** Interpretive reading: istighfār is placed last and singled out
for timing possibly because it is the item most naturally performed alone, at a specific private moment,
whereas patience, truthfulness, obedience, and spending are dispositions/acts diffused across a whole
life with no single occasion. Compare **33:35**'s ten-item, gender-paired list (Muslims, believers,
obedient, truthful, patient, humble, charitable, **the fasting men and women**, those who guard chastity,
"those who remember Allah often") — vocabulary overlaps heavily (ṣābirīn/ṣābirāt, ṣādiqīn/ṣādiqāt,
qānitīn/qānitāt appear in both lists) but **no item in 33:35 carries a time-word**, including the fasting
pair, and the list's *outcome*, not one of its items, is *maghfiratan* ("forgiveness") as a reward at the
close — a structurally different placement of the same root (`gfr`) from 3:17, where forgiveness-*seeking*
is itself a listed act. (The fasting-vocabulary overlap is left for the fasting-comparison agent.)

## 3. Istighfār generally — is pre-dawn timing distinctive, or incidental?

**12:98** — Yaʿqūb to his sons: *"sawfa astaghfiru lakum rabbī, innahu huwa al-ghafūru al-raḥīm"* — "I
will [in future] ask forgiveness for you." No time-word anywhere in the verse; the marker is **deferral**
(*sawfa*, "later"), not a clock-hour.

**71:10–12** — Nūḥ to his people: *"istaghfirū rabbakum innahu kāna ghaffārā. Yursili al-samāʾa
ʿalaykum midrārā, wa-yumdidkum bi-amwālin wa-banīn, wa-yajʿal lakum jannātin wa-yajʿal lakum anhārā"* —
"ask forgiveness of your Lord... He will send abundant rain, increase your wealth and children, and
grant you gardens and rivers." Istighfār here is attached to **consequences** (rain, wealth, offspring,
gardens), not to a time of day.

**Conclusion: pre-dawn timing is not the Quran's characteristic way of framing istighfār.** Of the
handful of verses that discuss forgiveness-seeking as a described act (not the divine attribute
`ghafūr`/`ghaffār`, which dominates the root's 234 occurrences as a name, not a timed practice),
timing appears only twice (3:17, 51:18); consequence-framing (rain/wealth/gardens, or a future promise)
is the more common shape. The pre-dawn hour is a real but minority motif, not the default.

## 4. 2:186 — the clearest "God answers" statement sits in the fast, not the night

**Verified.** *"wa-idhā saʾalaka ʿibādī ʿannī fa-innī qarīb, ujību daʿwata al-dāʿi idhā daʿāni,
fal-yastajībū lī wal-yuʾminū bī laʿallahum yarshudūn"* — "When My servants ask you about Me, I am near.
I answer the call of the caller when he calls Me..." **2:186 sits inside the fasting legislation
(2:183–187), between the fasting rules of 2:185 and 2:187 — not in any night-vigil passage.**

Checked every night-verse in `command-to-glorify`, `dua-and-nearness`, `istighfar-predawn`, and
`dhikr-general` for a comparable claim of *answered* prayer: **none exists.** The night material
commands glorification, standing, and forgiveness-seeking, and promises reward in the Hereafter or (for
Nūḥ's people) worldly increase — but it never states, as 2:186 and the untimed **40:60** ("call upon Me;
I will respond to you") do, that the call itself will be *answered*. The Quran's strongest statement on
answered prayer is doubly displaced from night: once into the fast, once into an untimed general
address (40:60). **Negative finding, stated flatly:** no night-verse makes this claim.

## 5. Dhikr the thing vs. dhikr the act — quantified

The noun `*ikor` (76 occ., the root's largest single lemma after the verb `*akara`) splits between
**"the Reminder"** (revelation/scripture: Qurʾān, Torah, or "a message from your Lord") and
**"remembrance"** (the human activity of recalling/mentioning God or a person). Classifying all 76 by
phrase-level reading (interpretive, but textually anchored — e.g. `anzalnā al-dhikr` [15:9], `ahl
al-dhikr` [16:43, 21:7], `hādhā dhikrun mubārak` [21:50], `māhuwa illā dhikrun li-l-ʿālamīn` [38:87,
68:52, 81:27] = **Reminder**; `bi-dhikri-llāh`, `li-dhikrī`, `ʿan dhikri rabbihim` = **activity**):

| Sense | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| The Reminder (revelation/scripture/message) | 41 | 54% |
| Remembering-as-activity | 31 | 41% |
| Mention/narrative-account/renown (other) | 4 | 5% |

**A slim majority of the noun `dhikr` denotes the revelation itself, not the act of remembering.** The
smaller noun lemmas reinforce this: `ta*okirap` ("tadhkirah," 9 occ.) is almost entirely the Qurʾān
described as a reminder (20:3, 69:12, 73:19, 74:49, 76:29, 80:11); `*ikoraY`` ("dhikrā," 23 occ.) is
predominantly the "lesson/reminder" drawn from a narrated sign or story, not a worship-verb. Combined
with `*ikor`, well over half of the root's noun-mass points at an **object** (the Book, a message, a
lesson) rather than a repeated devotional act. Consequence, interpretive: "remembrance at night" cannot
default to "reciting from memory as worship" without reckoning that the Quran's own dominant sense of
*dhikr*-the-noun is the revealed text — which folds this root back toward `tlw`/`rtl` (recitation) more
than toward a private nocturnal mantra.

## 6. 13:28 — the heart that settles, and what it is attached to

*alladhīna āmanū wa-taṭmaʾinnu qulūbuhum bi-dhikri-llāh, alā bi-dhikri-llāhi taṭmaʾinnu al-qulūb* —
"those who believe, whose hearts find rest in the remembrance of Allah — unquestionably, in the
remembrance of Allah do hearts find rest." Root `TmAn` (13 occ. total) describes settling/security. Of
its 13 occurrences, **6 attach explicitly to `qalb`/`qulūb`** (heart/hearts): 3:126, 5:113, 8:10, 13:28
(×2), 16:106. Checking what causes the settling in each:

- **3:126, 8:10** — identical clause (*"wa mā jaʿalahu Allāhu illā bushrā... wa li-taṭmaʾinna bihi
  qulūbukum"*): hearts settle **by military reinforcement** (angels sent at Badr/Uḥud) — an external,
  one-time divine intervention.
- **5:113** — the disciples' hearts settle **by eating from the heavenly table**, a confirming miracle.
- **16:106** — the heart is *"muṭmaʾinnun bi-l-īmān"* — settled **in faith** as a state, in a verse about
  compulsion to utter disbelief.
- **13:28 (×2)** — hearts settle **by dhikr Allāh** — the *only* one of the six where the settling is
  attached to a repeated act a person performs, not to an external event or a static state of belief.

**13:28 is unique in this small set: it is the sole verse where interior rest is the fruit of an
ongoing practice rather than a one-time sign or a description of faith itself.** This matches the
fasting investigation's finding that no interior state is ever attached to fasting — here one is
attached to dhikr, though (per §5) "dhikr" is a term that itself splits between an act and an object.

## Rabbit holes for synthesis

- Why does `sabbiḥ` absorb the time-words that `dhikr` "should" carry (3:41, 24:36) — is glorification
  functioning as dhikr's practical, timed proxy?
- The `khilfatan` logic of 25:62 (night/day as substitutable occasions) vs. Muzzammil's insistence on
  night specifically — direct tension worth a synthesis paragraph.
- 94:4 `dhikraka` ("we raised your repute") is an outlier sense of the root not covered above — minor,
  flag only.

## For the night

Do not lean on "the Quran commands dhikr/duʿāʾ/istighfār at night" as a citation-backed claim — it
mostly doesn't say that. What it does say: seek forgiveness in the last hour before dawn if you seek it
at a specific hour at all (51:18, 3:17); know that the Quran's clearest promise of an answered call
(2:186, 40:60) carries no night-restriction, so a daytime duʿāʾ is not lesser; treat night and day as
substitutable occasions for remembrance per 25:62, not as a hierarchy; and if you want the state 13:28
describes — a settled heart — the text ties it to the *content* of dhikr (recollecting God), not to
performing it specifically after midnight.
