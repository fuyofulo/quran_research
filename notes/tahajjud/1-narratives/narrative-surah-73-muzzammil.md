# Sūrat al-Muzzammil (73) — the founding text of night-worship

## Headline finding (100 words)

Al-Muzzammil opens the Quran's night-worship legislation with the highest-divergence verse in the
entire corpus (73:1, 0.933) and closes it, eleven verses later, with the second-highest (73:7,
0.917) — the two hardest words to translate frame the whole instruction. The command is not "pray
at night"; it is *stand* and *recite in tartīl* (73:4), the same word the Quran uses for how it was
revealed to begin with (25:32). The stated reason is not purification but preparation: a heavy word
is coming (73:5), and night-speech is straighter (73:6). Then, at 73:20, the Quran relaxes its own
command — using the identical construction it later uses to relax the fast.

---

## 1. The wrapped one: 73:1 and its twin

**[73:1]**

| | |
|---|---|
| Arabic | يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلۡمُزَّمِّلُ |
| Saheeh | O you who wraps himself [in clothing] |
| Khattab | O you wrapped ˹in your clothes˺ |
| Arberry | O thou enwrapped in thy robes |
| Pickthall | O thou wrapped up in thy raiment! |

Four translators, four different verbs for wrapping — *wraps, wrapped, enwrapped, wrapped up* — and
none can decide whether the address is about a physical cloak, a state of concealment, or both.
Divergence score 0.933, the single highest in the entire 184-verse night corpus. The Arabic word is
an intensive active-participle form (*muzzammil*, from `zml`, "to wrap/bundle") — heavier and more
repetitive in sound than any English gloss manages. The disagreement is not about vocabulary the
translators don't know; it's about a form of address the language doesn't have a clean equivalent
for: someone caught mid-gesture, wrapped up, addressed as such.

**[74:1]** opens the very next sūrah the same way: يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلۡمُدَّثِّرُ, "O you wrapped in
your mantle" (*muddaththir*, from `dtr`, a near-synonym of `zml`). Same address form, same
grammatical shape, same posture — someone wrapped up, presumably against cold, fear, or the shock
of first revelation.

But the two addresses diverge instantly on what comes next:

- 73:2 — *qumi al-layla illā qalīlā* — "stand the night, except a little" → the instruction is to
  **stand and recite**, turned inward, toward the night.
- 74:2 — *qum fa-andhir* — "arise and warn" → the instruction is to **stand and speak outward**,
  toward the people.

Two sūrahs, both opening on a figure wrapped up, both issuing the same imperative verb *qum* — then
splitting into two directions: the private discipline that prepares a person, and the public act
that person is being prepared for. Read together they form a diptych: 73 is the training, 74 is the
deployment. A structural observation, not a stated claim of the text — but the shared address form
and opening verb make the pairing hard to treat as coincidence.

## 2. Stand the night — with an exception built in

**[73:2]** *qumi al-layla illā qalīlā* — "stand the night, except a little." The exception clause is
in the command from its first utterance — not a later concession. The night-standing is scoped from
the outset, and 73:20 will scope it further still.

## 3. The fractions, and the hinge

**[73:3-4]** give the portions: *niṣfahu* (half of it), *aw-nquṣ minhu qalīlan* (or subtract a
little), *aw zid ʿalayhi* (or add to it) — roughly a third to two-thirds of the night, the same
range 73:20 later reports the Prophet actually kept.

Then the hinge: **wa-rattili al-Qurʾāna tartīlā** — "and recite the Qurʾān in tartīl." Divergence
0.869 — Saheeh has "measured recitation," Khattab "properly, in a measured way," Arberry "chant...
very distinctly," Pickthall "chant... in measure." The translators agree it's about *how* to
recite but not on a single English word, because English doesn't have one. This is the real content
of the command — the time fractions (half, less, more) are scaffolding for the act that follows:
recitation done a specific, describable way.

**`rtl` occurs exactly 4 times in the whole Quran** — two lemmas, two occurrences each, and both
lemmas appear together in only two verses: **25:32** and **73:4**.

**[25:32]**

| | |
|---|---|
| Arabic | وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ لَوۡلَا نُزِّلَ عَلَيۡهِ ٱلۡقُرۡءَانُ جُمۡلَةࣰ وَٰحِدَةࣰۚ كَذَٰلِكَ لِنُثَبِّتَ بِهِۦ فُؤَادَكَۖ وَرَتَّلۡنَٰهُ تَرۡتِيلًا |
| Saheeh | ...And [We] recited it [i.e., the Qur'an] with distinct, gradual recitation |
| Khattab | ...We have revealed it in stages so that We may reassure your heart with it. And We revealed it at a deliberate pace |
| Arberry | ...We have divided it, for We wanted to strengthen your heart thereby, and We have chanted it very distinctly |
| Pickthall | ...We have arranged it in right order, that We may strengthen thy heart therewith; and We have imparted it unto thee by (gradual) revelation |

25:32 answers a complaint — why wasn't the Qurʾān sent down all at once? — and the answer is that
God Himself sent it down *rattalnāhu tartīlā*, "We recited/revealed it in tartīl": the exact
verb-plus-cognate-object construction of 73:4, applied to God's own act of revealing. **The same
word describes how the Qurʾān was sent down and how the Prophet is commanded to recite it back at
night.** Tartīl is not a recitation technique invented for worship; it is named after the process by
which the text came into being — measured, unhurried, distinct. To recite in tartīl at night is to
reproduce, in one's own voice, the pace at which it was first given. This is the strongest textual
finding in this file: it rests on all four occurrences of the root, not on a single verse.

## 4. A heavy word is coming

**[73:5]** *innā sanulqī ʿalayka qawlan thaqīlā* — "We will cast upon you a heavy word." Divergence
0.827. A reason follows the standing-instruction immediately: something weighty is on its way, and
the vigil is readiness for it. *Sanulqī* ("We will cast") is future tense — the reason precedes the
thing it justifies. The vigil is preparatory by the plain sequence of the text, not by later
interpretation.

## 5. Night-speech is straighter

**[73:6]**

| | |
|---|---|
| Arabic | إِنَّ نَاشِئَةَ ٱلَّيۡلِ هِيَ أَشَدُّ وَطۡـٔٗا وَأَقۡوَمُ قِيلًا |
| Saheeh | Indeed, the hours of the night are more effective for concurrence [of heart and tongue] and more suitable for words |
| Khattab | Indeed, worship in the night is more impactful and suitable for recitation |
| Arberry | surely the first part of the night is heavier in tread, more upright in speech |
| Pickthall | Lo! the vigil of the night is (a time) when impression is more keen and speech more certain |

Divergence 0.829 — third-highest in the corpus. Two hapaxes collide in one verse. *Nāshiʾah* is the
feminine active participle of `n$A` ("to bring into being") — the root is common (28 occurrences,
mostly "originate/create"), but this exact form, "that which newly arises," occurs only here,
describing the onset of the night. *Waṭʾ* (from `wTA`, "to tread") is likewise a unique noun-form of
a root that elsewhere means "footstep" or "correspondence"; only at 73:6 does it mean *impact,
weight of impression*. The translators split the verse into two claims and can't agree which is
primary: (a) the night-rising has more *force/impression* (waṭʾan), (b) speech is more
*upright/certain* (qīlā) then. Both halves matter and are not the same claim — one is about what
the vigil impresses on the person, the other about the condition of speech during it. Read against
73:5, the sequence is continuous: a heavy word is coming (5) → impression lands hardest and speech
is straightest at night (6) → so stand at night (2-4).

## 6. The day is full

**[73:7]**

| | |
|---|---|
| Arabic | إِنَّ لَكَ فِي ٱلنَّهَارِ سَبۡحࣰا طَوِيلًا |
| Saheeh | Indeed, for you by day is prolonged occupation |
| Khattab | For during the day you are over-occupied ˹with worldly duties˺ |
| Arberry | surely in the day thou hast long business |
| Pickthall | Lo! thou hast by day a chain of business |

Divergence 0.917 — second-highest in the corpus, right behind 73:1. The noun *sabḥ* here (root
`sbH`, same root that elsewhere gives *tasbīḥ*, "glorify") occurs only twice in the whole Quran
(73:7 and 79:3) and here means "extended motion, occupation, a going-about." The justification for
using the night is not that night is holier; it is that **the day is already full**. Not "the night
is sacred," but "you have no room left in the day." The night is chosen by elimination as much as
by merit.

## 7. Name and cut yourself off

**[73:8]** *wa-dhkuri isma rabbika wa-tabattal ilayhi tabtīlā* — "remember the name of your Lord,
and devote yourself to Him wholly." `btl` — the root behind *tabattul* — **occurs exactly twice in
the entire Quran, both in this one verse** (verb and cognate object), nowhere else. The command to
cut oneself off in total devotion is given a word the Quran uses for nothing else, anywhere.

## 8. Preparation, not purification — testing the reading

The sequence 73:2-8 runs: stand (2) → recite in tartīl, the pace of revelation itself (4) → a heavy
word is coming (5) → night is when impression and speech are strongest (6) → the day has no room
left (7) → so name your Lord and cut yourself off wholly (8). At no point does the text say the
vigil purifies or atones — vocabulary that dominates the fasting passage (`kaffārah`, `taqwā`) is
absent here. What repeats instead is *capacity*: the vigil builds the condition (steadied heart,
straighter speech) needed to carry something heavy. This is the most direct sense
of 73:5-6 read together — the Quran's own stated rationale, not an imported theological frame,
echoing the outward act 74:2-3 puts to the same wrapped figure: *qum fa-andhir... wa-rabbaka
fa-kabbir* — rise and warn, and magnify your Lord. This is interpretive synthesis on data-grounded
sequencing; it should be tested further against the rest of the night corpus, but nothing in 73:1-8
pulls against it.

## 9. The relaxation — 73:20

**[73:20]** is the sūrah's longest verse and the only one in the Quran that explicitly walks back a
devotional obligation.

> إِنَّ رَبَّكَ يَعۡلَمُ أَنَّكَ تَقُومُ أَدۡنَىٰ مِن ثُلُثَيِ ٱلَّيۡلِ وَنِصۡفَهُۥ وَثُلُثَهُۥ
> وَطَآئِفَةࣱ مِّنَ ٱلَّذِينَ مَعَكَۚ وَٱللَّهُ يُقَدِّرُ ٱلَّيۡلَ وَٱلنَّهَارَۚ عَلِمَ أَن
> لَّن تُحۡصُوهُ فَتَابَ عَلَيۡكُمۡۖ فَٱقۡرَءُواْ مَا تَيَسَّرَ مِنَ ٱلۡقُرۡءَانِۚ عَلِمَ أَن
> سَيَكُونُ مِنكُم مَّرۡضَىٰ وَءَاخَرُونَ يَضۡرِبُونَ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ يَبۡتَغُونَ مِن فَضۡلِ ٱللَّهِ
> وَءَاخَرُونَ يُقَٰتِلُونَ فِي سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِۖ فَٱقۡرَءُواْ مَا تَيَسَّرَ مِنۡهُ...
> (Khattab) "...Allah knows that you ˹believers˺ are unable to endure this, and has turned to you
> in mercy. So recite ˹in prayer˺ whatever you can from the Quran. He knows that some of you will
> be sick, some will be travelling throughout the land seeking Allah's bounty, and some fighting in
> the cause of Allah. So recite whatever you can from it..."

Four moves in sequence: (1) the actual practice is reported — the Prophet and a group with him were
already standing "nearly two-thirds... or half... or a third" of the night, close to 73:3-4's range
but not fixed; (2) *wa-Allāhu yuqaddiru al-layla wa-l-nahār* — God, not any human timekeeper,
measures night and day, offered as the reason precise counting is hard; (3) *ʿalima an lan tuḥṣūhu
fa-tāba ʿalaykum* — "He knew you would not be able to count it, so He turned to you [in mercy]";
(4) the command changes from a scaled quota to *fa-qraʾū mā tayassara mina al-Qurʾān* — "recite what
is easy of the Qurʾān" — repeated twice.

### The tāba ʿalaykum parallel

**The exact construction *fa-tāba ʿalaykum* ("so He turned to you [in mercy]") occurs in only two
places tied to a relaxed obligation: 73:20 and [2:187]**, the verse easing the fasting restriction
on relations with one's wife on the night of the fast: *ʿalima Allāhu annakum kuntum takhtānūna
anfusakum fa-tāba ʿalaykum wa-ʿafā ʿankum* — "God knew you were betraying yourselves, so He turned
to you [in mercy] and pardoned you." Same shape both times: *ʿalima* [a human limitation] *fa-tāba
ʿalaykum* [so He turned to you] — in 73:20 the limitation is inability to count night hours, in
2:187 inability to keep a harsher self-restriction. **The Quran relaxes both night-standing and
fasting with the same verb, root (`twb`), and clause structure** — data-grounded: `twb`'s lemma
"tāba" occurs 63 times total, and "fa-tāba ʿalaykum" occurs at only these two worship-relaxation
locations. A fuller comparison is for a dedicated cross-cutting file.

### The exemptions match

73:20 gives three reasons some will not keep the full vigil: *marḍā* (sick), *yaḍribūna fī al-arḍ...
yabtaghūna min faḍl Allāh* (travelling, seeking God's bounty), and *yuqātilūna fī sabīl Allāh*
(fighting in God's way). The fasting relaxation at [2:184-185] gives exemptions for *marīḍan aw ʿalā
safar* — sick, or travelling — worded almost identically. **Illness and travel are the two
exemptions the fasting passage gives twice, and the first two of the three given here.** Fighting
has no fasting-side counterpart; it belongs to a text that, unlike most of Sūrah 73, names armed
struggle explicitly.

## 10. What is easy — the `ysr` root

*Mā tayassara* ("what is made easy") appears twice in 73:20, from root `ysr` (44 total occurrences,
9 lemmas). The scaled time quota is replaced by an unscaled, subjective standard: whatever is easy.
Same root as **[2:185]**'s *yurīdu Allāhu bikumu al-yusra wa-lā yurīdu bikumu al-ʿusr* — "God intends
ease for you, not hardship" — the verse licensing the fasting exemptions. **Both relaxations are
worded with the same root for "ease."** `ysr` is not reached for once; it is the Quran's standing
vocabulary for scaling back a devotional demand, deployed identically for fast and vigil.

## 11. The Meccan/Medinan seam — flagged, not settled

Sūrah 73 is classed Meccan; the versepack carries that tag through 73:20 as well. But the ayah
mentions *fighting in God's way* and *zakāt* — vocabulary elsewhere tied to a Medinan, post-Hijrah
social order with organized community and armed conflict, neither of which the Meccan period had.
Many traditional accounts place 73:20 as a later addition to an otherwise early sūrah. **This is a
claim about compositional history, not something the Uthmani text or this dataset settles** — the
versepack has no sub-sūrah revelation tagging, so it sits outside the strictly textual evidence base
used elsewhere here. Worth flagging: it would mean the relaxation was not simultaneous with the
command — the vigil ran, uneased, before the text itself scaled it back.

## For the night

- The command was never open-ended: "except a little" is in the instruction from 73:2, before any
  relaxation.
- Recite unhurried — *tartīl* is the same pace at which the Qurʾān itself came down (25:32/73:4), not
  a stylistic option.
- The reason given for the night is practical, not mystical: the day is already full (73:7) and
  impression and speech are steadiest at night (73:6).
- No fixed quota. 73:20 replaces the half/third/two-thirds scheme with "recite what is easy" — that
  replacement is itself the Quran's instruction, not a later softening.
- Sickness, travel, and struggle are named exemptions — the same categories the fast excuses, with
  the same "ease" vocabulary and mercy-formula.

For the night.
