# The two portraits — 51:15-19 and 32:15-17

## Headline finding (100 words)

These are the Quran's only two sustained *descriptions* of night-praying people — not commands. Both use third-person habitual grammar (`kānū`, `alladhīna`) to portray a type, not address a reader. Independently, they converge on an identical three-part sequence: leave sleep/bed, call on God, spend wealth on others. 32:16 and 51:17-19 are structurally the same list in the same order. 32:17 promises a reward and then withholds its content — a different silence than fasting's total silence on reward. Every actual night-worship *imperative* in the Quran (73:2, 17:79, 76:26, 50:40, 52:49, 20:130) is second-person masculine singular, addressed to the Prophet alone.

---

## 1. Adh-Dhāriyāt 51:15-19, in full

The muttaqīn (the God-conscious) are already in the reward — gardens and springs, receiving (`ākhidhīna`) what their Lord gave them. 51:16 gives the reason in one clause: `innahum kānū qabla dhālika muḥsinīn` — "indeed they were, before that, doers of good." Then three specifics, all still under `kānū`:

- **51:17** — `kānū qalīlan mina al-layli mā yahjaʿūn` — they used to sleep little of the night
- **51:18** — `wa-bi-l-asḥāri hum yastaghfirūn` — and in the pre-dawn hours they sought forgiveness
- **51:19** — `wa-fī amwālihim ḥaqqun li-l-sāʾili wa-l-maḥrūm` — and in their wealth was a right for the asker and the deprived

| Ref | Arabic | Saheeh |
|---|---|---|
| 51:17 | كَانُوا۟ قَلِيلًۭا مِّنَ ٱلَّيْلِ مَا يَهْجَعُونَ | They used to sleep but little of the night |
| 51:18 | وَبِٱلْأَسْحَارِ هُمْ يَسْتَغْفِرُونَ | And in the hours before dawn they would ask forgiveness |
| 51:19 | وَفِىٓ أَمْوَٰلِهِمْ حَقٌّ لِّلسَّآئِلِ وَٱلْمَحْرُومِ | And from their properties was [given] the right of the petitioner and the deprived |

**Three items: little sleep, pre-dawn forgiveness-seeking, a claim on wealth.** The sequence ends on *giving* — the same pattern the fasting investigation found (fasting's vocabulary clusters around feeding and the needy). Night-vigil and fasting both terminate outward, on someone else's need, not inward on the worshipper's own state.

**The grammar is the load-bearing fact.** `kānū` is past habitual — "they used to." This whole unit is `kānū...yahjaʿūn` / `hum yastaghfirūn` / (implicit `kāna`) — a report of settled, ongoing past character, delivered from the vantage point of people *already rewarded* in 51:15-16. **This is a description of who the muttaqīn already were, retold to explain why they are in the garden — not an instruction telling the reader what to do tonight.** The verb mood never shifts to imperative anywhere in the unit.

## 2. As-Sajdah 32:15-17, in full

32:15 describes those who truly believe in the signs: when reminded, they fall in prostration (`kharrū sujjadan`) and glorify with praise of their Lord (`sabbaḥū bi-ḥamdi rabbihim`), without arrogance. 32:16 gives the specifics — a relative clause still governed by `alladhīna` (those who) from 32:15, not a command:

**32:16** — `tatajāfā junūbuhum ʿani al-maḍājiʿi yadʿūna rabbahum khawfan wa-ṭamaʿan wa-mimmā razaqnāhum yunfiqūn` — their sides withdraw from the beds, they call on their Lord in fear and hope, and they spend from what We provided them

| Ref | Arabic | Saheeh |
|---|---|---|
| 32:16 | تَتَجَافَىٰ جُنُوبُهُمْ عَنِ ٱلْمَضَاجِعِ يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُمْ خَوْفًۭا وَطَمَعًۭا وَمِمَّا رَزَقْنَٰهُمْ يُنفِقُونَ | They arise from their beds; they supplicate their Lord in fear and aspiration, and from what We have provided them, they spend |
| 32:17 | فَلَا تَعْلَمُ نَفْسٌۭ مَّآ أُخْفِىَ لَهُم مِّن قُرَّةِ أَعْيُنٍۢ جَزَآءًۢ بِمَا كَانُوا۟ يَعْمَلُونَ | No soul knows what has been hidden for them of comfort of eyes, as reward for what they used to do |

### The three-part parallel — verified

Laying the two passages side by side, element by element:

| Element | 51:17-19 (Adh-Dhāriyāt) | 32:16 (As-Sajdah) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Leave sleep/rest | `kānū qalīlan mina al-layli mā yahjaʿūn` — little sleep | `tatajāfā junūbuhum ʿani al-maḍājiʿi` — sides withdraw from beds |
| 2. Call on God | `bi-l-asḥāri hum yastaghfirūn` — seek forgiveness at dawn | `yadʿūna rabbahum khawfan wa-ṭamaʿan` — call on their Lord in fear/hope |
| 3. Spend on others | `fī amwālihim ḥaqqun li-l-sāʾili wa-l-maḥrūm` — a right for the asker/deprived | `wa-mimmā razaqnāhum yunfiqūn` — spend from what We provided |

**This holds.** Two independent Meccan passages, different surahs, different vocabulary (`yahjaʿūn`/`yastaghfirūn`/`ḥaqq` vs. `tatajāfā`/`yadʿūna`/`yunfiqūn`), same three-part order: bodily withdrawal from sleep first, address to God second, material generosity third. Neither borrows the other's wording — this is structural convergence, not shared formula (no ayah-echo relationship between them in `data/structural/ayah-echoes.json`). That two unrelated portraits land on the identical sequence is the strongest data-grounded finding in this file. **Interpretive note:** the repetition suggests the Quran has one settled *shape* for what a night-devoted life looks like — night address to God is never described alone; it is always flanked by leaving sleep and giving money. Night-worship, in the Quran's own picture of it, is not a private, self-contained act.

## 3. 32:17 — the reward that names itself unnamed

32:17 is the reward verse, and it is a withholding. `fa-lā taʿlamu nafsun mā ukhfiya lahum min qurrati aʿyun` — "no soul knows what has been hidden for them of comfort of eyes." The Quran promises a reward for exactly this kind of person and then explicitly refuses to specify it — `ukhfiya`, "was hidden," passive, deliberate.

**Compare to fasting:** the fasting investigation found no reward promised for fasting at all — a total silence. Here there *is* a reward, stated as guaranteed (`jazāʾan`), but its content is declared unknowable in principle, not merely unstated. That is a stronger, more pointed silence than fasting's — fasting simply doesn't mention a reward; 32:17 mentions one and then locks the door on it. **Interpretive:** these are two different rhetorical moves, not the same move twice.

**`qurrata aʿyun`** (comfort/coolness of eyes) is data-traceable. The noun `qurrah` (root `qrr`) occurs exactly three times in the Quran, and all three are requests or descriptions of a good outcome, never night-specific outside 32:17:
- **32:17** — the hidden reward for the night-prostrators, examined above
- **25:74** — the believers' prayer: `rabbanā hab lanā min azwājinā wa-dhurriyyātinā qurrata aʿyun` — "grant us from our spouses and offspring comfort of eyes" (this ayah sits inside the `ʿibād al-raḥmān` portrait, 25:63-77, itself a night-standing passage — `wa-lladhīna yabītūna li-rabbihim sujjadan wa-qiyāmā`, 25:64)
- **28:9** — Pharaoh's wife, of the infant Moses: `qurratu ʿaynin lī wa-lak` — "a comfort of eyes for me and you"

A fourth, related occurrence uses the verb form rather than the noun: **19:26**, Maryam commanded `wa-qarrī ʿaynan` — "and be comforted" (literally "cool your eye") — after giving birth alone. The phrase's field is domestic and personal joy (a child, a spouse) in every other appearance; 32:17 is the only place it is attached to an eschatological reward and simultaneously hidden from view. The Quran uses its warmest, most human idiom for delight — and then declines to say what it looks like for the night-worshipper.

## 4. The emotional pair: fear and hope

32:16 pairs `khawfan wa-ṭamaʿan` (fear and hope/aspiration). The noun `ṭamaʿ` occurs exactly 4 times in the Quran, and in all 4 it co-occurs with `xwf` in the same ayah — **there is no occurrence of `ṭamaʿ` (the noun) anywhere in the Quran without `khawf` beside it.** The four: **7:56** (call on Him "in fear and hope," general exhortation to good conduct), **13:12** (lightning shown "in fear and hope," a cosmological sign), **30:24** (same lightning-sign formula), and **32:16** (the night-vigil portrait). 

**Only one of the four is night-specific.** The other three are about rain/lightning as portents or general supplication — the fear/hope pair is the Quran's stock idiom for a person's stance before uncertain divine action generally, not something coined for the night scene. **Interpretive:** night-prayer doesn't get a special emotional vocabulary; it borrows the same fear-hope pairing the Quran uses for weather-signs and general prayer. What makes 32:16 distinctive is not the emotion named but where it's placed — at 3am, having left a bed.

## 5. The imperative audit — the decisive grammatical finding

Every verb of address in 51:15-19 and 32:15-17 is third person, habitual or descriptive. Neither passage ever switches to command form. Checking this against the entire imperative inventory for night-worship in the Quran:

| Verse | Imperative | Form | Person/number | Addressee |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **73:2** | `qumi` (stand) | فعل أمر | 2nd masc. **singular** | the Prophet (`yā ayyuhā al-muzzammil`, 73:1) |
| **17:79** | `fa-tahajjad` | فعل أمر | 2nd masc. **singular** | the Prophet (`laka`, "for you," singular, same clause) |
| **76:26** | `fa-sjud...wa-sabbiḥhu` | فعل أمر | 2nd masc. **singular** | the Prophet (`ʿalayka`, 76:23, singular) |
| **50:40** | `fa-sabbiḥhu` | فعل أمر | 2nd masc. **singular** | the Prophet (`iṣbir ʿalā mā yaqūlūn`, 50:39, singular) |
| **52:49** | `fa-sabbiḥhu` | فعل أمر | 2nd masc. **singular** | the Prophet (`iṣbir li-ḥukmi rabbik`, 52:48, singular) |
| **20:130** | `fa-sabbiḥ` | فعل أمر | 2nd masc. **singular** | the Prophet (`iṣbir ʿalā mā yaqūlūn`, same verse, singular) |

**All six night-worship imperatives in the Quran are second-person masculine singular, and all six sit in passages addressing the Prophet by name or by singular pronoun in the surrounding verses.** There is no plural night-standing imperative anywhere in the corpus. The one nearby verse with plural command verbs, **73:20** (`fa-qraʾū mā tayassara`, `wa-aqīmū al-ṣalāh`, `wa-ātū al-zakāh`, `wa-aqriḍū Allāha`, `wa-staghfirū Allāh` — all 2nd masc. plural), is the *relaxation* verse: it acknowledges "a group of those with you" also stand at night, but its plural commands substitute general obligations — recite what's easy, pray, give zakat, lend well, seek forgiveness — for the specific night-standing duty. The word `qum` is never generalized to a plural form anywhere in the Quran.

**Both portrait passages (51:17, 32:16) are third-person `kānū`/relative-clause description; both sets of second-person night imperatives are singular and Prophet-directed. The Quran's two pictures of night-praying people are portraits of a type it praises — not a command it issues to the reader.** This is the single most consequential grammatical finding in this file.

## 6. The frames differ, the practice doesn't

51's portrait sits inside an eschatological scene — muttaqīn already in the garden, and the night-behavior is offered as *retrospective explanation* for why they're there (`innahum kānū...`). The frame is reward, looking backward. 32's portrait sits inside a revelation-response scene — those who truly believe `idhā dhukkirū bihā` (when reminded of the signs), and the night-behavior is *evidence of the belief itself*, not a reason for future reward stated separately (32:17's reward clause is a coda, `jazāʾan bimā kānū yaʿmalūn`, added after the description, not framing it). One frame is retrospective-reward, the other is present-evidentiary. **Interpretive:** despite the different frames, the practice described is identical in structure (§2's three-part parallel) — suggesting the Quran treats this behavior as a stable marker of a certain kind of person regardless of which theological lens is applied to it (garden-reward or sign-response). The frame changes; the picture of the person does not.

## For the night

- These verses were never commands — nobody reading 51:17 or 32:16 is being told to do anything tonight. They describe a kind of person.
- If you take them as aspiration rather than obligation, the actual pattern to aim at is three things in order: interrupt sleep, address God (in fear and hope, not certainty), then act generously with money — not just the prayer alone.
- The reward is deliberately not described (32:17) — don't expect the text to tell you what it feels like or looks like. That withholding is stated as a feature, not an omission.
- The only real commands to stand at night in the Quran were given to one person, in the singular, and were later partially relaxed for everyone else (73:20) into "recite what is easy" — a lower bar than `qum`.
- Fear and hope together, not certainty either way, is the Quran's own emotional register for standing before God at night — and it's the same register it uses for lightning and rain, not a special night-only feeling.
