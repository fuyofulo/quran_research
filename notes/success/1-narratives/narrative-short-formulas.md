# Short Success Formulas + the Four-Classes Verse

## Headline Finding

Five passages — 2:1-5, 87:14-15, 91:9-10, 103:1-3, 4:69 — together constitute the Quran's complete theory of success in miniature. The core pattern: success (flH) is not positive achievement but escape from a default human condition of loss (khusr, 103:2). The irreducible minimum across all five is PURIFICATION + PRAYER; everything else (communal exhortation, obedience, prior-revelation acceptance) fills in context. The four-classes verse [4:69] maps the endpoint: obedience places you "with" (maʿa) the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs, and the righteous — company, not rank.

---

## 1. 2:1-5 — The Foundational Equation

**Panel 1:**

> ذَٰلِكَ ٱلۡكِتَٰبُ لَا رَيۡبَۛ فِيهِۛ هُدٗى لِّلۡمُتَّقِينَ ٱلَّذِينَ يُؤۡمِنُونَ بِٱلۡغَيۡبِ وَيُقِيمُونَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَمِمَّا رَزَقۡنَٰهُمۡ يُنفِقُونَ وَٱلَّذِينَ يُؤۡمِنُونَ بِمَآ أُنزِلَ إِلَيۡكَ وَمَآ أُنزِلَ مِن قَبۡلِكَ وَبِٱلۡأٓخِرَةِ هُمۡ يُوقِنُونَ أُوْلَٰٓئِكَ عَلَىٰ هُدٗى مِّن رَّبِّهِمۡۖ وَأُوْلَٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلۡمُفۡلِحُونَ
>
> *"This is the Book, no doubt in it — a guidance for the muttaqīn: those who believe in the unseen, establish prayer, and spend from what We have provided them; and those who believe in what was revealed to you and before you, and of the Hereafter they are certain. Those are upon guidance from their Lord, and those — they are the muflihūn."* [2:2-5]

Three logical moves: (a) the Book is guidance FOR the muttaqīn; (b) five defining traits enumerate who the muttaqīn are; (c) those same people ARE the muflihūn.

The equation muttaqīn = muflihūn is placed at the Quran's opening — the second surah, fourth verse. It is definitional, not causal: the verse does not say "if you are muttaqīn, you will succeed." It says those people ARE the successful. Success is a present classification, not a future reward. The five traits span five domains: epistemic (belief in unseen), vertical-ritual (prayer), horizontal-economic (spending), historical-breadth (prior revelation), and existential (Hereafter certainty). No single domain is sufficient alone.

This is the Quran's FIRST believer-list. Every later list — 23:1-9, 70:22-35, 87:14-15 — is a variation on this template.

## 2. 87:14-15 — The Three-Step Formula

> قَدۡ أَفۡلَحَ مَن تَزَكَّىٰ وَذَكَرَ ٱسۡمَ رَبِّهِۦ فَصَلَّىٰ
>
> *"He has certainly succeeded who purifies himself, and mentions the name of his Lord, and prays."* [87:14-15]

Three actions in strict sequence: **tazakkā → dhakara isma rabbihi → ṣallā**. The conjunction fā (then-consequently) between dhikr and prayer indicates sequential-causal order, not mere listing: purification enables remembrance, remembrance enables prayer. You cannot offer prayer as a spiritual act if the self is unpurified; you cannot remember God's name authentically without an inner orientation that prayer then enacts.

Surah 87 is early Meccan, opened by an oath-formula ("Glorify the name of your Lord, the Most High"), and its final verses [87:18-19] invoke the scrolls of Abraham and Moses — locating this three-step formula in a trans-Quranic revelatory tradition predating Muhammad. The formula is ancient; the Quran is its latest instance.

Note the contrast at [87:16-17]: "but you prefer the worldly life, while the Hereafter is better and more enduring." The three-step formula is set against the worldly-preference that blocks purification. The dunya preference is identified as the proximate cause of failure — it prevents tazakkā from happening.

## 3. 91:9-10 — The Soul-Purification Binary and Khāba

**Panel 2:**

> قَدۡ أَفۡلَحَ مَن زَكَّىٰهَا وَقَدۡ خَابَ مَن دَسَّىٰهَا
>
> *"He has certainly succeeded who purifies it [the soul]. And he has failed who corrupts it."* [91:9-10]

Two differences from 87:14: (1) the object of purification is explicit — hā (it) referring back to the nafs (soul) named at [91:7]; (2) the failure pole uses KHĀBA (root xyb), not KHASIRA (root xsr).

The preceding 7 oaths are cosmic: sun, moon, day, night, sky, earth, soul. The soul is the seventh and final oath-object, placed after cosmic phenomena as the culminating sign. The binary verdict at 91:9-10 is therefore a COSMIC-SCALE JUDGMENT about the inner self, not a practical advice list.

**The khāba / khasira distinction is theologically significant:**

- **Khasira (xsr)** carries a commercial register: loss of what you had, like a failed transaction. Al-khāsirūn are those who lose themselves as a commodity [khasirū anfusahum, 6:12, 7:9].
- **Khāba (xyb)** means frustrated potential — hopes collapsed, something good that was possible did not happen. "To be disappointed, to go unrealized." The soul that is dassāhā (buried, smothered) does not merely lose — it FAILS TO BECOME what it could have been.

The Thamūd example immediately following [91:11-15] illustrates what khāba looks like in history: a people who had a messenger, a clear sign (the she-camel), and the capacity to respond — and who corrupted that potential by denying and killing. Their souls were dassāhā; their failure was khāba. The punishment came not as arbitrary wrath but as the consequence of frustrated potential. Dassāhā (root dss) means to bury, to smother — the soul is not destroyed, it is buried alive under the choices that prevented its emergence.

## 4. 103 — The Compressed Cosmic-Default-of-Loss

**Panel 3:**

> وَٱلۡعَصۡرِ إِنَّ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ لَفِي خُسۡرٍ إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ وَعَمِلُواْ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ وَتَوَاصَوۡاْ بِٱلۡحَقِّ وَتَوَاصَوۡاْ بِٱلصَّبۡرِ
>
> *"By time — indeed, mankind is in loss — except those who believe and do righteous deeds and mutually exhort to truth and mutually exhort to patience."* [103:1-3]

Three ayahs; the entire theological package. Structural notes:

1. The oath is BY TIME ITSELF — not by a divine attribute, not by a cosmic phenomenon, but by the medium through which all human existence runs out. Time is the oath because time is the mechanism of loss: being human in time means diminishment unless the exception conditions are met.

2. *Al-insān* (generic humanity) is IN loss — *lafī khusr* — present tense, locative: inside the condition, not approaching it. The bare noun *khusr* (not the fuller khusrān, not the verbal past khasira) makes the loss as elemental as possible.

3. The exception (illā) requires four traits: āmanū (believed) + ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt (righteous deeds) + tawāṣaw bi-l-ḥaqq (mutual exhortation to truth) + tawāṣaw bi-l-ṣabr (mutual exhortation to patience). The first two are individual; the last two are explicitly RELATIONAL — you must be embedded in a community of mutual accountability. The verb tawāṣā (Form VI, reciprocal) means to enjoin on each other — this cannot be done alone. Isolation fails by default.

Compare with 70:22-35: the same illā-exception architecture, but 70 lists nine traits and names the outcome explicitly as gardens of honor [70:35]. Surah 103 is the same theology compressed to its skeleton. The shared architecture — universal-loss + exception-clause — is the Quran's success argument form.

## 5. 4:69 — The Four Classes of the Favored

> وَمَن يُطِعِ ٱللَّهَ وَٱلرَّسُولَ فَأُوْلَٰٓئِكَ مَعَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَنۡعَمَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَيۡهِم مِّنَ ٱلنَّبِيِّـۧنَ وَٱلصِّدِّيقِينَ وَٱلشُّهَدَآءِ وَٱلصَّٰلِحِينَۚ وَحَسُنَ أُوْلَٰٓئِكَ رَفِيقࣰ ا
>
> *"And whoever obeys Allah and the Messenger — those will be with those upon whom Allah has bestowed favor: the prophets, the truthful (ṣiddīqūn), the martyrs (shuhadāʾ), and the righteous (ṣāliḥūn). And excellent are those as companions."* [4:69]

The verse pivots on one word: **maʿa** (with). The obedient are not told they BECOME prophets or martyrs — they are told they are WITH them. This is company, not rank reassignment. The four classes are the *anʿama Allāh ʿalayhim* (those upon whom God has bestowed favor) — the favored class named in al-Fātiḥa [1:7], here given their four sub-categories.

The four classes read as a nested hierarchy from most to least elevated:

| Class | Arabic | Register |
|-------|--------|---------|
| al-nabiyyūn | prophets | office, revelation-bearing |
| al-ṣiddīqūn | the truthful/confirming | sincerity + active confirmation of prophethood |
| al-shuhadāʾ | witnesses / martyrs | testimony through life or death |
| al-ṣāliḥūn | the righteous | the broad category of faith + deeds |

Al-ṣiddīqūn is the most disputed: the root ṣdq (truthfulness, sincerity) and the intensive form ṣiddīq points to those whose verification of truth is total — not merely accepting it but embodying and affirming it actively. In classical commentary this class includes those like Mary [66:12] and Abu Bakr (as "al-Ṣiddīq") — people who confirmed prophetic truth without themselves holding prophetic office. They are between prophethood and martyrdom: they possess the inner sincerity of prophets without the office.

The closing phrase "wa-ḥasuna ulāʾika rafīqā" — excellent are those as companions — confirms the maʿa reading: the obedient are companions (rafīq) of the four classes, not merged into them. [4:70] immediately adds: "that is bounty from God" — signaling that even this companionship is grace, not merit.

## 6. Cross-Passage Pattern: What's Repeated, What Differs

| Element | 2:1-5 | 87:14-15 | 91:9-10 | 103:1-3 | 4:69 |
|---------|-------|----------|---------|---------|------|
| Prayer (Slw) | Yes (2:3) | Yes (87:15) | Parallel (zky) | Implicit (ṣāliḥāt) | Implicit (obedience) |
| Purification (zkw) | Implicit (taqwā) | Yes (tazakkā) | Yes (zakkāhā) | Implicit (ṣāliḥāt) | — |
| Belief (Amn) | Yes (2:3) | Implicit | — | Yes (103:3) | Yes (obedience) |
| Spending/giving | Yes (2:3) | — | — | — | — |
| Communal exhortation | — | — | — | Yes (tawāṣaw × 2) | — |
| Prior revelation | Yes (2:4) | Historical ref (87:18-19) | — | — | — |
| Hereafter certainty | Yes (2:4) | Yes (87:17) | Thamūd counter | — | — |
| Obedience formula | — | — | — | — | Yes (yuṭiʿ) |

**Irreducible core:** Prayer and purification appear explicitly in the most direct formulas (87:14-15, 91:9-10) and are implicit or foundational in the others. No formula omits both.

**What differs by context:**
- Medinan passages (2:1-5, 4:69) add communal and structural elements: prior revelation acceptance, obedience to the Messenger, the four-class hierarchy. These reflect a community with institutional shape.
- Early Meccan passages (87, 91, 103) reduce to the irreducible: purify, remember, pray; or simply: purify vs. corrupt. No institutional framework assumed.
- Surah 103 is unique in making communal exhortation (tawāṣaw) explicitly part of the minimum conditions. Community is not added on top — it is built into the definition.

## 7. The Mukadhdhib Contrasts in Surahs 70 and 91

Both surahs frame success with a negation-side using those who DENY.

**Surah 70:** The believer-list [70:22-35] (nine traits, illā architecture) is immediately followed by [70:36-38]: "What is wrong with those who disbelieve, rushing before you in groups — does every one of them expect to enter a garden of pleasure? No." The contrast is stark: those who denied the truth expect paradise without the traits. [70:36-38] is mockery of this expectation. The deniers in 70 want the reward of the believer-list without inhabiting its conditions.

**Surah 91:** The success/failure binary at [91:9-10] is followed immediately by the Thamūd narrative [91:11-15]: "Thamūd denied by reason of their transgression (ṭaghwā) — when the most wretched of them was sent forth..." The Thamūd here are the historical instantiation of khāba (frustrated potential). They had the she-camel as a clear sign, had a messenger's warning, and still denied and slaughtered. Their story ends with total leveling: *fa-danda ma ʿalayhim rabbuhum bi-dhanbihim fa-sawwāhā* — the Lord leveled them for their sin, made them equal [with the dust] [91:14]. The frustrated-potential failure produces absolute consequence.

The contrast-architecture serves the same function in both surahs: the success formula is not free-floating optimism; it is the positive pole of a binary where the negative pole is illustrated concretely (mockery in 70, historical destruction in 91).

## 8. Verses for Direct Study

1. **[2:2-5]** — Full text of the foundational equation: muttaqīn traits → muflihūn identity. The Quran's first act of self-definition.
2. **[91:7-15]** — Nafs as the seventh oath-object → success/failure binary → Thamūd story. The cosmic-to-historical arc of soul-purification theology.
3. **[87:14-19]** — Three-step formula + its trans-Quranic anchor in scrolls of Abraham and Moses.
4. **[4:69-70]** — The four classes + the maʿa (companionship) framing + bounty of God as the closing qualifier.
5. **[70:22-35 vs 70:36-38]** — The longest believer-list in this cluster, followed by direct mockery of deniers expecting paradise without the list's conditions.

## 9. Rabbit Holes

- **tazakkā (Form V) vs. zakkāhā (Form II) in 87 and 91.** In 87:14 the subject does the purifying to himself (tazakkā, reflexive). In 91:9 someone purifies the soul — the agent is grammatically separate from the object (zakkāhā, with a third-person object suffix). Is there a theological distinction: self-purification vs. God-effected purification of the soul? The agent in 91:9 is ambiguous — is it the person themselves, or is God the one who purifies the soul placed in favorable conditions?
- **The ṣiddīqūn as a distinct rank.** The classical claim that ṣiddīqūn is a category between prophethood and martyrdom is not spelled out in the Quran's text — it is a commentary-tradition inference. A full ṣdq root analysis would test whether Quranic usage supports this distinct-class reading or whether ṣiddīqūn simply means "those who were entirely truthful/sincere" (a description, not an office).
- **The tawāṣaw (mutual exhortation) as the unique element in 103.** This Form VI verb (reciprocal action requiring two or more parties) appears only in 103 in the success-formula register. But nahy ʿan al-munkar / amr bi-l-maʿrūf (forbidding wrong / enjoining good) appears across many passages as a community obligation. Is tawāṣaw bi-l-ḥaqq a compressed form of the amr/nahy formula, or is it semantically distinct (exhorting to truth as opposed to enjoining specific righteous acts)?
- **Surah 87's claim of scriptural precedent [87:18-19].** The three-step formula (purify → remember → pray) is located in "the earlier scriptures — the scrolls of Abraham and Moses." This would make it the oldest attested success formula in the Quran's own framework. Cross-reference with the Quranic representation of Abraham's practice elsewhere (2:124-132, 21:51-73) to test whether the Abrahamic purification-prayer pattern is consistent.
- **The absence of fasting from all five formulas.** Prayer (Slw) appears in 2:3, 87:15, implicitly in 103 and 4:69. Spending/zakāt appears in 2:3. But sawm (fasting) never appears in any of the five compact success-formulas examined here, nor in the qad aflaHa formula cluster (23:1-9, 87:14, 91:9). This is negative space: fasting, despite being a major Medinan obligation, is not part of the Quran's compressed success-definitions. Investigate whether sawm appears in any muflihūn context.
