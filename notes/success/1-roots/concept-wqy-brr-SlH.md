# concept-wqy-brr-SlH: The Successful-Person Profile

## 1. Headline Finding

The Quran's "saved" profile is built from three interlocking roots: wqy (258 occ) supplies the orientation — shielding oneself from God's anger through obedience (taqwā); SlH (180 occ) supplies the behavioral proof — righteous deeds paired always with faith, never alone; brr (32 occ) supplies the elite sub-class — al-abrār, a small group characterized by pure-sincerity giving [76:8-9]. The decisive structural move: 2:177 ends its long definition of birr with "those are al-muttaqūn," collapsing brr into wqy. And 2:1-5 equates al-muttaqūn with al-muflihūn (the successful) as the Quran's opening move. The three classes are not synonymous but hierarchically nested.

## 3. wqy: The Shielding-Derivation Theology

The base verb is waqā (Form I, 16 occ): to protect, shield, guard. A shield's function is to stand between you and a harm. Form VIII ittaqā (166 occ) is reflexive: "to make oneself shielded." The Quranic meaning-grammar is thus: **taqwā = the practice of keeping God's protection between you and His punishment.**

The word is not "fear" in the trembling sense (that is khawf, khashyah). It is an active posture: ittaqū allāha means "take God as your shield/guard" — place yourself in a position of God-protection by obedience. The wāq (protector) appears three times [13:34, 40:21, 13:37] as an attribute: "they have no protector from Allah" — i.e., without taqwā there is no shield.

The logic: God's punishment threatens. Obedience to God's commands shields you from it. Therefore: the more you obey (= the more God-conscious you are), the more shielded you are. Taqwā is not a mood-state; it is an ongoing act of interposing obedience between yourself and consequence.

This explains why 2:197 commands "take provision, and the best provision is taqwā" — it is armor for the journey. And 49:13 — "the most honored of you with Allah is the most God-shielded (atqā)" — ranks humans by protective-obedience depth.

## 4. 2:1-5 — The muttaqūn = muflihūn Equation

**Panel 1 — the Quran's opening believer-profile [2:2-5]:**

> ذَٰلِكَ ٱلۡكِتَٰبُ لَا رَيۡبَۛ فِيهِۛ هُدٗى لِّلۡمُتَّقِينَ  
> ٱلَّذِينَ يُؤۡمِنُونَ بِٱلۡغَيۡبِ وَيُقِيمُونَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَمِمَّا رَزَقۡنَٰهُمۡ يُنفِقُونَ  
> وَٱلَّذِينَ يُؤۡمِنُونَ بِمَآ أُنزِلَ إِلَيۡكَ وَمَآ أُنزِلَ مِن قَبۡلِكَ وَبِٱلۡأٓخِرَةِ هُمۡ يُوقِنُونَ  
> أُوْلَٰٓئِكَ عَلَىٰ هُدٗى مِّن رَّبِّهِمۡۖ وَأُوْلَٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلۡمُفۡلِحُونَ

*"This is the Book, no doubt in it — a guidance for the muttaqūn: those who believe in the unseen, establish prayer, and spend from what We have provided them; and those who believe in what was revealed to you and what was revealed before you, and of the Hereafter they are certain. Those are upon guidance from their Lord, and those — they are the muflihūn."* [Saheeh/composite]

Three moves in four verses: (a) The Book is for the muttaqūn. (b) Their traits are enumerated (belief in unseen, prayer, spending, prior revelation, Hereafter certainty). (c) Those same people are labeled al-muflihūn — the successful. 

Five traits, none ritual-only: belief in the unseen (epistemic), prayer (vertical relation), spending (horizontal relation), prior-revelation acceptance (historical breadth), Hereafter certainty (existential grounding). The Quran opens not with a legal obligation but an inner orientation that expresses itself in five dispositions.

This is the Quran's **first believer-profile** and it equates two categories that could have been separate: being muttaqī (disposition) and being muflih (outcome/status). The equation is definitional, not causal — the muttaqūn **are** the muflihūn.

## 5. brr: 2:177's Definition-by-List

**Panel 2 — the Quran's explicit definition of birr [2:177]:**

> لَّيۡسَ ٱلۡبِرَّ أَن تُوَلُّواْ وُجُوهَكُمۡ قِبَلَ ٱلۡمَشۡرِقِ وَٱلۡمَغۡرِبِ وَلَٰكِنَّ ٱلۡبِرَّ مَنۡ ءَامَنَ بِٱللَّهِ وَٱلۡيَوۡمِ ٱلۡأٓخِرِ... أُوْلَٰٓئِكَ ٱلَّذِينَ صَدَقُواْۖ وَأُوْلَٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلۡمُتَّقُونَ

*"Righteousness is not turning your faces toward east or west. Rather, birr belongs to one who: believes in Allah, the Last Day, the angels, the Book, and the prophets; gives wealth, despite loving it, to relatives, orphans, the poor, the wayfarer, beggars, and for freeing slaves; establishes prayer, gives zakāh; fulfills promises; and is patient in suffering, adversity, and in battle. Those are the ones who have been true, and those — they are the muttaqūn."*

The verse is structured as a rejection-then-definition (laysa... wa-lākin...). Birr is not orientation of the body (ritual direction) — it is orientation of the whole person. The list runs: belief (5 objects) → giving wealth (6 beneficiary categories) → prayer + zakāh → promise-keeping → patience under three forms of difficulty. These are the same five-domain structure as 2:2-4 but expanded. 

The closing move is decisive: after defining birr, the verse says those people are "al-ṣādiqūn" (the truthful/sincere) and "hum al-muttaqūn." Birr is thus not a separate moral category — it is the content of taqwā. To be al-abrār is to be muttaqī with birr as the substance.

The abrār in later usage (76:5-9, 82:13, 83:18-22) are characterized by three things: vow-fulfillment [76:7], fearful expectation of Judgment Day [76:7-10], and feeding the needy/orphan/captive **solely for God's face** with no desire for reward or thanks [76:8-9]. That last trait — pure sincerity (li-wajhi allāh, seeking neither reward nor gratitude) — is what distinguishes the abrār profile from the broader muttaqūn. The abrār give more, and give silently.

## 6. SlH + the ʿAmal Ṣāliḥ Pairing Formula

SlH carries a structural ambiguity built into the root: ṣalaḥa (Form I, 2 occ) means "to be sound/fit/in-order"; aṣlaḥa (Form IV, 28 occ) means "to make sound" = to reform. The root describes both a STATE (being ṣāliḥ) and an ACTION (iṣlāḥ, reforming). This is not polysemy — it is a dual-register root: personal integrity + social repair simultaneously.

**The ʿamal ṣāliḥ formula (62 plural occurrences of ṣāliḥāt)** is the Quran's most-repeated compound descriptor. The top co-roots confirm this: Eml (93 ayahs, 55%) and Amn (82 ayahs, 48%) together appear with SlH at higher rates than any other root cluster in the corpus. The formula is "alladhīna āmanū wa-ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt" — those who believed AND did righteous deeds — first at 2:25.

Why always faith + deeds, never alone? A deed is ṣāliḥ when properly directed toward God. Without faith, deeds have no directionality — they cannot be ṣāliḥ. Faith without deeds leaves the ṣāliḥ state empty of practical expression. The pairing is constitutive: faith is the orientation; deeds are the evidence it is real.

3:114 shows the ṣāliḥūn in action: "they believe in Allah and the Last Day, enjoin good and forbid wrong, and race in good deeds — and those are among the ṣāliḥūn." The ṣāliḥūn here are defined by social engagement (amr bi-l-maʿrūf, nahy ʿan al-munkar) — confirming that ṣāliḥ is not quietist piety but outward rectification.

The iṣlāḥ (reform) sense adds a political-social dimension: aṣlaḥa typically refers to rectifying broken relationships, communities, or agreements [2:224, 4:114, 49:9-10]. The muṣliḥūn (reformers, 5 occ) appear as those who claim to reform but are accused of corruption [2:11] — which reveals that iṣlāḥ is a contested, claimed category. Real ṣāliḥ is validated externally; its opposite is fasād (corruption).

## 7. Cross-Comparison: muttaqūn vs. abrār vs. ṣāliḥūn

**Are these the same class named differently, or nested sub-classes?**

The data supports a **nested hierarchy**, not synonymy:

**ṣāliḥūn = the broadest practical class.** They believe and do righteous deeds. Ṣāliḥ is used for the full range: prophets [6:85], ordinary believers [4:69], angels [80:16]. The "alladhīna āmanū wa-ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt" formula applies at scale (50+ occurrences). Entry bar: faith + righteous deeds. SlH's Meccan dominance (113 vs. 67 Medinan) means ṣāliḥūn was the primary Meccan category before legal frameworks emerged.

**muttaqūn = the class defined by inner orientation.** Being muttaqī is not a deed — it is a disposition. The 166 verbal occurrences of ittaqā are imperatives and descriptions of inner stance, not external acts. The Quran "guides" the muttaqūn [2:2] — suggesting they are the ones who receive and act on the guidance, meaning taqwā is the *precondition* for benefiting from revelation, not its output. The muttaqūn are promised the earth [7:128], Paradise [3:133, 47:15], divine protection [45:19], and the Quran itself [2:2]. They are the most-promised class by a wide margin.

**abrār = the elite sub-class of muttaqūn.** Only 9 eschatological occurrences; always in contexts of otherworldly reward. 2:177 defines birr fully — and then collapses it into "those are the muttaqūn." Al-abrār are therefore muttaqūn who have realized birr to its maximum: the sincerity of feeding and giving "for God's face only, seeking neither reward nor thanks" [76:9]. The abrār are the muttaqūn who have internalized the disposition so completely that their giving is anonymous and unrewarded. In 3:193 believers *pray* to "die with the abrār" — implying abrār is an aspirational category above their current state.

**4:69 provides the explicit hierarchy:** the categories are prophets → ṣiddīqūn (truthful) → shuhadāʾ (martyrs) → ṣāliḥūn. The ṣāliḥūn are the fourth and broadest class, the company of all the others. The muttaqūn cut across all four (every prophet is muttaqī), and the abrār are the ṣiddīqūn of the ṣāliḥūn.

**Summary table:**

| Class | Root | Defining trait | Scale | Promise |
|-------|------|----------------|-------|---------|
| ṣāliḥūn | SlH | faith + righteous deeds | Broadest | Gardens [2:25] |
| muttaqūn | wqy | God-shielding disposition | Core class | Quran, earth, Paradise |
| abrār | brr | pure-sincerity giving | Elite (9 esch. occ) | Pleasure, ʿIlliyyūn [83:18-22] |

## 8. Negative Space

The Quran never defines the ṣāliḥūn or muttaqūn by ethnic, tribal, or national category — the formulas are open and apply to any believer. More significantly: neither taqwā nor birr is ever defined in purely ritual terms (prayer, fasting alone) — ritual always appears alongside economic and relational obligations, ruling out purely formal-observance readings.

## 9. Verses for Direct Study

1. **2:2-5** — The Quran opens with the muttaqūn's traits and equates them to the muflihūn. The Quran's first act is defining its own audience.
2. **2:177** — Birr defined by list, ending in "those are the muttaqūn." The structural collapse of brr into wqy.
3. **76:5-12** — The abrār drinking scene: their deeds (vow-fulfillment, feeding the needy) described, then the key phrase "we seek neither reward nor thanks" — sincerity as the elite distinguisher.
4. **3:133-136** — Paradise "prepared for the muttaqūn" (uʿiddat li-l-muttaqīn), followed by their traits: spending in ease and hardship, swallowing anger, forgiving. The muttaqūn here look like the abrār of 76.
5. **51:15-19** — Muttaqūn in gardens and springs; they used to sleep little by night, seek forgiveness at dawn, and give from their wealth to the asker and the deprived. Taqwā's behavioral output is night-prayer + forgiveness-seeking + redistribution.

## 10. Rabbit Holes

- **The atqā superlative [49:13].** "The most honored of you with Allah is the atqā." If taqwā is shielding, does the atqā simply have the most protective obedience, or is there a qualitative threshold where atqā becomes something different from muttaqī? The hapax tuqāh [3:28] may be a distinct term worth separate analysis.
- **The brr/barr polysemy problem.** The `bar~` lemma conflates geographical al-barr (land) with moral al-abrār. A morphology-level disambiguation (using the actual diacritical form) would give the true abrār count. The current 22-occ count is inflated by ~13 land-occurrences.
- **ṣiddīqūn as the joint between brr and wqy.** 2:177 ends "those who were ṣādiqūn, and those are the muttaqūn." Truthfulness/sincerity (ṣidq) appears to be the bridge concept linking birr (pure-sincerity giving) to taqwā (God-conscious orientation). A ṣdq root analysis could map this bridge.
- **The iṣlāḥ claim and fasād accusation.** In 2:11, those who corrupt say "we are muṣliḥūn." The contested nature of iṣlāḥ as a political claim (vs. real ṣāliḥ state) maps onto modern debates about who gets to define "righteous reform." Cross-reference with fsD root.
- **The SlH + ʿamal formula across revelation phases.** The Amn+SlH co-occurrence (82 ayahs) and Eml+SlH (93 ayahs): if mapped by Meccan vs. Medinan, do the deeds become more specific in the Medinan phase? SlH is more Meccan (113 vs 67), suggesting "righteous deed" as a general category gives way to specific legal requirements in Medina — worth testing.
