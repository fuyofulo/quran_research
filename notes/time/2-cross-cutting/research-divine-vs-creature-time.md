## 1. Headline: Lexical Apartheid as Theological Architecture

The Quran enforces a **clean, near-mechanical lexical partition** between two registers of permanence:

- A **creature-side** vocabulary that names abiding-states, appointed terms, postponements, and stretches of duration: **xld** (abide), **Abd** (forever-as-adverb), **Hyn** (a while), **Ajl** (appointed term), **wqt** (set time), **Amd** (extent), **ESr** (the age).
- A **divine-side** vocabulary that names self-subsistent being, remaining-after-perishing, first/last bracketing, oneness, and refuge: **qwm/qayyūm** (self-subsisting), **bqy/bāqī** (remaining), **Hyy/Hayy** (the Living), **Awl/al-Awwal** + **Axr/al-Ākhir** (the First, the Last), **wHd/wāḥid** + **AHd/aḥad** (the One), **Smd/al-Ṣamad** (the Eternal Refuge — once).

The partition is enforced by **two complementary silences**:

1. The creature-side roots are **never predicated of God**. Across 87 occurrences of *xld*, 28 of *Abd*, 35 of *Hyn*, 56 of *Ajl*, 13 of *wqt*, 4 of *Amd*, and 5 of *ESr*, **none** uses Allah/Rabb as the subject of the duration-attribute. (Verbal cases where Allah *grants* an *ajal* or *defers* a punishment do not count: God acts on creature-time, He is not measured by it.)
2. The divine-side roots are **almost never predicated of creatures in the same sense**. Where they overlap with creature reference (e.g., *bāqiyāt al-ṣāliḥāt* "the abiding good deeds," *al-Ḥayy* used adjectivally of "the living" plant or person), the Quran flags the difference: divine *al-Ḥayy* is qualified by *al-ladhī lā yamūt* ("who does not die," [25:58]), divine *al-Bāqī* is paired with *kullu man ʿalayhā fān* ("everyone on earth perishes," [55:26–27]).

The architecture is doctrinally load-bearing: time is a **created thing**, and the vocabulary that measures created time cannot, without category-error, be ported across to the un-created. The Quran's grammar will not let you say *Allāh xālid* or *Allāh abadī* — not because the words are unavailable, but because the **theology forbids the predication**. The divine names line is drawn at the lexical level.

This is not later kalām reading back into the text. It is the Quran's own syntactic discipline. The most economical evidence: in *Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ* [112], where God is most maximally compressed into four lines (*Aḥad — Ṣamad — lam yalid wa lam yūlad — lam yakun lahu kufuwan aḥad*), **none of the four lines uses any creature-time root**. The four-line creed is built entirely from the divine-side vocabulary plus negations (*lam yalid* = root *wld*; *lam yakun* = root *kwn*). The eternity is asserted by negating creature-properties, not by naming a duration.

The map shows three classes:

- **Class A — pure creature** (xld, Abd, Hyn, Ajl, wqt, Amd, ESr, dhr): never predicated of God. Eight roots, ~234 total occurrences. This is the **largest and cleanest** class.
- **Class B — pure divine** (Smd, qayyūm-lemma): never predicated of creatures. Smallest class but the most absolute.
- **Class C — shared lemma, partitioned reference** (Awl, Axr, ywm, bqy, Hyy, wHd, AHd): the same word is applied both to God and to creatures, but the **theological work is done by qualification, contrast, or context**. 57:3 is the signature: *huwa al-awwal wa-l-ākhir* — both endpoints belong to God; everything between belongs to creatures.

The Quran's "lexical apartheid" is therefore not absolute — it is **graduated**. There are roots God will never share (Class A); roots only God owns (Class B); and roots that name a relation in which God is the absolute term (Class C). Together, the three classes constitute a **theological grammar of time**.

## 3. The Divine-Eternity Vocabulary — Catalog

### 3.1 qwm — *al-Qayyūm* (the Self-Subsistent)

The root *qwm* (660 occurrences, 22 lemmas) is the Quran's largest single time-adjacent root. The vast majority is creature-grammar: *qiyāmah* (resurrection-standing, 70 occ.), *aqāma* (to establish, 54), *mustaqīm* (straight, 37), *qāʾim* (standing, 17), *muqīm* (residing, 10). The lemma the Quran reserves for divine attribution is **`qay~uwm` (قَيُّوم)**, used only **3 times**: [2:255], [3:2], [20:111]. Every occurrence pairs with *al-Ḥayy*.

- **[2:255]** *Allāhu lā ilāha illā huwa al-Ḥayy al-Qayyūm* — "Allah, there is no god but He, the Living, the Self-Subsisting."
- **[3:2]** identical formula, opening *Āl ʿImrān*.
- **[20:111]** *wa-ʿanati l-wujūhu li-l-Ḥayy al-Qayyūm* — "all faces will be humbled before the Living, the Self-Subsisting."

The semantic load is twofold: *qayyūm* is from the same root as *qiyām* (standing, establishment), but morphologically intensified — the **fa'ʿūl** form encodes "the one who *makes-standing*, who *upholds-being*." Translators converge on "Sustainer of all existence" (Saheeh, Khattab) or "Self-Subsistent" (Pickthall, Arberry). The Arabic compresses both meanings — God *stands by Himself* AND *makes everything else stand*. He is not in time; He is the upholder of the temporal order.

The cleanest argument that *qayyūm* is partitioned: the related lemma *qayyim* (5 occ.) is used of "the correct religion" ([9:36], [12:40], [30:30], [30:43]) and the "straight book" ([18:2]) — never of God. The morphological difference between *qayyim* (correct, upright) and *qayyūm* (self-subsisting) does the lexical separation work. The intensified form is reserved.

### 3.2 bqy — *al-Bāqī* (the Remaining), and the Scandal of the Construct

The root *bqy* (21 occurrences, 8 lemmas) is the Quran's "remaining" verb. Its divine usage is concentrated in **4 verses**:

- **[55:27]** *wa-yabqā wajhu rabbika dhū l-jalāl wa-l-ikrām* — "and there will remain the Face of your Lord, possessor of Majesty and Honor." Verb form *yabqā*.
- **[16:96]** *mā ʿindakum yanfadu wa-mā ʿinda llāhi bāq* — "what you have ends; what is with Allah is *bāq* (lasting)." Active participle *bāq*.
- **[28:60]** and **[42:36]** — both repeat the formula: *mā ʿinda llāhi khayrun wa-abqā* ("what is with Allah is better and more lasting"). Comparative *abqā*.

But — and this is the analytically decisive observation — the Quran also predicates *bāqī*-forms of **created things**, specifically **paradise-rewards and good deeds**:

- **[18:46], [19:76]** *al-bāqiyāt al-ṣāliḥāt* — "the abiding good deeds." Used twice as a frozen formula.
- **[20:73], [20:127], [20:131], [87:17]** *abqā* — comparative "more lasting," used of God's reward, the Hereafter, the abode-of-the-righteous.
- **[26:120], [37:77]** *al-bāqīn* — "the survivors" (those left after the Flood; Noah's descendants).
- **[2:248], [11:86], [11:116], [69:8], [43:28]** *baqiyya* / *bāqiya* — "remnant" (the Ark relics, Shuʿayb's residual provision, etc.).

So *bqy* is **NOT** strictly divine-only. It is **a transitive root**: it names the property of *outlasting-something-else*. When the Lord's face *yabqā*, it outlasts the perishing creation. When good deeds are *al-bāqiyāt*, they outlast the *zīna* (adornment) of dunyā [18:46]. When something is *abqā* (more lasting), it is **always in comparison to something less lasting**. The root encodes **relational permanence**, not absolute permanence.

This is a key structural finding — sharper than the surface "lexical apartheid" hypothesis. *Bqy* is a **comparator-root**: it works only against an explicit foil. The Lord's face is *bāq* against the perishing world; the deeds are *bāqiyāt* against the adornment; the reward is *abqā* against the dunyā-pleasure. Divine permanence and creature-derivative-permanence (paradise, deeds-stored-with-God) both need a **foil of creature-transience** to be named. This is why *bqy* never appears as a stand-alone divine attribute (no *Yā Bāqī!* invocation in the Quran itself; the name *al-Bāqī* enters the divine-names tradition later from contexts like 55:27 read in isolation).

The implication for the apartheid hypothesis: **the divine-side vocabulary is not just non-creaturely, it is contrastive**. It only makes sense against the foil of creature-time.

### 3.3 Hyy — *al-Ḥayy* (the Living)

*Hyy* (184 occurrences, 12 lemmas) is the most semantically loaded root in this set. The lemma *Ḥayy* (24 occ.) is split:

- **Divine attribute** (5 verses): [2:255], [3:2], [20:111], [25:58], [40:65]. Always paired with *al-Qayyūm* except [25:58] (*al-Ḥayy alladhī lā yamūt*) and [40:65] (*Huwa al-Ḥayy*).
- **Creature use**: alive vs dead; "living thing" ([21:30] — "every living thing from water"); paradise-martyrs are *aḥyāʾ* ([2:154], [3:169]); the contrast verses [3:27], [6:95], [10:31], [30:19] (*yukhriju al-ḥayya min al-mayyit* — "He brings the living from the dead").

The disambiguator is **[25:58]** — *wa-tawakkal ʿalā l-Ḥayy alladhī lā yamūt* — "rely upon the Living, **the One who does not die**." This is the verse that explicitly anchors divine *al-Ḥayy* as different from creature-living. Creatures live and die; God lives without dying. The qualifier *lā yamūt* surgically separates divine *Ḥayy* from creature *ḥayy*. The same lemma, but the predicate is radically different.

Co-occurrence with *qwm* in this divine sense is exclusive: every *al-Ḥayy al-Qayyūm* pair is divine. The two roots **interlock to form a single attribute** — Living + Self-Subsisting — and the pair is doing the work of "absolutely, intrinsically alive, not in time."

### 3.4 wHd / AHd — *al-Wāḥid*, *al-Aḥad*

*wḥd* (68 occ., 4 lemmas) and *AHd* (85 occ., 2 lemmas) are the unity-roots. Their divine deployment is dense:

- **[112:1]** *qul huwa Allāhu Aḥad* — "Say: He is Allah, the One." The opening word of the most compressed divine creed. Aḥad is the absolute uniqueness, not just the numeric one.
- **[2:163], [16:22], [18:110], [37:4], [41:6]** etc. — *ilāhukum ilāhun wāḥid* ("your god is one god"). Wāḥid = the numerical-singular.
- **[12:39], [13:16], [14:48], [38:65], [39:4], [40:16]** etc. — *Allāhu al-Wāḥidu al-Qahhār* ("Allah, the One, the Prevailing"). Standardized formula.

Why does unity belong in a study of *time*? Because the Quran's divine-time vocabulary is structurally **non-divisible**. *Aḥad* and *Wāḥid* assert that God cannot be split, paired, succeeded, or supplemented. Time, by contrast, is the medium of succession — moments come after moments, generations after generations. To call God *al-Aḥad* is, among other things, to **deny that He has temporal parts**. He has no before or after that He stands inside; He brackets before and after as *al-Awwal wa-l-Ākhir*. This is the Quran's quiet move of using oneness-vocabulary to negate temporal-decomposition.

### 3.5 Smd — *al-Ṣamad* (the Eternal Refuge)

*Smd* is the one root in this study with **exactly one occurrence**, the famous **[112:2]**: *Allāhu al-Ṣamad*. It appears nowhere else.

The lexicons are not in agreement on what *al-Ṣamad* means. Classical glosses span: "the one to whom all turn" (the resort, the refuge); "the one who is solid, has no hollow"; "the eternal, the everlasting"; "the self-sufficient, who needs nothing." Translators converge variably: Saheeh "the Eternal Refuge"; Pickthall "the eternally Besought of all"; Khattab "the Sustainer ˹needed by all˺"; Arberry "the Everlasting Refuge."

What matters for this investigation is that *Ṣamad* is **the only divine-time-adjacent vocabulary in the entire corpus that is morphologically and semantically isolated**. It has no verbal forms, no plural, no non-divine usage. *Smd* is structurally parallel to *Abd* (one lemma, one form) — both are frozen — but their polarities are inverse: *Abd* is the frozen creature-adverb, *Smd* is the frozen divine-noun. They are mirror-image hapaxes in the time-vocabulary.

The placement of *al-Ṣamad* is also significant: in *Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ* [112], it is the **second** of four divine attributions, sandwiched between *Aḥad* (oneness) and the negations *lam yalid wa lam yūlad* (does not beget and is not begotten). The surah's logic: God is one; God is *Ṣamad*; therefore neither bears nor is born; therefore has no equal. *Ṣamad* is the bridge between unity and uncausedness. Eternity here is **non-derivativeness** — God depends on nothing, is the dependence of everything.

### 3.6 Awl + Axr — *al-Awwal wa-l-Ākhir* (the First and the Last)

The pair appears together as divine attribution in exactly one verse — but it is the densest verse in the entire divine-time vocabulary:

**[57:3]** هُوَ ٱلۡأَوَّلُ وَٱلۡأٓخِرُ وَٱلظَّٰهِرُ وَٱلۡبَاطِنُۖ وَهُوَ بِكُلِّ شَيۡءٍ عَلِيمٌ
*"He is the First and the Last, the Manifest and the Hidden; and He is, of all things, Knowing."*

Two binary pairs in one line:
- *al-Awwal / al-Ākhir* — temporal bracketing
- *al-Ẓāhir / al-Bāṭin* — visibility bracketing

The architecture is **bracketing**: God is named not as a point inside time but as the **two endpoints** of time itself. There is no temporal range that God does not bracket. This is the divine-side counterpart to the creature-side *xld* + *Abd* combination — but where xld+Abd marks an **interior abiding-state-with-temporal-seal** ("they abide there, forever"), Awl+Axr marks an **exterior bracketing of the entire temporal series** ("He is the first and the last").

The pair is structurally asymmetric in the wider corpus: **Axr** appears 250 times (62% as *al-ākhira*, the Hereafter pole); **Awl** appears 170 times across four lemmas (al-awwal as divine name once; al-awwalūn = the ancients, frequently polemic). The polemical inversion is sharp: when disbelievers say *asāṭīr al-awwalīn* ("legends of the ancients") nine times across nine surahs, they use the same root that names *al-Awwal* (God Himself) at 57:3. The Quran's response is not lexical but semantic: what the deniers call the *al-awwalūn*-derived "legends" is actually delivered by *al-Awwal* Himself. **The disbelievers historicize the message by locating it in the past; the Quran de-historicizes it by locating its source at the bracket.**

The dunyā/ākhira pair, with *al-ūlā* (the prior life) and *al-ākhira* (the Hereafter), uses the same Awl+Axr roots in their **creature-temporal** sense: the two-life cosmology. So the same two roots do triple duty:
- Polemic (*al-awwalūn* = the ancients, the rejected genre)
- Cosmology (*al-ūlā / al-ākhira* = the two lives)
- Theology (*al-Awwal / al-Ākhir* = divine endpoints)

Three registers, one pair of roots. This is a Class C (shared) root par excellence.

## 4. The Contrast Structure — Where Transience and Eternity Face Off

The Quran does not just keep the two vocabularies apart. It **deliberately stages them in opposition**, in a small set of high-rhetorical verses where divine-permanence is explicitly contrasted with creature-perishing.

### 4.1 The Face-Verses — *kullu shay'in halikun illā wajhah*

**[28:88]** *kullu shayʾin hālikun illā wajhah* — "Everything is perishing except His Face."
- **kullu** (all) + **shayʾ** (thing) + **hālik** (perishing — root *hlk*) + **illā** (except) + **wajh** (face) + **-hu** (His).
- Then: *lahu al-ḥukm wa-ilayhi turjaʿūn* — "His is the Judgment, and to Him you return."

Note: this verse uses **hlk** (perish, destroy), not the contrast vocabulary of *bqy*. The next pair makes it explicit:

**[55:26–27]**
- 55:26 *kullu man ʿalayhā fān* — "Everyone on it [the earth] is *fān* (perishing — root *fny*)."
- 55:27 *wa-yabqā wajhu rabbika dhū al-jalāl wa-l-ikrām* — "and there will *yabqā* (remain — root *bqy*) the Face of your Lord, possessor of Majesty and Honor."

This is the cleanest, most syntactically symmetric face-off in the Quran:
- Creature side: **fny** (perish) + **kullu man** (everyone)
- Divine side: **bqy** (remain) + **wajh rabbika** (Face of your Lord)

The two roots — *fny* and *bqy* — are paired as direct antonyms. The grammar puts them in adjacent verses, with parallel syntax. The verse-pair establishes the **contrastive semantics of bqy**: it is the verb of *outlasting-the-perishing*. This confirms what 16:96, 28:60, 42:36 all assert in different formulations: **what is with God remains; what you have ends**.

### 4.2 The "What Is With Allah" Formula

A trio of verses uses the same construction:
- **[16:96]** *mā ʿindakum yanfadu wa-mā ʿinda llāhi bāq* — "what is with you ends; what is with Allah is lasting."
- **[28:60]** *wa-mā ʿinda llāhi khayrun wa-abqā* — "what is with Allah is better and more lasting."
- **[42:36]** *wa-mā ʿinda llāhi khayrun wa-abqā* — same formula.

Three verses, two roots: the creature side uses **nfd** (run out, exhaust) at 16:96; the divine side uses **bqy** (lasting) all three times. The formula's power is the contrast: it does not say "Allah's reward is eternal" in absolute terms; it says "Allah's reward is *more lasting* than yours." Comparative permanence, foil-required.

### 4.3 The Hereafter Comparator — *al-ākhiratu khayrun wa-abqā*

**[87:16–17]** *bal tuʾthirūna l-ḥayāta l-dunyā wa-l-ākhiratu khayrun wa-abqā* — "but you prefer the worldly life, while the Hereafter is better and more lasting."

Same formula, this time pivoting on the dunyā/ākhira pair. *Abqā* (more lasting) is doing the same work: it is a comparator that requires the dunyā-foil.

This builds a coherent **semantic field of contrastive permanence**: *bāq, abqā, yabqā, bāqiya* (root *bqy*) all live in the rhetorical position of *contrasting the perishing of one thing with the remaining of another*. They never appear as standalone abstract divine attributes (no "God is eternal" without a foil). The Quran's grammar makes divine eternity **always relational** — relative to the perishing of what is not God.

### 4.4 The 18:42–46 Sequence — wealth, deeds, eternity

The narrative of the destroyed garden in *Sūrat al-Kahf* sets up the contrast in a parable, then resolves it in a maxim:

- [18:32–43] — the garden owner's confidence: *mā aẓunnu an tabīda hādhihi abadan* ("I do not think this will perish, ever") at 18:35 — using *abadan* (root *Abd*) and *bāda* (perish).
- [18:42] — the garden's destruction.
- [18:46] — the maxim: *al-mālu wa-l-banūna zīnatu l-ḥayāti l-dunyā wa-l-bāqiyātu l-ṣāliḥātu khayrun ʿinda rabbika thawāban wa-khayrun amalan* — "Wealth and children are the adornment of worldly life; the abiding good deeds (*al-bāqiyāt al-ṣāliḥāt*) are better with your Lord in reward and better in hope."

The contrast vocabulary: *zīna* (adornment, root *zyn*) vs *bāqiyāt* (the abiding ones, root *bqy*). The story shows the *abadan* (root *Abd*) of the disbeliever's certainty being falsified; the maxim then re-deploys the *bqy*-root in a creature-derivative sense (the **deeds** are abiding) — but only because they are *ʿinda rabbika* (with your Lord). The deeds participate in *bqy* by being stored in the divine domain. This is the second half of the apartheid: created things can borrow divine permanence only by being *with* God. They cannot be *bāqī* on their own.

### 4.5 The architecture summarized

The contrast structure has three rhetorical moves:
1. **Universal perishing** of the creature side: *kullu shayʾin hālik* / *kullu man ʿalayhā fān* / *yanfadu* / *tabīda*.
2. **Exclusion clause** marking the exception: *illā wajhah* / *yabqā wajhu rabbik* / *mā ʿinda llāhi*.
3. **Positive predication** using the divine-side vocabulary: *bāq*, *abqā*, *yabqā*.

The grammar enacts the theology: to say "God remains" the Quran must first say "everything else perishes." The divine-permanence assertion is **logically and rhetorically dependent on the creature-transience assertion**. This is why the lexical apartheid is not just a dictionary fact — it is a **rhetorical engine**. The two vocabularies need each other to do their work.

## 5. The Dahr Refutation as Paradigm

The cleanest case of the Quran's strategy on time-vocabulary is **dhr**. Pre-Islamic Arabian theology had *al-dahr* — Time-as-impersonal-fate, the devourer of generations, to whom no prayer could be addressed. The Quran admits this word **exactly twice** (45:24 and 76:1), and the two occurrences enact a complete demotion:

**[45:24]** Quoted denial: *wa-mā yuhliku-nā illā al-dahr* — "and nothing destroys us but al-dahr." Followed by Quranic refutation: *wa-mā lahum bi-dhālika min ʿilm* — "they have no knowledge of that."

The Quran's surgical move: it does not refute *al-dahr* by replacing it with another noun. It **attacks the verb**: *uhliku-nā* (destroy us). Elsewhere in the Quran, *ahlaka* with God as agent is one of the most frequent destruction-verbs (~70 occurrences with Allah as subject). The deniers attribute to *al-dahr* the verb that the Quran consistently attributes to Allah. **The disagreement is not "is there destruction?" but "WHO destroys?"** Allah, not impersonal time. The verb gets restored to the right subject.

**[76:1]** Inert backdrop: *hal atā ʿalā l-insāni ḥīnun min al-dahr lam yakun shayʾan madhkūran* — "Has there come upon man a stretch of time when he was not a thing mentioned?"

Here *al-dahr* is **demoted from agent to partitive container**: it is the genitive substrate from which a *ḥīn* (a stretch) is carved out. From nominative subject of *yuhliku* (45:24) to genitive object of *min* (76:1). The grammar enacts the doctrine: *al-dahr* is not allowed to *do* anything as a subject in the Quran's own narrating voice.

This is the **paradigm case** of what the Quran does with time-vocabulary that has been deified: **lexical quarantine + grammatical demotion**. The word is admitted but not allowed agency.

### Does this paradigm appear elsewhere?

**Partial parallels:**

1. **Refusal to swear by *al-dahr*.** The Quran swears by every other temporal measure — *wa-l-ʿaṣr* [103:1], *wa-l-fajr* [89:1], *wa-l-layl* [92:1], *wa-l-ḍuḥā* [93:1], *wa-l-shams* [91:1], *wa-l-ʿādiyāt* [100:1] — but not *al-dahr*. The absence of this oath is structurally significant: God will not make the deniers' deity into His own oath-witness. (Detail in `concept-dhr.md`.)

2. **Refusal to substantivize *Abd*.** Classical Arabic has the noun *abad* (perpetuity), the adjective *abadī* (eternal), the plural *al-ābād*. The Quran uses **none** of these. The root has been **collapsed into one frozen adverbial form** (*abadan*, 28 occ.). The Quran could easily have said *Allāhu abadī* — neither is grammatically impossible. The **deliberate absence is the doctrine**. Allah's eternity is not measured in adverbial-time. *abadan* belongs to the world of duration; God belongs to a different lexical category. (Detail in `concept-Abd.md`.)

3. **Reservation of the intensified *qayyūm* form.** The root *qwm* generates many lemmas the Quran uses freely for creatures and creation (*qiyāmah*, *qāʾim*, *muqīm*, *qayyim*). The intensified *qay~uwm* form is reserved for God in 3 verses. The morphological choice does the partition.

4. **Polemical re-claiming of *al-Awwal*.** The disbelievers historicize the message as *asāṭīr al-awwalīn* (9 occurrences). The Quran responds by naming God as *al-Awwal* (57:3). The same root, repurposed as the divine bracket of what the disbelievers called "old fables." This is closer to a **polemical inversion** than a quarantine.

The dahr-refutation paradigm is **partially generalized**: the Quran consistently refuses to (a) attribute creature-time agency to false powers, (b) substantivize creature-temporal nouns into divine attributes, and (c) swear by quasi-deified temporal entities. But it does not always quarantine — sometimes (as with Awl/Axr, Hyy, qwm) it shares the root and **partitions by qualified predication** (intensified form, contrastive context, explicit qualifier *lā yamūt*).

## 6. Theological Synthesis: Time Is a Created Thing

The lexical apartheid is the corpus's own argument that **time is created**, and therefore the vocabulary that measures created time cannot, without category-error, be applied to the un-created Creator. Five strands of evidence converge:

### 6.1 God measures time differently from creatures

[22:47] *yawmun ʿinda rabbika ka-alfi sanatin mimmā taʿuddūn* — "a day with your Lord is like a thousand years of those which you count."
[32:5] same formula, this time about the *ascent of the matter*: a day = a thousand years.
[70:4] *yawmin kāna miqdāruhu khamsīna alfa sanah* — "a Day whose extent is fifty thousand years," the day of the angels' and Spirit's ascent.

These verses do something subtle: they use the creature-time word *yawm* of God's frame, but they immediately **specify a non-creature scale**. A day with God is not a day-by-our-counting; it is a thousand or fifty thousand years *by our counting*. The Quran does not say *Allāh has time*; it says *Allāh's time-equivalents are not your time*. The vocabulary is shared (*yawm*); the scale is incommensurable.

This is the partial overlap that proves the rule: the only place the Quran lets a creature-time root touch the divine is when it is immediately qualified by an incommensurability factor. *Yawm Allāh* / *yawm ʿinda rabbika* / *ayyām Allāh* (14:5, 45:14) all ride this rail — God's "day" is **categorically other** even when the word is shared.

### 6.2 God appoints, defers, and bounds time but is not bounded by it

The Quran's verbal use of time-roots with God-as-subject is consistent: God **acts on** time, He is not measured by it.

- *>ax~ara* (postpone, root Axr, 15 verb occurrences): God *defers* punishment ([14:42] *yuʾakhkhiruhum li-yawmin tashkhaṣu fīhi al-abṣār*), *delays* the sentence ([16:61], [35:45], [11:104], [71:4]). The verb is intransitive on the divine side: God's action stretches creature-time, but God is not himself stretched.
- *Ajl* — every *ajal* is *appointed* by Allah (*ajalun musamman*, "a specified term," ~21 occurrences). No one delays it when it comes ([7:34] *fa-idhā jāʾa ajaluhum lā yastaʾkhirūn sāʿatan wa-lā yastaqdimūn*). God sets the term; the term governs creatures, not God.
- *wqt* — *mawqūt* (timed): even prayer is *kitāban mawqūtan* ([4:103]), a "timed prescription." God timestamps the cult; God is not timestamped.

The grammar is consistent across all the creature-time roots: they take Allah as agent in **their verbal forms** (God appoints, defers, times) but never as subject in **their nominal/attributive forms** (God is not an *ajal*, a *waqt*, a *ḥīn*).

### 6.3 The category-error verses

Two verbs from the *xld* root capture the Quran's name for the **error of treating temporal things as eternal**:

- *axlada ilā l-arḍ* [7:176] — "he clung-as-if-eternal to the earth" (of Balaam, traditionally).
- *yaḥsibu anna mālahu axladahu* [104:3] — "he thinks his wealth has made-him-eternal."

The Quran has a verb for this error — *axlada* — and it is always negative. Treating the worldly as having khulūd is a sin of category-confusion. Likewise *abadan* in human mouths is **the rhetorical mark of the disbeliever's misplaced certainty** (the deceived garden owner at 18:35; the hypocrites at 48:12 and 59:11; cf. `concept-Abd.md`). The Quran has a name for the error of conflating the two registers, and it deploys the name often enough to make the category-distinction explicit.

### 6.4 Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ — the maximally compressed proof

[112] is the corpus's tightest divine-attribution. Four lines, no creature-time root:
- *qul huwa Allāhu Aḥad* — One (root AHd)
- *Allāhu al-Ṣamad* — Refuge/Eternal (root Smd)
- *lam yalid wa lam yūlad* — neither begets nor is begotten (root wld, twice negated)
- *wa lam yakun lahu kufuwan Aḥad* — none equal to Him (root kwn negated, root AHd)

The eternity is asserted entirely by **(a) divine-only roots (Smd), (b) negations of creature-properties (lam yalid, lam yūlad, lam yakun), and (c) uniqueness (Aḥad)**. There is no *al-Khālid*, no *al-Abadī*, no *al-Dāʾim* (perpetual). The Quran builds the most compressed creed without using any creature-time vocabulary. This is the **maximally clean instance** of the lexical apartheid.

### 6.5 The synthesis

Putting it together: the Quran's theology of time, as encoded in its lexical choices, is something like a **scriptural nominalism about time**. Time is not a transcendent metaphysical entity that God inhabits and creatures inhabit at different scales. Time is a **created medium** — appointed, partitioned, deferred, and ended by God — within which creatures abide, and outside which God is the bracketing First and Last. The vocabulary of creature-abiding (*xld, Abd, Hyn, Ajl, wqt, Amd*) is the vocabulary of *being-inside-the-medium*. The vocabulary of divine being (*qayyūm, bāqī, Hayy, Awl/Axr, Wāḥid, Aḥad, Ṣamad*) is the vocabulary of *being-the-bracket-and-uphold-of-the-medium*. The two cannot share predicates without category-error, and the Quran's grammar enforces this with mechanical consistency.

This is **scripturally embedded theology**: the doctrine is not stated as a thesis — it is enforced as a vocabulary discipline.

## 7. Negative Space — What Divine-Time Vocabulary Is Conspicuously Absent

The negative space is as load-bearing as the positive map.

### 7.1 No "infinite" — *lā nihāya, lā ḥadd*

The Quran has no abstract noun for "infinity." The root *nhy* (end, finality) is used of "ending" actions (*intahā*, "he ceased") and of *al-muntahā* ("the final goal," [53:14, 53:42]). The Quran does not say *Allāh lā nihāya lah* ("God has no end"). The negative is asserted differently: *al-Awwal wa-l-Ākhir* (He is the bracket), *bāqī* (He remains after perishing), *lā yamūt* (He does not die). **The absence of an abstract "infinity" noun is consistent with the broader nominalism**: the Quran refuses to substantivize the property; it predicates relations.

### 7.2 No "outside time" — *fawq al-zamān*

The Quran has no construction like "above time" or "outside time." The category of *zamān* (time as such) is post-Quranic philosophical Arabic; it does not appear in the corpus. **The Quran has no abstract noun for "time-as-such"**. It has many nouns for *modes* of time (yawm, ḥīn, ajal, waqt, dahr, ʿaṣr, amad), but no abstract container-time-noun that you could place God outside of. The architecture is *bracket-not-container*: God is the First and the Last, not the One Outside.

### 7.3 No divine "duration" attribute

There is no *Allāh dā'im* (Allah is perpetual), no *Allāh sarmadī* (everlasting; *sarmad* is post-Quranic), no *Allāh azalī* (pre-eternal). The Quran does not name God's eternity as a *duration-property* He has. It names it as:
- A **bracket-relation** (Awl/Axr)
- A **subsistence-mode** (Qayyūm)
- A **non-perishing-after-perishing** (Bāqī, with *lā yamūt*)
- A **non-derivativeness** (Ṣamad, *lam yalid wa lam yūlad*)
- A **uniqueness** (Wāḥid, Aḥad)

None of these is a "duration." The Quran's positive divine-time vocabulary is structurally **non-duratitive**. This is why creature-time roots cannot transfer: they are duration-words, and divine eternity is not a duration. It is a different *kind* of being.

### 7.4 No "before time" / "after time" creation language

The Quran does not say "God created time." It says "God created the heavens and the earth in six days" ([7:54], [10:3], [11:7], [25:59], [32:4], [50:38], [57:4]) — using *yawm* (day) as the measure of creation. The status of the *days* before the heavens and earth is unspecified; the question is not asked. The Quran does not explicitly answer the *aevum*-style question (does time begin with creation?). This is a real silence.

The closest the Quran comes is **76:1** itself: *hal atā ʿalā l-insāni ḥīnun min al-dahr lam yakun shayʾan madhkūran* — "Has there come upon man a stretch of time when he was not a thing mentioned?" Here *al-dahr* and *ḥīn* both name a time-stretch *before* the human existed. This implies time predates the human, but the verse is silent on whether time predates creation as a whole.

### 7.5 No oath by *al-dahr*

Already noted in §5. The Quran swears by virtually every other temporal phenomenon (*al-ʿaṣr, al-fajr, al-layl, al-ḍuḥā, al-shams, al-ʿādiyāt*) but refuses *al-dahr*. The oath-economy is doing theological work: God will not invoke as His witness the entity the deniers invoke as their destroyer.

### 7.6 No second-level eternity vocabulary for paradise

Paradise is *xulūd* (residence-eternity) and *ʿadn* (settled-abode); never *qayyūm*, *bāqī* (used relationally only), *ḥayy* (used predicatively only). The reward is real and unending, but it is **lexically sub-categorical to divine eternity**. Paradise inhabitants are *xālidīn* (abiders); they are not co-eternals with God. The vocabulary keeps the rank.

## 8. Rabbit Holes for Phase 3

1. **The Wajh-vocabulary as the residual-divine-attribute.** Both [28:88] and [55:27] use *wajh* (face) as the surviving locus when everything perishes. *Wajh Allāh* deserves a dedicated concept analysis — is the "face" the Quranic compromise vocabulary for "essence-of-God-that-remains" without committing to a duration-attribute? Does it map onto post-Quranic *dhāt*?

2. **The *yawm Allāh* / *yawm ʿinda rabbika* construction in detail.** Three verses (22:47, 32:5, 70:4) license a non-creature scale for divine "day." Are these the only loopholes? What is the precise grammatical move that makes them theologically safe? Does the structure *ʿinda rabbika* (with your Lord) generalize as a license for ascribing creature-vocabulary to God under qualification?

3. **The hadith qudsī "anā al-dahr" and the canonical resistance.** The Prophet is reported (Bukhārī, Muslim) to have transmitted *qāla Allāh: yuʾdhīnī ibn Ādam, yasubbu al-dahr, wa-anā al-dahr*. This identifies *al-dahr* with God — exactly what the Quran refuses to do. The post-Quranic tradition does work the Quran will not do; this is theologically fascinating. Map the relation between scriptural restraint and prophetic exposition.

4. **Are the divine-side roots themselves sub-partitioned?** *Qayyūm* appears 3x; *Aḥad* (divine sense) ~1x; *Ṣamad* 1x; *al-Awwal* 1x; *al-Ākhir* 1x; *Hayy* 5x; *Bāqī* 4x. **The most absolute divine-eternity vocabulary is staggeringly sparse** — about 16 verses total carry the entire weight. Compare to *xld* (87) and *Abd* (28). The corpus's economy of divine-time predication is itself a finding: God's eternity is asserted **rarely and densely**, not frequently and dispersively.

5. **The kfA root in [112:4]**, *lam yakun lahu kufuwan aḥad*. *Kufuw* (equal, equivalent) appears only here — another hapax in the divine-attribution layer. Mapping all hapax-divine-attribution roots (*Smd*, *kfA*, possibly others) might reveal a "frozen-divine-attribute" pattern: the rarest words are reserved for the most absolute predications.

6. **The relation between unity (*Aḥad/Wāḥid*) and non-temporality.** The argument hinted at in §3.4 — that oneness vocabulary partly does the work of denying temporal-decomposition — needs to be tested. Survey the *Wāḥid* + *al-Qahhār* formula (the One, the Prevailing) at [12:39, 13:16, 14:48, 38:65, 39:4, 40:16]: does *al-Qahhār* (the Subduer/Prevailing) carry temporal-superiority sense? Is "He overcomes all" a way of saying "He outlasts all opposition through time"?

7. **The Iblis-and-the-axladtanī verse [17:62]**: *la-in axxartanī ilā yawmi l-qiyāmati* — "if you defer me until the Day of Resurrection." Iblis explicitly **asks God for an ajal-extension** that lasts until eschatology. This is a unique case where a creature requests divine-time-action (deferral) for a creature-eternity-relevant purpose. The grammar of this exchange — creature-side *axxartanī* + divine-side response — would be worth a single-verse analysis.

8. **Cross-tradition comparison: Aristotelian *aevum* vs Quranic bracketing.** Western scholastic theology developed the category of *aevum* — a permanent duration neither time nor eternity — to handle creatures with eternal soul-life. The Quran has no aevum. The xālid in paradise is not in aevum; he is in *xulūd*, a duration-word. The divine-side is not duration at all; it is bracketing. This is a sharp contrastive theological framework worth developing.

9. **The "save what your Lord wills" exceptions in *xld*.** [6:128], [11:107], [11:108] introduce *illā mā shāʾa rabbuk* into the eternity-formula. The exception clause is a **creature-eternity reservation**: even khulūd is bracketed by divine will. There is no equivalent reservation in the divine-side vocabulary — God's *bqy* is never *illā mā shāʾa*. This asymmetry confirms the apartheid: creature-eternity is sovereign-conditional; divine eternity is not. (Detail in `concept-xld.md`.)

10. **Survey of **why no Yā Bāqī, Yā Qayyūm-style vocative invocations in the Quran itself.** The 99 names tradition (*al-Asmāʾ al-Ḥusnā*) crystallizes later; the Quran names God by attributes in declarative form ([57:3] *huwa al-awwal...*) and in formula ([2:255] *Allāhu... al-Ḥayy al-Qayyūm*) but not in vocative invocation. The divine-time vocabulary is **descriptive, not invocational** in the Quran. This is itself a finding about how the corpus uses these names.

## Methodology Note

This investigation extended the Phase-1 single-root analyses (`concept-xld.md`, `concept-Abd.md`, `concept-Awl.md`, `concept-Axr.md`, `concept-Hyn.md`, `concept-Ajl.md`, `concept-dhr.md`, `concept-ywm.md`) by intersecting their findings against the divine-side roots not yet given full single-root treatment (*qwm/qayyūm*, *bqy*, *Hyy*, *wHd*, *AHd*, *Smd*). The verification was done by:

1. Inspecting `data/concepts/{root}.json` for lemma tables and per-verse occurrence records.
2. Filtering each occurrence by translation context to identify subject (Allah vs creature) — manually for the small divine-vocabulary roots where every occurrence could be read.
3. Cross-checking key verses ([28:88], [55:26–27], [16:96], [28:60], [42:36], [87:16–17], [112:1–4]) against `data/morphology/words.jsonl` to confirm root-level assignments.
4. Triangulating with the Phase-1 reports' own negative-space sections (especially `concept-xld.md` lines 215–227, which first articulated the apartheid).

The lexical-apartheid hypothesis from Phase 1 is **confirmed** for the strict creature-bound roots (xld, Abd, Hyn, Ajl, wqt, Amd, ESr) and **refined** for the shared-but-partitioned roots (Awl/Axr, ywm, bqy, Hyy, wHd, AHd). The refinement: divine-side vocabulary is not just non-creaturely — it is **structurally relational/contrastive** (bqy needs a foil, Awl/Axr brackets a series, Hayy is qualified by *lā yamūt*). The apartheid is not a wall; it is a **graded grammar** with three classes (pure creature, pure divine, shared-but-partitioned).
