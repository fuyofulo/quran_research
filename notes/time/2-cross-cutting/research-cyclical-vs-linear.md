# Cyclical vs Linear: The Quran's Two Temporal Modes and Where They Touch

## Headline Finding: Two Temporalities, One Corpus, No Synthesis

The Quran operates two distinct temporal architectures simultaneously, and refuses to merge them. **Cyclical time** — the diurnal alternation, the lunar months, the "constancy" merism of *bi-l-ghuduwwi wa-l-āṣāl* — is the time of *creation*, of *signs*, of *recurring witness*. **Linear time** — *yawm al-qiyāma*, *ajalun musamman*, *al-ākhira*, *al-ūlā* — is the time of *history*, of *appointment*, of *terminal direction*. These are not stages, and they are not synthesised. They run in parallel. The cycle never folds itself into a kalpa-style cosmic respiration; the line never bends back into a return. **The cycle is what the Quran asks you to KNOW; the line is what the Quran asks you to DECIDE about.** Knowledge is anchored in repeating sign; decision is oriented toward unrepeatable end. The two modes exist for different rhetorical work.

This file maps that division. It tabulates which roots belong to which mode, examines the small set of boundary verses where the two touch, traces the eschatological collapse of the cycle into the line at [81:1–3] and [75:8–9], analyses the "alternation" formula as a theological argument distinct from "appointed term" arguments, and asks why the Quran refuses to extend its cyclical structure into eschatology. The closing section sketches the philosophical implication for Quranic time, comparing briefly to Stoic, Hebrew Biblical, Hindu/Buddhist, and modern-scientific counterparts.

## Part 1 — Mapping the Two Modes

### The Cyclical Inventory

Roots whose *primary* semantic work is naming a recurring frame within nature:

| Root | Occ | Lemma anchor | What recurs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **lyl** (ليل) | 92 | *layl* / *laylah* | the night phase, returning daily |
| **nhr** (نهر, *nahār* lemma only) | 57 | *nahār* | the daytime phase, returning daily |
| **SbH** (صبح) | 45 | *aṣbaḥa* / *ṣubḥ* | morning becoming, recurring at dawn |
| **fjr** (فجر) | 24 (6 are *fajr*) | *fajr* | dawn-as-rupture, splitting nightly |
| **gdw** (غدو) | 16 | *ghuduww* / *ghadāh* | morning-going, paired with evening-return |
| **$hr** (شهر) | 21 | *shahr* | the lunar month, sighted ~12× per year |
| **shams** (شمس) | 33 | *shams* | the sun (in *manāzil* / sign formulas) |
| **qmr** (قمر) | 27 | *qamar* | the moon (in *manāzil* / sign formulas) |

The cluster totals 315 occurrences across the diurnal, lunar, and luminary vocabulary. **Every one of these roots names a phenomenon that returns.** Night returns. Day returns. The morning splits the dark every twenty-four hours. The crescent rises and is sighted every twenty-nine or thirty days. The sun and moon traverse the *manāzil* and re-enter their stations.

A second-tier set names the cycle's *bracketing* terms:
- *al-ʿashiyy* / *al-āṣāl* — the late afternoon, partner of *ghuduww*
- *al-ḍuḥā* — the forenoon
- *al-ʿaṣr* — the late afternoon
- *masāʾ* — evening
- *bukrah* — early morning

These do not generate dedicated concept analyses in this corpus because each appears fewer than ten times, but their syntactic role is identical: they sit inside the daily merism that brackets human worship.

### The Linear Inventory

Roots whose primary semantic work is naming an *endpoint* or *direction* in non-recurring time:

| Root | Occ | Anchor | What it points at |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ywm** (يوم) | 405 | *yawm al-qiyāma* / *yawm al-dīn* / *yawm al-ākhir* | the unrepeatable Day |
| **Axr** (اخر) | 250 | *al-ākhira* | the Hereafter pole |
| **Awl** (اول) | 170 | *al-ūlā* | the First life (paired against Axr) |
| **Ajl** (اجل) | 56 | *ajalun musamman* | the appointed endpoint |
| **xld** (خلد) | 87 | *khālidīn* | eternal post-judgment dwelling |
| **Abd** (ابد) | 28 | *abadan* | endlessly forward |
| **wqt** (وقت) | 13 | *mīqāt* / *mawqūt* | the appointed instant |
| **Amd** (امد) | 4 | *amad baʿīd* / *amad qarīb* | the stretch toward the end |
| **swE** (سوع) | 49 | *al-sāʿah* | the Hour (singular eschatological event) |
| **qrn** (قرن) | 36 | *qarn* | the past generation now gone |

The linear cluster totals 1098 occurrences and is dominated by *yawm* (405) and *Axr* (250) — by themselves over half. **Every one of these roots names a phenomenon that does not return.** A *qarn* once erased does not return. An *ajal* once reached does not delay. *Al-ākhira* is by definition "the Last." *Al-ūlā* (the First life) is by definition non-repeatable: you do not get a second *dunyā*.

### Bridges, Not Merges

A small set of roots straddle the two modes:

- **ywm** itself is the deepest bridge. It carries (a) eschatological singular *yawm al-X*; (b) creation's six *ayyām*; (c) *ayyām maʿdūdāt* (counted ritual days) which are *cyclical* (Ramaḍān, Ḥajj). The same root holds linear and cyclical work — but the Quran *grammatically separates them*: the singular construct does linear work; the plural *ayyām* does cyclical/legal work. (See `concept-ywm.md` for the singular/plural split.)

- **shahr** ($hr) sits in both modes. *Shahr Ramaḍān* is a recurring lunar month (cyclical), but at [2:185] it is *the* month in which the Qurʾān was sent down (a linear revelation event). The dual-loading at [2:185] is the most concentrated boundary verse in the corpus.

- **shams** and **qmr** sit in both modes. They are cyclical when they name the *manāzil* (10:5, 13:2, 31:29, 35:13, 39:5 — all four "running for a stated term" verses), but linear when they appear in eschatology: the sun is *folded up* (*kuwwirat*) at 81:1; the moon is *split* (*inshaqqa*) at 54:1; sun and moon are *joined* (*jumiʿa*) at 75:9. Sun and moon are uniquely *cosmic-cycle objects with appointed terminations* — and the Quran exploits exactly this duality at the apocalypse.

- **fjr** straddles in a different way. The dawn-noun *fajr* is cyclical; the verb *fajara* (to burst through) is applied to apocalyptic seas at 82:3 — a linear, terminal event. Same root, same image of rupture, but morning-rupture recurs while sea-rupture happens once.

- **SbH** is overwhelmingly cyclical (the morning) but its dominant deployment is the **destruction-formula** *fa-aṣbaḥū fī diyārihim jāthimīn* ([7:78], [11:67], [11:94], [29:37]) — where the recurring time-of-morning becomes the moment a *unique, terminal* judgment becomes visible. The cycle delivers the verdict, but the verdict is linear.

These bridge roots are not contradictions. They are the Quran's way of *anchoring the line in the cycle*. The Day of Judgment will, in some sense, be a *day*. The destruction of Thamūd took place on a *morning*. The revelation of the Qurʾān happened in a *month*. The cycle frames the line, but does not dissolve it.

## Part 2 — Cycle as Sign vs Line as Destination: The Functional Split

The hypothesis: *cyclical time is what GROUNDS Quranic theology — the alternation of night and day is the most-repeated SIGN. Linear time is what ORIENTS Quranic theology — the Day of Judgment is the most-repeated DESTINATION.*

The data confirms this with unusual clarity.

### Cyclical Roots Travel With "Sign" Vocabulary

The cyclical sign-clauses cluster around a small set of words: *āyāt* (signs), *li-qawmin yaʿqilūn* (for a people who reason), *li-ūlī al-albāb* (for those of understanding), *li-qawmin yatafakkarūn* (for a people who reflect), *li-tabtaghū faḍlan min rabbikum wa li-taʿlamū* (that you may seek bounty and may know).

The signature formula:

> *إِنَّ فِي خَلْقِ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَٱخْتِلَٰفِ ٱلَّيْلِ وَٱلنَّهَارِ لَأٓيَٰتٍ لِّأُوْلِي ٱلْأَلْبَٰبِ* — [3:190]
>
> "Indeed in the creation of the heavens and the earth and the alternation of night and day are signs for those of understanding."

Compare:
- [2:164] — same alternation, "signs for a people who reason."
- [10:6] — alternation of night and day → "signs for a people mindful of God."
- [23:80] — He alternates night and day → "do you not then reason?"
- [45:5] — alternation → "signs for a people who reason."
- [24:44] — *yuqallibu Allāhu al-layla wa al-nahāra* → "lesson for those of vision."

The argument structure is invariant: **cycle is presented as evidence; the audience is presented as those-who-think.** The cycle is for cognition. It is offered to the intellect.

The same is true at [10:5]:

> *هُوَ ٱلَّذِى جَعَلَ ٱلشَّمْسَ ضِيَآءً وَٱلْقَمَرَ نُورًا وَقَدَّرَهُۥ مَنَازِلَ لِتَعْلَمُوا۟ عَدَدَ ٱلسِّنِينَ وَٱلْحِسَابَ ۚ مَا خَلَقَ ٱللَّهُ ذَٰلِكَ إِلَّا بِٱلْحَقِّ ۚ يُفَصِّلُ ٱلْءَايَٰتِ لِقَوْمٍ يَعْلَمُونَ*

The sun and moon are *manāzil* (stations) so that you may *know* — *li-taʿlamū*. The verse closes: *yufaṣṣilu al-āyāti li-qawmin yaʿlamūn* — He details the signs for a people who know. **The lunar cycle is here teleologised explicitly toward knowing.** And the same is reasserted at [17:12], where the day-sign and night-sign are made *that you may know the number of the years and the reckoning*.

### Linear Roots Travel With "Decide" Vocabulary

The linear roots cluster instead around: *amana / āmana* (believe), *ʿamila ṣāliḥan* (do righteous deeds), *ittaqā* (be mindful), *kafara* (disbelieve), *ḥāsabā* (account), *ʿuqbā* (outcome), *najā* (be saved).

The signature formula:

> *مَنۢ ءَامَنَ بِٱللَّهِ وَٱلْيَوْمِ ٱلْءَاخِرِ* — repeated 23+ times
>
> "Whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day..."

This is not an argument; it is a credal demand. The Last Day is not offered as evidence; it is the criterion by which a community is sorted. *Belief* and *deed* are the verbs that attach to it. **Linear time is for decision.**

Compare the deadline formula [7:34] / [10:49] / [16:61]:

> *وَلِكُلِّ أُمَّةٍ أَجَلٌ ۖ فَإِذَا جَآءَ أَجَلُهُمْ لَا يَسْتَأْخِرُونَ سَاعَةً وَلَا يَسْتَقْدِمُونَ*

"And for every nation is a stated term; when their term comes, they cannot delay it an hour nor advance it."

This is a structurally different argument from the cycle-sign formula. The cycle says: *look at this regularity → recognise the designer*. The deadline says: *this terminal point is fixed → adjust your life accordingly*. The first is epistemic; the second is ethical-existential.

### The Functional Split, Crystallised

| Mode | Rhetorical work | Audience verb | Quranic frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cyclical (lyl, nhr, SbH, fjr, gdw, $hr, shams, qmr) | **Sign / evidence** | yaʿqilūn, yatafakkarūn, yaʿlamūn, yubṣirūn | "alternation," "We made," "He decreed *manāzil*" |
| Linear (ywm, Axr, Ajl, swE, xld, Abd, qrn) | **Destination / verdict** | āmana, ʿamila, ittaqā, kafara | "Day of," "their term comes," "for those who" |

The cycle argues *for* God; the line argues *toward* God. The cycle is what a thoughtful person notices; the line is what a thoughtful person prepares for. **One is for inference, the other for orientation.** The Quran needs both.

## Part 3 — Where the Modes Touch: Boundary Verses

The two modes coexist most of the time in different verses. But there is a small, theologically dense set where they appear in the same ayah or passage. These are the boundary verses — and they reveal exactly *how* the Quran connects the cyclical to the linear without merging them.

### [10:5] — Sun and Moon → Knowing the Count of Years and Accounting

> *هُوَ ٱلَّذِى جَعَلَ ٱلشَّمْسَ ضِيَآءً وَٱلْقَمَرَ نُورًا وَقَدَّرَهُۥ مَنَازِلَ لِتَعْلَمُوا۟ عَدَدَ ٱلسِّنِينَ وَٱلْحِسَابَ*

The cyclical machinery (sun's radiance, moon's stations) is given a teleology: *li-taʿlamū ʿadada al-sinīna wa al-ḥisāb* — "that you may know the number of the years and the reckoning." Note the second word: *al-ḥisāb*. This is the same root that the eschatological vocabulary uses at *yawm al-ḥisāb* (the Day of Reckoning), at [14:41], [38:16], [38:26], [40:17]. **The Quran takes a cyclical infrastructure (lunar months, solar light) and ties it to a noun (*ḥisāb*) that is also the central verb of the linear endpoint.** The cycle is for *learning to count*; what you learn to count is what will eventually be *counted on you*.

This is the closest the Quran comes to a synthesis — and it is not a synthesis but a *bridge*. The cycle teaches the human capacity for accounting; the line is where the accounting will be applied. The two modes share a word (*ḥisāb*) without sharing an ontology.

### [17:12] — The Two Signs and the Calendrical Teleology

> *وَجَعَلْنَا ٱلَّيْلَ وَٱلنَّهَارَ ءَايَتَيْنِ ۖ فَمَحَوْنَآ ءَايَةَ ٱلَّيْلِ وَجَعَلْنَآ ءَايَةَ ٱلنَّهَارِ مُبْصِرَةً لِّتَبْتَغُوا۟ فَضْلًا مِّن رَّبِّكُمْ وَلِتَعْلَمُوا۟ عَدَدَ ٱلسِّنِينَ وَٱلْحِسَابَ*

The day and night are explicitly named as *two signs* (*āyatayn*). Their teleology is dual: (1) *li-tabtaghū faḍlan* — that you may seek bounty (economic life within the day-cycle); (2) *li-taʿlamū ʿadada al-sinīna wa al-ḥisāb* — that you may know the number of years and the reckoning. The cycle is presented as enabling both *worldly economy* and *the cognitive apparatus that prepares for the Reckoning*. Once again: the cycle teaches; the line judges.

### [17:78] — Prayer Time Bound to Sun Position

> *أَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ لِدُلُوكِ ٱلشَّمْسِ إِلَىٰ غَسَقِ ٱلَّيْلِ وَقُرْءَانَ ٱلْفَجْرِ ۖ إِنَّ قُرْءَانَ ٱلْفَجْرِ كَانَ مَشْهُودًا*

The prescribed ritual is *bound to* cyclical time markers: *dulūk al-shams* (the decline of the sun), *ghasaq al-layl* (the darkening of the night), *al-fajr* (dawn). The line — the believer's life of obedience oriented toward judgment — is calibrated against the cycle. **Devotion is hung on the sun's geometry.** This is the boundary verse in its most compact ritual form: linear obligation (prayer) is structured by cyclical time (sun position).

### [2:185] — Shahr Ramaḍān (Lunar Month) → Anzala (Linear Revelation Event)

> *شَهْرُ رَمَضَانَ ٱلَّذِىٓ أُنزِلَ فِيهِ ٱلْقُرْءَانُ هُدًى لِّلنَّاسِ وَبَيِّنَٰتٍ مِّنَ ٱلْهُدَىٰ وَٱلْفُرْقَانِ*

The single proper-named month in the entire Quran (*shahr Ramaḍān* — cyclical, recurring lunar unit) is fused to *the* unique revelation event (*anzala fīhi al-Qurʾān* — linear, unrepeatable). **The lunar cycle is named only once, and it is named precisely because it contained a linear event.** The cycle becomes the address; the line is what was delivered to that address. This is one of the densest boundary verses in the corpus.

The pairing has a parallel at [97:1] — *innā anzalnāhu fī laylati al-qadr* — where the linear act of revelation is located in a *night* (cyclical-frame) which is then declared *better than a thousand months* (the cycle is invoked only to be hyperbolically transcended). The Night of Power is a cyclical *laylah* that contains the linear seed of all subsequent history. **Cycle hosts line; line ennobles cycle.**

### [6:96] — God Splits the Dawn → Made Night for Rest

> *فَالِقُ ٱلْإِصْبَاحِ وَجَعَلَ ٱلَّيْلَ سَكَنًا وَٱلشَّمْسَ وَٱلْقَمَرَ حُسْبَانًا*

A single verse holds: *cleaver of the daybreak* (cyclical action), *made the night a rest* (cyclical mercy), *sun and moon as a reckoning* (*ḥusbānā* — a calculation, a count). Once again the cycle is anchored to a counting-noun. **Three cyclical acts, all tied to enumeration.** The implication: the cycle is not just a recurrence; it is a *tabulation*. And tabulation is the cognitive precondition of the eventual *ḥisāb*.

### [13:2] / [31:29] / [35:13] / [39:5] — Sun and Moon Run *li-Ajalin Musamman*

This is the most dramatic boundary set. Four near-identical verses say:

> *سَخَّرَ ٱلشَّمْسَ وَٱلْقَمَرَ ۖ كُلٌّ يَجْرِى لِأَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى*

"He subjected the sun and the moon, each running for a stated term."

Sun and moon — the central *cyclical* time-keepers — are explicitly inscribed inside the *linear* deadline-vocabulary. Each runs *li-ajalin musamman*: each has an appointed expiry. **The cycle has been given a deadline.** The Quran does not preserve cyclical time as autonomous; it places the entire cyclical machinery inside the linear horizon of *ajal*. The sun's daily course and its terminal eschatological shutdown are, grammatically, the *same kind of fact*: both are *jarayān li-ajalin musamman*.

This is the deepest theological move at the boundary. The Quran does not just acknowledge the cycle; it *subordinates* it. The cycle continues, but only until its *ajal* arrives. And when it does, [81:1] happens: *idhā al-shamsu kuwwirat* — when the sun is folded up. The very luminary that has been "running for a stated term" *reaches* its term.

### Boundary verse synthesis

The boundary verses are not arbitrary. They cluster around three operations:
1. **The cycle is given a calendrical-cognitive teleology** (10:5, 17:12, 6:96) — its purpose is to teach *ḥisāb*.
2. **The cycle is the address for a linear event** (2:185, 97:1) — the month/night holds the revelation.
3. **The cycle is itself given an *ajal*** (13:2, 31:29, 35:13, 39:5) — the recurring is interior to the appointed.

The two modes touch but do not fuse. The cycle is *placed inside* the line; it is not *equivalent* to it. **The cycle is the embedded clock; the line is the containing direction.**

## Part 4 — The Eschatological Collapse of the Cycle

When the line reaches its end, the cycle ends with it. The Quran's late-Meccan apocalyptic poetry returns repeatedly to a single image: **at the eschaton, the cyclical machinery breaks**.

### [81:1–3] — Sūrat al-Takwīr's Opening

> *إِذَا ٱلشَّمْسُ كُوِّرَتْ . وَإِذَا ٱلنُّجُومُ ٱنكَدَرَتْ . وَإِذَا ٱلْجِبَالُ سُيِّرَتْ*

"When the sun is folded up. And when the stars fall, darkening. And when the mountains are set in motion."

The verb *kuwwirat* ("folded up," "wound around itself") is the only Quranic deployment of a verb-of-rolling-up applied to the sun. The sun — the cycle's anchor luminary — is *terminated by being collapsed in on itself*. Then *al-nujūm inkadarat* — the stars (the *maṣābīḥ* of [41:12], [67:5], the morning-instruments) fall and darken. The lights go out. **The cyclical infrastructure of light-and-time is dismantled.**

The chain continues: 81:4 the pregnant camels are abandoned; 81:5 the wild beasts are gathered; 81:6 the seas blaze. The whole created order is unmade — but it begins with the sun, the *daily* sign, being switched off.

### [75:8–9] — Sun and Moon Joined

> *وَخَسَفَ ٱلْقَمَرُ . وَجُمِعَ ٱلشَّمْسُ وَٱلْقَمَرُ*

"And the moon is eclipsed. And the sun and the moon are joined."

This is the only place in the corpus where the sun and the moon are *united*. Throughout the cycle, they are *separate* — the sun for radiance (*ḍiyāʾ*), the moon for light (*nūr*) and for the *manāzil* that count the months. The Quran preserves the distinction with utter consistency. But at the eschaton, the distinction collapses: *jumiʿa al-shamsu wa al-qamar*. **The dual structure of the cycle (sun-day / moon-month) becomes a single object.** Time-keeping ends because the time-keepers fuse.

### [54:1] — The Moon Is Split

> *ٱقْتَرَبَتِ ٱلسَّاعَةُ وَٱنشَقَّ ٱلْقَمَرُ*

"The Hour has drawn near, and the moon has split." The eschatological *sāʿah* (the Hour — pure linear vocabulary) is announced *together with* a rupture of the moon. The cycle's most reliable counter (the lunar crescent that constitutes *shahr*) is broken. **The single noun for the linear endpoint (*sāʿah*) and the central cyclical signifier (*qamar*) appear in the same ayah, and the verb on the moon is destruction.**

### [82:1–4] — Sūrat al-Infiṭār

> *إِذَا ٱلسَّمَآءُ ٱنفَطَرَتْ . وَإِذَا ٱلْكَوَاكِبُ ٱنتَثَرَتْ . وَإِذَا ٱلْبِحَارُ فُجِّرَتْ . وَإِذَا ٱلْقُبُورُ بُعْثِرَتْ*

"When the sky is split open. And when the stars are scattered. And when the seas are made to burst forth. And when the graves are overturned."

The cycle's structural ceiling (*samāʾ*) is split; the *kawākib* (the lights that organise the night sky into navigation and time-keeping) are scattered; the seas (the *fajr*-verb is now applied apocalyptically) burst. **Every cyclical infrastructure — sky, stars, seas — is unmade in a four-verse cascade.**

### [99:1–3], [56:1–6], [69:13–17], [78:18–20]

The pattern repeats across late-Meccan apocalyptic poetry:
- [99:1–3] — the earth is shaken with its convulsion, the earth throws out its burdens. (The ground itself, the substrate of cyclical experience, breaks.)
- [56:1–6] — the inevitable Event occurs; mountains crumble to dust.
- [69:13–17] — when the trumpet is blown a single blast; the earth and the mountains are lifted and crushed.
- [78:18–20] — the trumpet is blown; you come in throngs; the sky is opened; the mountains are set in motion as if they were a mirage.

The constant elements: *the sun, the moon, the stars, the sky, the earth, the mountains, the seas* — every cyclical anchor — are *sequentially destroyed* before the linear event (the Day, the Reckoning) becomes operative. **The cycle is the platform; the platform is dismantled to make room for the verdict.**

### Rhetorical Work of the Collapse

Two observations:

1. **The collapse is described in the same poetic register as the oath-by-the-cycle**. *Wa al-shamsi wa ḍuḥāhā* (91:1) and *idhā al-shamsu kuwwirat* (81:1) are both late-Meccan, both short, both opening their respective surahs. The sun is sworn *by* in [91:1]; the sun is *folded* in [81:1]. **The same vocabulary that grounds the cycle as a witness is what dissolves at the end.** The witness's voice ceases; the trial begins.

2. **The collapse never inaugurates a new cycle.** This is critical. After [81:1–14], there is no [81:15] saying "and then a new sun rose." After [82:1–4], there is no resumption. **The cycle, once collapsed, stays collapsed.** This is the structural feature that makes the Quran's eschatology *linear* in the strict sense — and it is what most decisively distinguishes it from cyclic-cosmology traditions (see Part 8).

## Part 5 — The "Alternation" Formula as Theological Argument

The Quran's most repeated cycle-as-evidence formula is *ikhtilāf al-layli wa al-nahār* ("the alternation of night and day"). It appears at:

- [2:164] — "and the alternation of night and day are signs for a people who reason."
- [3:190] — "signs for those of understanding."
- [10:6] — "signs for a people mindful of God."
- [23:80] — "do you not then reason?"
- [45:5] — "signs for a people who reason."
- [24:44] — *yuqallibu Allāhu al-layla wa al-nahāra* (a verbal variant — "He alternates the night and the day") — "lesson for those of vision."

The formula is structurally invariant: *cyclical phenomenon + presented as āyāt + addressed to the cognitive faculties of those who think*. The argument has the form of a teleological inference from regularity to design.

Compare this with the **linear arguments**, which are structurally different:

The deadline formulas [7:34] / [10:49] / [16:61] do not argue from evidence; they *assert* a fact and prescribe a posture. *Wa li-kulli ummatin ajalun* — "for every nation is a term." There is no addressee-clause about reasoning, no invocation of *ūlū al-albāb*. The hearer is positioned as one who must accept and orient, not as one who must consider and infer.

Similarly, the credal formulas attached to *al-yawm al-ākhir* — *yu'minūn bi-llāhi wa al-yawm al-ākhir* — do not open with "in this is a sign." They open with the question of whether one *believes*. **Linear arguments do not argue; they demand.**

This is the second-deepest theological asymmetry in the Quran's time vocabulary:

| Argument type | Method | Audience |
|---|---|---|
| Cyclical (sign-from-recurrence) | inferential, from regularity to designer | *li-qawmin yaʿqilūn / yatafakkarūn / yaʿlamūn* |
| Linear (deadline / Hereafter / appointed term) | declarative, from authority to addressee | *yu'minūn / yattaqūn / yaʿmalūn ṣāliḥan* |

The Quran does not collapse these into a single mode. It uses cycle to argue *for* God's existence and design; it uses line to demand a *response* to that God. The two are complementary rhetorical engines.

A subsidiary observation: **the Arabic word *ikhtilāf* itself is interesting**. It carries both "alternation" (cyclical succession) and "difference" (contrast). The Quran exploits the ambiguity. The phenomenon is offered both as a *recurrence* (the night returns, the day returns) and as a *contrast* (the night differs from the day). Either reading sustains the inferential argument. (Translators split: Saheeh and Khattab use "alternation"; Pickthall uses "difference"; Arberry uses "alternation.")

## Part 6 — Negative Space Across Both Modes

What is missing from the Quran's temporal architecture? The absences are themselves diagnostic.

### Missing in the Cyclical Mode

1. **No four-seasons concept.** The Quran has *al-shitāʾ wa al-ṣayf* ("winter and summer," at [106:2]) — but only as the two journeys of the Quraysh, not as a cosmic-seasonal cycle. There is no spring, no autumn, no equinox vocabulary, no harvest-cycle as theological frame. The Hebrew Bible has Genesis 8:22's *zeraʿ wa-qatzir wa-qor wa-ḥom* ("seedtime and harvest, cold and heat"); Greek has the *hōrai*. The Quran has neither. **The Quran's cycle is the diurnal and the lunar; it does not extend to the seasonal.**

   Why? Hypothesis: because the Quran inherited a lunar (not solar) calendar, the seasonal cycle drifts every year by ~11 days relative to the lunar month. Months in the Quran are deliberately *decoupled* from seasons (cf. the explicit prohibition of *nasīʾ* — intercalary insertion — at [9:37]). The lunar calendar is a feature, not a bug. But it has the corollary that *seasons cannot be theologised*: a "month of harvest" cannot exist if months drift through harvests. The Quran's refusal to develop a seasonal vocabulary is therefore *internally consistent* with its lunar commitment.

2. **No precise day-hours.** No noon, no afternoon as a distinct phase, no equivalent of the Babylonian/Greek hour-system. *Sāʿah* exists but almost always means *the* Hour (eschatological). The day is bracketed by *fajr* / *ṣubḥ* (morning) and *maghrib* / *ʿishāʾ* (evening) — these are *edges*, not subdivisions of the daytime body. (See `concept-nhr.md` for the negative-space inventory.)

3. **No solar year.** Years exist as a count (*sinīn*, *sanah*, *ʿām*) but are not anchored to a solar return. *Sinīn* is built up from *shahr* (lunar month) without being independently named. (See `concept-$hr.md`.)

4. **No cyclical ritual mirroring the cycle's structure**. There is no *daily Eucharist* equivalent, no *full-moon festival*. The Quran prescribes prayer at sun-positions (17:78), fasting in *one* month (Ramaḍān), pilgrimage in *known months* — but these rituals are *legal-temporal calibrations*, not cycle-mirroring rites. The cycle is observed; it is not enacted.

### Missing in the Linear Mode

1. **No cyclical eschatology.** This is the most theologically decisive absence. There is no "and then the world begins again." There is no second Hour. There is no return from the Hereafter to a new earthly cycle. **The line wins, finally.** Once the *ajal* of the heavens and the earth ([30:8], [46:3]) is reached, the stated-term grammar terminates without resumption. *Khulūd* (eternal abiding, 87 occurrences) is the Quran's word for what comes *after* the Hereafter is established — and *xld* never names a *return*. It names an *unending state*. (See `concept-xld.md`.)

2. **No cosmic-cycle mythology.** The Quran knows the pre-Islamic Arabian *dahr* (eternal time, the impersonal devourer) and engages it with great hostility at [45:24]: *mā yuhlikunā illā al-dahru* — "nothing destroys us but Time" — is reported as a disbelievers' claim and rejected. *Dahr* appears only twice in the Quran ([45:24] and [76:1] *hal atā ʿalā al-insāni ḥīnun min al-dahri*). **The Quran refuses to grant *dahr* the explanatory work it had in pre-Islamic poetry.** Time is not the agent; God is.

3. **No second life of the cosmos.** [21:104] describes the heavens being *rolled up like a scroll for writings*; the implication is termination, not recycling. The verse continues: *kamā badaʾnā awwala khalqin nuʿīduhu* — "as We began the first creation, We will repeat it." But "repeat" here refers to the resurrection of *humanity*, not a re-instantiation of the cycle. (See `concept-Awl.md` for the *ūlā / ākhira* pair.) The cosmos itself is not resurrected; only the soul-bodies are. **The cycle of the world is never repeated; only the persons within it are remade.**

### Missing Across Both Modes

1. **No abstract noun for "time."** The Quran has *no equivalent* of Greek *chronos* / English "time" / Hindi *kāla*. *Dahr* and *waqt* are the closest, but neither functions as a generic time-noun. Time is always *specified*: it is *al-yawm*, *al-ʿaṣr* (the period — and only at [103:1] in this generic sense), *ḥīn*, *ajal*, *sāʿah*. **The Quran refuses to abstract time into a neutral noun.** Time is always relational — to a day, an event, an appointment.

2. **No "eternal recurrence" as either threat or promise**. The Stoic-Nietzschean horror of *eternal return* has no Quranic counterpart. Whether the world will repeat is not a *question* in the Quran; the answer is presupposed-no.

## Part 7 — Why the Two Modes Don't Merge

The negative space points to a single underlying theological commitment: **the cycle is created, contingent, and bounded; the line is the truth of created things relative to their Creator.**

The cycle is repeatedly named as *jaʿalnā* (We made), *sakhkhara* (He subjected), *yujallihā / yaghshāhā* (He displays / covers it). It is not eternal; it does not generate itself; it does not persist by its own nature. And precisely because it is *made*, it is *boundable*. Anything God made, God can fold up. The cycle's contingency is what makes [81:1]'s *kuwwirat* possible.

The line, by contrast, is the structure of the relationship between created things and their Creator. The line is the *time-shape of contingency itself*: a thing is created, runs for its appointed term, and arrives at its end. **The line is not a *thing* God made; it is the *grammatical form* of made-ness.** That is why every concept in the linear cluster — *ajal*, *yawm al-qiyāma*, *al-ākhira* — is presented in the passive participle ("*musamman* — named [by Him]"), with the agent unstated and the action divine. (See `concept-Ajl.md`, "Co-occurrence with Passive Participles.")

This reading makes the Quran's refusal to merge the modes intelligible. **A merger would require the cycle to be the line**, which would mean either (a) the line repeats — eternal recurrence — which would deny the unique authority of the eschaton, or (b) the cycle does not terminate — eternalist cosmology — which would deny the cycle's contingency. The Quran refuses both, and so refuses the merger.

The result is a theology with two temporal modes that *must coexist without synthesis*, because the synthesis would be heretical from either direction.

## Part 8 — The Implication for Quranic Philosophy of Time

The Quranic structure of time can now be stated philosophically:

> **There are two modes of time. The cycle is the time of created things considered as recurring witnesses to their Creator. The line is the time of created things considered as bounded subjects approaching their judgment. Every soul lives in both at once. The cycle gives the soul its evidence; the line gives the soul its task.**

This is structurally distinct from the four major comparison points:

### Compared to Greek/Stoic Cyclical Time

The Stoics held that the cosmos is periodically destroyed by *ekpurōsis* (conflagration) and reborn identically — the *eternal return*. The cycle *is* the line; the line *is* the cycle, infinitely. Nietzsche revives this in *The Gay Science* §341. **The Quran rejects this in the strongest possible way.** *Ḥusbān* and *ḥisāb* are inseparable from *jazāʾ* (recompense) and *yawm al-dīn*; reckoning is irreversible. There is no *ekpurōsis* followed by re-creation of the same cosmos. The eschatological collapse of [81:1–14] is *unique*. Once the sun is *kuwwirat*, it does not rise again to be folded a second time.

### Compared to Hebrew Bible

The Hebrew Bible is structurally closer: a linear history (creation → exodus → exile → return → eschaton) framed by a cyclical liturgical calendar (Sabbath, new moon, festivals). Genesis 1's *ʿerev wa-boqer* ("evening and morning") gives the day its cycle; the Sabbath gives the week its rest; the year has its festivals at *moʿadim* (appointed times). The Hebrew structural map is *line + festival cycle*.

The Quranic difference: the Quran's cyclical liturgy is *thinner*. There is no Sabbath, no equivalent of the *moʿadim* spread across a solar-lunar year. The Quran's daily prayer (*ṣalāt*, hung on the sun) is the only consistent cyclical ritual. The annual fast (*ṣawm Ramaḍān*) is lunar and drifts. The pilgrimage (*Ḥajj*) is in known months but happens once for most Muslims. **The Quran has shifted weight from the cyclical-ritual to the daily-prayer plus the orientation toward *al-ākhira*.** It is closer to the Hebrew Bible than to any other tradition, but with a thinner cyclical-liturgical layer.

### Compared to Hindu/Buddhist Cosmology

Hindu cosmology has *yuga*-cycles (Krta, Tretā, Dvāpara, Kali) that repeat, and *kalpa*-cycles in which the cosmos is created and dissolved repeatedly across vast time-spans. Buddhist cosmology has *kalpa*-cycles likewise, with the further structural feature that *saṃsāra* (the cycle of rebirth) *is itself the problem* — release (*nirvāṇa*) is escape from cyclical time.

The Quran rejects *both* features. There is no *yuga*-cycle of cosmic decline-and-renewal; the human history that runs through the line is unrepeated. There is no *saṃsāra* problem — the human soul does not transmigrate; it lives once (*al-ūlā*), dies, and is judged once. **The Quranic line is *one* line, with a single beginning and a single terminus.** This is structurally closer to Christian eschatology than to Vedic.

### Compared to Modern Scientific Cosmology

A possibly accidental but suggestive parallel: modern physical cosmology operates with *both* a linear cosmology (Big Bang → expansion → heat death or Big Crunch — there is debate, but most contemporary models are linear) and *cyclical local processes* (entropy, the seasons, planetary orbits). The cycle is local; the line is global. **This is structurally similar to the Quranic two-mode arrangement.** The cycle gives the laws of regularity within which experience occurs; the line gives the cosmological direction within which the cycle is embedded.

The disanalogy: in modern cosmology, the line has no *judgment* at its end; it has only thermodynamic exhaustion. The Quran's line is *moral* in a way physical cosmology's line is not. But the dual structure is recognisably similar.

### The Quranic Distinctive

Putting all four comparisons together, the Quran's distinctive is: **two modes, no synthesis, with the line carrying moral weight and the cycle carrying epistemic weight.** This is not Stoic (which collapses line into cycle), not Hindu (which makes the cycle the soteriological problem), not naively biblical (which has a richer festival cycle), and not modern-physical (which depopulates the line of moral content). It is its own structure.

## Rabbit Holes for Phase 3

1. **The *ḥisāb* bridge.** The same Arabic root (*ḥasaba*) does *cyclical* counting (lunar months, days of life) and *linear* reckoning (Day of Reckoning). Build a complete inventory of every *ḥisāb / ḥasaba / muḥāsabah / aḥṣā* occurrence and trace the bridge. Hypothesis: *ḥisāb* is the single semantic node where cycle and line legitimately *do* synthesise — the cycle teaches counting, the line audits the count.

2. **Why no four-seasons concept?** The structural argument is that lunar drift makes seasonal-theology impossible. Test against pre-Islamic Arabian poetic vocabulary: did the *jāhiliyya* poets have a richer seasonal vocabulary that the Quran *suppressed*? If yes, the suppression is a deliberate theological move, not an inheritance.

3. **The sun and moon's *ajal*.** The four near-identical verses (13:2, 31:29, 35:13, 39:5) — *kullun yajrī li-ajalin musamman* — deserve their own analysis. Why exactly four? Is the repetition itself a sign-formula (cf. the Paradise-rivers formula's repetition)? What does *jarayān* ("running") mean for celestial bodies — is it the same root that does *anhār* (rivers, also "flowing")? If so, the cosmic-flow vocabulary is integrated.

4. **The collapse-of-the-cycle inventory.** Map every late-Meccan apocalypse passage (sūras 56, 69, 70, 75, 77, 78, 79, 81, 82, 84, 99, 100, 101) and tabulate which cyclical anchors are dismantled in each. Hypothesis: each apocalypse passage destroys a different *layer* of the cycle (sun-and-moon, stars, sky, mountains, seas, earth) and the full collapse-set across all passages dismantles the entire cosmological-cyclical inventory.

5. **The *ʿaṣr* cluster.** The root ʿṣr (49 occurrences in this corpus, see `concept-Esr.md`) — does it belong to the cyclical or the linear mode? *Al-ʿaṣr* (103:1) is the period; *ʿaṣaru-llāh* is the squeezing/pressing of time toward judgment. Investigate.

6. **Pre-Islamic *dahr* and the Quranic counter-move.** [45:24]'s *mā yuhlikunā illā al-dahr* and [76:1]'s *ḥīnun min al-dahr* are the only two *dahr* occurrences. Compare to the role of *dahr* in pre-Islamic poetry (the impersonal devourer of all things). The Quran's near-erasure of *dahr* is itself a major structural-theological claim worth a dedicated investigation.

7. **The Hebrew Bible parallel: comparative cycle-and-line.** A focused comparative study of Genesis 1's *ʿerev wa-boqer* (evening-and-morning, the daily cycle), the Sabbath cycle, the *moʿadim* festival cycle, against the Quran's thinner cyclical-liturgical layer plus its preserved *ikhtilāf al-layl wa al-nahār* sign-formula. Hypothesis: the Quran has *kept* the Hebrew Bible's cycle-as-sign and *thinned* its cycle-as-liturgy.

8. **Why does the Quran prefer night-counting?** The Moses 40-nights, Zechariah 3-nights, ʿĀd 7-nights pattern (see `concept-lyl.md`, rabbit hole 1). This may be evidence that *the night is the privileged element of the cycle*, possibly because it is the cycle's *negative* phase (less light, less cognition, more vulnerability) and thus the place where divine action is most legibly inscribed. If the cycle is for cognition, the night is the *interval* during which the cognitive capacities rest and the divine is most active — *Layl al-Qadr*, the *Isrāʾ*, Lot's escape, the destruction-narratives' verdicts.

9. **The *nasīʾ* prohibition (9:37) as defense of the line.** The pre-Islamic Arabs intercalated months to keep them in season — that is, to *bend the lunar cycle toward the solar*, preserving the season. The Quran prohibits this. This is the *only* place in the Quran where a calendrical practice is named and prohibited. Hypothesis: the prohibition exists because intercalation would have *yoked the cycle to a deeper natural regularity*, weakening the cycle's contingency. By forbidding intercalation, the Quran preserves the cycle as *purely lunar* — that is, *purely a month-counting device* — and frees the months from any natural-seasonal anchor. This is structurally what allows months to be *signs* rather than *natural facts*.

10. **The cycle's epistemic role and the linear demand: a unified Quranic anthropology.** If the cycle is for cognition and the line is for decision, then the Quranic human is a being whose cognitive capacities are formed by *signs* and whose existential orientation is structured by *appointment*. This is recognisably *both* an empiricist epistemology (knowledge from observed regularity) and an existentialist ethics (life lived under finitude). The conjunction is itself unusual — most empiricist epistemologies are not paired with strong eschatologies, and most strong eschatologies are not paired with empiricist epistemologies. The Quranic anthropology is a *combination of two structurally distinct features* that the two-modes-of-time analysis makes intelligible.

**The decisive move:** noticing that the cycle is offered to the *cognitive faculties* (*yaʿqilūn / yatafakkarūn / yaʿlamūn*) while the line is offered to the *moral-existential faculties* (*āmana / ittaqā / ʿamila*), and that the Quran's grammar systematically maintains this division. **The two modes are not collapsed into a single time because they perform two different rhetorical jobs.** Once the functional split is noticed, the absence of seasonal vocabulary, the absence of cyclical eschatology, the suppression of *dahr*, the prohibition of *nasīʾ*, and the structure of the eschatological collapse passages all become consistent expressions of one underlying commitment.
