# Research Synthesis: The Quran's Eschatological Time Architecture

**Phase 2 cross-cutting integration of seven concept analyses (swE, ywm, Axr, xld, Abd, Ajl, wqt) and two structural analyses (time co-occurrence, time divergence).**

---

## 1. Headline — the integrated finding no single agent could see

The Quran does not narrate the End. It **catalogs** the End across at least four lexical layers, and **each layer is built around a different geometric primitive of time**. Reading the seven concept files as one corpus, what emerges is not a single eschatological vocabulary but a **layered lexical system in which every "end-time" word is grammatically specialised** to do exactly one job:

- **al-Sāʿa (swE)** = the **threshold-instant** at which world-time terminates — only ever named, never narrated.
- **Yawm (ywm)** = the **frame-duration** within which all eschatological events occur — the only word that can take both a name (yawm al-X) and a measurement (1,000 years, 50,000 years, 6 days).
- **Mīqāt (wqt)** = the **appointed-meeting-instant** — the same word for Sinai, the daily prayer, and the universal gathering. *Resurrection is grammatically a Sinai-tryst scaled to the species.*
- **Ajal (Ajl)** = the **terminating-edge** — the deadline that closes any finite span (a soul, a nation, the cosmos, a debt). The only "eschatological" word that also governs commercial loans.
- **al-Ākhira (Axr)** = the **abode-after** — paired against al-dunyā, defined entirely by negation and relation, never by sensory description.
- **Khulūd (xld)** = the **state-noun of post-judgement abiding** — bivalent (paradise + hell), 94% nominal-participial, never an action.
- **Abadan (Abd)** = the **adverbial-modal seal** of temporal absoluteness — 79% inside negations.
- **The Trumpet (nfx + Swr)** = the **cinematic-narrative trigger** — the eschatological object the Hour-vocabulary is forbidden to share an ayah with.

The integrated finding: **the Quran's eschatological vocabulary is a non-overlapping coordinate system, not a thesaurus.** Each word fills exactly one slot. Where slots look adjacent (Hour vs Day, Mīqāt vs Ajal, xld vs Abd, Trumpet vs Hour), the texts are formally segregated — not just semantically distinct but ayah-level disjoint. The seven roots together perform what no single root can perform alone: they construct a multi-axis description of an event whose **timing is sequestered in God**, whose **duration is plural and incommensurable** (1,000 / 50,000 years / "blink of an eye"), whose **inhabitants are bivalent**, whose **knowledge is symmetric across all roots** ("with God"), and whose **interior is described only by what does not happen**.

What no single Phase-1 agent could state: **the Quran does not contain an eschatology. It contains a grammar for one.** The doctrinal content is residual — what survives after every word has been correctly placed in its slot.

## 3. Hour vs Day vs Trumpet — three eschatological objects, three rhetorical registers

The Phase-1 swE agent stated this provisionally; the integrated picture confirms and extends it. The same eschatological event (the End) is referred to by three lexical apparatuses that **never collapse**.

### Hour (al-Sāʿa) — the rhetorical name

- 38 determinate occurrences ([7:187], [33:63], [54:1], [79:42] ...). Always with the article. Always feminine.
- Five dominant formulas: *yasʾalūnaka ʿan*, *baghta*, *qarīb / iqtarabat*, *lā rayba fīhā*, *yawma taqūmu 's-sāʿa*.
- Cluster 4: *Revelation, Speech & Disbelief*. The Hour is **polemical**. It is the noun the Quran reaches for when confronting denial.
- 87% Meccan. The Hour-rhetoric **recedes** in Medina.
- The Hour is grammatically a **subject of motion toward** unbelievers (*taʾtīhim, jāʾathum, iqtarabat*) — never the destination of human motion.

### Day (yawm) — the narrative frame

- 405 occurrences across 75 surahs. Single lemma.
- Splits into 80 T-forms (accusative *yawma + verb* — the temporal frame) and 325 N-forms (the substantive Day-as-thing).
- The named-day catalog (§4) is the Quran's segmentation of the End into stages.
- Day is the only word in the eschatological vocabulary that can take a **measurement** ([22:47] 1,000 years; [70:4] 50,000 years; [50:38] 6 days of creation).
- The "Day" can therefore be both an **instant** (yawma yunfakhu) and a **duration** (kāna miqdāruhu khamsīna alfa sana). The grammar refuses to distinguish.

### Trumpet (Sūr / nfx) — the cinematic image

Verified directly from `data/morphology/words.jsonl`:

- **nfx (blowing)** appears in 18 ayahs; **Swr (the Trumpet horn)** appears in 17 ayahs; their intersection is **10 ayahs** (the Trumpet-blowing pair: [6:73], [18:99], [20:102], [23:101], [27:87], [36:51], [39:68], [50:20], [69:13], [78:18]).
- **Trumpet × Hour overlap = exactly 0 ayahs.** Confirmed by intersection: `nfx_ayahs ∩ swE_ayahs = ∅` and `Swr_ayahs ∩ swE_ayahs = ∅`. Phase 1's negative finding is real and load-bearing.
- **Trumpet × Day overlap = 5 ayahs**: [6:73], [20:102], [27:87], [50:20], [78:18]. The Trumpet is bound to *yawm*, not to *al-sāʿa*.

The three registers map onto three **rhetorical jobs**:

| Object | Job | Posture invited | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|---|
| al-Sāʿa | confrontation of denial | "do you believe?" | polemic surahs (Meccan) |
| yawm | narration of consequence | "see what happens" | every register |
| al-Ṣūr / nafkh | cinematic theophany | "look" | descriptive theophany passages |

The doctrinal stake of the Hour-Trumpet zero-overlap is sharper than the swE agent suggested. The Trumpet is the visible apparatus of the End; the Hour is the named-but-hidden trigger. **The Quran refuses to put the visible apparatus and the hidden trigger in the same syntactic clause.** This is consistent with the Hour's whole epistemic architecture: the Hour belongs to the *unwitnessed* (because its timing is sequestered, see §7), while the Trumpet belongs to the *witnessed* (it is the moment at which the unwitnessed becomes seen). The Quran's grammar refuses to let one ayah straddle both.

A useful corollary: when the Quran wants to *describe* the End, it uses Trumpet vocabulary and yawm. When it wants to *demand belief* in the End, it uses Hour vocabulary. The two are theologically the same event filmed by two cameras the editor refuses to splice.

### What the named-day pattern tells us

Three structural readings emerge that no single concept agent could state.

**(a) The End is segmented, not unitary.** *Al-Sāʿa* is one. *Yawm* is many. The Hour collapses to a single threshold-instant; the Day fans out into at least **20 distinct construct chains** plus an explosion of singleton names. This is the Quran's most aggressive form of theological multiplication: one event, many names, each name foregrounding a different aspect of what happens within the frame (resurrection, gathering, separation, reckoning, regret, loss, eternity, opening, coming-out). The Hour is the *what*; the named-days are the *aspects*.

**(b) The hapax names are doing rhetorical work, not naming separate events.** Yawm al-ḥasra (regret), yawm al-taghābun (mutual loss), yawm ʿaqīm (barren), yawmun ʿasīr (difficult), yawmun thaqīl (heavy) — each occurs **once** and each foregrounds an *affective* dimension of the same Day. Together they form what one might call the Quran's affect-catalog of the End: regret, loss, barrenness, difficulty, weight. The Day is named once for each major emotion it forces. This is the same rhetorical strategy the Hour uses with its single name (*al-sāʿa*) — except the Hour multiplies *formulas* (5 dominant), while the Day multiplies *names* (~30+).

**(c) The catalog answers a question Phase 1 only posed.** The ywm agent asked: *what does the named-day pattern tell us about the segmentation of the eschatological event?* Integration with swE answers: the segmentation is **rhetorical, not chronological**. The Quran does not order these days. There is no "first comes yawm al-baʿth, then yawm al-ḥashr, then yawm al-ḥisāb, then yawm al-dīn." The order in which names appear is *narrative*, not *temporal*. Each name foregrounds the aspect needed by the local rhetorical purpose. This is consistent with the swE agent's "epistemic darkness about timing, total certainty about occurrence" — extended now to: epistemic darkness about *internal sequence*, total certainty about *each named aspect*.

## 5. Eternity asymmetry (heaven vs hell)

The xld agent found ~42 paradise vs ~30 hell occurrences of the *xālidīn fīhā* formula and called the distribution "near-symmetric." The integrated picture sharpens this into a more precise asymmetry.

### Three asymmetries the agents partially saw

**(1) Vocabulary is symmetric; intensification is not.** *Xālidīn fīhā* is identical for both fates. But when *abadan* (Abd) is added as an emphatic seal, the distribution skews 8 paradise vs 3 hell ([4:57, 4:122, 5:119, 9:22, 9:100, 64:9, 65:11, 98:8] paradise; [4:169, 33:65, 72:23] hell). This is a real asymmetry: when the Quran wants to seal eternity with maximum emphasis, paradise gets the *abadan* about 2.7× as often as hell.

**(2) Construct phrases of *al-xuld* invert popular usage.** From the xld agent: *dār al-xuld* (the abode of eternity) is applied to **Hell** ([41:28]), not to Paradise. *Jannat al-xuld* (Garden of eternity) appears once ([25:15]). *ʿAdhāb al-xuld* (eternal punishment) appears twice ([10:52], [32:14]). The Quran's own construct distribution gives: 2× *ʿaḏāb al-xuld* (Hell) + 1× *dār al-xuld* (Hell) + 1× *jannat al-xuld* (Paradise). This is the **opposite** of the post-Quranic devotional usage. Hell is the more lexically explicit "eternity-house" in the Quran's own grammar.

**(3) The *illā mā shāʾa rabbuk* exception clauses are asymmetric in their closure.** The xld agent identified this; the integrated reading sharpens it. At [11:107] (hell) and [11:108] (paradise), both verses bracket khulūd with the cosmic-duration clause and the divine-will exception. But the closures differ:

- **[11:107] (hell):** *xālidīna fīhā mā dāmati 's-samāwātu wa-l-arḍ illā mā shāʾa rabbuk; **inna rabbaka faʿʿālun li-mā yurīd*** — closes with a bare assertion of divine sovereignty.
- **[11:108] (paradise):** *xālidīna fīhā mā dāmati 's-samāwātu wa-l-arḍ illā mā shāʾa rabbuka; ***ʿaṭāʾan ghayra majdhūdh*** — closes with a positive affirmation of unbroken gift.

A third exception clause appears at **[6:128]**, applied to hell-jinn-residency: *al-nāru mathwākum xālidīna fīhā illā mā shāʾa Allāh*.

**Doctrinal stake.** The hell-eternity is *grammatically conditional* in a way the paradise-eternity is not — but the conditionality is identical syntactically, and what differs is the *closing affirmation*. Paradise-eternity is conditioned-but-unbroken (*ʿaṭāʾan ghayra majdhūdh*); hell-eternity is conditioned-and-only-asserted-by-divine-sovereignty. This is the lexical seam from which Ibn Taymiyya and Ibn Qayyim derived the *fanāʾ al-nār* (annihilation of the Fire) hypothesis. It is also the single most contested exception clause in Sunni and Shīʿī theology. The Phase-1 xld agent flagged the seam; the integrated reading makes it visible as an **asymmetry of closure, not an asymmetry of conditioning**.

### Cross-finding: xld + Abd partition

The Abd agent and the xld agent both surfaced a clean lexical partition:
- **xld** = substantive eternity (state-noun, 94% nominal); bivalent across paradise and hell.
- **abadan** = modal eternity (100% adverbial); 79% inside negations.
- Together they form the **maximal-emphasis construction** in 11 verses.

The integrated picture: when the Quran wants to assert "abides forever as a state," it uses *xālidīn*. When it wants to assert "at no time will X happen," it uses *abadan* in negation. The 11 overlap verses are precisely where the **highest-stakes** eschatological declarations occur. Every other "forever" formulation is one or the other, never both. This is one of the cleanest grammatical apartheids in the Quran's time vocabulary.

## 6. Divine time-scaling (the 1000-year and 50000-year day)

Three verses dilate the Day by orders of magnitude:

| Verse | Construction | Domain |
|---|---|---|
| [22:47] | *yawman ʿinda rabbika ka-alfi sanatin mimmā taʿuddūn* | rebuke to those demanding punishment now |
| [32:5] | *fī yawmin kāna miqdāruhu alfa sanatin mimmā taʿuddūn* | divine governance (*tadbīr*); ascent of affairs |
| [70:4] | *fī yawmin kāna miqdāruhu khamsīna alfa sana* | angelic + Spirit ascent |

### What integration adds

The ywm agent posed the puzzle; the swE agent gave a rigorous parallel. At **[16:77]**: *wa-mā amru 's-sāʿati illā ka-lamḥi 'l-baṣari aw huwa aqrab* — "the matter of the Hour is but as a glance of the eye, or nearer." This is the **inverse** of the 50,000-year scaling. The Hour is sub-perceptual in duration; the Day can be 50,000 years.

Read together, these verses do not establish a "real" duration of the End. They establish that **the End is not measured by a single clock**. The Hour is *instantaneous* from the human-perceptual side ([16:77]); the Day is *vast* from the angelic-administrative side ([70:4]). The same event admits both descriptions because the question "how long does it last?" is the wrong question.

### Three readings the integrated picture supports

**(a) The Day is dilated, not the Hour.** The dilation verbs (*kāna miqdāruhu, ka-alfi sanatin*) only attach to *yawm*, never to *al-sāʿa*. The Hour is always near-instantaneous (*baghtatan, ka-lamḥi 'l-baṣar*); the Day is always extensible. This confirms the Hour-as-threshold-instant vs Day-as-frame distinction at the level of duration-vocabulary.

**(b) The dilation is contextual, not metaphysical.** Each of the three "dilated Day" verses sits in a different domain — punishment-impatience [22:47], governance [32:5], angelic ascent [70:4]. The Quran refuses to commit to a single dilation factor. This is consistent with the broader pattern of the Quran refusing to fix any cosmological duration (no precise creation length, no specific Hour-time, no calculable interval).

**(c) The dilation supports the Mīqāt reading of the End.** Once the End is a *mīqāt* (an appointment-instant, [44:40]), it can be experienced differently by different parties to the appointment — instantaneous to one set, 50,000-year-long to another. The grammar of Quranic eternity makes room for this perspectival multiplicity by separating *yawm* (the elastic frame) from *al-sāʿa* (the hard threshold). Without that lexical partition, the dilation verses would be incoherent. With it, they are predictions.

### A finding no single agent could make

The number "50,000 years" at [70:4] is not the Day's duration. It is the Day's **scale-from-the-angelic-side**. The number "1,000 years" at [22:47] is the Day's scale-from-the-divine-perspective-on-human-impatience. The number "blink of an eye" at [16:77] is the Hour's scale-from-the-human-perceptual-side. **The Quran systematically refuses a single scalar measure of the End and systematically supplies multiple non-equivalent ones.** This is structurally identical to the *ajal musamman ʿindahu* pattern at [6:2] (the term is named, but the referent is withheld from humans). The duration is named, but the referent — what calendar measures it — is similarly withheld.

## 7. The knowledge-withholding architecture

Each agent saw a piece of this. Integration shows it is the single most-attested doctrinal pattern across the eschatological vocabulary.

### Per-root inventory of withholding formulas

**swE (the Hour) — strongest pattern**
- *ʿinda rabbī / ʿinda 'llāh ʿilmuhā* at [7:187], [31:34], [33:63], [41:47], [43:85] (verified by direct query: swE+End co-occurrence = 5 ayahs; swE+Elm co-occurrence = 10 ayahs).
- "The five things with God" list at **[31:34]**: the Hour, rain, womb, tomorrow, place-of-death. The Hour heads the list. Grouped not with cosmic events but with intimate human unknowns.
- *akādu ukhfīhā* at [20:15]: "I almost conceal it" — the most unusual divine speech in the corpus.
- *yasʾalūnaka ʿan al-sāʿa* — every instance of asking is attributed to disbelievers; the believer's stance is *mushfiqūn min-hā* (apprehensive of it), never curious about it.

**ywm (the Day) — extends the pattern**
- *yawm al-waqt al-maʿlūm* at [15:38], [38:81] — "the Day of the known time" (*maʿlūm* = known passive; known by whom is unstated, i.e., God).
- *yawmin maʿlūm* at [26:38], [56:50] — "a known Day."
- *fa-Allāh yaḥkum baynahum yawm al-qiyāma* — God-as-judge sequesters all dispute-resolution to the Day.

**Ajl — extends further**
- *ajalun musamman ʿindahu* at [6:2] — "a stated term **with Him**." Two terms, the second sequestered.
- *ʿindahu ʿilmu 's-sāʿa* at [31:34] — verbatim same construction.
- The *passive-participle pattern*: *musammā* (named), *maʿlūm* (known), *muʾajjal* (term-fixed), *mawqūt* (time-fixed) all use the passive to leave the agent (= God) grammatically unstated. The Ajl agent called this *the apophatic grammar of bounded time*.

**wqt (the Mīqāt)**
- *lā yujallīhā li-waqtihā illā huwa* at [7:187] — "none reveals its time except Him."
- The four eschatological *mīqāt* verses ([26:38], [44:40], [56:50], [78:17]) all carry *maʿlūm* tags or imply unstated divine appointment.
- The verb *waqqata* (active, "to appoint a time") is **never used**; only the passive *uqqitat* and the participle *mawqūt* appear. The agent is structurally suppressed.

**Axr (the Hereafter)**
- "Hereafter is never described in first-person experiential terms." The Axr agent's negative-space finding: it is reported, prophesied, threatened, promised — but never witnessed. The Quran refuses to give the Hereafter sensory descriptions in revelation-speech.

**xld**
- The exception clauses *illā mā shāʾa rabbuk* preserve divine knowledge as a residual reservation against any seemingly-categorical statement.

**Abd**
- *abadan* never gets an exception clause. The Abd agent confirmed: *abadan* is binary, the modal-temporal seal cannot be partially negated. But the very binary character is itself a withholding — there is no grade of permanence, no "almost forever," only the absolute scope.

### The integrated finding

The Quran's epistemic architecture for the End has **five non-redundant withholdings** distributed across the seven roots:

1. **Timing** (when does the Hour arrive?) — sequestered via *ʿinda Allāh ʿilmuhā* + *baghtatan* + *akādu ukhfīhā*.
2. **Internal sequence** (what comes first within yawm al-qiyāma?) — sequestered via the named-day catalog never giving a sequence.
3. **Duration** (how long does the End last?) — sequestered via the multiple non-equivalent dilations (instant / 1,000 years / 50,000 years).
4. **Sensory content** (what does paradise/hell *feel* like?) — sequestered via the Axr agent's "never first-person experiential" finding.
5. **Boundary conditions** (does hell-eternity end?) — sequestered via *illā mā shāʾa rabbuk* + the asymmetric closures of [11:107]/[11:108].

What is *not* sequestered: **occurrence** (the End will happen, *lā rayba fīhā*) and **moral seriousness** (every deed is reckoned). The Quran is binary in its distribution of disclosure: *that-something-will-happen* is asserted with maximal certainty; *what-it-will-be-like in any quantifiable detail* is hidden behind the apophatic-passive grammar of *musammā / maʿlūm / mawqūt / muʾajjal*.

This is **the most consistent doctrinal pattern in the entire eschatological vocabulary**. It is not local to one root; it is the integrating logic of the whole field. The Quran's epistemology of the End is: *we know that, we do not know when, how long, in what order, or in what experiential content*. Faith is asked precisely at the place where description is refused.

## 8. The preference formula (al-ākhiratu khayrun wa abqā) and its theological work

### Inventory

Verified by direct morphology query: **14 ayahs** contain the lemma *A^xir* (= al-ākhirah) together with a *xyr* (better) word; **2 ayahs** contain *A^xir* + *bqy* (more lasting). Together these are the lexical core of the "Hereafter preference formula."

The most explicit formulations:
- **[87:16-17]** *bal tuʾthirūna 'l-ḥayāta 'd-dunyā wa-l-ākhiratu khayrun wa-abqā* — the canonical full formula.
- **[20:131]** *wa-rizqu rabbika khayrun wa-abqā* — closely parallel; "your Lord's provision is better and more lasting."
- **[93:4]** *wa-la-l-ākhiratu khayrun laka mina 'l-ūlā* — "the Hereafter is better for you than the first."
- **[16:30]** *wa-la-dāru 'l-ākhirati khayr* — "the abode of the Hereafter is better."
- **[12:109]**, **[7:169]**, **[6:32]**, **[2:220]**, **[3:114]**, **[4:77]**, **[12:57]**, **[22:11]**, **[9:74]**, **[5:114]** — variants.

### What is being preferred

Three registers emerge from integration:

**(a) Hereafter preferred over dunyā as moral object** — [6:32], [16:30], [87:17]. The dominant register. The comparative is between two *spheres of attention*: the lower-nearer life vs the abode-after.

**(b) Hereafter-reward preferred over worldly reward** — [4:77], [12:57], [16:41], [42:20] (the harvest formula). Here the comparative is between two *kinds of recompense*. [42:20]'s asymmetry is decisive: the Hereafter-seeker is upgraded in his harvest; the dunya-seeker gets dunya *with explicit exclusion from the Hereafter*. The choice is non-symmetric and non-hedgeable.

**(c) Hereafter as comparative anchor for righteous individuals** — [3:114], [9:74], [22:11]. Here *ākhira* names the moral test by which character is graded.

### The lexical key — *abqā* is from a different root

The xld agent flagged this: *abqā* (more lasting) is the elative of *baqāʾ*, root **bqy** — the same root the Quran reserves for **divine eternity** (al-Bāqī, [55:27]). Note the lexical economy: when the Quran wants to say the Hereafter is *more lasting*, it borrows the divine-eternity vocabulary rather than the *xld* (creaturely-residence) vocabulary. **The Hereafter is "more lasting" in God's lexical category, not in the inhabitants' category.** The grammar embeds a doctrine: the Hereafter is durative because it is *closer to divine time*, not because its inhabitants are themselves deathless. *Khulūd* (the inhabitants' state) is downstream of *baqāʾ* (the abode's eternity).

### What integration adds beyond the Axr agent

The Axr agent identified the formula but did not connect it to the xld agent's *bqy*-finding or to the lexical apartheid (xld for created residency, bqy for divine remaining). Read together: the preference formula performs a **lexical migration** — it moves the Hereafter-as-place into divine-eternity vocabulary while keeping the Hereafter-as-residence in creaturely vocabulary. The formula is the Quran's most efficient way of saying "the abode-after participates in divine duration; its inhabitants are merely abiding in it." This is consistent with the third negative finding of xld: *xld* never describes God. The preference formula is the only place where *baqāʾ* (divine remaining) is predicated of the Hereafter as place — bridging the lexical apartheid for one specific rhetorical purpose.

### The implicit-beneficiary observation

The Axr agent noted that "the Hereafter is better and more lasting" leaves the beneficiary implicit. Integration with the swE finding (*the believer is mushfiq min-hā*, apprehensive *of* the Hour) suggests this is deliberate: the ambiguity invites the reader to *insert themselves*. The formula works because it doesn't say *better for whom*. Every reader who hears it must answer that question. This is the same rhetorical move as the Hour's *yasʾalūnaka ʿan al-sāʿa* — the questioner-as-disbeliever frame forces the reader to choose a different posture.

## 9. Negative space across the eschatological vocabulary

Each agent catalogued absences within its own root. Integration produces a **system of absences** that is internally consistent and theologically diagnostic.

### What the Quran systematically does NOT say about the End

**(a) No date, no calendar.** Confirmed across all roots:
- swE: *ʿinda Allāh ʿilmuhā*; *akādu ukhfīhā*.
- ywm: never co-occurs with units of time *except* the three dilation verses (22:47, 32:5, 70:4).
- wqt: *lā yujallīhā li-waqtihā illā huwa*; *yawm al-waqt al-maʿlūm* (known to whom is unstated).
- Ajl: *ajalun musamman ʿindahu*; the agent of *musammā* is never grammaticalized.

**(b) No precondition list, no calculable signs.** The Quran has no equivalent of the Olivet discourse's signs catalogue. Even the famous "moon-splitting" of [54:1] is paired with *iqtarabati 's-sāʿa* not as a *condition* of the Hour but as an already-occurred *companion-event*. The Trumpet is similarly not a *precondition* — it is the visual register *of* the End, not a *prediction* of it.

**(c) No "day of mercy."** The ywm agent found this: God's mercy is never temporalized to a single day. Mercy is ongoing in dunyā; the Day is for requital. Forgiveness happens *before* the Day, not *on* it.

**(d) No spatial geography of paradise/hell beyond imagery.** The Axr agent: "the Hereafter is never described in first-person experiential terms." Gardens, rivers, fire, throne — all imagery, never coordinates. There is no "paradise is to the east of throne, hell is to the west," no map. Even the signature *jannāt tajrī min taḥtihā 'l-anhāru* is repeated formulaically without ever specifying *where*.

**(e) No individual eschatology in detail.** The Quran does not narrate the experience of any single soul's resurrection. The signature event is *al-nās ajmaʿīn* ([44:40] *mīqātuhum ajmaʿīn*) — collective, simultaneous. Individuated narratives are absent. (Compare: the Quran narrates many individual *prophets'* lives; it never narrates an individual believer's resurrection.)

**(f) No duration-scale, no calendar of the End.** The three dilation verses are perspectival (§6), not constructive. The Quran does not say "the Day will last X."

**(g) No succession order within the named-days.** The catalog (§4) is not chronological. *Yawm al-baʿth* and *yawm al-ḥashr* and *yawm al-faṣl* and *yawm al-dīn* are never sequenced in any verse. Each name is an aspect, not a stage.

**(h) No self-prediction by any creature.** *Yasʾalūnaka ʿan al-sāʿa* — the asking is always rebuked. Even Iblīs asks for reprieve "until the day they are raised" and Allah grants something *different* ("until the Day of the known time" — [15:38], [38:81]). No creature in the Quran successfully predicts the End.

**(i) No "abadan" of God; no "khulūd" of God; no "ajl" of God.** The lexical apartheid identified in xld and Abd agents extends across the eschatological vocabulary: divine eternity uses *Hyy* (life), *qwm* (subsistence), *bqy* (remaining), *Awl/Axr* (first/last). The seven eschatological roots in this study are reserved for **created** eternity, **created** appointment, **created** abiding. The Quran's grammar refuses to predicate creaturely-eternity vocabulary of God — and refuses to predicate divine-eternity vocabulary of creatures (except in the preference formula, see §8).

**(j) No causal chain of judgement.** The Quran does not say "X causes Y on the Day." The Day is the place where consequences are *revealed*, not where they are *generated*. The generation happens in dunyā; the revelation happens in ākhira.

### The integrated reading

Add up these absences and the Quran's eschatological architecture becomes legible: **the End is not a description, it is a structure of accountability**. The Quran refuses to give the End a date, a length, a sequence, a geography, an individual narrative, a calculable sign, or a creature able to predict it — because the End's function is moral, not informational. The Quran's negative space is not gaps to be filled by hadith or kalām; it is the **load-bearing structure** of the doctrine. To fill the gaps is to convert a structure of accountability into a description of an event, and lose the accountability.

Phase 1 saw pieces of this. Integration makes it the single most important methodological finding of the Phase-2 synthesis: **the Quran's eschatology is a grammar of withholding, and the withholding is the doctrine.**

## 10. Rabbit holes for Phase 3

The synthesis identified at least nine concrete questions that warrant dedicated Phase-3 work.

**(1) The full list of "five things with God" at [31:34].** Hour, rain, womb, tomorrow, place-of-death. Each has its own concept-pass to do. Together they may form the Quran's most explicit ontology of the unknown-by-creatures. A future *concept-Five-Unknowns.md* would tie this together.

**(2) The Iblīs reprieve and *yawm al-waqt al-maʿlūm*.** Iblīs requests reprieve *ilā yawmi yubʿaṯūn*; Allah grants *ilā yawmi al-waqti al-maʿlūm*. Why does Allah change the formula? The classical commentaries split — is *yawm al-waqt al-maʿlūm* (a) the resurrection, (b) Iblīs's prior death, (c) some intermediate reckoning? The grammatical underdetermination at [15:38] / [38:81] mirrors the *ajalun musamman ʿindahu* underdetermination at [6:2]. Worth a dedicated cross-reading.

**(3) The Trumpet vocabulary (Sūr / nfx) deserves its own concept-pass.** This synthesis verified the zero-overlap with the Hour but did not deeply analyze the 12 Trumpet ayahs. What rhetorical machinery does the Trumpet do that the Hour cannot? Does the Trumpet always use the same verbs? Is the Trumpet always blown twice (the *first blast* / *second blast* tradition) or does the Quran itself only attest a single blowing? [39:68] explicitly has *thumma nufikha fīhi ukhrā* ("then it is blown again"). How does this relate to the rest?

**(4) The apophatic-passive grammar of time.** *Musammā, maʿlūm, mawqūt, muʾajjal, maʿdūd* — five passive participles, identical grammatical move (suppress the agent = God), distributed across at least four roots (Ajl, ywm, wqt, Edd). This is a single syntactic pattern doing apophatic theology. A dedicated study would catalog every instance and formalize the rule.

**(5) The ḥasra / taghābun / ḥūr / ʿaqīm catalog of hapax day-names.** Each appears once and each foregrounds an *affective* aspect of the End. The Quran has at least 12 such hapax names. A study mapping them to surah-position and rhetorical function would surface the affect-architecture of Quranic eschatology.

**(6) The exception clauses and *fanāʾ al-nār*.** [6:128], [11:107], [11:108] bracket khulūd with *illā mā shāʾa rabbuk*. Ibn Taymiyya and Ibn Qayyim drew on these to argue Hell is not literally eternal. The integrated reading sharpens the question: if the closure-asymmetry between [11:107] (bare divine sovereignty) and [11:108] (positive unbroken gift) is the lexical seam, what other asymmetries cluster in adjacent paradise/hell verses ([10:26]/[10:27], [39:72]/[39:73])? A full cataloguing would test the *fanāʾ al-nār* reading against the actual Quranic asymmetry pattern.

**(7) The dilation verses and the angelic-perspective hypothesis.** [22:47] (1,000 years), [32:5] (1,000 years, governance), [70:4] (50,000 years, angelic ascent). Are these the same dilation factor seen from different angles, or three independent factors? [70:4]'s 50× factor over [22:47] suggests independence. A comparative-cosmology pass against pre-Islamic Arabian and Late Antique apocalyptic time-scaling traditions would test whether 1,000 and 50,000 are arbitrary or systematic.

**(8) The Hour-Trumpet segregation and the editor-camera metaphor.** The synthesis claims the Hour and the Trumpet are "two cameras the editor refuses to splice." What does the Quran's narrative-cinematography look like when traced systematically across all eschatological pericopes? Surahs 36, 39, 50, 75, 78, 81 each cluster Hour + Trumpet but in adjacent verses. A pericope-level analysis would map the *editing*.

**(9) The preference formula and the *bqy*–*xld* lexical apartheid.** The Hereafter borrows divine-eternity vocabulary (*abqā*) while keeping creaturely-residence vocabulary (*xld*). This is a small but load-bearing lexical migration. A dedicated study of every instance where divine-eternity vocabulary touches creaturely-residence vocabulary would map the **single bridge** the Quran allows in its otherwise strict apartheid.
