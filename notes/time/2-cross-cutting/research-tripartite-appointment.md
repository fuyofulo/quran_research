# The Tripartite Grammar of Divine Appointment — Phase-2 Cross-Cutting Synthesis

## 1. Headline Finding

The Quran does not have a single word for "appointed time." It has a **system** — three roots that tile the temporal architecture without overlapping, each carrying its own geometry:

- **Ajl (أجل)** — the appointed **endpoint** (when the term *expires*) — 56× — *ajalun musamman*
- **wqt (وقت)** — the appointed **instant** (when the appointment *occurs*) — 13× — *mīqāt*, *mawqūt*, *yawm al-waqt al-maʿlūm*
- **Amd (أمد)** — the appointed **stretch** (how long the interval *runs*) — 4× — *amadan baʿīdan*, *ṭāla al-amad*

Phase-1 established the geometric tripartition. Phase-2 extends it. Three further findings:

1. **The tripartition is enforced by a passive participle architecture that extends well beyond time.** *Musammā* (named), *mawqūt* (timed), *maʿlūm* (known), *muʾajjal* (deferred), *maʿdūd* (counted), *mafrūḍ* (imposed), *maqḍī* (decreed), *maktūb* (written), *maḥtūm/ḥatm* (sealed) form a **single grammatical paradigm of unstated divine agency**. The same paradigm marks rizq (provision), ḥaqq (right/due), maqām (station), kitāb (writing), qadar (measure), and umma (nation). Time is one face of a much larger grammar — *the world is full of m-prefixed passive participles whose unstated agent is Allah*.

2. **Appointment = naming + writing + decree + measure.** Four roots — *smy* (name), *ktb* (write), *qḍy* (decree), *qdr* (measure) — converge on the appointed term. *Qaḍā ajalan* (decreed a term) at [6:2]; *kataba ʿalayhim* (wrote upon them) at [4:77], [5:32]; *ajalin musamman* (a named term) 19+×; *qadar maʿlūm* (a known measure) at [15:21], [77:22]; *kitāban muʾajjalan* (a deferred writ) at [3:145]; *kitāban mawqūtan* (a timed writ) at [4:103]; *amran maqḍiyyan* (a decreed matter) at [19:21], [19:71]; *ḥatman maqḍiyyan* (a sealed decree) at [19:71]; *qadaran maqdūran* (a measured measure) at [33:38]. The temporal vocabulary is one flange of a metaphysical machine that names, writes, decrees, and measures *all things*.

3. **The economic homology is real.** The longest verse in the Quran, [2:282], uses *ajalin musamman* twice for the maturity date of a loan — the **identical phrase** that names the cosmic boundary at [30:8], [46:3]. The Quran instructs believers to *write* (*uktubūhu*) the term, just as Allah *writes* (*kitāban muʾajjalan*, *li-kulli ajalin kitāb*) the term of life. Commercial scribal practice is the rehearsal of submission to divine bookkeeping. The universe is, in Quranic grammar, a debt structure: **named term + written contract + irreversible due date + day of accounting (*yawm al-ḥisāb*)**.

The integrated picture: **everything finite is on a contract. The contract has been written. The term has been named. The decree has been measured. None of this is movable by humans. The agent is named only in adoration — never in the noun phrase that does the appointing.**

## 2. The Tripartite Map (Geometric Model)

| Root | Geometry | Question | Signature modifier | Signature verb | Movable? | Frequency |
|------|----------|----------|--------------------|----------------|----------|-----------|
| **Ajl** | endpoint, edge | "until when?" | *musamman* (named) | *jāʾa* (it comes) | No (lā yastaʾkhirūna sāʿatan wa-lā yastaqdimūn) | 56× |
| **wqt** | instant, point | "at what moment?" | *mawqūt / maʿlūm* | *uqqitat* (passive) / *jāʾa* | No (sequestered to Allah's knowledge) | 13× |
| **Amd** | interval, stretch | "for how long?" | *baʿīd / ṭāla* | *ṭāla* (it grew long) | Extendable — but only by Allah | 4× |

**Example anchors:**

- *Ajl* — [6:2] *qaḍā ajalan wa-ajalun musamman ʿindahu* — "He decreed a term, and a stated term [is] with Him" — the lifespan and the resurrection as endpoints.
- *wqt* — [4:103] *inna al-ṣalāta kānat ʿalā al-muʾminīna kitāban mawqūtan* — "prayer is upon the believers a time-fixed writ" — the prayer-instants.
- *Amd* — [57:16] *fa-ṭāla ʿalayhimu al-amadu fa-qasat qulūbuhum* — "the stretch grew long over them, and their hearts hardened" — the duration that corrodes.

The three geometries together exhaust the Quran's vocabulary for *appointed* time. Other roots (*ywm*, *sāʿa*, *ḥīn*, *qrn*, *dhr*) name *time-substance* (the day, the hour, the while, the generation, the age) — but the **appointment** of that substance is always *Ajl/wqt/Amd*. Substance vs appointment is the fundamental Quranic distinction.

## 3. Substitution-Probe Results

The cleanest test of the tripartite hypothesis is to ask: where could one root substitute for another, and where would substitution break the verse?

### Where *ajal* sounds right but *waqt/amad* would be wrong

- **[7:34]** *fa-iḏā jāʾa **ajaluhum** lā yastaʾkhirūna sāʿatan wa-lā yastaqdimūn* — "when their **term** comes…" — substituting *waqt* (instant) would soften the *expiry* sense; substituting *amad* (stretch) would be incoherent ("when their stretch comes" — stretches do not come, they run).
- **[2:282]** *iḏā tadāyantum bi-daynin ilā **ajalin** musamman* — the maturity date of a loan. *Wqt* would refer to a moment within the loan, not its terminating edge. *Amd* would mean the *length of the loan*, not its endpoint. Only *ajal* names the **boundary owed-by**.
- **[3:145]** *kitāban muʾajjalan* — death is a writ deferred-to-its-term. *Mawqūtan* would mean "timed at fixed instants" (which is what prayer is, not death). *Maʿdūdan* would mean "counted." The participle *muʾajjal* uniquely names a writ whose *terminating edge* has been fixed.

### Where *waqt/mīqāt* sounds right but *ajal/amad* would be wrong

- **[4:103]** *kitāban **mawqūtan*** — prayer is a *time-fixed* writ. *Muʾajjalan* would imply prayer has a deadline-to-expire (it doesn't — prayer recurs). *Maʿdūdan* would imply counted (number of prayers, not their instants). The instant-stamp is structurally required.
- **[44:40]** *inna yawma al-faṣli **mīqātuhum** ajmaʿīn* — "the Day of Decision is **their appointment** all together." *Ajaluhum ajmaʿīn* would imply the world's *boundary*; the verse instead names the *meeting-instant* of judgment. The two are not synonyms even when referring to the same Day.
- **[7:142]** *mīqātu rabbihi arbaʿīna laylatan* — Moses's appointment with his Lord at forty nights. *Ajalu rabbihi* would mean "his Lord's deadline" (incoherent — God has no deadline). *Amadu rabbihi* would mean "the duration of his Lord" (incoherent). Only *mīqāt* names the **meeting-point**.
- **[2:189]** *al-ahillatu mawāqītu li-l-nāsi wa-l-ḥajj* — new moons as appointment-markers. *Ajālun li-l-nāsi* would mean "deadlines for people" (no — they recur). *Amādun* would mean "stretches" (no — they are points). The *recurring instants* sense requires *mīqāt*.

### Where *amad* sounds right but *ajal/waqt* would be wrong

- **[18:12]** *aḥṣā li-mā labithū **amadā*** — "calculated the stretch they had tarried." *Ajalan* would mean "calculated the deadline they had tarried" (incoherent — deadlines aren't tarried-in). *Waqtan* would mean "calculated the instant they had tarried" (incoherent — instants have no length to count). Only *amad* names the **measurable interval of stay**.
- **[57:16]** *ṭāla ʿalayhimu al-**amad*** — "the stretch grew long over them." *Ṭāla al-ajal* is incoherent (fixed endpoints cannot grow). *Ṭāla al-waqt* is incoherent (instants have no length to lengthen). Only *amad* — a stretch — can be subject of *ṭāla*.
- **[3:30]** *bayna…wa-bayna…**amadan baʿīdan*** — "a far stretch between." A *spatial-temporal* interval. *Ajalan baʿīdan* would mean "a far deadline" (the verse isn't about a deadline). *Waqtan baʿīdan* would mean "a far instant" (instants have no farness in this sense). Only *amad* gives **distance-as-interval**.
- **[72:25]** *yajʿalu lahū rabbī **amadā*** — "my Lord may set for it a stretch." Set against *qarīb* (near). *Ajalan* would force a fixed expiry; *amad* leaves the interval open as *length*.

### The substitution probe confirms the tripartition

Every test case shows: **the three roots cannot substitute for each other without semantic damage.** The Quranic time vocabulary is not redundantly synonymous; it is a deliberate three-coordinate system. The poverty of *Amd* (4×) and *wqt* (13×) is not lexical sparseness — it is the **rarity of contexts** in which only that geometry will do. *Ajl* is frequent because endpoints are theologically constant; *wqt* is rare because appointment-instants are reserved for ritual and eschaton; *Amd* is rarest because the abstract stretch is invoked only when measurement fails.

### The structural pattern

Every passive participle in this list has the morphology *m-prefix + root letters + uw/ū vowel*. They are the *ism al-mafʿūl* — the **patient noun** — naming *the thing acted upon*. Each leaves the **agent unstated**, and in every theologically loaded case, the unstated agent is **Allah**.

This is what Phase-1 called *grammatical theology*. The believer reading "*ajal musammā*" or "*kitāb mawqūt*" is being told, in Arabic grammar itself: *named by Someone, timed by Someone, known by Someone, written by Someone, decreed by Someone, counted by Someone, imposed by Someone, sealed by Someone, measured by Someone* — and the Someone is never grammaticalized into the noun phrase. The phrase is theologically complete *because* it withholds the agent.

### The five families and what they pass through

| Family | Participle | Domain |
|--------|------------|--------|
| **Naming** | *musamman* | the term-of-existence (Ajl) |
| **Timing** | *mawqūt* | the writ-of-prayer |
| **Knowing** | *maʿlūm* | the day, the instant, the measure, the provision, the station, the right, the writ, the months |
| **Deferring** | *muʾajjal* | the writ-of-death |
| **Counting** | *maʿdūd / maʿdūdat* | the deadline, the days of fasting, the days of hajj, the price-of-Joseph, the nation |
| **Writing** | *maktūb* | the description-of-the-Prophet |
| **Imposing** | *mafrūḍ* | the share-of-inheritance |
| **Decreeing** | *maqḍī* | the matter (Maryam's conception, hellfire-passage) |
| **Sealing** | *ḥatm* | the matter that is decreed |
| **Measuring** | *maqdūr* | the decree itself |

The cumulative grammar: **the world is full of *m-uw* participles. Each names a thing that has been done-to. The doer is the same one. He is not in the noun phrase.** The temporal vocabulary is a slice through this; it is not a separate structure.

### The systematic suppression of active verbs

Across the temporal vocabulary, the **active verbs of appointing are conspicuously rare**:

- *ajjala* (to appoint a term): **2× of 56** — and one of these is *back-translated through the damned's mouth* at [6:128] (*ajjalta lanā* — "which **You** appointed for us"); the other is the passive *ujjilat* at [77:12]. Neither is a free active divine subject.
- *waqqata* (to time): **never used in the active**. The only verbal occurrence is the passive *uqqitat* at [77:11].
- *ammada* (to stretch): **does not exist**. *Amd* has no verbal form at all.
- *sammā* (to name): **8× total** — 7 of those are humans naming idols/children/angels; only **[22:78]** *huwa sammākum al-muslimīn* ("He named you 'the submitting ones'") has Allah as active subject of naming-as-appointment.

The pattern is exact: **the only place the Quran actively names the divine appointer is when the damned arrive at their appointment ([6:128]) or when believers are named ([22:78])**. Otherwise the agent is grammatically erased into the participle. *To name the namer is to break the structural reticence the participles enforce.* The Quran observes this taboo with extraordinary consistency.

## 5. Connection to qadar / ktb / qḍy / smy

The temporal-appointment vocabulary is the visible edge of a wider *decree-vocabulary*. Tracing the co-occurrences:

### qaḍā + ajal — "He decreed a term"

Verified co-occurrences (Ajl + qḍy in same ayah): [6:2], [6:60], [10:11], [28:28], [28:29], [39:42], [42:14] — **7 ayahs**.

- **[6:2]** *ḫalaqakum min ṭīnin **ṯumma qaḍā ajalan***. The pairing is foundational: to **create** is to **decree a term**. Creation and appointment are a single divine act.
- **[6:60]** *li-yuqḍā ajalun musamman* — passive: "so that a stated term may be decreed-out" (run-down through nightly sleep-cycle).
- **[10:11]** *la-quḍiya ilayhim ajaluhum* — "their term would have been decreed-against them" (if Allah hastened evil as humans hasten good).
- **[28:28–29]** Moses-Shuʿayb: *ayyamā al-ajalayni qaḍaytu* — "whichever of the two terms I fulfill" — *qaḍā ajalan* at the human-contractual level.

This is the only place the active verb of decree (*qaḍā*) takes *ajal* as direct object. Conclusion: **decree and appointment are paired only at the top of the metaphysical hierarchy**. Below it, the passive participle (*muʾajjal*, *maqḍī*) does the work.

### kitāb + ajal — "for every term, a writing"

Verified co-occurrences (Ajl + ktb in same ayah): [2:231], [2:235], [2:282], [3:145], [4:77], [5:32], [13:38], [42:14] — **8 ayahs**.

The decisive verse is **[13:38]**: *li-kulli ajalin kitāb* — "for every term [there is] a writing." This is the doctrine in compressed form. Every appointed boundary in creation is *backed by an inscription*. The believer's commercial scribe at [2:282] (*fa-ktubūh*) imitates the divine kātib at [13:38].

The **kitāb-participle pattern** is the structural keystone:

| Verse | Construction | Subject | Reading |
|-------|--------------|---------|---------|
| [3:145] | *kitāban muʾajjalan* | death | a writ deferred-to-its-term |
| [4:103] | *kitāban mawqūtan* | prayer | a writ fixed-at-its-times |
| [2:282] | *kitāb* + *ajal musamman* | debt | a writing tied to a stated term |
| [15:4] | *kitābun maʿlūm* | a doomed town | a writ known [in advance] |
| [13:38] | *li-kulli ajalin kitāb* | every term | every term has a writ |
| [2:235] | *yablugha al-kitābu ajalahu* | iddah | until the writ reaches its term |

**Six different subjects, six instances of *kitāb* + temporal participle, one underlying ontology**: *the writ-of-bounded-obligation*. Death, prayer, debt, communal destruction, the universal grammar, and the marriage-waiting period are all instances of the same form: **inscribed appointment**.

### qadar + temporal vocabulary — "by a known measure"

Verified co-occurrences in the data:
- *qadar maʿlūm* — [15:21] (everything sent down), [77:22] (the human seed in the womb)
- *qadaran maqdūran* — [33:38] (the matter of Allah is a decree decreed)
- *qaddara → fahadā* — [87:3] (He measured, then guided)
- *miqdāruhū alfa sanatin* — [32:5] (a day whose measure is a thousand years)
- *qadīr* (capable) + *ajal* — [17:99] (Allah is capable of recreating; He set them a term in which there is no doubt)

The pattern: **qadar quantifies what ajl bounds.** A term is bounded; a measure is what fills it. The Cave story's 309 years are an *amad*, but they are also a *qadar* — the *quantity* the *amad* contains. *Inna kulla shayʾin khalaqnāhu bi-qadar* ([54:49]) is the doctrinal floor: every created thing has a measure. *Ajal* gives that measure its **edges**; *wqt* gives it its **moments**; *amad* gives it its **length**; *qadar* gives it its **quantity**.

### smy (naming) + ajal — "the named term"

Verified co-occurrences (Ajl + smw in same ayah): **23 ayahs**, almost all of which are the *ajal musammā* formula. The connection between *naming* and *appointing* is the deepest etymological-theological move in the temporal vocabulary.

To **name** in Quranic grammar is not merely to label — it is to *appoint*, to *fix*, to *bring into a determinate relation*. When Allah *names* a term, He brings it from the indefinite to the definite, from the open to the bounded. This is why the rhetorical move at [13:33] — *qul **sammūhum*** ("name them" — said challengingly to those who claim partners with Allah) — is devastating: the polytheists' "gods" cannot even bear naming, because naming is a divine prerogative. Likewise [7:71], [12:40], [53:23] — repeated three times — *asmāʾun sammaytumūhā antum wa-ābāʾukum* ("[mere] names you and your forefathers named") — naming-without-divine-authority is the structural sin of idolatry.

The contrastive verse is **[22:78]** *huwa sammākum al-muslimīn* — "**He** named you 'the submitting ones.'" *The Muslim community is named by Allah in exactly the way an ajal is named by Allah*. The same theological grammar: divine naming = divine appointing. To be a *musamman* is to have been brought from indefiniteness into a determinate, divinely-fixed identity. *Ajal musamman* and *muslimūn musammawn* operate on the same logic.

This recasts the *musamman* of *ajal* as more than "named" in a technical sense. It is the **core theological act** — the act by which God *fixes* a thing into determinate existence. Every appointed term has been called into its definiteness by a divine naming. The cosmos is full of named things; their being-named is what makes them *bounded*.

## 6. The Irreversibility Axiom

Phase-1 found the famous formula at [7:34], [10:49], [16:61]:

> *fa-iḏā jāʾa ajaluhum lā yastaʾkhirūna sāʿatan wa-lā yastaqdimūn*
>
> "When their term comes, they will not delay [it] an hour nor advance [it]."

This is the **irreversibility axiom for *Ajl***. Phase-2 asks: does it apply to *wqt* and *Amd*?

### For *Ajl* — fully fixed

The variants ([15:5], [23:43], [63:11], [71:4]) confirm: *no delay, no advancement*. The formula always pairs delay (*ʾakhkhara*) with bringing-forward (*qaddama*). The asymmetry is theologically pointed: most religions imagine fate as resistance to being-moved-forward (you can't escape death). The Quran also denies movement-backward — you cannot rush the term. **The door is double-locked.** *Istiqdām* (advancing) is impossible. This forecloses both Stoic resignation ("let it come now") and martyrdom-as-acceleration as theological postures.

### For *wqt* — sequestered to divine knowledge

The *wqt* analogue is at **[7:187]**: *lā yujallīhā li-waqtihā illā huwa* — "**none reveals it [the Hour] at its time except He**." The verse does not say the *waqt* cannot be moved (the *mīqāt* is fixed). It says *even the information about the waqt is divinely sequestered*. This is a **stronger** form of fixity: the *wqt* is not only unmovable, it is unknowable. The reprieve formula at [15:38], [38:81] confirms — *yawm al-waqt al-maʿlūm* — "the Day of the time *known [only to Him]*."

The contrast is structural: *Ajl* is **fixed and ungovernable**; *wqt* is **fixed and unknowable**. *Ajl* you cannot move; *wqt* you cannot even time.

### For *Amd* — extendable, but only by Allah

This is where the axiom genuinely shifts. *Amd* **can** lengthen — [57:16]'s *ṭāla al-amad* names exactly that. But the lengthener is not the human. The *amad* "grew long *over* (*ʿalā*) the People of the Book" — they did not lengthen it; it lengthened *over* them. At [72:25], *yajʿalu lahū rabbī amadā* — "**my Lord** may set for it a stretch." The setter is divine.

The asymmetry holds. *Ajl* stays fixed and humans cannot move it. *Wqt* stays fixed and humans cannot know it. *Amd* can grow but humans cannot grow it. **Across all three roots, agency is stripped from the human and concentrated in the divine** — just under different geometric guises.

### The unified irreversibility

Stating the axiom in its general form: **no human can move, advance, delay, lengthen, shorten, or know any divinely-appointed time.** *Lā yastaʾkhirūn* (cannot delay) *wa-lā yastaqdimūn* (cannot advance) *wa-lā yujallīhā illā huwa* (none reveals except He) *wa-amaduhū ʿindahu* (its stretch is with Him).

The four-fold human incapacity matches the four-fold divine prerogative: **delay, advance, reveal, extend** — all with God, none with the human. This is the metaphysical floor.

## 7. Multi-Scalar Appointment

The Phase-1 *Ajl* note documented that *ajal* applies at three scales: **individual (death), national (umma), cosmic (sun/moon, heavens-and-earth)**. Phase-2 asks: is this multi-scalar pattern also true for *wqt* and *Amd*?

### Ajl: confirmed at all three scales

| Scale | Verses |
|-------|--------|
| Individual | [6:2], [6:60], [3:145], [22:5], [40:67], [63:11], [71:4] |
| National (umma) | [7:34], [10:49], [15:5], [23:43], [16:61], [35:45], [11:8] (umma maʿdūda) |
| Cosmic | [13:2], [31:29], [35:13], [39:5] (sun/moon); [30:8], [46:3] (heavens & earth) |
| Legal | [2:231–235], [65:2], [65:4] (iddah); [2:282] (debt) |

### wqt: present at multiple scales but with different theatres

| Scale | Verses |
|-------|--------|
| Individual prophet | [7:142], [7:143], [7:155] (Moses's Sinai *mīqāt*) |
| National ritual | [2:189] (lunar mawāqīt for hajj); [4:103] (prayer) |
| Eschatological/universal | [44:40], [56:50], [78:17], [26:38] (yawm al-faṣl as universal *mīqāt*); [7:187] (the Hour); [15:38], [38:81] (yawm al-waqt al-maʿlūm); [77:11] (messengers being time-appointed) |

The *wqt* root spans **prophetic-individual, communal-ritual, and cosmic-eschatological** scales. The structural pattern is identical to *Ajl*: a single root carries the *kind of event* across all scales.

### Amd: only individual and communal — no cosmic stretch

| Scale | Verses |
|-------|--------|
| Individual psychological | [3:30] (the soul wishes a far stretch); [18:12] (Cave-Sleepers cannot count their stretch); [72:25] (the Prophet's ignorance about the Hour's distance) |
| Communal | [57:16] (the People of the Book — the stretch grew long over them) |

*Amd* is conspicuously **not** used for cosmic durations. The cosmos is *bounded* (Ajl) and *appointed* (wqt) but its *stretch* is never named with *amad*. Hypothesis: cosmic stretches are too long to be subject to the moral-psychological "drift" semantics that *amad* carries. The *amad* is reserved for stretches **experienced by sentient beings** — beings who can wish, count, drift, or be set near or far. The cosmos does not experience its own duration in this sense.

This is itself a finding: **the three roots overlap in scope but Amd is restricted to the experiential domain**. *Ajl* is metaphysical and legal; *wqt* is ritual and eschatological; *amad* is psychological and communal. The three together cover almost all the registers in which the Quran speaks about time.

## 8. The Economic Homology

The most under-noticed move in the entire temporal grammar is the seamless transit between cosmic appointment and commercial obligation. The same word — *ajalin musamman* — names the world's expiry at [30:8], [46:3] and the maturity of a loan at [2:282].

Tracing the homology in full:

### The contractual elements

1. **The named term** — *ajalin musamman* (named by God / named by parties)
2. **The writ** — *kitāb* (Allah's writ of life / the scribe's writing of the loan)
3. **The witnesses** — *shuhadāʾ* at [2:282] / *shahīd* on the Day at [50:21], [85:3]
4. **The accounting** — *yawm al-ḥisāb* at [14:41], [38:16], [38:26], [40:17] (the day of reckoning, where every deed is weighed)
5. **The debt** — *dayn* at [2:282] / the soul's "debt" of its existence
6. **The release** — *qaḍā ajalan* (decree-fulfillment / loan-fulfillment): *qaḍā* means both "to decree" and "to settle [a debt]"
7. **The defaulter's plea** — [6:128] *istamtaʿa baʿḍunā bi-baʿḍin wa-balaghnā ajalanā allaḏī ajjalta lanā* — the damned naming the appointment that closes on them, like debtors hauled before the court
8. **The interest-free terms** — *qarḍ ḥasan* ([2:245], [5:12], [57:11], [57:18], [64:17], [73:20]) — believers are invited to "lend a goodly loan to Allah," explicit theological-commercial language

### What the homology means

Three readings, in increasing depth:

**Mundane reading**: the Quran uses commercial vocabulary because its first audience were merchants. Mecca was a trading hub; Medina ran on contracts. To say *ajalin musamman* to a Quraysh trader was to make the eschaton *legible*.

**Catechetical reading**: the *kitāb mawqūt* of prayer ([4:103]) and the *kitāb muʾajjal* of death ([3:145]) and the *kitāb* of debt ([2:282]) train the believer in the same posture across all three. Writing-down the loan is rehearsal for accepting that *one's life is also written-down*. Commercial honesty becomes a structural rehearsal of submission.

**Ontological reading**: the universe is *literally* a debt structure. Every finite thing exists on borrowed term (*matāʿ*, "provision," is a rental-word). Every soul is **owed back** to its Lender — *innā lillāhi wa-innā ilayhi rājiʿūn* ([2:156]) — "we belong to Allah and to Him we return" is the *acknowledgment of repayment due*. The day of judgment is the **balance-sheet day** (*yawm al-ḥisāb*). Hellfire and paradise are the **settlements of the account**.

The homology is so structural that whole vocabulary clusters cross-fertilize:
- *kasaba* / *iktasaba* ("to earn") is used both commercially and morally — *bimā kasabat aydīhim* ([42:30], [30:41]) is moral earning that *causes* corruption
- *jazāʾ* ("recompense, payment") is the universal word for divine reward and punishment
- *thawāb* ("reward") and *ʿiqāb* ("punishment") share commercial roots
- *ribā* (usury) is the **economic crime** that mirrors *shirk* (the **theological crime**) in its violation of the bounded-obligation structure
- *ḥasanāt* / *sayyiʾāt* (good / bad deeds) are *counted*, *weighed*, *recorded* — the accounting metaphor extends to the deed itself

**The integrated picture**: the Quran is not using commercial language as metaphor for cosmic relationships. It is presenting cosmic relationships *as themselves* a contractual structure. The metaphor goes the other way — commercial contracts are a *miniature* of the cosmic structure. The believer signing a loan at [2:282] is **practicing** the metaphysics; he is not being given a parable.

This is why the longest verse in the Quran is the debt verse. It is the place where the **scaffolding of bounded obligation** is most visible. Every other appointment-verse refers back to it implicitly. The believer who has *actually written down a debt* with witnesses understands *ajal musamman* in a way no abstract reasoning can teach.

## 9. Integrated Theory of Divine Appointment

Synthesizing the Phase-1 analyses, the Phase-2 substitution probes, the participle architecture, the connection to *qadar/ktb/qḍy/smy*, the irreversibility axiom, the multi-scalar pattern, and the economic homology — an integrated picture emerges:

### Six axioms of Quranic temporal theology

1. **Geometric tripartition**. Time has three appointment-geometries: endpoint (*Ajl*), instant (*wqt*), stretch (*Amd*). Each occupies a non-overlapping grammatical slot. They are not three near-synonyms; they are three coordinates.

2. **Grammatical passivization**. The temporal vocabulary is dominated by passive participles (*musamman*, *mawqūt*, *maʿlūm*, *muʾajjal*, *maʿdūd*, *maktūb*, *mafrūḍ*, *maqḍī*, *ḥatm*, *maqdūr*). Each leaves the agent unstated. The unstated agent is always Allah. The grammar **embeds the doctrine** — to say *musamman* is to say *named-by-the-Namer-not-here-named*.

3. **Convergence on writing-naming-decree-measure**. Appointment is paraphrased by four overlapping divine acts:
   - **Naming** (*sammā*, *musamman*) — to name is to bring into definiteness
   - **Writing** (*kataba*, *kitāb*, *maktūb*) — to inscribe is to fix
   - **Decreeing** (*qaḍā*, *maqḍī*) — to decree is to settle
   - **Measuring** (*qaddara*, *qadar*, *maqdūr*) — to measure is to bound
   
   Each act is the theological duplicate of the others: *the named is written, the written is decreed, the decreed is measured*. Together they form a single act seen from four sides.

4. **Multi-scalar architecture**. Appointment applies at five scales — individual (death, embryology), legal (debt, marriage, iddah), national (umma), cosmic (sun, moon, heavens), and eschatological (the Hour, the Day, the Iblīs reprieve). The same vocabulary, the same grammar, the same passive participles serve all scales. There is **no separate vocabulary for cosmic vs human appointment**. This is the radically *univocal* metaphysic of Quranic temporality.

5. **Economic homology**. The cosmos is contract-structured. *Ajalin musamman* is the maturity of a loan and the boundary of the world. Believers' commercial scribal practice is rehearsal for acceptance of the divine bookkeeping. The day of judgment is the day of accounting. The soul is on *borrowed term*. *Innā lillāhi wa-innā ilayhi rājiʿūn* is the *acknowledgment of the debt*.

6. **Universal irreversibility**. No human can move, advance, delay, lengthen, shorten, or know any divinely-appointed time. The verbs *yastaʾkhir* (delay) and *yastaqdim* (advance) are barred to the human. Even **information** about the *waqt* is sequestered ([7:187]). The *amad* extends only by Allah's act ([57:16], [72:25]). This is the metaphysical floor: **all temporal agency is divine**.

### The theology this entails

- **God is the only temporal agent.** Humans live within appointed structures they cannot move. They can only *act* — and the *outcomes* of their acts will arrive on God's clock.
- **Time is a moral medium.** The *amad* hardens hearts ([57:16]); the *ajal* closes accounts ([6:128]). Drift through time is a moral failure mode. Vigilance through time is the corrective.
- **Naming is the most fundamental theological act.** To name is to bring into bounded existence. Allah names the term, names the day, names the community ("Muslims"). False worship is the sin of naming-without-divine-warrant.
- **Writing is the second-most-fundamental act.** The cosmos is *written* (*kitāb*). The body of revelation is *written* (*kitāb*). The believer's commercial honesty involves *writing* (*fa-ktubūh*). The day of judgment opens the *book* (*kitābun yanṭiqu bi-l-ḥaqq*, [23:62]). Inscription is the medium through which appointment becomes durable.
- **The eschaton is structurally a contract settlement.** The Day is the *day of accounting*. Paradise and hellfire are settlements. The vocabulary is unmistakably commercial, and that commerciality is not metaphorical — it is the underlying structure to which commerce is itself a microcosm.
- **The believer's posture is one of accepting bounded existence.** Five-times-daily prayer (*kitāban mawqūtan*) is the rehearsal of bounded-instant. Death (*kitāban muʾajjalan*) is the acceptance of bounded-term. Commercial integrity (*fa-ktubūh*) is the practice of bounded-obligation. Ramadan (*ayyāman maʿdūdāt*) is the practice of bounded-counting. The whole life is structured by *m-uw* participles.

### The historical-political claim it underwrites

*Wa-li-kulli ummatin ajalun* — "for every nation a term." This is not a description; it is a *theology of historical termination*. Every political community exists on borrowed time. None outruns its appointment ([15:5], [23:43]). When the term arrives, no delay is possible — *lā yastaʾkhirūna sāʿatan wa-lā yastaqdimūn*. This is the underlying principle of the Quran's polemical surveys of past nations (the people of ʿĀd, Thamūd, Madyan, the Pharaohs, the People of the Book). They did not survive their *ajal*. Neither will any nation that follows them. The political consequence: **no community has a metaphysically-secured future**. The Muslim community itself is warned at [57:16] — do not let your *amad* grow long, or your hearts will harden, and you will become like those before you.

The integrated theology, in one sentence: **everything finite has been named, written, decreed, and measured into bounded existence by Allah, who alone moves the clock; the believer's task is to accept the bounds, prepare for the accounting, and treat both worship and commerce as instances of the same submission to inscription.**

## 10. Rabbit Holes for Phase 3

Several deep questions surfaced that exceed the present scope but warrant dedicated investigation:

1. **The full participial paradigm beyond time.** The participles *maʿlūm*, *maʿdūd*, *maktūb*, *mafrūḍ*, *maqḍī*, *maḥtūm*, *maqdūr*, *musamman* form a structurally unified field. A separate analysis should map this field across the entire Quran — not just the temporal slice. *Rizq maʿlūm*, *naṣīb mafrūḍ*, *amr maqḍī*, *qadar maqdūr* are all the same theological grammar applied to different objects. The full paradigm may be **the most pervasive single structure in Quranic theology**.

2. **The *kitāb*-modifier inventory.** *Kitāban muʾajjalan*, *kitāban mawqūtan*, *kitābun maʿlūm*, *kitāb mubīn*, *kitābun marqūm*, *kitāb maknūn*, *kitāb yanṭiqu*, *kitāb fī ʿilliyyīn / sijjīn*. Every *kitāb*-modifier is theologically loaded. A separate study should map the participial fingerprints on the kitāb-vocabulary.

3. **The economic vocabulary as theology.** *Dayn*, *qarḍ*, *jazāʾ*, *thawāb*, *ʿiqāb*, *ribā*, *kasaba*, *iktasaba*, *ājir*, *amān*, *ʿahd*, *mīthāq* — the contract-vocabulary is enormous. The thesis "cosmos = contract" needs a separate corpus analysis to test rigorously. Special attention to *qarḍ ḥasan* (the goodly loan to Allah) — six occurrences, all explicit reversals of the human-as-borrower into human-as-lender.

4. **The naming-as-appointment doctrine in cognate Semitic.** Hebrew *qārāʾ shem* (to name) and Akkadian naming-rituals; the Genesis 2 naming of animals as a creative-bounding act; Adam's *al-asmāʾ kullahā* at [2:31]. There may be a **broader Semitic theology of naming-as-establishing** of which the Quran's *musamman* is the densest crystallization.

5. **The *yawm al-waqt al-maʿlūm* and Iblīs's reprieve.** Three time-roots in three consecutive words. Iblīs asked for *yawm yubʿaṯūn* (resurrection day) and got *yawm al-waqt al-maʿlūm* (the day of the known time). The discrepancy is theologically loaded — Iblīs's terminus is not the resurrection. What is it? Classical commentaries split. The verse is a place where the **passive participle (*maʿlūm*) does its strongest apophatic work** — known to whom, and known how? Worth a focused study.

6. **The umma maʿdūda at [11:8].** The only place where a *nation* (umma) is described with *maʿdūda* (counted). "If We delay the punishment from them *to a counted nation*" — a stunning metonymy: the *waiting period* is itself called a "counted nation," as if the deferral were measured in *generation-units*. The image deserves unpacking.

7. **The connection to ḥafiẓa / kataba / qaḍā as scribal vocabulary.** The recording angels at [50:17–18], [82:10–12], [43:80] are the visible counterpart of the *kitāb maktūb*. Every word is recorded; every deed is noted. The scribe-verbs (*katabnā*, *yastansikhu*, *yaktub*, *raqqama*, *ḥasaba*) and the participle-archive (*maktūb*, *marqūm*, *maḥṣīy*, *maḥfūẓ*) form a sub-architecture worth its own study.

8. **The fixation/extension asymmetry across the trio.** *Ajl* cannot be extended. *Wqt* cannot be revealed. *Amd* can be extended (only by Allah). Why this distribution of divine prerogatives across geometries? Hypothesis: the geometries themselves dictate what kind of divine action is possible. Endpoints can be moved (but only by the appointer, not the appointed); instants can be revealed (but only by the knower); stretches can be extended (because they are *quantitative*, not *positional*). The geometric distinctions may be *generative* of the theological distinctions, not just *expressive* of them.

9. **The legal *ajal* of [2:235] *yablugha al-kitābu ajalahu*.** The **writ** reaches **its** term. The writ has its own ajl. This is the only place a *kitāb* itself has an *ajal*. Explore: is the Qurʾān itself a *kitāb* with an *ajal*? Does the cosmos's *kitāb* have an *ajal*? The reflexive structure (writing of writing, term of term) deserves attention.

10. **Cross-Semitic comparison of *amad* as duration-without-endpoint.** Hebrew *ʿōlām*, Aramaic *ʿālmā*, Akkadian *dāriû*, Greek *aiōn*. Is *amad* the closest Arabic cognate to the *ʿōlām*-zone? If so, the conspicuous absence of the *cosmic* sense in *amad* (it's reserved for human-experiential stretches) marks a Quranic divergence from the wider Semitic field. Worth a comparative study.
