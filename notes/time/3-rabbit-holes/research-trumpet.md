# Research: The Trumpet (al-Ṣūr / nfx) — the cinematic camera of the Quranic apocalypse

> Phase-3 rabbit-hole following the eschatological-architecture finding that the Trumpet and the Hour never co-occur in any ayah. Builds on `research-eschatological-architecture.md`, `concept-swE.md`, `concept-ywm.md`. Methodology entry to be added to `notes/methodology-log.md`.

---

## 1. Headline finding

The Trumpet (*al-Ṣūr*, الصور) is the Quran's **second eschatological camera**. Where *al-Sāʿa* (the Hour) names a hidden, unwitnessed, calendrical instant whose timing belongs only to God, *al-Ṣūr* names the **audible-visible event that humans actually perceive when the eschaton breaks open.** The two vocabularies are surgically separated. Of 49 ayahs containing *al-Sāʿa* and 10 ayahs containing *al-Ṣūr*, **the intersection is empty.** No ayah uses both words. No ayah pairs the Trumpet with *al-qiyāma* either. The Hour is epistemic; the Trumpet is phenomenal. The Quran refuses to merge them.

Three derivative findings flow from this:

1. **The Trumpet is bound to *yawm* (Day), not to *al-Sāʿa* (Hour).** All 5 of the *Sūr* ayahs that include a temporal frame use *yawm*: [6:73, 20:102, 27:87, 50:20, 78:18]. The other 5 *Sūr* ayahs ([18:99, 23:101, 36:51, 39:68, 69:13]) use the conditional *fa-iḏā* ("then-when"), which functionally *replaces* a temporal noun with a narrative pivot. The Trumpet never receives an Hour-tag.
2. **The number of blasts is grammatically uncertain.** The text gives material for both a one-blast reading ([69:13] *nafkhatun wāḥida*, [36:53] *Sayhatan wāḥida*, [79:13] *zajratun wāḥida*) and a two-blast reading ([39:68] *thumma nufixa fīhi uxrā*). Tradition harmonizes them; the Quran does not.
3. **The blower is never named.** The verb is overwhelmingly passive (*yunfaxu*, *nufixa* — "is blown" / "was blown"), 8 out of 10 *Sūr* occurrences. The agent is unstated. Israfīl, the angel-of-the-Trumpet of later tradition, **does not appear in the Quran at all.** Only Jibrīl and Mīkāl are named ([2:97-98, 66:4 / 2:98]; *miykaAl* appears once in the morphology data).

These three observations re-shape the picture: the Quran preserves a deliberate gap between the *moment God knows*, the *signal humans hear*, and the *agent who sounds the signal*. Each is held in a separate grammatical slot.

## 2. Lemma disambiguation: Ṣūr vs nfx vs nāqūr (vs rajifa/rādifa vs Sayha)

### 2a. Root **Swr** (صور, 19 occurrences) — splits cleanly into TWO unrelated lemmas

The Buckwalter root *Swr* houses two semantic streams that the morphology data preserves clearly:

- **`S~uwr`** (الصُّور, "the Horn/Trumpet") — **10 occurrences, all definite, all eschatological.** Only this lemma carries the Trumpet sense.
  - [6:73], [18:99], [20:102], [23:101], [27:87], [36:51], [39:68], [50:20], [69:13], [78:18]
- **`Saw~ara`** (verb, "to fashion/shape") — 5 occurrences, exclusively about God forming creatures: [3:6, 7:11, 40:64, 64:3] (and the participle *muSaw~ir* at [59:24]).
- **`Suwrap`** (form/shape, noun) — 2 occurrences ([40:64, 82:8]).
- **`Suro`** (verb, "to incline toward you," at [2:260]) — Abraham + the four birds.
- **`muSaw~ir`** ("the Fashioner," divine name at [59:24]).

The Trumpet sense (`S~uwr`) is morphologically isolated. All 10 occurrences are with the definite article *al-*, and all 10 are inside the prepositional phrase *fī al-Ṣūr* ("in the Horn"). The phrase is a frozen formula — no genitive construct ("trumpet of X"), no plural, no possessive suffix. There is exactly one Trumpet, named exactly one way.

### 2b. Root **nfx** (نفخ, 20 occurrences) — splits into THREE semantic theatres

The verb "to blow" appears 20 times across two lemmas (`nafaxa` 19x, `nafoxap` 1x). The 20 occurrences cluster into three theatres that share a verb but not a referent:

| Theatre | Count | Ayahs | What is blown |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cosmic / eschatological (Trumpet)** | 11 | [6:73, 18:99, 20:102, 23:101, 27:87, 36:51, 39:68 (×2), 50:20, 69:13 (×2), 78:18] | *al-Ṣūr* (the Horn) — explicit object in 10 ayahs; one stand-alone *nafkhatun wāḥida* at [69:13] |
| **Divine in-breathing (creation of life)** | 6 | [15:29, 21:91, 32:9, 38:72, 66:12, 38:72]; also [3:49, 5:110] (Jesus blowing into clay-bird) | *min rūḥī* ("of My spirit") into Adam, Mary, the clay bird |
| **Industrial / smelting** | 1 | [18:96] | bellows ordered by Dhū-l-Qarnayn ("*unfuxū* — Blow!") at the iron rampart |

Two structural observations:

- **Voice asymmetry.** The Trumpet uses the **passive** ("is blown" — *yunfaxu, nufixa*) in 8 of 10 ayahs. The two exceptions ([6:73, 39:68 second instance]) are also passive but phrased as *yawma yunfaxu* and *nufixa fīhi uxrā*. **The creation-of-life sub-theatre uses the active** ("I blew" — *nafaxtu, nafaxnā*) — God speaks in first person. The smelting at [18:96] is imperative ("Blow!"). The grammar of voice already separates the theatres: blowing-life is something God *does*; blowing-the-Horn is something that *happens*.
- **The shared metaphor.** Nfx in all three theatres is a **transfer of breath that initiates a state-change**. Into clay → life. Into iron → fusion. Into the Horn → the apocalypse. The Quran reuses the same verb because the underlying conceptual shape is identical: *something inert receives a breath, and something new begins.* The Trumpet inherits this metaphor — the eschatological state is "blown into" existence the way Adam was. This is a small but real semantic load that Israfīl-as-instrument later traditions sometimes flatten into "he blew on a horn"; the Quran's grammar is closer to "creation by exhalation."

### 2c. Root **nqr** (نقر, 4 occurrences) — *al-Nāqūr* at [74:8] is a hapax-pair

The root has 4 occurrences total. Two are *naqīr* (the "speck on a date-stone," [4:53, 4:124]) — a tiny-amount metaphor, unrelated. The other two are at [74:8] alone: *fa-iḏā nuqira fī al-nāqūr* — "And when the trumpet [*al-nāqūr*] is sounded [*nuqira*]." Both the verb (*nuqira* — "is struck/sounded") and the noun (*al-nāqūr* — "the trumpet") are **morphological hapaxes** in the Quran. Neither word reappears anywhere else.

Translators almost universally render *al-nāqūr* as "the trumpet," collapsing it to *al-Ṣūr*. Saheeh: "And when the trumpet is blown." Pickthall: "For when the trumpet shall sound." Khattab: "When the Trumpet will be sounded." Arberry: "For when the Trump is sounded." But morphologically these are different words, different verbs, different roots. *Nqr* in Arabic carries the sense of *striking / pecking / tapping*; *al-nāqūr* is "the thing that is struck." It may be a different image — a struck metal plate or a percussive signal — rather than a blown horn.

The lonely [74:8] is also positionally special: it sits in the early Meccan opening of *al-Muddaththir*, one of the earliest Meccan surahs. **It is the Quran's earliest eschatological signal-event lexeme**, predating the *al-Ṣūr* vocabulary that dominates the middle Meccan period. The shift from *al-nāqūr* (1 occurrence, early) to *al-Ṣūr* (10 occurrences, mid-late Meccan) is itself a small diachronic fact — the trumpet word changes once and then stabilizes.

### 2d. Roots **rjf / rdf** (رجف/ردف) — *al-Rājifa* and *al-Rādifa* at [79:6-7]

Surah 79 (*al-Nāziʿāt*, early Meccan) opens with five oath-clauses about angels (vv. 1-5), then pivots to the eschatological sequence:

> [79:6] *yawma tarjufu al-rājifa* — "On the Day the Quaker quakes"
> [79:7] *tatbaʿuhā al-rādifa* — "Following it is the Follower"

The morphology shows that **al-rājifa** (root *rjf*, "to quake/convulse") is built from the same root that elsewhere describes earthquakes destroying nations: [7:78, 7:91, 7:155, 29:37] all use *al-rajfa(tu)* for the earthquake that wiped out Thamūd, Madyan, and Moses' seventy. The same root names a worldly judgment (earthquake) and a cosmic one (the first blast). **al-rādifa** (root *rdf*, "to follow / come behind") is a hapax noun built off a 3-occurrence root whose other uses are mundane ("rank-on-rank" angels at [8:9], "close behind" at [27:72]).

Tradition reads *al-rājifa* as the first Trumpet blast and *al-rādifa* as the second, harmonizing with [39:68]'s "blown again." The text itself does not use the word *Ṣūr* or *nfx* anywhere in surah 79 — it names a quake and a follower without identifying them with the Horn. Whether the rājifa/rādifa pair *is* the two trumpet blasts or whether it is a parallel non-trumpet sequence is an interpretive move not licensed by the lexicon alone.

### 2e. The **Sayha** (الصيحة, "shout/blast") — a third trumpet vocabulary?

The root *SyH* gives 13 occurrences of *Sayha*, which translators frequently render as "blast" or "shout [of the Horn]":

- **8 worldly-destruction uses** ([11:67, 11:94, 15:73, 15:83, 23:41, 29:40, 54:31, 63:4]) — the cry that destroyed Thamūd, Lot's people, Madyan, etc.
- **3 eschatological uses** with *wāḥida* ("one"): [36:29 — destruction of one community], [36:49, 36:53] — the one blast that gathers all
- **1 explicit "Day they hear the Sayha"** at [50:42]: *yawma yasmaʿūna al-Ṣayhata bi-l-ḥaqq* — "the Day they will hear the Cry in truth, that is the Day of Coming-Forth (*al-xurūj*)"
- **1 hypocrites-fearing-every-shout** at [63:4]

**The *Sayhatun wāḥida* formula at [36:53]** is structurally parallel to [69:13]'s *nafkhatun wāḥida*: both insist there is *one* triggering sound, both produce immediate gathering. This suggests the Quran has at least three names for the same eschatological signal — *al-Ṣūr* (blown horn), *al-nāqūr* (struck trumpet), *al-Ṣayha* (cry/blast) — used by different surahs with different rhetorical needs. Adding *al-rājifa/al-rādifa* (quake/follower) makes four. The single-blast vs two-blast tension exists *within* this distributed vocabulary, not just within the Ṣūr texts.

## 3. The two-blasts theology — what does the Quran actually say?

The two-blasts framework is a **post-Quranic harmonization** of texts that, taken individually, give different counts. Here is the actual evidence ranked by directness:

### Direct one-blast attestations

- **[69:13]** — *fa-iḏā nufixa fī al-Ṣūri nafkhatun wāḥida* — "When the Horn is blown with **one blast**." The next ayah continues: "and the earth and the mountains are lifted and leveled with one blow (*dakkatan wāḥida*)" [69:14]. The whole pericope is built on the *wāḥida* count: one blow, one shock. Resurrection follows immediately.
- **[36:53]** — *in kānat illā Ṣayhatan wāḥidatan fa-iḏā hum jamīʿun ladaynā muḥḍarūn* — "It will not be but **one shout**, and at once they are all brought present before Us." Resurrection-by-one-cry, no second event needed.
- **[36:29]** — *in kānat illā Ṣayhatan wāḥidatan fa-iḏā hum xāmidūn* — "It was not but **one shout**, and immediately they were extinguished." Worldly destruction, single sound.
- **[36:49]** — *mā yanẓurūna illā Ṣayhatan wāḥidatan taʾxuduhum wa-hum yaxiṣṣimūn* — "They await not but **one blast** which will seize them while they are disputing."
- **[38:15]** — *mā yanẓuru hāʾulāʾi illā Ṣayhatan wāḥidatan mā lahā min fawāq* — "These await not but **one blast**, for it there will be no delay (*fawāq*)."
- **[79:13]** — *fa-innamā hiya zajratun wāḥida* — "Indeed, it will be but **one shout**." Followed by [79:14]: "and suddenly they will be alert upon the surface."
- **[54:31]** — *innā arsalnā ʿalayhim Ṣayhatan wāḥidatan* — "We sent upon them **one shout**" (Thamūd's destruction).

The "one X" formula is the dominant pattern: 7+ ayahs across 4 different signal-words insist on a *single* blast.

### The two-blast attestation

- **[39:68]** is the **only** ayah that explicitly counts two: *wa-nufixa fī al-Ṣūri fa-Ṣaʿiqa man fī al-samāwāti wa-man fī al-arḍi illā man shāʾa Allāh, **thumma nufixa fīhi uxrā** fa-iḏā hum qiyāmun yanẓurūn* — "And the Horn will be blown, and whoever is in the heavens and earth will fall dead except whom Allah wills. **Then it will be blown again**, and at once they will be standing, looking on."

That is the entire textual basis for the two-blasts doctrine: one ayah, with the second blast specified by *uxrā* ("another"). The word "second" (*ṯāniya*) is not used.

### The rājifa/rādifa attestation (counted-as-two by tradition)

- **[79:6-7]** — *yawma tarjufu al-rājifa, tatbaʿuhā al-rādifa* — "On the Day the Quaker quakes, the Follower follows it." Two named events, sequenced by *tatbaʿuhā* ("follows it"). But these are not called blasts and not bound to the Trumpet vocabulary.

### What the data warrants

- The Quran's most common picture is **one signal → immediate resurrection/gathering** (the *wāḥida* tradition).
- [39:68] supplies a **two-blast variant** with no explicit indication that this contradicts the single-blast verses — possibly because in [39:68] the first blast *kills the living* and the second *raises the dead*, while in the *wāḥida* verses the assumption is already that everyone is dead (in graves: [36:51]).
- The two pictures **harmonize naturally** if you read the *wāḥida* verses as describing the *resurrection* blast specifically, with the prior killing blast presupposed but un-mentioned. This is not interpretive cheating; it is the simplest read.
- The rājifa/rādifa pair at [79:6-7] is **suggestive evidence** for a two-stage event but is lexically independent — no Ṣūr, no nfx. It may be the same two blasts described under different names; it may be two earthquakes; it may be the trumpet plus another signal. The text does not specify.

**The honest summary: the Quran asserts at least one trumpet/cry event, plausibly implies two (most directly at [39:68], suggestively at [79:6-7]), and never enumerates a third.** The "three blasts" of some later traditions is extra-Quranic.

## 4. The unstated agent — who blows?

Of the 11 cosmic-Trumpet *nfx* tokens, **9 are passive** (*yunfaxu, nufixa* — "is blown / was blown"). The two non-passives are still grammatically intransitive in surface form: *wa-nufixa fī al-Ṣūr* (49 — passive again) and *thumma nufixa fīhi uxrā* (also passive). The agent slot is never filled.

This is **structurally identical** to the wider Quranic eschatology vocabulary, which I have noted in `concept-swE.md` and `research-eschatological-architecture.md`: the agent of the Hour is always God ("with God"), but the *operator* of the Hour's mechanisms is rarely named. The voice grammar enacts a theology: God's command is the cause; the angelic-or-elemental machinery is silent.

**Israfīl is not in the Quran.** The morphology data confirms it: there is no *isrāfīl* lemma. The named angels are:

- **Jibrīl** (جبريل) — at [2:97, 2:98, 66:4]
- **Mīkāl** (ميكال) — at [2:98] only (1 occurrence)
- **Generic *malāʾika*** (the angels) — many

The angel of the Horn is a tradition built on extra-Quranic sources (primarily ḥadīth, e.g. the Bukhārī report of *al-ṣūr qarn yunfaxu fīh* — "the Sūr is a horn that is blown into") and pre-Islamic / late-antique Near Eastern apocalyptic (the Jewish Israʾel angel, the Christian eschatological trumpet of 1 Cor 15:52 / Matt 24:31). The Quran inherits the *event* but not the *angelic name*.

This raises a methodological point that recurs in this corpus: **the Quran is denser in eschatological *verbs* than in eschatological *nominal cast*.** The text gives us blowing, gathering, scattering, raising, but withholds the named cast of operators. Tradition fills the named slots; the Quran leaves them deliberately empty.

## 5. What the blowing does — the trigger function

Tabulating the consequences across the 10 *Sūr* ayahs:

| Ayah | The Trumpet → | Operator |
|---|---|---|
| [6:73] | "His is the dominion the day the Horn is blown" | sovereignty-disclosure |
| [18:99] | "they surge over each other... then We assemble them" | gathering |
| [20:102] | "and We will gather the criminals that day blue-eyed" | gathering + identification of the guilty |
| [23:101] | "no kinship between them that day, nor will they ask" | severance of social bonds |
| [27:87] | "all in heavens and earth are terrified (*fa-faziʿa*)" | terror |
| [36:51] | "from the graves to their Lord they hasten (*yansilūn*)" | exit-from-graves |
| [39:68] | "swoon (*Ṣaʿiqa*)... then blown again... standing, looking on" | death + resurrection |
| [50:20] | "That is the Day of the Threat (*al-Waʿīd*)" | threat-fulfillment |
| [69:13] | one blast → earth and mountains leveled, sky splits | cosmic destruction |
| [78:18] | "you will come forth in multitudes (*afwājan*)" | mass-arrival in crowds |

The pattern: **the Trumpet is never the destination, always the threshold.** It is the cinematic cut that opens onto the next sequence — gathering, terror, severance, judgment, dominion-disclosure. In every case the blast precedes a state-change in *humans* (or in *every-soul-in-heavens-and-earth*).

The vocabulary of post-blast response is rich and varied:

- **Faziʿa** (فزع — terror, [27:87, 27:89])
- **Ṣaʿiqa** (صعق — to be struck dead / to swoon, [39:68])
- **Yansilūn** (ينسلون — to slip out hastily, [36:51])
- **Yamūju** (يموج — to surge in waves, [18:99])
- **Yaʾtūna afwājan** (يأتون أفواجا — coming in crowds, [78:18])
- **Lā ansāba baynahum** (لا أنساب بينهم — no kinship between them, [23:101])
- **Qiyāmun yanẓurūn** (قيام ينظرون — standing, looking on, [39:68])

These verbs share a common shape: **involuntary motion on the human side, while the agent of the blast remains hidden.** Humanity is the *patient* of the Trumpet — terrified, levelled, sliding from graves, arriving in crowds, severed from kin. The Quran's grammar of the Trumpet is the grammar of *being acted upon*.

## 6. The Trumpet vs the Hour — non-overlap theology

Direct check: do *al-Sāʿa* (root *swE*, lemma *saAEap*) and *al-Ṣūr* (root *Swr*, lemma *S~uwr*) ever appear in the same ayah?

- *al-Sāʿa*: 49 occurrences across 44 ayahs.
- *al-Ṣūr*: 10 occurrences across 10 ayahs.
- **Intersection: zero.**

Same check for *al-qiyāma* (the Resurrection, derived from root *qwm*):
- *al-qiyāma* (the determinate noun "the Standing-up / Resurrection") appears in dozens of ayahs.
- **Co-occurrence with *al-Ṣūr*: zero.**

Same check for *al-Sāʿa* with *nfx*-cosmic verbs (just to be sure):
- All 11 cosmic-*nfx* ayahs checked. **None contains *swE* root.**

This is not a small effect. The Quran has 6,236 ayahs, but it preserves an absolute rule: the noun the Quran uses to name the *moment of judgment* (Hour) and the noun it uses to name the *signal of the moment of judgment* (Trumpet) are kept in separate ayahs.

The corollary to investigate: do they appear in the same **surah**? Yes, sometimes — most strikingly in **surah 79 (*al-Nāziʿāt*)**:
- [79:6-7] — *al-rājifa, al-rādifa* (the trumpet pair, in the trumpet vocabulary group)
- [79:42] — *yasʾalūnaka ʿan al-sāʿati ayyāna mursāhā* — "They ask you about the Hour: when is its arrival?"

The same surah holds both events but in two separate sections, 36 ayahs apart. The opening describes the cinematic trigger; the closing returns to the epistemic withholding. The surah's compositional structure mirrors the lexical structure: trumpet-architecture in the prologue, Hour-architecture in the epilogue, no contact.

**The theological reading.** I am extending here, but the data warrants it:

- *al-Sāʿa* answers the question *"when?"* — and the answer is *withheld*. Knowledge of timing is sequestered to God ([7:187, 31:34, 33:63]).
- *al-Ṣūr* answers the question *"how does it begin?"* — and the answer is *witnessed*. The blast is heard; humans are the patients of the event.
- The two questions are not the same question. Christianity (e.g. 1 Thess 4:16, *the trumpet of God*) tends to fuse them — the trumpet *is* the moment of judgment. The Quran insists on the separation: the moment-as-known and the moment-as-perceived are different grammatical entities.

This is the same epistemic move the Quran makes about Adam's earthly descent (the *bi-mā kunta tafʿalu* / *bi-mā taʿmalūn* split), about ajal vs amad (point vs span), about the divine "Be / kun" (the command precedes the perceptible effect). The Trumpet/Hour split is one more instance of the corpus-wide pattern: **what God knows and what creatures experience are held in different vocabularies even when they describe the same underlying event.**

## 7. The eschatological narrative sequence

Reading the Trumpet ayahs in their immediate surrounding context lets us reconstruct the *order of operations* the Quran arranges for the apocalypse. Note this is not a single verse-list — it is a synthesis from multiple short pericopes that each shows a slice.

| Phase | Quranic anchor | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **0. Pre-trigger** | [22:1-2, 79:8-9] | "Hearts that day will tremble; their eyes humbled" |
| **1. The first blast (rājifa / Sayha / killing-blast)** | [39:68a, 79:6, 36:49, 36:53] | Cosmic shock, all-souls-die-or-extinguish |
| **2. Cosmic upheaval** | [69:14-16, 78:19-20, 73:14, 27:88] | Earth and mountains leveled, sky splits, mountains "pass like clouds" |
| **3. The second blast (rādifa / resurrection-blast)** | [39:68b, 79:7] | "Blown again, and behold them standing, looking on" |
| **4. Exit-from-graves and gathering** | [36:51, 78:18, 18:99, 70:43] | "Hasten from the graves," "come in crowds," "We assemble them" |
| **5. Severance of social bonds** | [23:101, 70:10-14] | "No kinship between them, nor will they ask of one another" |
| **6. Standing in terror** | [27:87, 39:68, 79:8-9, 80:34-37] | "Faziʿa man fī al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ" — terror grips all |
| **7. Records produced** | [39:69, 17:13-14, 69:19-31, 84:7-15] | "The record will be placed; prophets and witnesses brought" |
| **8. Judgment** | [39:69-70, 99:6-8, 101:6-9] | "Judged in truth"; scales weighed |
| **9. Garden / Fire** | [69:21-37, 88:8-16, dozens of others] | The two destinations |

The Trumpet sits at phases 1 and 3 — the **pivot points** that shift the action from worldly to other-worldly and from death to life-after-death. It is not a continuous element; it is two cuts in the film. Everything between and after is the consequence-cascade.

Two structural observations:

- **The Hour is everywhere and nowhere in this sequence.** It does not appear as a phase. It is the *name of the whole event*, not a phase within it. The phases use *yawm*, *iḏā*, *fa-*, *thumma* — temporal connectives, not "at the Hour."
- **The Trumpet localizes the otherwise-distributed apocalypse in time.** Without it, the Quran's eschatological vocabulary tends to scatter across yawm-formulae (Day of Resurrection, Day of Judgment, Day of Threat, Day of Coming-Forth, Day of Separation, etc. — see `concept-ywm.md`). The Trumpet pulls these *yawm*-images onto a single cinematic spine: *blast → leveling → blast → rising → judgment*.

## 8. al-Nāqūr (74:8) — separate signal or synonym?

The case for **same**:
- All four English translators render *al-nāqūr* identically with *al-Ṣūr*: "the trumpet."
- Functionally [74:8-10] does the same work as the *Sūr* verses: a sound triggers the *yawm ʿasīr* ("difficult day").
- The early-Meccan dating of *al-Muddaththir* (surah 74) places it *before* the standardized *al-Ṣūr* vocabulary; *al-nāqūr* could be an early synonym that fell out of use.

The case for **separate**:
- The root *nqr* means "to peck / strike / tap." It is the verb for what a woodpecker or chisel does. Strictly, *al-nāqūr* is "the struck-thing," not "the blown-thing." Lexically it is closer to a percussive instrument (a struck bell, a metal plate, a drum) than a horn.
- The verb *nuqira* ("is struck") is grammatically distinct from *nufixa* ("is blown"). Fully synonymous variants would more naturally use the same verb-noun pair.
- Both *nuqira* and *al-nāqūr* are hapax legomena — they appear nowhere else, not even in surah 74's neighbors. If they were synonyms for *al-Ṣūr*, the convergence would be expected to recur.

**My read.** Lexically *al-nāqūr* is most likely a different image (a struck signal) than *al-Ṣūr* (a blown signal), but **functionally** the Quran treats both as the same eschatological trigger. Surah 74 uses the early-Meccan word once, then never again; the corpus settles on *al-Ṣūr*. This is the same diachronic pattern that appears with other early-Meccan eschatological lexemes that get replaced or absorbed (e.g. *al-Sāhira* at [79:14], *al-Ḥāqqa* at [69:1], *al-Qāriʿa* at [101:1] — each is a near-hapax eschatological name that does not become standard vocabulary). The Quran's eschatological lexicon **stabilizes over time toward the *Ṣūr* / *Sāʿa* / *qiyāma* / *yawm* core**, leaving the early picturesque terms as marked, surah-bound singletons.

## 9. Translation divergence

From `data/structural/translation-divergence.json` for the trumpet ayahs:

| Ayah | Combined divergence | Notable outlier | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| [79:7] *tatbaʿuhā al-rādifa* | **0.768** (high) | none | "the second follows it" / "the second blast follows" / "second Blast" / "the subsequent" — translators split on whether *al-rādifa* is "the second" (counting it) or "the follower" (naming a function) |
| [79:6] *yawma tarjufu al-rājifa* | **0.713** | none | "the blast that convulses creation" / "the first trump resoundeth" / "the quaking Blast" / "the first blast shivers" — Pickthall and Khattab insert "first," interpreting; Saheeh smooths to "blast"; Arberry preserves the strange tautology *yawma tarjufu al-rājifa* ("the day the quaker quakes") |
| [74:8] *fa-iḏā nuqira fī al-nāqūr* | **0.680** | none | All four translators collapse *nāqūr* to "trumpet"; none preserves the distinct percussive verb *nuqira* |
| [39:68] | 0.642 | Pickthall | Pickthall: "blown a second time"; others: "blown again." The numeral move is interpretive. |
| [69:13] *nafkhatun wāḥida* | 0.640 | Pickthall | "shall sound one blast" / "shall be blown with one blast" — uniform |
| [36:51] | 0.615 | none | Khattab inserts "˹a second time˺" — interpretive harmonization with [39:68] |
| [6:73] | 0.503 | none | Standard rendering |

**Three patterns.**

1. The *rājifa/rādifa* ayahs are at the high-divergence end (0.71-0.77) because the words are rare and translators reach for either *function* names ("the follower," "the subsequent") or *count* names ("the first blast," "the second"). This is the same kind of disagreement that signals **morphological singularity** elsewhere in the corpus.
2. *al-nāqūr* (0.68) shows divergence in *form* but unanimity in *target meaning* — every translator gives "trumpet." The disagreement is on phrasing, not interpretation. This is consistent with translators having silently merged it into the Ṣūr concept.
3. **Khattab uniquely inserts "˹a second time˺" at [36:51]** to harmonize that single-blast ayah with [39:68]'s two-blast picture. None of the other three translators does this. This is a textual fingerprint of the harmonization tradition — Khattab is reading [36:51] *through* [39:68].

The translation-divergence data confirms what the morphology already shows: the *fringe* trumpet vocabulary (*nāqūr*, *rājifa*, *rādifa*) destabilizes the translators; the *core* trumpet vocabulary (*al-Ṣūr*, *yunfaxu*) is stable.

## 10. Negative space

What the Trumpet does **not** do, where it does **not** appear, who does **not** blow:

- **The Trumpet never appears with *al-Sāʿa*.** Already the headline. Zero ayahs intersect.
- **The Trumpet never appears with *al-qiyāma*.** Zero ayahs intersect, even though one would naturally expect the Day of Resurrection to be the day of the trumpet.
- **The Trumpet has no agent.** Israfīl is absent. The verb is passive in 9 of 11 cosmic occurrences.
- **The Trumpet does not sound in the very earliest Meccan oath surahs.** Surahs 81 (*al-Takwīr*), 82 (*al-Infiṭār*), 84 (*al-Inshiqāq*), 99 (*al-Zalzala*), 101 (*al-Qāriʿa*) all describe cosmic dissolution — sun darkened, stars scattered, earth shaken, mountains scattered like wool — without naming the Trumpet. The Trumpet comes later and replaces those earlier picturesque triggers with a single named instrument.
- **The Trumpet does not blow at the death of an individual.** It is collective only. The sound is for "all in heavens and earth" ([27:87, 39:68]) — never for a single soul. Individual death uses different vocabulary (*tawaffā*, *mawt*).
- **The Trumpet does not announce mercy or reward.** All consequences are aversive in the immediate moment: terror, severance, levelling, hastening from graves. The mercy/reward sequence comes *later* in the eschatological narrative; the Trumpet itself is invariably *threatening* in tone — explicit at [50:20]: *ḏālika yawmu al-Waʿīd*, "That is the Day of the Threat."
- **The Trumpet does not have a counter-Trumpet.** There is no answering blast for the saved separate from the blast for the damned. The same Trumpet sounds for all.
- **The Trumpet is never described visually.** No "horn of gold," no description of size, color, location. It is a pure auditory event whose only material specification is the preposition *fī* — something is blown *into* it.
- **The Trumpet does not appear in any of the most quoted "Day of X" formulas as an attribute** — there is no *Yawm al-Ṣūr* ("Day of the Trumpet"). The Trumpet is *blown on* a day; it does not *name* the day.
- **The Trumpet vocabulary skips the Medinan period almost entirely.** All 10 *Sūr* ayahs are Meccan (revelation profile from the morphology data). The 8 cosmic-blowing *nfx* ayahs are also all Meccan. The single Medinan *nfx* uses are creation-of-life ([3:49, 5:110, 66:12]). **The eschatological Trumpet is a Meccan-period vocabulary item.** Once the community is established, the focus shifts.

This last point is significant. Medinan revelation is preoccupied with law, community, hypocrites, war, ritual. The aural-cinematic eschatology recedes. The Trumpet-as-trigger belongs to the **Meccan period of warning** — it functions to confront unbelievers with a perceptible threshold. Once the audience is the community of believers, the Trumpet vocabulary is no longer needed; the same eschatology is invoked using *yawm* formulae and *al-qiyāma*.

## 11. Rabbit holes for further investigation

1. **The unified *wāḥida* family.** I count at least six distinct "one X" trigger formulas: *nafkhatun wāḥida* [69:13], *Sayhatun wāḥida* [36:29, 36:49, 36:53, 38:15, 54:31], *zajratun wāḥida* [79:13, 37:19], *dakkatan wāḥida* [69:14], *Saʿqatan wāḥida* (implicit at [50:42]), *kalimatun wāḥida* (different domain). Build a concept profile of *wāḥida* + cosmic-event-noun pairs. Is there a meta-pattern of *one-shot* events in eschatological Quranic grammar?
2. **The Sayha as missing link.** *Sayhatun wāḥida* and *nafkhatun wāḥida* are formally identical. Are they two names for the same event, or is *Sayha* the *worldly* analog (used for Thamūd, Lot's people, Madyan) and *nafkha* the *cosmic* analog (used only for the universal eschaton)? The 8 worldly + 3 eschatological split of *Sayha* suggests so; needs a dedicated check.
3. **The diachronic shift from *nāqūr* to *Ṣūr*.** Date the surahs containing the trumpet vocabulary (early-Meccan vs middle-Meccan vs late-Meccan, using e.g. Nöldeke's chronology). Test whether *nāqūr* (74) is genuinely earlier than the *Ṣūr* attestations.
4. **The rājifa/rādifa pair as a whole.** Are these the trumpet, or are they earthquakes (consistent with the worldly *rajfa* uses at [7:78, 7:91, 29:37])? If they are earthquakes, the Trumpet gets a *cosmic-quake* parallel, suggesting the eschatological trigger has multiple modalities (sonic + seismic). The *rdf* root's "follow" sense is itself worth tracking.
5. **The grammar of *fa-iḏā*.** Five of the *Sūr* ayahs use *fa-iḏā nufixa* ("then-when it is blown") rather than *yawma yunfaxu* ("the day it is blown"). This conditional construction is the Quran's narrative-pivot grammar. A study of *fa-iḏā* across the corpus might isolate the eschatological subset and reveal how the Quran constructs apocalyptic *suspense* grammatically.
6. **Iblīs's exhalation paralleled to the divine in-breathing.** *Nfx* in the divine sub-theatre always has Allah blowing *of His spirit*. The eschatological Trumpet is also blown — by an unnamed agent. The parallel suggests a structural rhyme: at creation God blew life *into* clay; at the eschaton an unnamed agent blows the *end* of life *into* a horn. **Both are state-changes initiated by exhalation.** Worth testing whether other cosmic events follow the breath-metaphor (e.g. the wind-of-destruction *al-rīḥ al-ʿaqīm* at [51:41]).
7. **Compare with the angel-of-the-trumpet figure in late-antique apocalyptic.** 1 Thess 4:16, 1 Cor 15:52, Matt 24:31; rabbinic literature on the *shofar* of the messianic age; the Zoroastrian Soshyant tradition. The Quran's deletion of the named agent against this near-eastern background is itself a finding — but to ground it requires literature outside this corpus.
8. **The blue-eyed / *zurq* people at [20:102].** The trumpet-day descriptor is unique to this ayah. Translators split on whether it means "blue-eyed from terror," "blind," "thirsty (with blue lips)," or "with eyes staring." A focused study of *zurq* and the visual-effect descriptors at the trumpet event would test whether they are physiological responses (terror, thirst) or metaphysical markers (blindness as judgment).
9. **The *al-Sāhira* hapax at [79:14].** "And suddenly they will be on the *Sāhira*" — translated as "earth's surface" or "the awakened plain." A unique post-trumpet location-name in the same surah as the rājifa/rādifa. Worth a hapax investigation.
10. **The grammar of the divine *wa-lahu al-mulk* at [6:73].** The Trumpet ayah is the only one that explicitly says "His is the dominion the Day the Horn is blown." This is *lahu* (to-Him) plus the definite *al-mulk* — a highly marked sovereignty assertion. Compare with the other trumpet-day frames ([20:102, 27:87, 50:20, 78:18]). Is [6:73] uniquely tying the trumpet to *the disclosure of God's sovereignty*, while the others tie it to gathering, terror, threat, multitude? This would suggest a fivefold typology of what-the-trumpet-discloses.
