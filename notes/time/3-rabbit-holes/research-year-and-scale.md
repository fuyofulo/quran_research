# Research: The Quranic Year and the Scale of Time — *Sana*, *ʿĀm*, and the Theology of Duration-Collapse

> Phase-3 rabbit-hole investigation. Builds on Phase-2 *disjoint-clocks* (which found that the Quran refuses to use the year as a bridge between months and generations) and on Phase-2 *cyclical-vs-linear* (which flagged *ḥisāb* as the only legitimate cycle/line bridge). This note integrates the year-vocabulary, the cosmological time-scaling verses (22:47, 32:5, 70:4), the Sleepers' 309 (18:25), Noah's 950 (29:14), the 100-year sleeper (2:259), the *nasīʾ* prohibition (9:37), and the *ḥisāb* bridge — and asks what the Quran is doing with **duration** as a theological category.

---

## 1. Headline — Years Count, but They Do Not Anchor

The Quran has two distinct year-words, *sana* (س-ن-و) and *ʿām* (ع-و-م), totalling 27 word-tokens across 25 ayahs. Numerically that is more than *shahr* (21 in 17 ayahs) and almost as many as *qarn* (36 in 34 ayahs). The year is not lexically scarce. Yet four structural facts make the year the **anti-bridge** of Quranic time:

1. **The year never co-occurs with *qarn*** (generation), never co-occurs with *Ajl* (appointed term), never co-occurs with *al-sāʿa* (the Hour). It co-occurs with *shahr* exactly once, at [46:15], where both are biographical, not calendrical.
2. **Where the year does appear with a number, the number is either round-and-cosmic (1000, 50,000, 950, 309, 100, 40), small-and-biographical (2 years weaning, 7 years famine, 40 years maturity), or vague-and-quasi-indefinite (*biḍʿa sinīn*, *ʿadada sinīn*).** There is no calendrical date in the Quran. No event is dated by year.
3. **Every "round" year-number is paired with a perception-collapse mechanism.** 1000 years = a Day with God (22:47, 32:5). 50,000 years = a Day of ascent (70:4). 100 years = "a day or part of a day" (2:259). 309 years = "a day or part of a day" (18:19). 950 years among the people = the lifetime of a single messenger swallowed by the Flood. The big year-numbers are not chronological data; they are **scale-rebukes**.
4. **The *nasīʾ* prohibition of [9:37] explicitly destabilises the year as a unit.** By outlawing intercalation, the Quran locks the calendar to twelve lunar months ([9:36]) and lets the year *drift* relative to natural seasons. The *year* is therefore not a fixed natural unit; it is a counting-tool that recurs when twelve cycles of the moon are completed.

Synthesis: **the Quran has a deliberate numerical theology of time in which years exist to be counted but not to anchor.** The bridge between cycle and line is not the year (the unit you would expect) but *ḥisāb* (the act of accounting). The year is allowed; the year-as-frame is refused.

## 3. *Sana* vs *ʿām* — Is There a Semantic Distinction?

The two words are usually translated identically as "year" (and English translators do not preserve the distinction). But the Arabic distribution is *not* random.

### 3.1 The patterns

**Sana / sinīn appears in:**
- All four cosmological time-scaling verses (22:47 *alfi sana*, 32:5 *alfa sana*, 70:4 *khamsīna alfa sana*).
- The hyperbolic life-wish (2:96, *alfa sana*).
- The famine-and-trial cluster (7:130 *bi-l-sinīn*, 12:42, 12:47, 26:205).
- The cave-sleep (18:11, 18:25).
- The astronomical-reckoning verses (10:5, 17:12).
- The biographical lifespan of biblical figures (5:26 wandering, 20:40 Moses in Madyan, 26:18 Moses with Pharaoh, 46:15 maturity).
- The eschatological "how long did you tarry on earth" (23:112).
- The geopolitical prophecy "in a few years" (30:4).

**ʿĀm appears in:**
- The 100-year sleeper (2:259), where the Quran also coins the verb *yatasannah*.
- The annual cycle of pilgrimage law (9:28, 9:126).
- The *nasīʾ* prohibition itself (9:37, twice — one *ʿām* lawful, the next *ʿām* unlawful).
- The third year of Joseph's prophecy that breaks the famine (12:49).
- The 50-year subtraction in Noah's lifespan (29:14).
- The 2-year weaning period (31:14).

### 3.2 The shape of the distribution

A pattern is visible. *Sana* dominates whenever the year is being **stacked into a cosmic or hyperbolic count** (1000, 50,000, 950, 100, 40, 309) or when years are being **endured** as trial-time (famine, prison, exile, sleep). *ʿĀm* dominates whenever the year is being **specified as a calendrical unit returning in a cycle** (this *ʿām* of the polytheists, every *ʿām* once or twice, the next *ʿām* of relief, the lawful *ʿām* and the unlawful *ʿām*) or when it is being **cited as a small biographical interval** (two years weaning, 50-year remainder).

A coarse two-way characterisation:

- ***sana* — the long, the painful, the unmeasured-by-the-counter.** Years that no human inhabits in continuity: the cave-sleeper does not experience them, the famine-stricken experience them as suffering, Noah's audience experiences them as missionary-time across generations, and the cosmological day-equations measure them only against divine time (*mimmā taʿuddūn*, "of those *you* count").
- ***ʿām* — the calendrical, the rhythmic, the marked-by-cycle.** Years that you actually *go through*: the next pilgrimage year, the recurring trial year, the two years of weaning, the relief year that breaks the seven famine years.

### 3.3 The 29:14 collision — *alfa sanatin illā khamsīna ʿāman*

[29:14] is the only ayah that uses both year-words. The construction is striking:

> وَلَقَدۡ أَرۡسَلۡنَا نُوحًا إِلَىٰ قَوۡمِهِۦ فَلَبِثَ فِيهِمۡ أَلۡفَ سَنَةٍ إِلَّا خَمۡسِينَ عَامࣰا

> "We sent Noah to his people, and he tarried among them **a thousand *sana* less fifty *ʿām*.**"

If the two words were synonyms, why switch? Three readings deserve consideration:

1. **Stylistic anti-repetition.** Classical Arabic rhetoric prefers variety where two identical words would meet syntactically. The Quran refuses to write *alfa sana illā khamsīn sana*. (Plausible but weak — the Quran is full of deliberate repetition where it serves emphasis.)
2. **Semantic-bulk distinction.** *Sana* carries the *bulk* of the duration (1000) — the long, the cosmic, the un-experienced. *ʿĀm* carries the *subtraction* (50) — the calendrical, the human-scale, the actually-lived. The construction is not "1000 minus 50 of the same unit" but "a thousand-of-the-cosmic-kind, minus fifty-of-the-human-kind." On this reading the verse is performing a subtle theological operation: Noah's mission is told in the cosmic-duration register, but the *subtraction* — the 50 he does not have — is in the calendrical-human register. Noah comes 50 *ʿām* short of the cosmic round figure, and the shortfall is itself in the unit of human-lived time. (Most defensible from the distributional pattern in §3.1.)
3. **Calendar-shift interpretation (lunar/solar).** Some classical commentators tried to read the construction as 1000 lunar *sana* minus 50 solar *ʿām*, or vice versa. Numerically this fails: if the two words named different calendars, the arithmetic ("subtract") would not make sense, since you cannot subtract solar from lunar without conversion. (Unworkable.)

I take reading **(2)** to be the textual finding: the Quran is using lexical duality to perform a subtle scale-shift inside a single arithmetic. This is the same texture we will see in 18:25 (Sleepers) and 2:259 (the 100-year man) — *the Quran encodes scale-discontinuities in micro-grammatical choices*.

## 4. The Cosmological Time-Scaling Verses — 22:47, 32:5, 70:4

Three verses give explicit equivalences between divine days and human years. They are the Quran's only quantified statements about scale-of-time.

### 4.1 [22:47] — A Day with your Lord = a thousand of *your* years

> وَإِنَّ يَوۡمًا عِندَ رَبِّكَ كَأَلۡفِ سَنَةࣲ مِّمَّا تَعُدُّونَ

> "And indeed, a Day **ʿinda rabbika** (with your Lord) is as a thousand *sana* **mimmā taʿuddūn** (of those which you count)."

The *context* is critical: the disbelievers are demanding hastened punishment ("they urge you to hasten the chastisement"). The verse responds by contesting their *calendar*. They count days; God counts in a different unit. What looks to them like delay (the punishment is taking too long) is, on the divine clock, no delay at all.

Two grammatical features deserve close attention:
- **ʿinda rabbika** — "with / in the presence of your Lord." This is the same *binary-grammar* spatial-co-presence preposition flagged in `research-binary-grammar.md`. The Quran does not say "in God's time" or "from God's perspective"; it says *with your Lord*. Time is being relocated as a *spatial* mode of presence, not a parallel timeline.
- **mimmā taʿuddūn** — "of those which **you** count." The pronoun shift is decisive. The Quran does not say *mimmā naʿuddu* (which we count) or even *mimmā yuʿaddu* (which is counted). It explicitly attributes the counting to *you*. The 1000 is not a divine measurement; it is a human one. **God does not count days; humans do.** The "1000 years = 1 Day" formula is therefore not a rate-of-time-flow equation; it is a statement that *the human counter, working with the human unit, will count to 1000 in the duration that God registers as 1*.

### 4.2 [32:5] — The day of governance = a thousand of *your* years

> يُدَبِّرُ ٱلۡأَمۡرَ مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ إِلَى ٱلۡأَرۡضِ ثُمَّ يَعۡرُجُ إِلَيۡهِ فِي يَوۡمࣲ كَانَ مِقۡدَارُهُۥٓ أَلۡفَ سَنَةࣲ مِّمَّا تَعُدُّونَ

> "He arranges the *amr* (matter / decree / command) from the heaven to the earth; then it ascends to Him in a Day, the *miqdār* (measure / extent) of which is a thousand *sana* of those which you count."

Here the context is divine governance: the *amr* descends from heaven to earth and then ascends back. The "Day" is the cycle of divine governance (one descent-and-return), and its measure-against-human-counting is again a thousand years. Note the noun *miqdār* — "measure, extent, ratio." The Quran does not say the Day *is* a thousand years; it says its *measure* is. This is a ratio language, not an identity language.

The *mimmā taʿuddūn* qualifier returns. Same pronoun-shift, same theological move: divine action operates in a unit that, when measured by *your* counting-system, will require 1000 of *your* units to express.

### 4.3 [70:4] — The day of ascent = fifty thousand years

> تَعۡرُجُ ٱلۡمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ وَٱلرُّوحُ إِلَيۡهِ فِي يَوۡمࣲ كَانَ مِقۡدَارُهُۥ خَمۡسِينَ أَلۡفَ سَنَةࣲ

> "The angels and the Spirit ascend to Him in a Day, the *miqdār* of which is fifty thousand *sana*."

Note what changes and what stays. The verb *yaʿruju* (to ascend) is the same as in 32:5. The noun *miqdār* is the same. But two things shift:
- The number jumps from 1000 to 50,000.
- **The qualifier *mimmā taʿuddūn* is dropped.** This is a textual signal usually missed in translation. 70:4 does not anchor the 50,000 to "your counting"; it states the measure absolutely.

### 4.4 Is this a contradiction, or two different days?

A long classical-exegetical tradition (Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Kathīr, Rāzī) separates the three verses by referent:

- **22:47** — the duration of the punishment / the time-scale of God's promise being kept (eschatological-judgment day).
- **32:5** — the time-scale of divine governance (the daily *amr* cycle).
- **70:4** — the time-scale of angelic ascent on the Day of Resurrection / the Day of the Hour.

The text supports the distinction but not the certainty. What the *text* unambiguously establishes is the following:

1. *Yawm* in these verses is **not the calendar day**. The *yawm* of 22:47/32:5/70:4 is qualified with *miqdāruhu* (its measure is) — a measure-equation, not an identity. The calendar-day stays at 24 hours; the *divine yawm* is a *category*, not a calendar unit.
2. The two numbers (1000 and 50,000) refer to **two different divine measures**. The Quran does not say "a Day is a thousand years and also fifty thousand years." It says one *kind* of Day is a thousand years and another *kind* is fifty thousand years. Whether these are two days or one day measured two ways is exegesis; the *grammatical fact* is that the two verses use different referents for *yawm* (one with *ʿinda rabbika*, one with *yaʿruju ilayhi al-malāʾika*).
3. Both equivalences are **scale-ratios**, not atemporality-claims. The Quran does *not* say "with God there is no time" or "God is outside time." It says God's unit, when translated into your counting-system, requires a multiplication factor. This is a much weaker — and structurally more interesting — claim than divine atemporality.

**The deeper finding**: the cosmological scale-verses are not metaphysical statements about time-itself; they are **rhetorical responses to creature-time impatience**. 22:47 responds to "hasten the punishment." 32:5 contextualises the visible apparent delay of divine governance. 70:4 sets the scene for the eschatological day in which the entire human history ascends. **The Quran uses scale-of-time language to defeat the human demand for fast resolution.** The 1000 and the 50,000 are not data; they are pedagogy.

This connects to `research-divine-vs-creature-time.md`: the Quran's persistent move is to deflate human duration-claims by re-anchoring them in a divine measure that exposes their smallness. The cosmological verses are the *quantitative* version of this move.

## 5. The Sleepers' 309 — Lunar/Solar Precision in [18:25]

> وَلَبِثُواْ فِي كَهۡفِهِمۡ ثَلَٰثَ مِاْئَةࣲ سِنِينَ وَٱزۡدَادُواْ تِسۡعࣰا

> "And they tarried in their cave for three hundred *sinīn* and added nine."

The "+9" is not a casual round-up. The Quran could have said "three hundred years" or "three hundred and nine years" plainly. Instead it splits the count: 300 + 9. Classical commentary has long noted that this construction tracks the **lunar/solar gap**.

### 5.1 The math

Solar year ≈ 365.2422 days. Lunar year (12 lunar months) ≈ 354.367 days.

300 solar years = 300 × 365.2422 / 354.367 ≈ **309.207 lunar years.**

309 lunar years = 309 × 354.367 / 365.2422 ≈ **299.799 solar years.**

The fit is clean. **300 solar years ≈ 309 lunar years**, with the solar-to-lunar conversion factor ≈ 1.0307. The Quran's "+9" is the conversion correction.

### 5.2 What the construction is doing

Two readings are textually defensible:

1. **The Quran is registering the same duration in two reference frames.** "300" is the duration as it would be told by an external observer using a solar-year frame; "+9" is the conversion to the lunar-year frame that the Quran has just (in the immediate sequel of revelation) locked into place via [9:36] and [9:37]. This is **calendar-acknowledgement without calendar-endorsement** — the Quran is showing it knows about the discrepancy and refusing to pick a frame.
2. **The Quran is performing a quantum-of-precision flag.** The "+9" announces *we are counting precisely now; do not round*. The number 309 is one of the few exact non-round numbers in the Quran, and it is positioned exactly where it would call attention to itself.

Reading (1) is supported by the chronological proximity: surah 9 (which contains the *nasīʾ* prohibition and the lock-to-lunar-year ruling) is among the very last revealed; the *kahf* narrative is at most a decade earlier. The Quran is one revelation away from saying "from now on, the year is 12 lunar months." [18:25]'s "+9" is, on this reading, a quiet preview: *we already know, and the difference matters, and we are going to standardise it*.

But notice what is *not* present: the verse does not commit to either frame. It does not say "300 solar = 309 lunar"; it says "300 *sinīn* and added 9." The reader is left to make the conversion mentally. **This is the same anti-bridging move we see everywhere with the year**: the Quran provides the data for the conversion and refuses to do the conversion itself.

### 5.3 The companion-verse [18:19] — "we have remained a day or part of a day"

Earlier in the same Cave-narrative ([18:19]), when the Sleepers wake, they ask each other: *"How long have you remained?" They said, "We have remained a day or part of a day"* (*labithnā yawman aw baʿḍa yawm*). The Quran then has the exterior narrator state the actual duration in [18:25]: 309 years.

This is the **duration-collapse pattern**. The interior subjective experience is "a day or part of a day"; the exterior divine/textual measure is "three hundred years and nine." The same exact phrase recurs at [2:259] for the 100-year sleeper, and a structurally identical move (long-time / no-time-felt) recurs at [10:45] (*"On the day He gathers them, as if they had not tarried but an hour of the day"*) and [79:46] (*"On the Day they see it, it will be as if they had not tarried but an evening or its morning"*). The pattern is system-wide.

## 6. Noah's 950 — [29:14] Re-Read

We addressed the *sana / ʿām* duality in §3.3. Let me pull together the *theological* reading of the round number.

**Why 1000 minus 50?** The Quran could have said simply "950 years." It deliberately constructs the figure as a near-miss of a cosmic round number. This pattern echoes elsewhere:
- 309 = 300 + 9 (a near-miss of 300 in the other-frame-conversion)
- 950 = 1000 − 50 (a near-miss of 1000)

The reading suggested in §3.3 — bulk in *sana*, subtraction in *ʿām* — gives the construction theological weight: Noah's mission is *almost* on the cosmic millennium scale (1000 *sana*), but it falls 50 *ʿām* short. The 50 *ʿām* is the *human-calendar shortfall* between divine cosmic round and actual lived mission. Noah's mission is *cosmically long but humanly bounded*.

This matters for the Quran's broader anthropology of duration. **No human (not even Noah) gets the cosmic round number.** The 1000 belongs to the divine-day (22:47, 32:5); when applied to a human life, it always has to be reduced. 950 is the *largest* human lifespan in the Quran, and even it is constructed as a deficit-from-cosmic. The 1000-year life-wish at [2:96] is indicted as a futile human greed: *"and to live (a thousand years) would be no means remove him from the doom."* The cosmic round is unreachable from below.

## 7. The 100-Year Sleeper — [2:259] and the Theology of Duration-Collapse

> فَأَمَاتَهُ ٱللَّهُ مِاْئَةَ عَامࣲ ثُمَّ بَعَثَهُۥۖ قَالَ كَمۡ لَبِثۡتَۖ قَالَ لَبِثۡتُ يَوۡمًا أَوۡ بَعۡضَ يَوۡمࣲۖ قَالَ بَل لَّبِثۡتَ مِاْئَةَ عَامࣲ فَٱنظُرۡ إِلَىٰ طَعَامِكَ وَشَرَابِكَ لَمۡ يَتَسَنَّهۡ

> "So Allah caused him to die for a hundred *ʿām*, then He raised him up. He said: 'How long have you tarried?' The man said: 'I have tarried a day or part of a day.' He said: 'No, you have tarried a hundred *ʿām*. Look at your food and your drink — *they have not been aged-by-years (lam yatasannah)*. And look at your donkey…'"

This is the most condensed time-theology in the Quran. Six features:

1. **The man's question.** Before death/sleep, the man asks how God will revive a destroyed town. The 100-year sleep is a *demonstration*, not a punishment. Time is being used as a pedagogical instrument: God reveals power-over-resurrection by demonstrating power-over-duration.

2. **The interval is precise.** *Miʾata ʿām* — exactly 100. Not "a long time," not "many years." A precise round-number-but-in-the-calendar-unit (*ʿām*, not *sana*). The Quran is telling us: this is *one human century* (the *ʿām*-unit), the actually-lived calendar century.

3. **The subjective collapse.** *Yawman aw baʿḍa yawm.* This is the **identical formula** the Sleepers use at [18:19] and that the resurrection-narratives use at [10:45] and [79:46]. The same five words, four times, across totally different scenes. **The Quran has a fixed liturgical formula for the perception of long-duration-from-the-other-side.**

4. **The corrective.** *Bal labithta miʾata ʿām.* The corrective is delivered by God directly. Note the verb *labitha* on both sides: the man and God agree on the *category* (you "tarried"); they disagree only on the *measure*. The Quran is preserving the experiential phenomenology (the man genuinely *did* experience nothing) while overriding the measurement.

5. **The signs.** Three signs are given simultaneously: (a) the food and drink that *did not yatasannah* (a hapax verb deliberately etymologically related to *sana*), (b) the donkey, whose bones must be re-clothed, and (c) the man himself as a *sign for the people*. The food-not-aged is a *negative-time* sign (some things did not change); the donkey-decayed is a *positive-time* sign (some things did change). *Both happen in the same 100 years.* Time selectively binds and selectively releases — depending on what God wills as the sign.

6. **The neologism *yatasannah*.** The verb is a Quranic hapax built on the *sana* root. By coining a verb meaning "to be aged-by-years" inside the very ayah where *ʿām* is used (not *sana*) for the duration, the Quran encodes a paronomastic commentary: the ʿām-count says "100 years"; the sana-derived verb says "no aging happened." The two words for "year" cohabit in a single sentence and do opposite work.

**Synthesis.** [2:259] is the Quran's clearest *experimental* statement on time: the same physical duration produces (a) zero-experience for the conscious subject, (b) zero-aging for some objects, (c) full-aging for others. **Duration is not a property of the duration; it is a property of what the divine will assigns to the duration.** This is not a relativity statement (nothing about reference frames); it is a theological *contingency* statement. *How much "year" is in a year* depends on what God has decreed for that particular *ʿām*.

## 8. The *Nasīʾ* Prohibition — [9:36–37] and the Destabilisation of the Year

> إِنَّ عِدَّةَ ٱلشُّهُورِ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ ٱثۡنَا عَشَرَ شَهۡرࣰا فِي كِتَٰبِ ٱللَّهِ يَوۡمَ خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضَ مِنۡهَآ أَرۡبَعَةٌ حُرُمࣱ … (9:36)

> إِنَّمَا ٱلنَّسِيٓءُ زِيَادَةࣱ فِي ٱلۡكُفۡرِۖ يُضَلُّ بِهِ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُواْ يُحِلُّونَهُۥ عَامࣰا وَيُحَرِّمُونَهُۥ عَامࣰا (9:37)

[9:36] establishes that the months — twelve, four sacred — are God's count *from the day He created the heavens and the earth*. The unit is *shahr*, the lunar month. [9:37] then declares the practice of *nasīʾ* (intercalation — the pre-Islamic insertion of a thirteenth month every few years to keep the lunar calendar synced with the solar seasons) as *ziyāda fī l-kufr* — "an addition to disbelief."

The legal effect is: the lunar year is 12 lunar months full stop. No intercalary month, no realignment with solar seasons. The Islamic ḥijrī year drifts ~11 days earlier each solar year, completing a full cycle through the seasons every ~33 solar years. *Ramaḍān* will be a winter month in one generation and a summer month in the next.

### 8.1 What this does to "the year" as a unit

Pre-Islamic Arabia had practical control of the year: by intercalating, they could keep harvests and pilgrimages in fixed seasons. The Quran takes that control away. The result is that the *year* (sana / ʿām) becomes:
- A pure 12-cycle of the moon — no longer tethered to solar seasons.
- A drifting unit relative to natural cycles (the year travels through the seasons).
- A counting-tool for stacking 12 months — but no longer a *natural* anchor.

[9:37] uses *ʿām* twice (lawful one *ʿām*, forbidden another *ʿām*) — exactly the calendar-cycle sense identified in §3.2. The *ʿām* is the unit being defended, but defended *as a counter*, not as a season-anchor. **The Quran rejects the *natural* year and keeps only the *ritual-counted* year.**

### 8.2 Why this matters for the disjoint-clocks finding

The Phase-2 finding was that the Quran refuses to use the year as a bridge between *shahr* (months) and *qarn* (generations). [9:36–37] explains *why*: a year that drifts cannot anchor. The year is locked into 12 lunar months but unlocked from solar seasons; it is a mathematical aggregation, not a natural unit. **The Quran refuses to bridge two clocks with a unit it has just deliberately destabilised.**

This is internally consistent. The *shahr* is anchored to a phenomenon (the moon). The *qarn* is anchored to a phenomenon (a generation of humans). The *yawm* is anchored to a phenomenon (sun-cycle). But the *sana / ʿām* is anchored to nothing physical — it is a pure count of twelve *shahr*. As a unit, the year is **purely arithmetic, not ostensive**. The Quran will not use a non-ostensive unit as the master-clock. The year *enters* the Quran as a counter; it never *anchors* anything.

### 8.3 The single co-occurrence of *shahr* and *sana* — [46:15] revisited

[46:15] says: *His mother carried him with hardship and gave birth to him with hardship; **his bearing and weaning is thirty months** (*thalāthūna shahran*) … until when he reaches **forty years** (*arbaʿīna sanatan*)…*

Note the unit-shift. The *small* biographical interval (gestation + nursing) is in *months*, the natural lunar unit. The *long* biographical interval (maturity) is in *years*, the arithmetic count. The shift happens precisely where the Quran stops measuring against natural cycles and starts measuring against accumulated counting. **The year is the unit you switch to when months get unwieldy.** That is its *only* function.

## 9. *Ḥisāb* — the Cycle-Line Bridge

The root *ḥsb* (ح-س-ب) appears 109 times across 8 lemmas. The two key nouns are *ḥisāb* (39 occurrences — "account / reckoning / calculation") and *ḥasīb* (4 — "the Reckoner / the One who suffices"). The verb *ḥasiba* (44 — "to count, to reckon, to suppose") is the substrate.

### 9.1 The two contexts of *ḥisāb*

**Cosmic / cyclical *ḥisāb*** (the cycle teaches counting):
- [10:5] — sun and moon in stations, *li-taʿlamū ʿadada al-sinīna **wa l-ḥisāb*** (that you may know the number of years and the *ḥisāb*).
- [17:12] — day and night as two signs, *li-taʿlamū ʿadada al-sinīna **wa l-ḥisāb*** (identical formula).
- [55:5] — *al-shamsu wa l-qamaru bi-**ḥusbān*** (sun and moon by reckoning) [different lemma but same root].
- [6:96] — *wa-jaʿala al-shamsa wa l-qamara **ḥusbānā*** (made the sun and moon a reckoning).

**Eschatological / linear *ḥisāb*** (the line audits the count):
- [38:16, 38:26, 38:53, 14:41, 40:17, 40:27] — *yawm al-ḥisāb* (the Day of Reckoning), repeatedly.
- [69:20, 69:26] — the resurrected calling out *ḥisābiyah* ("my account!").
- [88:26] — *ʿalaynā ḥisābahum* (their reckoning is on Us).
- [21:1] — *iqtaraba li-l-nāsi ḥisābuhum* (their reckoning has drawn near).
- [4:6, 4:86, 17:14, 33:39] — *ḥasīban* (sufficient as a reckoner).

**Provision / divine grant without *ḥisāb*** (the third register):
- [3:27, 3:37, 24:38, 38:39, 39:10, 40:40, 65:8] — *bi-ghayri ḥisāb* / *yarzuqu man yashāʾ bi-ghayri ḥisāb* (gives provision without reckoning). The divine *gift* is precisely what is *not* counted.

### 9.2 The bridge structure

The same word does three jobs:

1. **In the cycle** — *ḥisāb* names the *cognitive faculty* the cycle is meant to train. Sun, moon, day, night exist *so that* you may *ḥisāb*. The cycle is pedagogical for a counting-skill.
2. **At the line** — *ḥisāb* names the *eschatological audit*. Everything you have done is counted on the Day of *ḥisāb*. Every soul gets its *kitāb* (book / record).
3. **In the divine generosity** — *bi-ghayri ḥisāb* names the *exception* to counting. Divine provision (rizq) and divine reward (ajr) are explicitly *not* subject to *ḥisāb*. The very faculty God trained you in (counting) is suspended in His own giving.

The structure is theological:
- **You count** because the cosmos was given to you as a counting-school.
- **You will be counted** because the line ends in audit.
- **God does not count** when He gives.

This is the **only legitimate cycle-line bridge in the Quranic time-grammar**. The cycle (sun-moon-day-night) and the line (Day of Reckoning) share the word *ḥisāb*. They do not share *yawm* (which works differently in cycle and in line), they do not share *sana* (which is purely a counter), they do not share *qarn* (which is exclusively past-historical). They share **only the act of accounting**.

### 9.3 *Ḥasīb* as divine name

The four occurrences of *ḥasīb* ([4:6, 4:86, 17:14, 33:39]) are notable. The word means simultaneously "reckoner" and "sufficient." In [17:14] (*kafā bi-nafsika al-yawma ʿalayka ḥasībā* — "sufficient is your soul against you today as a reckoner") the word collapses *the one doing the count* and *what is enough*. The reckoner does not need anyone else; the count is sufficient unto itself. **God is the *Sufficient-Reckoner*: the count, when He performs it, is final and needs no second opinion.**

### 9.4 What this means for the year-question

The Quran offers the believer two counting-tasks:
- **Count the years and the *ḥisāb*** ([10:5], [17:12]) — the cyclical, the calendrical.
- **Be ready for the day of *ḥisāb*** ([38:16] et al.) — the eschatological, the linear.

The *years* (sinīn) are the legitimate object of cyclical counting. They are what you tally. But the *years themselves* are not the audit-unit. **You count years; you will be audited on deeds.** The year is the *training tool* for the counting-faculty; the *deed* is the *output* the counting-faculty will produce when called upon.

The Quran's separation of "what you count" (years) from "what you will be counted on" (deeds) is precise. It avoids the temptation of either:
- A pagan eternal-return (in which the cycle *is* the audit), or
- A nominalist atemporality (in which there is no count at all).

The cycle teaches you how to count. The line is what you are counted on. The year is how the cycle is aggregated. The deed is how the line is summed. **The year and the deed never meet.** This is the Quranic time-anthropology.

## 10. Synthesis — Years Count, but They Do Not Anchor

Three propositions integrate the year-and-scale findings:

### Proposition 1 — Years are devalued by design

The Quran has the year-vocabulary it needs (snw + Ewm = 27 tokens, comparable to *shahr* and *qarn*). It uses it. But it never grants the year:
- A calendrical anchor (no event in the Quran is dated by year).
- A natural anchor (the year is locked to the lunar count and unlocked from the solar season).
- A bridge function (the year never co-occurs with *qarn*, *Ajl*, *al-sāʿa*; co-occurs with *shahr* once, biographically).
- A measurement role (in the cosmological scale-verses, the year is what *you* count, not what God counts).

The year is what you do when you cannot use a more grounded unit. It is the *fall-back unit* of Quranic time — used when months would be too small and generations too large.

### Proposition 2 — The cosmological scale-verses are pedagogy, not physics

22:47 (1000 years = 1 Day with God), 32:5 (1000 years = 1 governance-cycle), 70:4 (50,000 years = 1 ascent-day) are not metaphysical equations. They are:
- Rhetorical responses to creature-impatience (22:47 directly answers "hasten the punishment").
- Scale-ratios that subordinate the human counter to a divine measure (the *mimmā taʿuddūn* qualifier explicitly attributes the counting to humans).
- Statements about *category-of-day*, not about *time-itself* (the *miqdār* noun preserves the ratio language).

The Quran is not doing scientific cosmology with these numbers. It is using *very large* year-counts to *embarrass* the human demand for fast resolution.

### Proposition 3 — Duration-collapse is the master pattern

Across the corpus, the same structure recurs:
- **309 years felt as a day or part of a day** (Sleepers, [18:19] vs [18:25]).
- **100 years felt as a day or part of a day** (the man at [2:259]).
- **An hour felt as zero-time** ([10:45], [79:46], at the resurrection).
- **A 1000-year day from God's side felt as no-delay** ([22:47]).
- **A whole life felt as an evening or its morning** ([79:46]).

Long human-time *systematically collapses* on the divine clock. The Quran is teaching that **duration as experienced is not a stable quantity**. What feels long is short to God; what feels short *will be* long under audit. The year-counts of the Quran are not *durations*; they are *measure-equivalences* between two clocks that produce radically different counts of the "same" interval.

### Proposition 4 — *Ḥisāb* is the only legitimate bridge

The cycle-line gap is bridged by exactly one word: *ḥisāb*. The cycle exists to train the counting-faculty; the line exists to apply it. The year is the *exercise unit* of the cycle, not the *bridge unit* between cycle and line. **The bridge is an act, not a unit.** Counting is what crosses; what is counted (years) is just the practice material.

This makes deep sense of the disjoint-clocks finding. The Quran refuses to *unitize* the bridge between cycle and line because the bridge is *operational* (you must perform the count, you will be audited on the deed) rather than *quantitative* (so many years equals so many generations). A unit could be measured externally; an operation must be lived.

### Proposition 5 — The Quran has a numerical theology of time

The year-numbers in the Quran are remarkable:
- **309** (Sleepers — the lunar/solar quantum)
- **950** (Noah — the cosmic-millennium-deficit)
- **100** (the sleeper of 2:259 — the human-century demonstration)
- **1000** (cosmological day-equation)
- **50,000** (eschatological ascent-day)
- **40** (the wandering, the maturity)
- **7** (Joseph's plenty / famine cycle)
- **2** (weaning)
- **biḍʿa** (a few — Joseph in prison, Romans' victory)

These are not random. They form a tier-structure:
- *Cosmic-round* (1000, 50,000) — assigned to divine days only.
- *Cosmic-near-miss* (309, 950) — assigned to human extremes when the cosmic round must not be reached.
- *Demonstration-numbers* (100, 40) — used as pedagogical intervals.
- *Prophetic-cycles* (7) — used in narrative.
- *Human-scale* (2, *biḍʿa*) — used in everyday biographical contexts.

The Quran's numbers are *precise* but their *meaning* requires divine interpretation. 309 is not just 309; it is 300 with the 9-correction. 950 is not just 950; it is 1000 minus 50. 100 is not just 100; it is *miʾata ʿām* in which food does not *yatasannah*. **Numbers in Quranic time-talk are always doing more than counting.**

## 11. Rabbit Holes Surfaced

This investigation opens several follow-on questions worth documenting for later phases:

1. **The *miqdār* concept.** The cosmological scale-verses use *miqdār* (measure, extent) as the bridge-noun between *yawm* and *sana*. *Miqdār* is from the *qdr* root (q-d-r), which also gives *qadar* (decree), *qudra* (power), *taqdīr* (determination). Is the *miqdār*-of-a-day the same conceptual object as the *qadar*-of-a-life? If so, the cosmological scale-verses are not about time-arithmetic but about divine decree-extent. Worth a dedicated *qdr* concept investigation.

2. **The *yatasannah* hapax.** A whole concept-investigation of derived verb-forms in time-vocabulary may be warranted. *Yatasannah* coins a new verb from *sana*; are there parallel coinages from *shahr*, *yawm*, *qarn*? If so, the Quran has a deliberate practice of *verbing* time-nouns to do specific theological work.

3. **The 50-year subtraction in 29:14.** Why exactly 50? 50 is also the *khamsīn* of 70:4 (50,000-year day). Is there a numerological link between the 50-year subtraction in Noah and the 50,000-year day of ascent? Both round numbers within near-cosmic constructions. Worth checking the *fifty* number-pattern across the corpus.

4. **Lunar/solar acknowledgement elsewhere.** [18:25] is the only place I have identified where the Quran clearly registers the lunar/solar conversion. Are there other quiet lunar/solar tells? Check *wa-jaʿalnāhu manāzil* (we made it stations) at [10:5, 36:39] — does the *manāzil* count refer to the 28 lunar mansions (a solar-tracking device)?

5. **The *bi-ghayri ḥisāb* exception-class.** Seven verses of "without reckoning" — all related to divine giving. This is the *negation* of *ḥisāb* in a way that should illuminate what counting *is* in Quranic theology. A focused trace would clarify whether *bi-ghayri ḥisāb* applies exclusively to *rizq* (provision) or extends to other domains.

6. **The duration-collapse formula.** *Yawman aw baʿḍa yawm* / *labithnā yawman aw baʿḍa yawm* / *mā labithū illā ʿashiyyatan aw ḍuḥāhā* / *ka-annahum yawma yarawnahā lam yalbathū illā ʿashiyyatan*. Build a complete catalog of every "they will think they tarried only…" formula. Hypothesis: this is a Quranic *meme* — a fixed liturgical-rhetorical move repeated to install a single eschatological insight.

7. **The *labitha* verb.** All the duration-collapse verses use *labitha* (to tarry / remain in place). Is this verb specifically chosen to flag durations-that-are-perceptually-collapsible? Compare with *makatha*, *baqiya*, *staqarra*, *aqāma* — does *labitha* uniquely bear the perception-collapse load?

8. **The biographical year-cluster.** Five verses use the year for biographical lifespan ([5:26, 20:40, 26:18, 29:14, 46:15]). All concern *prophets* (Moses, Moses, Moses, Noah, the unnamed pious "human" of 46:15). The biographical year is reserved for prophet-lives. Worth a focused trace.

9. **The Romans' "few years" prophecy at [30:4].** *Biḍʿi sinīn* — "between three and nine years" (the lexical sense of *biḍʿ*). This is the closest the Quran comes to a *future-historical date-prediction*. Worth investigating whether the deliberate vagueness (*biḍʿ* = 3-to-9, not a specific number) is the Quran defending itself against a falsifiable date — and whether this connects to the broader anti-anchoring of the year.

10. **The *ʿinda rabbika* preposition.** Used in 22:47 ("a Day with your Lord"). The same preposition does heavy lifting in *al-ʿilm ʿindahu* (the knowledge is with Him), *ummu l-kitāb ʿindahu* (the mother of the book is with Him), *ʿinda rabbihim yurzaqūn* (with their Lord they are provided). The *ʿinda*-preposition is the spatial-co-presence form of the binary grammar (per `research-binary-grammar.md`). Worth a focused trace of every *ʿinda llāh* / *ʿinda rabbika* construction to map the *cognitive-locative* theology of God's *side*.

## 12. Coda — The Year as Counter, the Deed as Audit

The Quran has every reason to make the year do bridging work — it has the vocabulary, it has the narratives that span centuries, it has the cosmological events that demand chronological scaffolding. It refuses. The year stays a counter and never becomes an anchor.

What the Quran installs *instead* is a counting-discipline (*ḥisāb*) trained by the cycle and audited on the line. The years exist to be tallied (10:5, 17:12); the deeds exist to be weighed (101:6–9); the bridge is the act of tallying-and-weighing. The 1000 years that look long to humans are short to God (22:47); the 100 years that the sleeper experienced are *no time at all* (2:259); the 309 years that the cave held are *a day or part of a day* (18:19). What the year measures *for the human* is not what it measures *with God*.

This is consistent with the broader Quranic time-architecture. *Time is not a thing; time is what God assigns to a duration.* The number 1000 is precise; what 1000-years *means* depends on whose clock is reading. The Quran's gift to its reader is not a measurement system but a posture toward measurement: count carefully, but know that you are not the one whose count finally matters.
