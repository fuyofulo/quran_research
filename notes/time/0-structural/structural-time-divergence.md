# Time-Bearing Ayah Divergence Analysis: Deep Dive Report

## Executive Summary

This analysis identifies 1,485 ayahs containing one or more of the 22 Quranic time roots (ywm, swE, Axr, xld, etc.)
and ranks them by translation divergence. The finding: **time-vocabulary ayahs are significantly more
contested than the corpus average**, with legal contexts showing even higher divergence than eschatological ones
—contradicting initial hypothesis. Khattab and Arberry are the primary outliers; certain time roots
(especially *mhl*, *SbH*, *xld*) generate persistent translator disagreement.

### Key Findings

- **1,485 time-bearing ayahs** analyzed across all 22 roots
- **Top contested root**: *mhl* (0.6689 avg divergence) — delay/respite
- **Most stable root**: *lyl* (0.5696 avg divergence) — night
- **Highest translator outlier rate**: Khattab (13.1% of time verses) — twice Saheeh's rate
- **Legal vs eschatological**: Legal ayahs slightly MORE contested (0.6211 vs 0.6005)
- **Most contested surah**: Surah 68 (Qalam) with 12 time-bearing ayahs, 0.7195 avg divergence

### Root Analysis: Hypothesis and Observations

#### Highly Contested Roots (>0.64)

**mhl (0.6689)** — Delay, respite, postponement
- Translators diverge most on temporal deferral concepts
- Likely spans multiple grammatical frames (noun vs verb, attribute vs state)
- High theological sensitivity: does Allah defer, grant respite, or impose delay?

**SbH (0.6561)** — Morning, forenoon, daybreak
- Eschatological context makes morning metaphorical vs literal ambiguous
- Temporal transitions in vision language ("in the morning" → figurative?)

**xld (0.6493)** — Eternal, abiding, dwelling forever
- This root appears in paradise/hellfire descriptions where translators vary on finality and perpetuity
- Philosophical disagreement: eternal dwelling (abstract) vs concrete 'staying'

**Hyn (0.6450)** — When, at which time, occasion
- Temporal conjunctions vary in English (when, while, as, whenever)
- Conditional vs narrative use produces systematic translator splits

#### Moderately Contested Roots (0.61–0.64)

**Ajl, Esr, gdw, qrn, Awl, bEd, qbl, Amd, Axr, Abd** — All cluster tightly at 0.614–0.631
- Broad agreement that these are 'standard' time concepts
- Minor stylistic variance rather than semantic schism

#### Most Stable Roots (<0.60)

**nhr (0.5844)** — Day/daytime (distinct from ywm/yawm)
- Clearer semantics, less theological weight

**lyl (0.5696)** — Night
- Most stable time root; translators agree on literal sense
- Minimal metaphorical extension in Quran

## Part 4: Legal vs Eschatological Divergence Split

**Legal surah time verses** (e.g., Surah 2, 4, 5): 375 ayahs, avg divergence **0.6211**

**Eschatological surah time verses** (e.g., Surah 51, 81, 93): 50 ayahs, avg divergence **0.6005**

### Counterintuitive Finding

**Legal contexts show HIGHER translator divergence (0.6211 vs 0.6005).**

This contradicts the corpus-wide hypothesis. Hypothesis revision:
- Legal time phrases (conditions, prescribed periods, waiting times) embed in complex grammatical chains
- Eschatological time uses are more isolated, more metaphorically stable
- Example: "then wait three days" (legal) has multiple translation frames; "the Day" (eschatological) is formulaic

### Divergence Metric Explanation

**Combined divergence** = weighted average of:
- Length variance (do translators produce texts of wildly different word count?)
- Jaccard similarity (do they share vocabulary?)
- Lexical disagreement (normalized count of unique words)

A score of 0.90+ indicates maximal translation schism.

## Part 7: Rabbit Holes and Synthesis Notes

### Observed Patterns for Phase-4 Synthesis

1. **Temporal Finality**: xld, Awl, bEd consistently diverge. Translators split on whether time-bound concepts
   should emphasize closure (end) or continuity (eternal). Theological implications.

2. **Modal Shift in Time**: fjr (future dawn), Ajl (hastening), qbl (waiting) — translators struggle when
   time-root combines with potential/modal frames. "What may come" vs "what comes."

3. **Eschatological Formulae**: Meccan surahs use ywm (day) formulaically in oath-frame. Yet divergence
   remains high—suggests oath-context itself is translation-difficult, not the time-root.

4. **Legal Complexity Hypothesis**: Medinan ayahs with time-roots embed in legal conditions (waiting periods,
   oath timing, divorce waiting). Multiple clauses = more divergence vectors. Test with 5-ayah legal samples.

5. **Arberry's Literary Inversion**: Arberry frequently inverts SVO order for poetic effect, turning temporal
   markers into fronted positions. Worth stylistic study.
