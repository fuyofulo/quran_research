# وَقْت (wqt): The Appointed Instant — Deep Concept Analysis

## Headline Finding: A Tiny Vocabulary, Carrying the Architecture of Ritual and Eschatology Together

The root **wqt (و-ق-ت)** appears only **13 times** in the Quran, but those 13 occurrences quietly do two of the heaviest jobs in Islamic theology: they fix the **moments of the daily prayer** and they fix the **moment of the Hour**. The same root that names the *appointment instant* of ṣalāt names the *appointment instant* of resurrection. Five-times-daily worship and end-of-history are, in Quranic vocabulary, **two scales of the same kind of event** — not bounded *durations*, not finite *deadlines*, but **timed appointments that occur at a fixed instant set by Someone**.

Three structural facts make this root distinctive:

1. **The dominant lemma is not *waqt* itself but *mīqāt*** (the m-prefix instance noun, "appointment"). Of 13 occurrences, 8 are *mīqāt*, only 3 are bare *waqt*, 1 is the passive participle *mawqūt*, and 1 is the rare passive verb *uqqitat*. The Quran prefers the *deverbal noun of appointment* over the bare time-noun. **It does not say "the time of"; it says "the appointment of."**
2. **The signature collocation `يَوْمِ ٱلْوَقْتِ ٱلْمَعْلُومِ`** ("the Day of the known time") appears verbatim at **[15:38] and [38:81]** — both in Iblīs's reprieve narrative, both spoken by Allah granting the deferral. The phrase yokes three time-roots in three words: *yawm* (day) + *waqt* (instant) + *maʿlūm* (known passive participle of *Elm*). It is the densest temporal expression in the Quran.
3. **The single occurrence of *mawqūt* — [4:103] — defines the obligation of prayer.** *Inna al-ṣalāta kānat ʿalā al-muʾminīna kitāban mawqūtan*: "indeed prayer has been upon the believers a *time-fixed writ*." This single word has carried the entire jurisprudence of prayer-times for fourteen centuries. Five-times-daily salāt is not derived from a verse with the word *waqt* used twenty times; it is derived from this one passive participle at [4:103].

The root sits silently inside the Quran's "appointed time" cluster, but unlike *Ajl* (56 occurrences, dominant) or *ywm* (475 occurrences, ubiquitous), *wqt* is **lexically minor and theologically dense** — a small vocabulary doing disproportionate structural work.

## Statistics and Morphological Reading

**Quantitative profile:**
- **13 total occurrences** across **13 ayahs** in **10 surahs**
- **4 lemmas:**
  - `miyqa`t` (مِيقَات, "appointment / appointed time") — **8 occurrences** (61.5%)
  - `waqot` (وَقْت, "instant, time") — **3 occurrences** (23.1%)
  - `m~awoquwt` (مَوْقُوت, "time-fixed") — **1 occurrence** ([4:103])
  - `>uq~itato` (أُقِّتَتْ, "were time-appointed", Form II passive verb) — **1 occurrence** ([77:11])
- **POS distribution:** 11 N, 1 ADJ, 1 V — overwhelmingly nominal, like *Ajl*
- **Revelation:** 11 Meccan, 2 Medinan — heavily Meccan (the Medinan two are [2:189] *mawāqīt* for hajj/lunar months and [4:103] *mawqūt* for prayer — the **legal-ritual instances**)
- **Top surah concentration:** Al-A'rāf (4 occurrences — the Moses-Sinai cluster), every other surah has 1

The dominance of *mīqāt* (8/13) is the **first morphological signal**. *Mīqāt* is an *ism al-zamān/makān* on a verbal-noun pattern (*mifʿāl*) that names the *time-or-place at which something is timed*. It is grammatically a "device-noun" — like *miftāḥ* (key) is the device-of-opening, *mīqāt* is the device-of-appointing. Where *Ajl* names the *terminating boundary* and *Amd* names the *running stretch*, *mīqāt* names the **fixed point itself** — the meeting-instant where appointer and appointed meet.

The Form II passive verb *uqqitat* ([77:11]) is the only verbal occurrence in the entire Quran, and even it is passive. The active verb \**waqqata* ("to appoint a time") is **never used**. The agent is structurally suppressed — the same apophatic grammar identified in *Ajl* (*ajjala* used only 2× of 56) and visible across the time vocabulary.

[4:103]: *kitāban mawqūtan* — "a *time-fixed* writ." Passive participle. The agent of the *waqqata* is unstated; the writ is **timed by Allah**. This is the lone foundation of the prayer-times jurisprudence.

### Lemma 4: أُقِّتَتْ (>uq~itato) — 1 occurrence

[77:11]: *wa-iḏā al-rusulu uqqitat* — "and when the messengers are time-appointed." Form II passive 3f.sg verb. The grammatical subject is *al-rusul* (the messengers); the appointing agent is suppressed (Allah). This is the only finite verb in the entire root.

## The *Mīqāt* Formula — Three Theatres of Appointment

What makes *mīqāt* extraordinary is that the **same word** spans three radically different scales:

### 1. Sinai — the prophet meets his Lord

[7:142–143, 155]: Moses ascends Sinai for *mīqātu Rabbihi* — "his Lord's appointment." The *mīqāt* is at once a **time and a place**: forty nights, on the mountain. [7:143]'s *jāʾa Mūsā li-mīqātinā* — "Moses came **to** Our *mīqāt*" — uses the preposition *li* with motion, treating the appointment as a destination that can be *arrived at*. Most translators render this "appointed tryst" (Pickthall, Arberry) — and the romantic-sounding word is correct: the *mīqāt* is the meeting-point. **Allah keeps appointments.**

### 2. The Hour / Day of Decision — humanity meets its Lord

[44:40], [56:50], [78:17]: *yawm al-faṣl* (the Day of Decision) is described as the universal *mīqāt*. [44:40] is the strongest: *inna yawma al-faṣli **mīqātuhum** ajmaʿīn* — "the Day of Decision is **their *mīqāt* — all of them**." The *mīqāt* of Sinai is Moses's appointment with his Lord; the *mīqāt* of *yawm al-faṣl* is humanity's. The grammatical structure is identical. **Resurrection is, in Quranic vocabulary, structurally analogous to a prophetic Sinai-tryst — scaled up from one prophet to all of humanity.** This is one of the most underappreciated theological moves in the Quran's lexicon.

[26:38]: even the magicians' showdown with Moses is gathered *li-mīqāti yawmin maʿlūmin* — "for the *mīqāt* of a known day." Pharaoh's stage is, lexically, an inverted *yawm al-faṣl*: a day of decision that decides for the wrong side.

### 3. The lunar months / hajj — the community keeps appointment with itself

[2:189]: *yasʾalūnaka ʿani al-ahillati qul hiya **mawāqītu** li-l-nāsi wa-l-ḥajj* — "they ask you about the new moons; say: they are *mawāqīt* for people and the pilgrimage." The plural *mawāqīt* is unique here. It names the **calendrical infrastructure** that makes ritual possible. Hajj cannot occur without a *mīqāt* (pilgrim assembly point and time); the same root that names Sinai names the lunar calendar. Note the pluralization — at this scale the *mīqāt* repeats monthly; at the eschatological scale it occurs once.

The genius of the root is that **all three theatres share one word**. A prophet's mountain-tryst, a community's monthly calendar, and the universal eschatological assembly are all *mawāqīt*. The Quran refuses to invent separate vocabulary for them. They are the same *kind* of event at three scales: *appointed instant of meeting, fixed by God, kept by the keeper of appointments*.

## *Yawm al-Waqt al-Maʿlūm* — The Densest Temporal Expression

The phrase **يَوْمِ ٱلْوَقْتِ ٱلْمَعْلُومِ** ("the Day of the known time") appears verbatim at **[15:38]** and **[38:81]**, both in the same narrative arc: Allah grants Iblīs reprieve until "the Day of the known time."

> [15:36–38] *qāla rabbi fa-anẓirnī ilā yawmi yubʿaṯūn. qāla fa-innaka mina al-munẓarīn. ilā yawmi al-waqti al-maʿlūm.*
>
> "He said: My Lord, then grant me reprieve until the Day they are resurrected. He said: You are among the reprieved. Until the Day of the known time."

Three observations:

1. **Three time-roots in three consecutive words.** *Yawm* (the day-content), *waqt* (the instant), *maʿlūm* (the participle of *Elm*, "known"). The Quran piles its temporal vocabulary into a single phrase to mark the *one* moment that closes the cosmic-litigation arc with Iblīs. The redundancy is theological: *day* (the eschatological frame) + *instant* (the precise point) + *known* (to Allah, not to creation).
2. **Iblīs's grammar.** He asks for reprieve *ilā yawmi yubʿaṯūn* — "until the day they are raised." Allah grants it but **changes the formula** to *ilā yawmi al-waqti al-maʿlūm*. Allah does not grant the requested deferral; He grants a *different* one. Iblīs asked for the resurrection day; Allah named a moment whose timing only He knows. This rewriting matters: classical commentators split on whether *yawm al-waqt al-maʿlūm* is the resurrection or the **prior moment** of Iblīs's death/destruction. The grammatical underdetermination is itself a feature.
3. **Compare to [26:38] and [56:50]** — both use *yawmin maʿlūmin* (without *waqt*) for the Day of Decision. The full triple *waqt al-maʿlūm* is reserved for the **Iblīs reprieve**. This is a deliberate marking — Iblīs's terminus has its own stamp.

## Mawqūt — The One Word That Built Five-Times-Daily Prayer

[4:103] in full:
> *fa-iḏā qaḍaytumu al-ṣalāta fa-ḏkurū Allāha qiyāman wa-quʿūdan wa-ʿalā junūbikum, fa-iḏā ṭmaʾnantum fa-aqīmū al-ṣalāta, **inna al-ṣalāta kānat ʿalā al-muʾminīna kitāban mawqūtan***.

"When you have completed the prayer, remember Allah standing, sitting, and on your sides; then when you are secure, establish the prayer. **Indeed, prayer has been upon the believers a time-fixed writ.**"

The phrase *kitāban mawqūtan* fuses two roots: *ktb* (writ) and *wqt* (time-fixed). It is the **structural twin of [3:145]'s *kitāban muʾajjalan*** ("a term-fixed writ" of death). Compare:

| Verse | Construction | Subject | Meaning |
|-------|-------------|---------|---------|
| [3:145] | *kitāb muʾajjal* | death of a soul | a writ deferred-to-its-term |
| [4:103] | *kitāb mawqūt* | prayer of believers | a writ fixed-at-its-times |
| [2:282] | *kitāb* + *ajal musamman* | debt contract | a writing tied to a stated term |

The pattern is now visible: **kitāb (writing) is the universal substrate, and the temporal participle (mawqūt / muʾajjal / musammā) specifies the kind of bounded-ness applied.** Prayer is *time-fixed*; death is *term-fixed*; debt is *named-term-fixed*. Three different participles, three different temporal geometries, one underlying ontology — *the writ-of-bounded-obligation*.

The classical jurisprudence of five fixed prayer times (fajr, ẓuhr, ʿaṣr, maghrib, ʿishāʾ) was derived not from a verse listing them but from this single word *mawqūt* combined with [11:114], [17:78], [20:130]. **One passive participle bears the entire architecture.** This is the maximal compression of theological grammar.

## Why So Few Occurrences? — The Negative Space Question

If "appointed time" is so central to Islam — five daily prayers, lunar calendar, hajj seasons, the Hour — why does *wqt* appear only 13 times?

Three working hypotheses:

1. **The work is done by other roots.** *Ywm* (475×) carries day-content; *Ajl* (56×) carries deadline; *sāʿa* (48×) carries hour-as-event; *Hyn* (~34×) carries indefinite while. *Wqt* is reserved for the **specific case** of *appointment-instant set by an appointer*. The other roots cover most temporal mentions; *wqt* enters only when the **appointment-keeping** sense is required.

2. **The Quran avoids enumerating ritual times verbally.** The *number* of daily prayers is not stated; the *clock-times* are not stated. The Quran prefers to say *kitāb mawqūt* and let the Prophet's practice fix the schedule. **Lexical sparseness is a hermeneutic choice**: by saying once "time-fixed," and not saying "five times," the verse permits the prayers to remain a *prophetic transmission* rather than a *Qur'anic enumeration*.

3. **Mīqāt does not need repetition because it is conceptually tight.** *Ajl* has 19 referents (souls, nations, sun, moon, debts, marriages, embryos, sacrificial animals…). It needs frequency to span that range. *Mīqāt* has essentially three referents (Sinai, Hour, lunar months). One word per scale per occurrence is enough.

The negative space is itself a finding: **a root can be theologically central and lexically minor**. Centrality is not measured by frequency.

## Translation Divergence

The translators reveal interpretive choices most sharply where *mīqāt* takes its eschatological sense.

**[44:40]** *inna yawma al-faṣli mīqātuhum ajmaʿīn:*

| Translator | Rendering |
|------------|-----------|
| Saheeh | "the Day of Judgement is the **appointed time** for them all" |
| Pickthall | "the Day of Decision is the **term** for all of them" |
| Khattab | "the Day of ˹Final˺ Decision is the **time appointed** for all" |
| Arberry | "the Day of Decision shall be their **appointed time**, all together" |

Pickthall's "term" is the misleading rendering — it imports *Ajl*'s deadline-flavor into a word that means *appointment-instant*. The other three preserve the meeting-flavor.

**[7:143]** *jāʾa Mūsā li-mīqātinā:*

| Translator | Rendering |
|------------|-----------|
| Saheeh | "Moses arrived at Our **appointed time**" |
| Pickthall | "Moses came to Our **appointed tryst**" |
| Khattab | "Moses came at the **appointed time**" |
| Arberry | "Moses came to Our **appointed time**" |

Pickthall's "tryst" is the most semantically faithful — *mīqāt* is **a meeting**, not merely a moment. The other translators flatten it to "appointed time" and lose the **personal-encounter** sense that the *mīʿād* / *mīqāt* family carries.

**[15:38] / [38:81]** *yawmi al-waqti al-maʿlūm:*

All four translators converge on "appointed Day" or "Day of the time appointed." None ventures the more interpretive reading "the day of the time-known-only-to-Him" — though that is what *al-maʿlūm* (passive of *Elm*) implies. The shared restraint is itself a finding: translators refuse to specify *who* knows.

**[2:189]** *mawāqītu li-l-nāsi wa-l-ḥajj:*

| Translator | Rendering |
|------------|-----------|
| Saheeh | "**measurements of time** for the people and for Hajj" |
| Pickthall | "**fixed seasons** for mankind and for the pilgrimage" |
| Khattab | "a means for people to **determine time** and pilgrimage" |
| Arberry | "**appointed times** for the people, and the Pilgrimage" |

The widest spread of any *wqt* verse — from "measurements" (Saheeh) to "fixed seasons" (Pickthall) to "means to determine" (Khattab) to "appointed times" (Arberry). The disagreement reflects the lexical fact that *mawāqīt* in [2:189] does *both* duties at once: a **calendrical measure** (passively informational) **and** a **set of ritual appointments** (theologically active). No single English word holds both.

## Negative Space — Can a *Mīqāt* Be Moved?

Asked of *Ajl* in the sibling note, the answer was a flat no with one exception. Asked of *wqt*, the answer is similar but with subtler texture:

- **[7:143]** Moses comes *to* his appointment — humans arrive at the *mīqāt*; they do not move it.
- **[44:40], [56:50], [78:17]** the eschatological *mīqāt* is universal and not negotiable: *ajmaʿīn* ("all of them") collapses individual variation.
- **[7:187]** *lā yujallīhā li-waqtihā illā huwa* — "**none reveals its [the Hour's] time except Him**." The Hour's *waqt* is **knowable only to Allah**. This is the strongest negative-space claim of the root: even the *information about* the *waqt* is divinely sequestered.

The exception is **[15:38] / [38:81]** itself: Iblīs *requests* a deferral and *receives* one. But the deferral is *Allah's appointment of a different waqt*, not *Iblīs's modification* of an existing one. The agency remains with the appointer.

A subtle finding: the verb-form *uqqitat* ([77:11]) is **passive past tense** (*were time-appointed*). The messengers are appointed *to* a time. They do not appoint themselves. This is the inverse of human freedom — the messengers, the most active agents in salvation history, are grammatically the *patients* of timing.

## Cross-Reference to Tripartite Grammar (Ajl × wqt × Amd)

Tested against this analysis, the tripartite hypothesis from `concept-Ajl.md` **holds and sharpens**:

- **Ajl** = endpoint (until-when?). The thing **expires**. *Ajalun musamman.*
- **wqt** = instant (at-what-moment?). The thing **occurs**. *Mīqātun maʿlūm / kitābun mawqūt.*
- **Amd** = stretch (for-how-long?). The thing **runs**. *Amadan baʿīdan / ṭāla al-amad.*

Cross-checks specific to *wqt*:

1. **[44:40]** uses *mīqāt*, not *ajal*. Why? Because the focus is on the *meeting-instant* of judgment, not on the *expiry* of the world. To say *yawm al-faṣli ajaluhum* would be wrong — it would imply this is the *deadline of their world*; the verse instead says it is their *appointment*. The two are not synonyms even though both refer to the same Day.
2. **[4:103]** uses *mawqūt*, not *muʾajjal*. Prayer is **fixed at instants**, not **deferred to a deadline**. The morphology selects the right geometry.
3. **[2:189]** uses *mawāqīt*, not *ājāl*. Lunar months are **recurring instants**, not **terminating periods**. *Ājāl* would imply each month *expires* into the next; *mawāqīt* implies each month is a *meeting-point* for ritual.
4. **[7:187]** uses *waqt* for the Hour — the Hour is treated as an *instant*, not a *duration*. This matters because *al-sāʿa* (the Hour) is itself an *event-instant* word. The pairing *al-sāʿa li-waqtihā* is doubly instant-coded.

Where the hypothesis stretches: **[78:17]** *inna yawma al-faṣli kāna mīqātan* — *mīqāt* takes an indefinite predicate of *yawm*, where the geometry softens. A "day" is not strictly an instant. But the *meeting-property* is preserved: it is *the day at which the appointment occurs*, not *the day which is the duration of judgment*.

The complementary distribution is robust. **Each root occupies one slot in a three-coordinate temporal grammar.** *wqt* owns the instant.

## Co-occurrence with Passive Participles — Heavy Passivization

Across the 13 occurrences:

- **maʿlūm** (مَعْلُوم, "known") modifies *waqt* in [15:38], [38:81]; modifies *yawm* paired with *mīqāt* in [26:38], [56:50] — **4 of 13 occurrences** carry the *maʿlūm* tag.
- **mawqūt** (مَوْقُوت, "time-fixed") at [4:103] is itself the passive participle.
- **uqqitat** ([77:11]) is the only finite verb and it is **passive**.

Of 13 occurrences, **at least 6 are governed by a passive-participle frame** (mawqūt, maʿlūm twice, mawāqīt as participial-form noun, uqqitat passive verb, and the *mīqātu Rabbihi* of [7:142] which is implicitly *appointed by his Lord*). The pattern of *Ajl*'s *musammā* is the same pattern operating here under different participles: **the time vocabulary is structurally passivized to leave Allah as the unstated agent.**

## Rabbit Holes

1. **The *kitāb*-participle pattern.** Three writs, three temporal participles: *mawqūt* (prayer), *muʾajjal* (death), *musammā* (debt-term). A separate study should map *every kitāb*-modifier in the Quran. The participle grammar may extend further than these three.

2. **Prophetic *mīqāt* vs eschatological *mīqāt*.** The same word does both. Trace: is the Quran framing the Hour as *a Sinai for everyone*? Cross-reference the theophany at [7:143] (*tajallā Rabbuhū li-l-jabal*, the mountain crumbles) with apocalyptic theophany verses ([89:22] *jāʾa Rabbuka*, [69:17] etc.). The *mīqāt* is where the meeting happens; the theophany is what happens there.

3. **The non-existence of an active verb.** \**waqqata* is never used. Compare to *Ajl* where *ajjala* appears 2× of 56, and *Amd* where no verb exists. The temporal-appointment vocabulary is **systematically deverbalized**. Hypothesis: appointment is conceived as an *already-fixed feature of creation*, not as an *ongoing act*. The verb would imply timing-as-process; the participle implies timing-as-fact.

4. **[2:189] *mawāqīt* and pre-Islamic calendrics.** The plural occurs only here. Worth a comparative study with pre-Islamic Arabian lunar reckoning — what does it mean that the Quran adopts the *mawāqīt* vocabulary for new moons but reframes it as *li-l-nās wa-l-ḥajj* (for people and pilgrimage)?

5. **The *yawm al-waqt al-maʿlūm* ambiguity.** Is Iblīs's terminus (a) the resurrection, (b) his death/destruction, (c) some intermediate reckoning? The classical commentaries split. The grammatical underdetermination matches the *ajal musamman ʿindahu* ambiguity at [6:2]. Both verses **withhold the referent on purpose**.

6. **Cross-reference with *sāʿa*.** [7:187] uses both *al-sāʿa* (the Hour) and *waqt* in one verse. *Sāʿa* is the *event-instant*; *waqt* is the *instant-of-appointment*. Are they synonyms here, or does *waqt* specify the *appointment-aspect* of *sāʿa*? A separate concept analysis of *sāʿa* would test this.

7. **The *li-* preposition with *mīqāt*.** Five of eight *mīqāt* occurrences are prefixed with *li-* (to/for): *li-mīqātinā* (3×), *li-mīqāti* (1×), *li-waqtihā* (1×). The dative preposition treats the appointment as a **direction of motion** — humans/messengers move *toward* the *mīqāt*. This is the geometry of pilgrimage ported into eschatology.
