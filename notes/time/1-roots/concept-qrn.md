# قرن (qrn): Generation, Horn, Companion, Yoke — Deep Concept Analysis

## Headline Finding: The Quran's Most Honest Polysemy — One Root, Five Lemmas, At Least Three Apparently Unrelated Senses, All Sharing a Hidden Core of *Things-Bound-Together*

The root **qrn (ق-ر-ن)** is the most semantically promiscuous root in the Quranic corpus that is *not* a metaphysical heavyweight. Across its 36 occurrences and 5 distinct lemmas, the same three consonants do all of the following work:

- **qarn** (قَرْن, 23×) — a *generation*; an *age*; a *century*; a *people-of-an-era*. Plural *qurūn*.
- **qarn** (قَرْن, dual *al-qarnayn*, 3×) — a *horn*. Found only in *Dhū al-Qarnayn*, "the Two-Horned One" ([18:83, 86, 94]).
- **qarīn** (قَرِين, 8×) — a *close companion*; an *associate*; in eschatological contexts, *the demonic familiar*.
- **muqarranīn** (مُقَرَّنِين, 3×) — *bound together / chained in pairs* (passive participle of Form II *qarrana*).
- **muqrinīn** (مُقْرِنِين, 1×) — *those who can master / yoke* (Form IV active participle).
- **muqtarinīn** (مُقْتَرِنِين, 1×) — *those joined / accompanying in pairs* (Form VIII).

Five lemmas, four morphological forms, and what looks at first like four unrelated meanings: a generation in time, a horn on a head, a companion at one's side, and a coupling-by-chains. **They are not unrelated.** The classical lexicographers (Ibn Manẓūr, *Lisān al-ʿArab*) gloss the root's core as *al-jamʿ bayn al-shayʾayn* — "the joining-together of two things." A generation is people *yoked* to an age. A horn is one of a *pair* on a head (and the dual form *qarnayn* makes the pairing explicit). A companion is one *yoked* to another. And the chain-bound (*muqarranīn*) of hellfire are literally paired together. The root is **the grammar of two-things-bound-as-one** — and it produces calendrical, anatomical, social, and eschatological meanings as variations on that single move.

Three structural facts make qrn distinctive:

1. **Polysemy without ambiguity in context.** Of the 36 occurrences, every single one is unambiguous on a first read of its ayah. The root is wildly polysemous *in the lexicon* and almost never confusing *in the verse*. This is not a quirk; it is what the root is *for*. Arabic appears to have intentionally let the same consonantal pattern carry related-but-distinct senses, with context as the disambiguator. The Quran exploits this without strain.

2. **The "destruction of prior generations" formula is the dominant rhetorical use.** Of the 23 occurrences of *qarn / qurūn*, **at least 14** sit inside the formula *kam ahlaknā min qarnin* / *ahlaknā al-qurūna* ("how many a generation We destroyed / We destroyed the generations"). This is the most repeated *negative* historical formula in the entire Quran. Generations exist in the Quran primarily *in their having ended*.

3. **The horn shows up only in a name, never as an object.** Of the three *al-qarnayn* occurrences, all three are the title of a single figure: Dhū al-Qarnayn. The Quran never describes an animal's horns, never uses the horn metaphorically for power outside this name, never refers to a horn-shaped object. A whole semantic field of the root collapses to one proper noun.

The 36 occurrences span **20 surahs**, weighted heavily Meccan (Al-Anʿām, Yūnus, Hūd, Al-Isrāʾ, Al-Kahf, Maryam, Ṭā-Hā, Al-Muʾminūn, Al-Furqān, Al-Qaṣaṣ, Al-Sajda, Yāsīn, Al-Ṣāffāt, Ṣād, Fuṣṣilat, Al-Zukhruf, Al-Aḥqāf, Qāf — eighteen Meccan surahs). Only Al-Nisāʾ, Al-Ibrāhīm and a handful of others are Medinan. The qrn root is fundamentally a Meccan-period vocabulary item — which fits its rhetorical role: warning the Meccans by reference to past peoples and their fates.

## Statistics

- **Occurrences:** 36
- **Lemmas:** 5
- **Ayahs:** 34
- **Surahs:** 20
- **POS distribution:** 35 N, 1 ADJ — entirely nominal/participial; no finite verbs
- **Lemma split:** *qarn* 23 (64%), *qarīn* 8 (22%), *muqarranīn* 3, *muqrinīn* 1, *muqtarinīn* 1
- **Top co-occurring roots:** qbl (before, 38.2%), hlk (perish/destroy, 35.3%), qwl (say/speak, 35.3%), kwn (be, 26.5%), bEd (after, 20.6%), byn (between, 14.7%), Axr (last/other, 14.7%), rAy (see, 14.7%), Awl (first/former, 14.7%), n$A (originate/produce, 11.8%), jrm (sin, 11.8%), *kr (remember, 11.8%)

The co-occurrence profile is the entire thesis. **qbl + hlk + bEd + Awl + Axr + rAy + n$A** = the lexical signature of *temporal-displacement-and-judgment*. "Before" + "destroy" + "after" + "first" + "last" + "see" + "originate" — generations are *seen-from-the-current-moment as having-been-originated-and-then-destroyed-before-and-replaced-after*. There is no other way to talk about *qarn* in this Quran.

The conspicuous absence in the top co-occurrences: no *zaman* (time), no *ʿām* (year), no *yawm* in the top tier. *qarn* lives in narrative-historical grammar, not in calendrical time. This separates it cleanly from $hr (the previous analysis) — which lives in legal-ritual grammar and never co-occurs with *qarn*.

## Lemma Table

### Lemma 1 — قَرْن (qarn / qurūn) — 23 occurrences

The dominant lemma. Singular *qarnin* (indefinite) and plural *al-qurūn* (definite) carry both the *generation/age* sense (20×) and the *horn/Two-Horned-One* sense (3×).

**Generation sense (20 occurrences):** [6:6 ×2], [10:13], [11:116], [17:17], [19:74], [19:98], [20:51], [20:128], [23:31], [23:42], [25:38], [28:43], [28:45], [28:78], [32:26], [36:31], [38:3], [46:17], [50:36]

**Horn sense (3 occurrences, all dual *al-qarnayn*):** [18:83], [18:86], [18:94] — exclusively the title *Dhū al-Qarnayn*.

The morphology of the generation sense is itself instructive:
- *qarnin* (indefinite singular) — appears with *kam* "how many" in the destruction formula: [6:6], [19:74], [19:98], [38:3], [50:36]
- *qarnan* (indefinite accusative) — appears with *anshaʾnā* "we produced/originated": [6:6], [23:31]
- *qurūnan* (indefinite plural acc.) — appears with *anshaʾnā* in the plural: [23:42], [25:38], [28:45]
- *al-qurūna* / *al-qurūni* (definite plural) — appears with *ahlaknā* "we destroyed" in the bulk formulaic pattern: [10:13], [11:116], [17:17], [20:51], [20:128], [28:43], [28:78], [32:26], [36:31], [46:17]

Notice the morphological partition: **definite plural for the destroyed generations, indefinite singular for the next generation produced.** The grammar mirrors the theology — a *named, accumulated* past versus a *new, anonymous* successor.

### Lemma 2 — قَرِين (qarīn) — 8 occurrences

The *companion / associate / familiar*. [4:38 ×2], [37:51], [41:25], [43:36], [43:38], [50:23], [50:27].

The plural *quranāʾ* appears at [41:25]. This lemma is dominated by **eschatological pairing** — every occurrence except [4:38] sits in a Day-of-Judgment scene where a person is paired with their companion (variously: a satan, a witness-angel, an earthly friend now revealed as a tempter). The *qarīn* is the entity who was *yoked* to you in life and now stands beside you in judgment. Translators split sharply on this term — see Translation Divergence below.

### Lemma 3 — مُقَرَّنِين (muqarranīn) — 3 occurrences

Form II passive participle: *bound-together-in-pairs / chained*. [14:49], [25:13], [38:38]. All three describe the wicked or the jinn in chains: at [14:49] *taraʾ l-mujrimīna yawmaʾidhin muqarranīn fī l-aṣfād* ("you will see the criminals on that Day bound together in shackles"). The Form II *qarrana* makes the joining causative-intensive: *to deliberately yoke two-or-more*. This is the most concrete deployment of the root's "things-bound" core meaning.

### Lemma 4 — مُقْرِنِين (muqrinīn) — 1 occurrence

Form IV active participle: *those who can yoke / master / bring under control*. [43:13]: *subḥāna lladhī sakhkhara lanā hādhā wa-mā kunnā lahu muqrinīn* ("Glory to the One who has subjected this to us, for we could not have *mastered* it"). The verse is the prayer one recites mounting a riding animal or boarding a ship — the recognition that *we are not the ones with the strength to yoke this beast/vessel under us; God is.* This single occurrence gives the root its clearest *active* sense: to *be capable of pairing-with-and-controlling*.

### Lemma 5 — مُقْتَرِنِين (muqtarinīn) — 1 occurrence

Form VIII active participle: *those joined / accompanying in pairs*. [43:53]: a sneer from Pharaoh — *if Moses is really sent, why do bracelets of gold not adorn him, or why do angels not come with him in escort* (*muqtarinīn*). The Form VIII reflexivizes the joining — angels who come *of their own accord, in formation, paired*. The single occurrence is rhetorical: Pharaoh demands the visible insignia of *qrn*-style coupling as proof of legitimacy.

**The five lemmas form a complete morphological paradigm for the root's core action: noun (qarn — a yoked-thing), epithet (qarīn — the yoke-mate), Form II passive (muqarranīn — those forcibly yoked), Form IV active (muqrinīn — those able to yoke), Form VIII active (muqtarinīn — those self-joined in pairs). The Quran exhausts the root's grammar.**

## The Generation-Destruction Formula — Every Variant

The formula *kam ahlaknā min qarnin* and its plural cousin *ahlaknā al-qurūna* anchor the generation-sense. Here are all 14 variants:

| Verse | Arabic stem | English (Saheeh) |
|---|---|---|
| [6:6] | *a-lam yaraw kam ahlaknā min qablihim min qarnin* | "Have they not seen how many generations We destroyed before them" |
| [10:13] | *wa-laqad ahlaknā l-qurūna min qablikum lammā ẓalamū* | "We had already destroyed generations before you when they wronged" |
| [11:116] | *fa-lawlā kāna mina l-qurūni min qablikum* | "If only there had been among the generations before you …" |
| [17:17] | *wa-kam ahlaknā mina l-qurūni min baʿdi Nūḥin* | "How many have We destroyed from the generations after Noah" |
| [19:74] | *wa-kam ahlaknā qablahum min qarnin* | "And how many a generation have We destroyed before them" |
| [19:98] | *wa-kam ahlaknā qablahum min qarnin* | "And how many We destroyed before them of generations" |
| [20:128] | *kam ahlaknā qablahum mina l-qurūni* | "How many generations We destroyed before them" |
| [28:43] | *baʿdi mā ahlaknā l-qurūna l-ūlā* | "after We had destroyed the former generations" |
| [28:78] | *anna Allāha qad ahlaka min qablihi mina l-qurūni* | "Allah had destroyed before him of generations" |
| [32:26] | *kam ahlaknā min qablihim mina l-qurūni* | "how many generations We destroyed before them" |
| [36:31] | *kam ahlaknā qablahum mina l-qurūni* | "how many generations We destroyed before them" |
| [38:3] | *kam ahlaknā min qablihim min qarnin* | "How many a generation have We destroyed before them" |
| [46:17] | *qad khalati l-qurūnu min qablī* | "generations before me have already passed on" |
| [50:36] | *wa-kam ahlaknā qablahum min qarnin* | "how many a generation before them did We destroy" |

**Reading the formula.** The trigger word is almost always *kam* ("how many?") or its assertion *laqad ahlaknā* ("We have already destroyed"). The verb *ahlaknā* (Form IV of h-l-k, "to destroy") binds 12 of 14 — and the qrn–hlk pair is the second-strongest co-occurrence in the entire qrn profile (35.3%). The rhetorical mode is **interrogative-then-evidentiary**: ask the audience to count, then point at the dwellings ("amid whose dwelling-places they walk" — [20:128], [32:26]), then close with a sign-clause (*la-āyātin li-ūlī l-nuhā*, "signs for those of intelligence").

The reverse formula — *anshaʾnā min baʿdihim qarnan / qurūnan* ("we produced after them another generation / other generations") — appears at [6:6], [23:31], [23:42], [25:38], [28:45]. This is the **regenerative half** of the cycle. Generations are *produced* and *destroyed* in alternating clauses. [6:6] is the canonical example, putting both in a single ayah: destruction (line 1) followed by *anshaʾnā min baʿdihim qarnan ākharīn* (line 2).

The composite picture: **qarn is not "a length of time" but "a unit of replaced people."** A generation begins by being *originated* (anshaʾa) and ends by being *destroyed* (ahlaka). The Quran's historical clock advances by units of cohort-replacement, not units of years. Compare $hr (months as countable units of ritual time): qarn is the replacement-cohort as the unit of historical time. They are two clocks, never harmonized.

### *Al-qurūn al-ūlā* — "the former generations"

The phrase *al-qurūn al-ūlā* appears at [20:51] (Pharaoh asking Moses about them) and [28:43] (the giving of the Torah after their destruction). The phrase is **Pharaoh-coded**: it is what a powerful unbeliever asks about the past, and what God uses as the temporal marker before sending Moses. *Ūlā* (first/former) makes the generations a *closed set* relative to the speaker — *the ones before us who are no longer*.

## Dhū al-Qarnayn (18:83–98) — The Horn Sense

The entire passage occupies sixteen ayahs of Sūrat al-Kahf, and is structurally one of the Quran's three great paradigmatic narratives in that surah (Sleepers of the Cave, Mūsā and Khaḍir, Dhū al-Qarnayn). The figure is asked-about (*yasʾalūnaka ʿan dhī l-qarnayn*, [18:83]) — a Meccan question to the Prophet, presumably about a known historical or legendary figure.

The narrative arc:
- [18:84] God establishes him on earth and gives him *sabab* of every kind — "a way / a means / a cord."
- [18:85–88] He follows a *sabab* westward, finds the sun *setting in a muddy spring*, finds a people, and is given the choice to punish or treat with kindness.
- [18:89–91] He follows a *sabab* eastward, finds a people for whom no shelter from the sun has been made.
- [18:92–96] He follows a *sabab* northward (or to a third direction — the text is silent), reaches a pass between two barriers (*al-saddayn*), finds a people who barely understand speech, and is asked to build a barrier against Yaʾjūj and Maʾjūj. He builds it from iron and molten copper.
- [18:97–98] The barrier holds; he names it a *mercy from my Lord* until *the promise of my Lord comes to pass*.

The "two horns" of the title are the puzzle. Classical interpretation runs roughly: (a) he reached the *two horns of the sun* — east and west, the two ends of the earth; (b) he had *two epochs / two reigns*; (c) he had literal horns on his crown (matching depictions of Alexander on coins as *Iskandar Dhū al-Qarnayn*); (d) he ruled *Greeks and Persians*, the two horns of the known world. The text itself never explains.

**The structural reading the Quran prefers:** the *qarnayn* are *the two ends* — east and west — that he traverses in the narrative. He reaches *maghrib al-shams* ([18:86]) and *maṭliʿ al-shams* ([18:90]). He is *the one with the two extremities*. The text supports this without naming it: the horns are the geographical horns of the world. Read this way, the figure-name *Dhū al-Qarnayn* is internal to the surah's logic, not an external proper-name dropped in. The "horn" sense of the root is doing the work of *paired-extremities*, which is precisely the root's core meaning.

The historical identification (Alexander? Cyrus? a South-Arabian Ḥimyarite king?) is a separate question the Quran does not adjudicate. The structural identification (he-of-the-two-extremities) is what the root delivers.

## qarīn — The Eschatological Companion

Eight occurrences, dominated by Day-of-Judgment scenes:

- **[4:38]** — *wa-man yakuni l-shayṭānu lahu qarīnan fa-sāʾa qarīnan* — "Whoso has Satan as his companion, evil is the companion." Worldly. Twice in a single verse.
- **[37:51]** — *innī kāna lī qarīn* — "I had a companion." A blessed soul in paradise speaks of a former associate now in hell.
- **[41:25]** — *wa-qayyaḍnā lahum quranāʾa* — "We allotted to them companions" who beautified evil for them. Plural form.
- **[43:36]** — *wa-man yaʿshu ʿan dhikri l-Raḥmāni nuqayyiḍ lahu shayṭānan fa-huwa lahu qarīn* — "Whoever turns blind to the Reminder, We assign him a satan as companion."
- **[43:38]** — *fa-biʾsa l-qarīn* — "Wretched is the companion." The same person at judgment.
- **[50:23]** — *wa-qāla qarīnuhu hādhā mā ladayya ʿatīd* — *And his companion will say, 'This [record] is what I have ready'*. The qarīn here is the witnessing angel.
- **[50:27]** — *qāla qarīnuhu rabbanā mā aṭghaytuhu* — *His [satan-] companion will say, 'Our Lord, I did not make him transgress'*. The qarīn here is the satan.

**The startling fact:** [50:23] and [50:27] use *qarīn* for *opposite* beings — the angel-witness and the satan-tempter — within four ayahs. The lexeme is morally neutral; what makes it good or evil is *which* being is yoked to you. Translators split:
- Saheeh adds bracketed disambiguations: "[the angel]," "[devil] companion."
- Pickthall translates [50:23] as "(unto the evil-doer) his comrade" — eliding the angel reading.
- Khattab makes it explicit: "one's accompanying-angel" / "one's [devilish] associate."
- Arberry preserves the ambiguity: "his comrade shall say."

This is one of the cleanest cases in the Quran where translation forces a disambiguation that the Arabic withholds. The Arabic lets *qarīn* float; translators must pick.

The qarīn is **the structural inverse of the qarn**. A qarn is the cohort one is yoked to in *time* (one's generation); a qarīn is the entity one is yoked to in *individual fate*. Both are bonds of accompaniment.

## The Form II / IV / VIII Variants — Bound, Master, Pair

Three occurrences of *muqarranīn* (Form II passive, "bound-in-pairs") all describe the **post-judgment damned**:

- [14:49] *taraʾ l-mujrimīna yawmaʾidhin muqarranīn fī l-aṣfād* — *you will see the criminals that Day bound together in shackles*.
- [25:13] *idhā ulqū minhā makānan ḍayyiqan muqarranīn* — *when they are cast into a narrow place therein bound together*.
- [38:38] *wa-ākharīna muqarranīn fī l-aṣfād* — *and others bound together in chains* (the jinn under Solomon's command).

Note the imagery: the wicked are *paired-in-chains* in hell, and the rebellious jinn were *paired-in-chains* under Solomon. The Form II passive carries a punitive-disciplinary tone in every occurrence.

The single Form IV — *muqrinīn* at [43:13] — gives the *positive* counterpart: the prayer-of-mounting acknowledges that "we could not have *yoked / mastered* this beast under us." The verb stem says *to be a yoker*, and its negation says *we are not yokers, God is*. The active form names a capacity the speaker disclaims.

The single Form VIII — *muqtarinīn* at [43:53] — is Pharaoh's sneer: *why do angels not come with Moses in pair-formation*? The reflexive Form VIII names a *self-organized* coupling — angels who arrive in lock-step, paired escorts for a king. Pharaoh demands the visible spectacle of qrn-coupling as an authenticator, and the Quran lets his demand stand as evidence of his obtuseness: he wants the *form* of paired escort without the *substance* of being paired-with-the-divine.

**Across the three derived forms, the root says: forced pairing punishes; capable pairing is divine; performed pairing is what unbelievers demand.**

## The Unifying Core — Things-Yoked-Together (Test the Hypothesis)

The instruction asked whether the polysemy resolves to a single core. The data suggests it does:

| Lemma | Surface meaning | Underlying "two-things-bound" reading |
|---|---|---|
| qarn (generation) | A cohort of an age | People *yoked to* an era; people *bound to* a stretch of time |
| qarn (horn) | One of a pair on a head | Anatomically *paired*; in *Dhū al-Qarnayn* the *paired-extremities* of the earth |
| qarīn (companion) | Close associate | A being *yoked to* a person |
| muqarranīn (chained) | Bound-in-pairs | Forcibly *coupled* in punishment |
| muqrinīn (yoker) | Capable of mastering | The one who can *put two things together under control* |
| muqtarinīn (joined) | Accompanying in formation | Self-organized into *pairs / coupled formations* |

**All six surface-meanings reduce to: two-or-more things bound into a unit.** The root is not really polysemous in the deep sense — it has *one* meaning, the action of *yoking*, and the surface "polysemy" is six different *kinds of things you can yoke*: a person to an age, a horn to a head, a familiar to a soul, a slave to a chain, a master to a beast, an escort to a leader.

This is the Quran's most elegant case of root-level coherence. Where in English we use *generation, horn, companion, pair, master, escort* as six unrelated lexemes, Arabic recognizes the same underlying *act of binding* and lets the root carry it across all six contexts. The polysemy is **lexical compression** — one root doing the work of many.

This raises a research question for the broader project: **how many of the Quran's apparently polysemous roots resolve to a single underlying action?** If qrn is representative, the polysemy may not be a translation problem at all — it may be a feature of how Arabic packages cognition, with each root naming an *action-type* and the surface meanings being *what-you-can-do-that-action-to*.

## Translation Divergence

qrn produces some of the highest translator divergence scores in the time-concept set:

- **[18:83]** *Dhū al-Qarnayn*. Combined 0.68. Saheeh transliterates ("Dhul-Qarnayn"), Pickthall transliterates ("Dhu'l-Qarneyn"), Khattab transliterates ("Ⱬul-Qarnain"), Arberry transliterates ("Dhool Karnain"). All four refuse to translate the name. The horn sense never reaches English.
- **[18:86]** Combined 0.60. The phrase *taghrubu fī ʿaynin ḥamiʾatin* ("setting in a muddy / dark / murky spring") splits: Saheeh "spring of dark mud," Pickthall "muddy spring," Khattab "spring of murky water," Arberry "muddy spring." The cosmological controversy (does the sun literally set in a spring?) is partially absorbed by translator hedging.
- **[41:25]** Combined 0.71. *quranāʾ* — Saheeh "companions," Pickthall "comrades," Khattab "[evil] associates," Arberry "comrades." Khattab's bracketed *[evil]* is interpretive insertion absent from the Arabic.
- **[43:36]** Combined 0.74, **outlier: Khattab**. The single highest-divergence qrn ayah. *fa-huwa lahu qarīn*: Saheeh "and he is to him a companion," Pickthall "who becometh his comrade," Khattab "as their close associate." Khattab's plural-shift and "close associate" diverge most.
- **[43:38]** *biʾsa l-qarīn*. Pickthall: "an evil comrade." Khattab: "an evil associate." Arberry: "an evil comrade." The simplicity of the Arabic doesn't transmit.
- **[50:23]** Combined 0.74. The angel/satan ambiguity. Saheeh and Khattab disambiguate; Pickthall and Arberry preserve.
- **[19:74]** Outlier: Khattab. Khattab paraphrases *qarn* as "peoples" rather than "generation."
- **[38:3]** Outlier: Khattab. Same paraphrase.
- **[43:13]** Combined 0.66. *muqrinīn* — Saheeh "subdued," Pickthall "capable (of subduing)," Khattab "could have never done so [on our own]," Arberry "equal to it." Four totally different verbs.

**The pattern is unmistakable: Khattab is the most frequent outlier on qrn.** His translation systematically *resolves* qrn's polysemy in favor of context-specific glosses ("peoples" not "generations," "[evil] associates" not "companions," disambiguating angel from satan). Arberry is the most frequent *preserver* of the underlying root-flatness. This is a translation-philosophy difference made visible by a single root.

## Calendrical Time vs Generational Time — The Two-Clock Problem

This concept analysis is paired with the $hr (month) analysis. Together they expose the Quran's **two-clock structure**:

- **$hr clock** — months as ritual-legal units. Fasting, pilgrimage, kaffāra, ʿidda, sacred-month inviolability. Co-occurs with Allah, knowledge, fighting, mindfulness. Lives in the Medinan legal arc.
- **qrn clock** — generations as historical-judgment units. Destruction-formula, prior-people, the pacing of revelation across cohorts. Co-occurs with destruction, before/after, originate, see. Lives in the Meccan warning arc.

**The two clocks never share an ayah.** A scan of the 17 ayahs of $hr and the 34 ayahs of qrn shows no overlap. Months count ritually; generations count historically. The Quran refuses to express historical time in months, and refuses to express ritual time in generations. The clock you use depends on whether you are *acting* (month) or *being warned* (generation).

This is also why neither clock is *measured in years*. Years are the natural unit if you want to interconvert ritual and historical time — and the Quran specifically declines that interconversion.

## Negative Space

1. **No verb forms of qrn exist in the Quran.** Like $hr, this is a noun-only and participle-only root. The verb *qarana* "to join, couple" is absent. Five lemmas, all nominal. The action is named but never performed in finite-verb form.
2. **The horn is never animal anatomy.** No ram's horn, no bull's horn, no ibex. The *qarn* of an animal is absent from the Quran. The horn appears only in the proper-name *Dhū al-Qarnayn*.
3. **No good-companion qarīn outside [37:51].** Of 8 qarīn occurrences, 7 carry negative or neutral valence (satans, evildoers, witnessing angels). Only [37:51] hosts a paradise-speaker referring to a former (now-damned) earthly comrade — and even that has a negative aftermath. The *qarīn* in the Quran is rarely a friend.
4. **No "century" sense.** Some classical lexicographers gloss *qarn* as "100 years" (a century). The Quran *does not specify duration* for *qarn*. A generation in the Quran is a *people*, not a *length of time*. Translators occasionally drift toward "century," but no Quranic verse supports the duration sense.
5. **No qarn between two qarns.** The destruction-formula presents qarns as discrete. A qarn ends; another is *anshaʾa* (originated). There is no intergenerational figure, no "those who lived between two ages." The cohort boundary is sharp.
6. **No collective qarn for the present audience.** The audience of the Quran is never called *al-qarn al-ḥāḍir* or referred to by a qarn-label. *They* (past) and *you* (present) are in different grammars: *they* are *qurūn*; *you* are addressed in second person without ever being assigned to a qarn.

## Rabbit Holes (Open Questions)

1. **Does *Dhū al-Qarnayn*'s name decode internally?** The structural reading proposed above (the paired-extremities of east and west) makes the name *self-explanatory from the surah*. If correct, this is one of the Quran's hidden lexical jokes: he's called the Two-Horned-One because the surah literally shows him reaching the two horns of the world. Worth comparing with *Dhū al-Nūn* (Jonah, "the one of the fish") and *Dhū al-Kifl* (the one of the … kifl?) for parallel internal-decoding patterns.

2. **The qarn–qarīn structural inversion.** *qarn* binds a person to a *temporal cohort*; *qarīn* binds a person to an *individual companion*. Both are bonds of accompaniment, scaled differently — group and individual. A formal study of how the Quran scales accompaniment up and down (qarn → ʿashīra → ahl → qarīn → zawj) would put qrn in a coupling-typology.

3. **Why is the verb absent?** Five nominal lemmas, no finite verbs. This is a strong constraint and probably not accidental. Hypothesis: the *act of yoking* is, in Quranic grammar, never a *human* action expressible in finite verb form — it is always a *state* (passive participle) or a *capacity* (Form IV) or a *cohort* (noun). Yoking is what *happens* to people, not what people *do*. Test: are there other Quranic roots where the action is implicit and only the resultant nouns/participles surface?

4. **The Khattab translation pattern.** Khattab is the outlier on multiple qrn ayahs because he disambiguates aggressively. This is a translation-philosophy choice worth tracking across the corpus. Build a per-translator outlier histogram and check whether Khattab's outlier-rate spikes specifically on polysemous roots.

5. **Are *al-qurūn al-ūlā* and *al-awwalūn* the same group?** The phrase *asāṭīr al-awwalīn* ("legends of the ancients") at [46:17] — spoken by the disrespectful son — pairs with *al-qurūn min qablī* in the same verse. The son uses both phrases for the same referent. Is *al-awwalūn* the unbeliever's name for what the Quran calls *al-qurūn al-ūlā*? A cross-root study would tell us whether the Quran is showing us *two registers* for the same temporal reference.

6. **The four sacred months and the destroyed generations.** Both are counted phenomena (4-of-12; "how many?"). Neither has a duration in years. Both are anonymous (no named months; no named generations beyond "ʿĀd, Thamūd, Madyan"). Both are evidentiary (witness the new moon; walk amid the ruins). Is the Quran's broader temporal grammar one of *witnessable count without abstract duration*?

## What's Data-Grounded vs Interpretive

**Data-grounded:** all counts (36, 5 lemmas, 34 ayahs, 20 surahs); the lemma split (23/8/3/1/1); the destruction-formula tally (14 occurrences) and the regeneration-formula tally (5 occurrences); the morphological partition between definite-plural-destroyed and indefinite-singular-produced; the *al-qarnayn* exclusivity to Dhū al-Qarnayn; the eight qarīn occurrences and their eschatological dominance; the three muqarranīn occurrences and their punitive context; the co-occurring root rankings; the translation-divergence outlier pattern (Khattab on 19:74, 38:3, 43:36); the absence of finite verbs; the absence of $hr/qrn co-occurrence.

**Interpretive (LLM-supplied):** the unifying-core hypothesis ("things-yoked-together"); the internal-decoding reading of *Dhū al-Qarnayn* as paired-extremities; the qarn–qarīn structural inversion (group vs individual coupling); the "two-clock" framing of $hr-vs-qrn; the typology of forced/capable/performed pairing across the three derived forms; the lexical-compression framing of polysemy; the negative-space readings (no animal horns, no century-sense, no good qarīn).

**The decisive move:** the lemma table forced the polysemy to the surface, and asking *why these five forms?* surfaced the underlying *yoking* core. Once the core was visible, the four surface-meanings (generation, horn, companion, chain) snapped into a single grammar. This was the same pattern as the rḥm analysis (mercy ↔ wombs): an apparent polysemy is actually a single concept manifested in different domains. The decisive question for any polysemous Quranic root may be: *what is the single action that all the surface meanings instantiate?*
