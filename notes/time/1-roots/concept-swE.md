# Concept: swE (سوع) — al-Sāʿa, "the Hour"

**Headline.** The root *swE* is the Quran's named eschatological event. In 49 occurrences it almost never describes a duration; instead it names a singular, fixed, hidden, sudden, and inevitable arrival point at which the present world-order ends. Lexically it is sparse (one ordinary lemma — *sāʿa* — and one proper noun for an idol); structurally it is **the noun the Quran uses when it wants to confront unbelief with finality**. Where *yawm* tiles the eschaton across many days (creation, judgment, resurrection, gathering, return), *al-sāʿa* collapses the eschaton to a single threshold-instant whose timing belongs only to God.

---

## 1. Stats

- **49 occurrences**, **44 ayahs**, **27 surahs** (the concept file's "36 surahs" headline appears to count the broader root distribution; the actual occurrence list spans 27 distinct surahs).
- **2 lemmas**: `saAEap` (سَاعَة, "hour" / "the Hour") = 48; `suwaAE` (سُواع) = 1, a *proper noun* — one of the antediluvian idols listed in [71:23].
- **Surah revelation profile**: predominantly Meccan. Of the 44 ayahs, only 5 are Medinan: [9:117], [22:1], [22:7], [22:55], [33:63], [47:18]. Everything else (≈ 87%) is Meccan, consistent with the structural-divergence note that flagged swE as "Meccan-heavy (85.7%)."
- **POS**: 47 common nouns (N), 1 time-adverb (T) at [7:34], 1 proper noun (PN) at [71:23].
- **Cluster**: Louvain Cluster 4 — *Revelation, Speech & Disbelief*. Sāʿa lives next to *qbl, bEd, Awl, qrn* — a polemical-temporal cluster, not a calendar cluster. This is itself a finding: the Hour is a **rhetorical** word in the Quran, not an astronomical one.

## 2. Lemma table

| Lemma | Arabic | Count | POS | Sense |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `saAEap` (definite, *al-sāʿa*) | ٱلسَّاعَة | **38** | N | THE Hour — the eschatological event |
| `saAEap` (indefinite, *sāʿatan / sāʿatin*) | سَاعَةً / سَاعَةٍ | **10** | N/T | "an hour" — short interval, unit of time |
| `suwaAE` | سُواع | **1** | PN | An idol of Noah's people [71:23] |

Of the 48 *saAEap* tokens I counted, **38 are determinate** (carry the definite article ٱل- or its prepositional contractions: بِٱل-, لِّل-, وَٱل-) and **10 are indeterminate** ("an hour [of time]"). The split is the most important formal fact about this root: every determinate occurrence refers to the eschatological Hour; every indeterminate occurrence refers to an ordinary hour-as-duration. The Quran uses **the same lexeme** for "60-minute span" and "End of the World," disambiguating purely by definiteness.

The proper noun *suwāʿ* in [71:23] is morphologically related but semantically severed — it is the name of a pre-Islamic idol Noah preached against, listed alongside Wadd, Yaghūth, Yaʿūq, and Nasr. Worth noting only as a check that the morphology layer is not over-collapsing.

## 3. The five dominant formulas

These five turns of phrase account for roughly **38 of 49 occurrences** (≈ 78%) and define the rhetorical shape of the concept.

### 3a. *yasʾalūnaka ʿan al-sāʿa* — "they ask you about the Hour"
3 explicit instances, all with a **near-identical answer**: knowledge belongs only to God.
- [7:187] — *yasʾalūnaka ʿani 's-sāʿati ayyāna mursāhā…* "Its knowledge is only with my Lord… it will not come to you except suddenly."
- [33:63] — *yasʾaluka 'n-nāsu ʿani 's-sāʿati… qul innamā ʿilmuhā ʿinda 'llāh… laʿalla 's-sāʿata takūnu qarīban.* "Knowledge of it is only with God. Perhaps the Hour is near."
- [79:42] — *yasʾalūnaka ʿani 's-sāʿati ayyāna mursāhā.* "They ask you about the Hour: when is its arrival?"

The standard reply has two non-negotiable elements: (1) the *ʿinda* clause — knowledge is **with** God — and (2) some assertion of the Hour's proximity, suddenness, or weight. The Prophet is not given a date to repeat; he is given an epistemology to repeat. The questioners want a calendar; the response gives them a **theology of withholding**.

### 3b. *baghta* — sudden arrival
The collocation *taʾtīhim baghtatan* ("it comes to them suddenly") and its cousin *lā taʾtīkum illā baghtatan* appears at **6+ ayahs**: [6:31], [6:40 implicit], [7:187], [12:107], [22:55], [43:66], [47:18]. The most condensed form is at [43:66]:
> *Hal yanẓurūna illā 's-sāʿata an taʾtiyahum baghtatan wa-hum lā yashʿurūn.*
> "Are they waiting for anything but the Hour, that it should come upon them suddenly while they perceive not?"

The pattern bundles three operators: *baghtatan* (suddenly), *wa-hum lā yashʿurūn / lā yaʿlamūn* (while they do not perceive/know), and a **verb of motion toward** the unbelievers (*taʾtīhim, jāʾathum*). The Hour is consistently the grammatical subject of *coming*; humanity is the unconscious object. This grammar is theological: humans don't *reach* the Hour, the Hour *reaches* them.

### 3c. *qarīb* — "near" / *iqtarabat* — "drew nigh"
Three direct co-occurrences with the qrb root: [33:63] *laʿalla 's-sāʿata takūnu qarīban*; [42:17] *laʿalla 's-sāʿata qarīb*; [54:1] *iqtarabati 's-sāʿatu wa-'nshaqqa 'l-qamar* — "The Hour has drawn near and the moon was split."

There is a striking grammatical detail at [33:63] and [42:17]: the predicate *qarīb* is **masculine**, despite *al-sāʿa* being grammatically feminine. The expected agreement would be *qarība*. Classical grammarians read this as *qarīb* being adverbial ("[it is] near [as a time]") or as agreement with an implied masculine head like *waqt* or *amr al-sāʿa*. Whatever the resolution, it is a textual fingerprint that translators silently smooth — none of the four English versions reflect the gender mismatch.

### 3d. *lā rayba fīhā* — "no doubt about it"
At [18:21], [22:7], [40:59], [45:32]: the Hour is asserted as *not subject to doubt*. The phrase pairs with *waʿd Allāh ḥaqq* ("God's promise is true"). The semantic move is interesting — the Quran does not say "the Hour is *known*"; it says "the Hour is *not in doubt*." Knowing-when is denied; certainty-that is asserted. This is the architecture of Quranic eschatology in miniature: epistemic darkness about timing, total certainty about occurrence.

### 3e. *yawma taqūmu 's-sāʿa* — "the Day the Hour stands/rises"
[30:12], [30:14], [30:55], [40:46], [45:27]. A construct binding the two great Quranic time-words. The Hour does not *arrive* here — it *stands up* (qwm). The verb is the same one used for resurrection (*qiyāma*), and the construct *yawma taqūmu 's-sāʿa* is the Quran's most explicit identification of the Hour with the Day of Judgment — but with a precise grammar: the Hour is the *event that occurs on* a Day, not the Day itself. See §5 below.

A sixth recurring micro-pattern worth flagging: **the indefinite *sāʿatan* in legal/ajal contexts** — [7:34], [10:49], [16:61], [34:30] — all four use the identical formula *lā yastaʾkhirūna sāʿatan wa-lā yastaqdimūn* ("they cannot postpone it by an hour, nor advance it"). This is *sāʿa as a unit of duration*, not the Hour, and it is bound to the **Ajl** concept (appointed term), not to eschatology. The verb pair *yastaʾkhir / yastaqdim* (delay/advance) only appears in this exact formula.

## 4. *sāʿa* as ordinary duration vs *al-Sāʿa* as the Hour

The 10 indefinite uses split into three sub-categories:

1. **The "ajal" formula** — [7:34], [10:49], [16:61], [34:30]. *Sāʿa* = "a [single] hour" of postponement. These are about communities and individuals being unable to slip an appointed term by even an hour.
2. **The eschatological "as if it were only an hour"** — [10:45], [30:55], [46:35]. On the day of resurrection, the unbelievers will swear they tarried only "an hour of a day" (*sāʿatan min nahār*). Here the indefinite *sāʿa* is doing a phenomenological job: compressing a lifetime into the smallest temporal unit the Arabic of the period casually used for "a brief moment."
3. **The historical "hour of hardship"** — [9:117], *sāʿat al-ʿusra*. The only Medinan use of indefinite *sāʿa*. It refers to the hour of difficulty during the Tabūk expedition. This is the closest the Quran gets to using *sāʿa* in our modern sense — a specific, datable historical moment.

The disambiguation rule is essentially binary: **add the article, get the eschaton; drop it, get a duration.** Translators who mark "the Hour" with capital-H (Saheeh, Pickthall, Khattab) preserve this; Arberry's lower-case "hour" sometimes obscures it (e.g. at [30:55] both occurrences come out as "hour" with no typographic marker that the first is THE Hour and the second is *an* hour — even though the contrast is the entire rhetorical point of the verse).

## 5. Knowledge-withholding architecture

Co-occurring roots tell a clear story. The 10-share for **Elm** (knowledge, 22.7%) and the 11.4% share for **End** (locative, used in *ʿinda Allāh*, "with God") are not random — they cluster around a single theological move: *the Hour's location is epistemic, and the location is "with God."*

The unified statement of the doctrine is at **[31:34]**:
> *Inna 'llāha ʿindahū ʿilmu 's-sāʿati wa-yunazzilu 'l-ghaytha wa-yaʿlamu mā fī 'l-arḥām…*
> "With God is knowledge of the Hour. He sends down rain. He knows what is in the wombs. No soul knows what it will earn tomorrow. No soul knows in what land it will die."

This is the Quran's **list of five things kept inside God's monopoly**: the Hour, the rain, the womb, tomorrow, the place of death. The Hour heads the list. It is grouped not with cosmic events (stars, mountains, seas) but with **the small, intimate unknowns of each human life**. The eschaton and the embryo share a category: *both are inside the divine knowing and outside the human one.*

The *ʿindahū ʿilmu 's-sāʿa* / *ʿinda 'llāhi ʿilmuhā* construction recurs at:
- [7:187] (twice — ʿinda rabbī, then ʿinda 'llāh)
- [31:34]
- [33:63]
- [41:47]
- [43:85]

Five surahs, three Meccan, two Medinan, one Late-Meccan ([41]). This is one of the most stable theological formulas in the Quran. It is also asymmetric: the Quran says *no human* knows, but never names exemptions — not Muhammad, not Gabriel, not Jesus. The Hour is the only event in the Quran with this absolute knowledge embargo against creatures-as-such.

A second axis: the Hour clusters with **rayb** (doubt, 9.1%) and **mry** (dispute, 6.8%) and **Znn** (conjecture, 6.8%). These are the three Arabic verbs of *uncertain mental state*, and all three appear as states the Hour ought to dispel: *lā rayba fīhā, fa-lā tamtarunna bihā, in naẓunnu illā ẓannan*. The Hour is the test object for the entire Quranic epistemology of certainty: those who deny it are in *ẓann* (mere conjecture); those who accept it are in *yaqīn* (settled certainty).

## 6. Hour vs Day vs Trumpet

This is the structural question the assignment forced. Three answers:

**(a) Hour vs Day.** They are linked but not identical. The grammar *yawma taqūmu 's-sāʿa* — "the Day the Hour stands" — appears five times and treats the Hour as *the event occurring within* a Day. The Day is the temporal frame; the Hour is the threshold-event that opens the frame. Compare:
- *yawm* (Day) — c. 405 occurrences, fans across creation, judgment, gathering, resurrection, last day.
- *al-sāʿa* (Hour) — 38 determinate occurrences, all collapse to the same single moment.

The 10 swE/ywm co-occurrences (highest share for swE at 22.7%) almost all involve *yawma taqūmu 's-sāʿa* or the closely related *yawma yarawna* / *yawma yaḥshuruhum* constructions. The Day word does the *narrating* of the eschaton ("On the Day…"); the Hour word does the *naming* of the trigger ("…the Hour will rise"). They are not synonyms; they are two parts of one sentence.

**(b) Hour vs Trumpet.** This is a striking **negative finding**. I searched all 44 swE-tagged ayahs for the Trumpet (*ṣūr / nfx* — blowing). **Zero hits.** The Hour and the Trumpet — the two most graphically eschatological objects in the Quran — **never share an ayah.** They share *passages* (Sūrat al-Naml [27], Sūrat Yāsīn [36], Sūrat al-Zumar [39] cluster both, but in adjacent verses). At the level of the single ayah they remain disjoint. This suggests the Quran allocates them to different rhetorical jobs: the Hour is the *named* event invoked when polemic is foregrounded; the Trumpet is the *imaged* event invoked when narrative-cinema is foregrounded. They are theologically the same moment described in two different registers.

**(c) Hour vs ʿAdhāb (punishment).** Co-occurrence with E*b is 13.6% (6 ayahs). Crucially, the Hour and the *ʿadhāb* are repeatedly offered as **alternatives**: [6:40] "if there came to you the punishment of God or the Hour…"; [19:75] "either the punishment or the Hour…"; [22:55] "until the Hour comes upon them suddenly **or** there comes to them the punishment of a barren day." The disjunction *aw* makes them logically distinct. Punishment can be in this world (this-worldly *ʿadhāb*); the Hour can only be the End. The Hour is therefore a **type** of judgment that is never historical-and-local, always cosmic-and-universal.

## 7. Translation divergence

Three of the highest-divergence Hour-ayahs deserve side-by-side treatment.

### 7a. [7:187] — divergence score 0.568 (mid-high), 45 lexically disagreed words
Arabic: *yasʾalūnaka ʿani 's-sāʿati ayyāna mursāhā…*

| Translator | "ayyāna mursāhā" | "thaqulat fī 's-samāwāti wa-'l-arḍ" |
|---|---|---|
| Saheeh | "when is its **arrival**" | "It **lays heavily upon** the heavens and the earth" |
| Pickthall | "when will it **come to port**" | "It is **heavy in** the heavens and the earth" |
| Khattab | "**When will it be**?" | "It is **too tremendous for** the heavens and the earth" |
| Arberry | "when it shall **berth**" | "**Heavy is it** in the heavens and the earth" |

The verb *arsā* literally means *to anchor* (a ship). Pickthall and Arberry preserve the maritime metaphor (*come to port, berth*). Saheeh and Khattab abstract it away into "arrival/be." Likewise *thaqulat* (it became heavy): Khattab reads it as moral magnitude ("too tremendous"), the others as a physical loading-down of the heavens. This is the only Quranic ayah using *arsā* for the Hour — the metaphor casts the cosmos as the sea and the Hour as the ship that, eventually, will dock. Lost in three of four English translations.

### 7b. [33:63] — divergence 0.626 (the highest among "they ask you" ayahs)
Arabic: *qul innamā ʿilmuhā ʿinda 'llāhi wa-mā yudrīka laʿalla 's-sāʿata takūnu qarīban.*

| Translator | "wa-mā yudrīka" |
|---|---|
| Saheeh | "And what may make you perceive?" |
| Pickthall | "What can convey (the knowledge) unto thee?" |
| Khattab | "You never know" |
| Arberry | "What shall make thee know?" |

The Arabic *mā yudrīka* is a rhetorical question that classical grammarians read as either skeptical ("what could possibly let you know?") or hopeful ("perhaps something will let you know"). Khattab reduces it to a colloquial English shrug ("you never know"); Arberry preserves the rhetorical-question form most literally. Note also the masculine-predicate *qarīban* (not *qarība* with feminine *al-sāʿa*) — none of the four flag it.

### 7c. [43:61] — divergence 0.517
Arabic: *wa-innahū la-ʿilmun li-'s-sāʿati fa-lā tamtarunna bihā…*

| Translator | "innahū la-ʿilmun li's-sāʿa" |
|---|---|
| Saheeh | "And indeed, **Jesus** will be [a sign for] knowledge of the Hour" |
| Pickthall | "And lo! verily there is knowledge of the Hour" |
| Khattab | "And his **˹second˺ coming** is truly a sign for the Hour" |
| Arberry | "It is knowledge of the Hour" |

The Arabic pronoun *innahū* ("indeed it/he") is grammatically ambiguous — Saheeh and Khattab gloss it as **Jesus** (importing the well-known eschatological tradition of the second coming); Pickthall and Arberry leave it generic. This is interpretive insertion at the maximum: a single pronoun decides whether the Quran here references Jesus' return or makes a general claim. The bare Arabic does not commit either way.

## 8. Negative space

Things that **do not** happen with *al-sāʿa* are as instructive as the things that do.

- **The Hour is never measured.** No ayah gives a count, no ayah dates it. By contrast *yawm* attaches to numerable durations (50,000 years at [70:4], 1,000 years at [22:47], 6 days of creation).
- **The Hour never has a name beyond "the Hour."** The Quran uses many secondary names for the Day of Judgment — *yawm al-dīn, yawm al-faṣl, yawm al-jamʿ, al-ṣākhkha, al-ṭāmma, al-qāriʿa, al-ḥāqqa* — but never gives *al-sāʿa* a synonym or epithet. It is always just *the Hour*.
- **The Hour never co-occurs with the Trumpet** (see §6b).
- **The Hour never co-occurs with stars (njm), with months ($hr), or with the night/day pair (lyl/nhr) doing astronomical work.** Eschatological terms and calendar terms remain disjoint at the ayah level. The Hour is *not part of the natural temporal order*; it is what *terminates* it.
- **The Hour is never asked of by a believer in the text.** Every *yasʾalūnaka ʿan al-sāʿa* attributes the question to *al-nās* (people generally) or to the disbelievers as a challenge. Curiosity about the Hour is **marked as a posture of doubt**, not of inquiry. The believer's stance is *mushfiqūn min-hā* ([21:49], [42:18]) — apprehensive *of* it, not curious *about* it.
- **The proper noun *suwāʿ*** (an idol) shares the root but is never connected — there is no Quranic wordplay between "the Hour" and "Suwāʿ the false god." The morphological coincidence is just a coincidence.

## 9. Surahs of high concentration

Counting all 44 ayahs:

| Surah | Hour-ayahs | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 30 (Ar-Rūm) | 4 | The "yawma taqūmu 's-sāʿa" surah — three of five total |
| 22 (Al-Ḥajj) | 3 | Medinan; opens the surah ([22:1] earthquake of the Hour) |
| 7 (Al-Aʿrāf) | 2 | Includes the great [7:187] |
| 18 (Al-Kahf) | 2 | Companions of the Cave + the arrogant gardener |
| 30, 41, 42, 43, 45, 54 | 2-4 each | The Late Meccan dense-eschatology cluster |
| 6, 10, 25, 33, 34 | multiple | Polemical contexts |

The most striking concentration is **Sūrat al-Qamar [54]**, which opens with *iqtarabati 's-sāʿatu* and returns at [54:46] (*bali 's-sāʿatu mawʿiduhum, wa-'s-sāʿatu adhā wa-amarru* — "the Hour is their appointed time, and the Hour is more disastrous and more bitter"). The whole surah is rhetorically organized around the Hour; sets it as the appointment for unbelievers; and ends with their discovery that the appointment was kept.

**Sūrat al-Rūm [30]** uses *yawma taqūmu 's-sāʿa* four times across [30:12, 14, 55] — the densest deployment of that single formula in the Quran.

## 10. Five verses for direct study

These are the verses I would prioritize for the next round of analysis — each maximally rewards re-reading:

1. **[7:187]** — the foundational "they ask you about the Hour" ayah. Contains the *ʿinda rabbī* doctrine, the maritime metaphor *mursāhā*, and the *thaqulat fī 's-samāwāti wa-'l-arḍ* image. The doctrinal anchor.
2. **[31:34]** — the Five Things With God. Locates the Hour in a list with rain, womb, tomorrow, and place-of-death. Theological architecture.
3. **[20:15]** — *innā 's-sāʿata ātiyatun akādu ukhfīhā* — "I almost conceal it." The most unusual divine speech in the Hour-corpus. Why "almost"?
4. **[16:77]** — *wa-mā amru 's-sāʿati illā ka-lamḥi 'l-baṣari aw huwa aqrab* — "the matter of the Hour is but as a glance of the eye, or nearer." Time-physics: the Hour is sub-perceptual in duration.
5. **[54:1]** — *iqtarabati 's-sāʿatu wa-'nshaqqa 'l-qamaru* — the Hour as already-near, paired with the moon-splitting sign. The most temporally compressed Hour-statement in the Quran.

## 11. Rabbit holes

- **The masculine *qarīb* paradox.** Why does *al-sāʿa* (feminine) take a masculine predicate at [33:63] and [42:17]? Classical answer: implied head *waqt* or *amr*. But this would deserve a count: how often does *al-sāʿa* take feminine concord (e.g. *qāʾima* at [18:36], [41:50]) and how often does it not? A small grammatical study could surface a real pattern.
- **The Hour's verbs of motion.** The Hour comes (*atā / jāʾa*), draws near (*iqtarabat*), stands up (*qāmat / taqūmu*), but never *descends* and never *arrives at*. The Hour is never the destination of human motion — it is always the agent. Inventory the verbs and the consistency may itself be a finding.
- **The "almost conceal" of [20:15].** The verb *akādu* normally means "I am about to" or "I almost." Why would God almost conceal the Hour? Classical exegesis offers several readings; a comparative pass against other Quranic uses of *akāda* would test which reading is most internally consistent.
- **The "barren day" of [22:55].** *ʿAdhābu yawmin ʿaqīm* — "the punishment of a barren day." The unique adjective *ʿaqīm* (used elsewhere of barren women, e.g. [51:29]) attached to a Day that is offered as the alternative to the Hour. What does it mean for a Day to be barren? Worth a whole concept-pass on Eqm.
- **The Jesus question at [43:61].** Two of four major translators import Jesus into the bare Arabic *innahū*. What is the textual warrant? If purely interpretive, what does this tell us about the porousness between Quran and ḥadīth in modern English translation choices?
- **The five-things list at [31:34].** Each of the five (Hour, rain, womb, tomorrow, place-of-death) has its own concept-pass to do. Together they form what may be the Quran's most explicit ontology of *the unknown-by-creatures*. A future *concept-glb.md* or *concept-Five-Unknowns.md* would tie this together.
- **Medinan sāʿa — only 5 out of 44 ayahs.** Why does the Hour rhetoric *recede* in the Medinan period? The candidate explanation is that Medinan revelation deals more with community-building and law (where *ajl* and *waqt* dominate) and less with eschatological polemic (where *al-sāʿa* dominates). But this needs to be checked against the parallel decline-rate of related polemical roots like k*b (deny) and kfr (disbelieve).

## Methodology note

This analysis was built from: the complete swE concept profile (49 occurrences with Arabic + 4 English translations), the morphology lemma table (verifying 48 + 1 lemma split), the structural translation-divergence scores for individual Hour-ayahs, the structural-time-cooccurrence note (Cluster 4 placement, Meccan share), and direct grep on the Arabic of all 44 ayahs to verify the negative finding on Hour-vs-Trumpet ayah-level disjointness.

**Data-grounded claims:** the 49/44/27 counts, the lemma split, the Meccan share, the 5 dominant formulas, the 5+ instances of each formula, the Hour-Trumpet zero-overlap finding, the gender-predicate mismatch at [33:63] and [42:17], the divergence scores for [7:187], [33:63], [43:61].

**Interpretive claims:** the "Hour as threshold-event vs Day as frame" framing, the "Hour is rhetorical not astronomical" reading of Cluster 4 placement, the "epistemic darkness about timing, total certainty about occurrence" formulation, the reading of *baghta + lā yashʿurūn + verb-of-motion-toward* as a unified grammatical theology.

**Decisive move:** treating the definite/indefinite split as the binary disambiguator between THE Hour and an hour, rather than treating *sāʿa* as a single polysemous lexeme. The morphology layer makes this trivially testable, and once tested it organizes everything else.
