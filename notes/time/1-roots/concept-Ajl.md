# أَجَل (Ajl): The Appointed Term — Deep Concept Analysis

## Headline Finding: Every Finite Thing Has Two Sides — A Stated Term and a Door That Will Not Open

The root **Ajl (ا-ج-ل)** carries one of the Quran's most theologically loaded ideas: that everything finite — a soul, a nation, the cosmos itself, a marriage, even a debt — has been **assigned a stated endpoint** (*ajal musamman*) that is *known to God, written, and ungovernable by the creature it terminates*. The word does not name "time in general"; it names the **edge** of time. And the Quran's grammar around it is almost entirely passive, almost entirely deterministic, and almost entirely formulaic.

Three facts make this root distinctive:

1. **The signature collocation `أَجَلٌ مُسَمًّى`** (*ajalun musamman*, "a stated term") appears **at least 19 times** — making it one of the most repeated theological set-phrases in the Quran. The passive participle *musamman* ("named") is doing the theological work: by whom? Allah, never explicitly stated as agent in the phrase itself.

2. **The deadline formula** — *fa-iḏā jāʾa ajaluhum lā yastaʾkhirūna sāʿatan wa-lā yastaqdimūn* ("when their term comes they cannot delay it an hour nor advance it") — appears **almost verbatim three times** at [7:34], [10:49], and [16:61], with [15:5] and [23:43] giving compressed variants. This is the closest the Quran comes to stating a "law of historical termination."

3. **The same word — *ajal* — does both divine appointment AND commercial debt.** [2:282], the longest ayah in the Quran (the debt-contract verse), uses *ajalin musamman* for the maturity date of a loan. The God who fixes the death of a soul and the God who instructs you to write down the term of a loan are using identical vocabulary. This is not a homonym. It is a single grammar of bounded obligation.

The root sits inside the "Creation & Appointed Term" Louvain cluster, tied closely to خلق (xlq, creation) and ywm (day) — confirming what the verse-level reading shows: *to create something is, in Quranic grammar, to assign it an ajal*.

## Statistics and Morphological Reading

**Quantitative profile:**
- **56 total occurrences** across **48 ayahs** in **29 surahs** (25.4% of the Quran)
- **4 lemmas**, dominated by one:
  - `>ajal` (أَجَل, "term/endpoint") — **52 occurrences** (92.9%)
  - `>aj~ala` (أَجَّلَ, "to appoint a term") — 2 occurrences ([6:128], [77:12])
  - `m~u&aj~al` (مُؤَجَّل, "appointed/deferred") — 1 occurrence ([3:145])
  - `>ajol` (أَجْل, "for the sake of / because of") — 1 occurrence ([5:32])
- **POS distribution**: 53 N, 2 V, 1 ADJ — almost entirely nominal
- **Revelation**: roughly balanced — Meccan ~33, Medinan ~23 (the Medinan share is unusually high for a "fate" word; this is the marriage/debt law contribution)
- **Top surah concentration**: Al-Baqara (6 occurrences), Al-A'raaf (4), Al-An'aam (4), Faatir (3), Ibrahim, Yunus, Talaaq, An-Nahl, Hud, Nooh — eschatological surahs and the legal core

The single-lemma dominance matters: this is not a polysemous root with many derived stems. It is a **single concept, deployed under varying syntactic frames**. Ajl-as-term is monolithic in its semantics; what varies is *whose* term, *what kind* of term, and *what God does or refuses to do* with it.

### Lemma 2: أَجَّلَ (>aj~ala, Form II verb) — 2 occurrences

The **causative active verb**: "He appointed (a term)." Notably rare. God says it once *back to humanity through humanity's mouth* ([6:128] — humans say *ajjalta lanā*, "which You appointed for us"), and the passive *ujjilat* appears once at [77:12] ("for what Day was it postponed?"). The verb form is conspicuously suppressed; the Quran prefers to leave the agent of *ajal*-setting unstated. This is the root's first major grammatical signature.

### Lemma 3: مُؤَجَّل (m~u&aj~al) — 1 occurrence

The **passive participle**, [3:145]: *kitāban muʾajjalan* — "a decree determined / a writ deferred-to-its-term." Note the pairing: *kitāb* (writing) + *muʾajjal* (term-fixed). The decree of death is *written* and *term-fixed*. This will recur as a structural pattern.

### Lemma 4: أَجْل (>ajol) — 1 occurrence

[5:32]: *min ajli ḏālika* — "for the sake of that." This is a **causal preposition**, not a temporal noun. Saheeh: "Because of that..." It is the lexical fossil of an older meaning ("on account of, by reason of"). The fact that it survives only here, in the famous "whoever kills a soul" verse, is striking — and worth noting as a rabbit hole: the same root that names the **endpoint** of life also names the **causal ground** for protecting it.

## The *Ajal Musamman* Formula — Who/What Gets a Named Term

Tabulating the 19 occurrences of *ajal musamman* by **what is being given a stated term** is the analytical pivot of this root. The grammar is uniform; the *referents* are not. Five categories emerge:

### 1. The individual soul / human death

- **[6:2]** — *ḫalaqakum min ṭīnin ṯumma qaḍā ajalan wa-ajalun musamman ʿindahu* — "He created you from clay then decreed a term, and a stated term [is] with Him." **Two terms**: a "decreed term" (the lifespan) and a "stated term with Him" (the Hour, or per Khattab, the resurrection). This is the single most important *ajal* verse.
- **[6:60]** — *li-yuqḍā ajalun musamman* — through nightly sleep-and-waking, your stated term is being run down.
- **[3:145]** — *kitāban muʾajjalan* — death only by Allah's leave, at a writing-fixed term.
- **[71:4], [14:10]** — Allah will *yuʾakhkhir* (defer) you to a stated term if you repent.
- **[40:67]** — embryology → child → maturity → old age → "that you reach a stated term."
- **[22:5]** — same cycle, from the womb: *fī al-arḥām mā nashāʾu ilā ajalin musamman*.

### 2. The nation / community (umma)

- **[7:34]** — *wa-li-kulli ummatin ajalun* — "for every nation a term."
- **[10:49]** — verbatim repetition of [7:34].
- **[15:5]** — *mā tasbiqu min ummatin ajalahā wa-mā yastaʾkhirūna* — no nation outruns its term, none lags behind.
- **[23:43]** — verbatim repetition of [15:5] in a different surah (Mursalāt context).
- **[16:61], [35:45]** — Allah defers people *ilā ajalin musamman*; when their term comes, no delay.

### 3. The cosmos — sun and moon

- **[13:2], [31:29], [35:13], [39:5]** — four near-identical verses: *sakhkhara al-shamsa wa-l-qamar, kullun yajrī li-ajalin musamman* — "He subjected the sun and the moon, each running for a stated term." The cosmos has an **expiry date.** The sun's daily course and its terminal eschatological shutdown are, grammatically, the same kind of fact.

### 4. The cosmos — heavens and earth as a whole

- **[30:8]** — *mā ḫalaqa Allāhu al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍa ... illā bi-l-ḥaqqi wa-ajalin musamman* — "He did not create the heavens and the earth ... except in truth and for a stated term."
- **[46:3]** — verbatim parallel.

This is structurally the same proposition as (1): individuals have *ajal*, **and so does creation itself**. The Quran refuses to grant the cosmos a different temporal ontology from the human soul.

### 5. The legal / contractual

- **[2:282]** — *iḏā tadāyantum bi-daynin ilā ajalin musamman fa-ktubūhu* — "when you contract a debt for a stated term, write it down." The famous debt-verse.
- **[11:3]** — *yumattiʿkum matāʿan ḥasanan ilā ajalin musamman* — provision granted for a stated term (also bridging into category 1).
- **[22:33]** — sacrificial animals: benefits to you *ilā ajalin musamman*, then their place is the Ancient House.
- **[29:53], [42:14]** — postponement of judgment of disputants until a stated term.
- **[39:42]** — souls released from sleep *ilā ajalin musamman*.

**The headline:** the same syntactic frame that says "your soul has a stated term" says "your loan has a stated term." This is not metaphor. It is the same theological grammar applied to commercial obligation.

| Communal | nation (*umma*) | 7:34, 10:49, 15:5, 23:43, 16:61, 35:45 | duration of communal existence | *li-kulli ummatin ajalun* + the *fa-iḏā jāʾa ajaluhum* deadline |

The **same grammatical frame is reused at every scale**. This is the Quran's most economical theological move: *ajal* is a **scale-invariant grammar of finitude**.

## The Deadline Formula — Every Variant

The negative-prohibition formula on the term's arrival:

| Verse | Formula |
|-------|---------|
| **[7:34]** | *fa-iḏā jāʾa ajaluhum lā yastaʾkhirūna sāʿatan wa-lā yastaqdimūn* — "when their term comes, they will not delay [it] an hour nor advance [it]" |
| **[10:49]** | identical wording |
| **[16:61]** | identical wording (with *fa* instead of *wa-*) |
| **[15:5]** | *mā tasbiqu min ummatin ajalahā wa-mā yastaʾkhirūn* — "no nation outstrips its term, nor do they put it back" |
| **[23:43]** | identical to [15:5] |
| **[63:11]** | *wa-lan yuʾakhkhira Allāhu nafsan iḏā jāʾa ajaluhā* — "Allah will never delay a soul when its term comes" |
| **[71:4]** | *inna ajala Allāhi iḏā jāʾa lā yuʾakhkhar* — "the term of Allah, when it comes, cannot be delayed" |

Two morphological observations:

- The verbs are **istafʿala stems** (*yastaʾkhirūn*, *yastaqdimūn*) — the seeking-stem. Humans don't merely *fail* to delay; they cannot even successfully *seek* delay.
- The formula always pairs **delay (ʾakhkhara)** with **bringing-forward (qaddama)**. The asymmetry is deliberate: the term is fixed *in both directions*. It is not just unmovable forward; it is unmovable backward.

A live theological corollary: **the door is double-locked**. Most religious traditions imagine fate as resistance to being moved *forward* (you can't escape death). The Quran also denies movement *backward* — you cannot rush your term either. This is what closes off Stoic resignation as a posture: there is no "let it come now" because *istiqdām* is also impossible.

## Ajl in Marriage Law (Baqara, Talāq) — Legal vs Cosmic

The Medinan ajl is largely **legal**, governing the *ʿidda* (waiting period) of women:

- **[2:231]** *fa-iḏā ṭallaqtumū al-nisāʾa fa-balaghna ajalahunna* — divorce: when they reach their term, retain or release.
- **[2:232]** same opening, applied to remarriage.
- **[2:234]** widows: 4 months 10 days *fa-iḏā balaghna ajalahunna*.
- **[2:235]** marriage proposal during ʿidda: *ḥattā yablugha al-kitābu ajalahu* — "until the writ reaches its term."
- **[65:2]** divorce procedure: *fa-iḏā balaghna ajalahunna*.
- **[65:4]** pregnant women: *ajaluhunna an yaḍaʿna ḥamlahunna* — their term is **birth**.

What is striking is that the legal *ajal* uses **identical grammar** to the cosmic *ajal*. The verb of arrival is *balagha* ("to reach") rather than *jāʾa* ("to come"), but the bounded-period structure is the same. [2:235] is especially important: *yablugha al-kitābu ajalahu* — "the writ reaches its term." The writ has its own ajl. **The legal term is conceived as a small parallel to the cosmic term.** This is why no semantic distinction is necessary.

The single most beautiful instance is [65:4]: for a pregnant woman, *ajaluhunna an yaḍaʿna ḥamlahunna* — "their term is that they put down their burden [give birth]." Here *ajal* is not an abstract date but a **bodily event**: the term is birth. This is the rare case where *ajal* is given an explicit referent by the verse itself.

## Ajl + ʾatā ("comes") / balagha ("reaches") — Temporal Grammar of Arrival

The two arrival verbs cluster differently:

| Verb | Quranic use with ajl | Semantic register |
|------|----------------------|-------------------|
| **`جَآءَ`** (jāʾa, "to come") | nations and souls: *jāʾa ajaluhum / ajaluhā* (7:34, 10:11, 10:49, 16:61, 35:45, 63:11, 71:4); also Allah's own term: *jāʾa ajalu Allāh* (29:5, 71:4) | *the term comes to you* — agency is the term itself |
| **`بَلَغَ`** (balagha, "to reach") | women's ʿidda and the writ: *balaghna ajalahunna* (2:231, 2:232, 2:234, 65:2); *yablugha al-kitābu ajalahu* (2:235) | *you reach the term* — agency is the human/document |

This is a real grammatical asymmetry. **Death-and-judgment terms come *to* you. Legal-waiting terms are *reached by* you.** The eschatological term is active; the legal term is passive (from the human's standpoint). It maps the metaphysics onto the syntax.

A third verb, **اقترب** (*iqtaraba*, "draws near"), appears in [7:185]: *qadi-qtaraba ajaluhum* — "perhaps their term has drawn near." This is the rhetorical mode: the term *approaches* before it *arrives*.

## Taqdīm / Taʾkhīr — Bringing Forward / Holding Back

The morphology of "moving the term" is rich. The Quran uses three verbs:

- **`أَخَّرَ / يُؤَخِّرُ`** (akhkhara, "to defer") — divine reprieve: Allah defers *to* a stated term ([14:10], [16:61], [35:45], [39:42], [71:4]).
- **`قَدَّمَ / يَسْتَقْدِمُ`** (yastaqdimu, "to seek to advance") — humans cannot seek to advance the term ([7:34], [10:49], [16:61]).
- **`نُؤَخِّرُ`** in [11:104] is striking: *wa-mā nuʾakhkhiruhū illā li-ajalin maʿdūdin* — "We delay it only for a counted term." The first-person plural divine voice **acknowledges its own deferral**: God defers, not arbitrarily, but to a *maʿdūd* (counted) term.

Note the morphological pair: God's *taʾkhīr* (active II-stem) vs. humanity's failed *istiʾkhār* (X-stem, the seeking-stem). Allah defers; humans cannot seek to delay.

## Ajl as Debt-Term — Why Does the Same Word Do Both?

[2:282], the longest ayah in the Quran, twice uses *ajal*:

> *yā-ayyuhā al-laḏīna āmanū iḏā tadāyantum bi-daynin ilā ajalin musamman fa-ktubūh ... wa-lā tasʾamū an taktubūhu ṣaġīran aw kabīran ilā ajalih*

"O you who believe, when you contract a debt for a **stated term**, write it down ... and do not be reluctant to write it, small or large, **[recording] its term**."

The very same phrase — *ajalin musamman* — that names the cosmic boundary of creation (30:8, 46:3) names the maturity date of a loan. Why?

Three working hypotheses:

1. **The Quran is constructing a single ontology of bounded obligation.** A debt is a microcosm of mortality. To owe is to live under a clock. To die is to repay an ultimate debt. The borrower-debtor reflects the creature-Creator: both have a *kitāb* (writing) attached to a *musamman* (named term). [3:145] makes this latter pairing explicit at the cosmic scale: death = *kitāb muʾajjal*. [2:282] makes it explicit at the legal scale: debt = *kitāb* + *ajal musamman*.

2. **The instruction "write it down" applies in both cases.** Allah writes the soul's term ([3:145], [13:38]: *li-kulli ajalin kitāb* — "for every term, a writ"). Believers must write the debtor's term. The act of writing is the human imitation of the divine *kitāba* of fate. Commercial honesty is a structural rehearsal of submission to divine appointment.

3. **It naturalizes the eschatological term.** Every Muslim who has signed a loan knows what *ajal musamman* feels like — a fixed date, written, with someone owed. The Quran uses that mundane experience to render the cosmic ajl unsurprising.

This is the most under-noticed move in the entire root. The doctrine of *ajal musamman* is taught not only in Mecca's eschatological warnings but in Medina's most administratively detailed verse. The bridge between the two is the *kitāb*.

## Translation Divergence — The Translators Diverge On Whether to Name the Referent

The translators reveal interpretive choices most strikingly at **[6:2]**, the foundational two-terms verse:

| Translator | Rendering of *qaḍā ajalan wa-ajalun musamman ʿindahu* |
|------------|---|
| Saheeh | "decreed a term and a specified time [known] to Him" — minimal, leaves both terms unspecified |
| Pickthall | "decreed a term for you. A term is fixed with Him" — same minimalism |
| Khattab | "appointed a term ˹for your death˺ and another known only to Him ˹for your resurrection˺" — **inserts both referents** |
| Arberry | "determined a term and a term is stated with Him" — most literal |

Khattab's insertion is the classical exegesis (al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī): first ajl = death, second ajl = resurrection. **But the Quran refuses to specify.** The grammatical underdetermination is itself a feature: the believer is told only that *two* boundaries exist, both with Him.

A second sharp divergence: **[7:185]** *qadi-qtaraba ajaluhum*.

- Saheeh: "perhaps their appointed time has come near" (collective)
- Pickthall: "their own term draweth nigh" (collective)
- Khattab: "perhaps their **end is near**" — collapses *ajal* into "end"
- Arberry: "their term is already nigh"

Khattab's "end" is the most interpretive — it implies death rather than communal-eschatological term. The Arabic permits both.

A third: **[14:10]** *yuʾakhkhirakum ilā ajalin musamman*.

- Saheeh: "He delays your **death** for a specified term"
- Pickthall: "reprieve you unto an appointed term"
- Khattab: "delay your end until your appointed term"
- Arberry: "defer you to a term stated"

Saheeh and Khattab both **insert "death/end"**. Pickthall and Arberry leave the verb objectless. The Arabic is literally "defers you" — not "defers your death." The translators differ on whether the *thing* deferred is *you* (Pickthall, Arberry) or *your death* (Saheeh, Khattab).

This matters: if Allah defers *you*, you exist within a granted lease. If Allah defers *your death*, your death is the active datum and you are merely the object on which the deferral acts. The Quran's grammar prefers the former; some translators prefer the latter.

## The Tripartite Grammar of Divine Timing — Cross-Finding (Ajl × wqt × Amd)

A working hypothesis after reading the three roots together:

| Root | Quantitative scope | Grammatical signature | Semantic core |
|------|--------------------|----------------------|---------------|
| **Ajl** | 56× | *ajalun musamman* (passive participle, "named") | the **endpoint** — when the term *expires* |
| **wqt** | 13× | *mawqūt*, *mīqāt* (passive participles, "timed/appointed") | the **instant** — when the appointment *occurs* |
| **Amd** | 4× | always indefinite, *amadan* + adjectives (*baʿīdan*, *qarīban*) | the **stretch** — how *long* the interval *runs* |

The three roots divide the temporal architecture **non-overlappingly**:

- *Ajl* answers **"until when?"** — the deadline.
- *wqt* answers **"at what moment?"** — the appointment.
- *Amd* answers **"for how long?"** — the duration.

Test cases that confirm:

1. **[18:12]** (Cave of the Sleepers): *li-naʿlama ayyu al-ḥizbayni aḥṣā li-mā labithū amadā* — "to know which of the two parties calculated the *amad* [duration] they had tarried." Not the *ajl* (no endpoint at issue) and not the *wqt* (no appointment) — the *length of stay*. Only *amad* fits.

2. **[44:40]**: *inna yawma al-faṣli **mīqātuhum** ajmaʿīn* — "the Day of Decision is their **appointment-instant** all together." Here *mīqāt*, not *ajal*. The Day of Judgment as a *gathering point*, an instant, is *wqt*-coded; it is *ajal*-coded only when the focus is on the **boundary** of the dunyā.

3. **[2:282]**: *ilā ajalin musamman* — the loan's **maturity edge**, not its instant of repayment, not its stretch.

4. **[57:16]**: *fa-ṭāla ʿalayhimu al-amad* — "the *amad* [stretch] grew long over them, so their hearts hardened." A duration metaphor. Could not be *ajal* (no deadline), could not be *wqt* (no instant) — only *amad* names the stretch that hardens.

5. **[4:103]**: *kāna ʿalā al-muʾminīna kitāban **mawqūtan*** — prayer is "a *mawqūt* [time-fixed] writ" upon the believers. Not *ajal* (no deadline of the prayer itself), not *amad* (no duration). The instant-stamp of *wqt*.

The complementary distribution is so clean it functions as a **grammatical system**, not three near-synonyms. Each occupies a distinct slot. The Quran is not lexically rich here for redundancy; it is lexically rich because *time has three different geometries* and each needs its own word.

This system is **invisible if any one root is studied alone**. It surfaces only on triangulation.

## Co-occurrence with Passive Participles — Why Is Quranic Time Vocabulary Passivized?

Across all three roots, the dominant adjectives are **passive participles**:

- **musammā** (مُسَمًّى, "named") — modifies *ajal* 19+ times
- **mawqūt** (مَوْقُوت, "time-fixed") — modifies *kitāb* in [4:103]
- **maʿdūd** (مَعْدُود, "counted") — modifies *ajal* in [11:104]
- **maʿlūm** (مَعْلُوم, "known") — modifies *yawm al-waqt al-maʿlūm* in [15:38], [38:81]; *yawmin maʿlūmin* in [26:38], [56:50]
- **muʾajjal** (مُؤَجَّل, "term-fixed") — modifies *kitāb* in [3:145]

This is not a stylistic choice. It is theological grammar:

- *named by whom?*
- *known to whom?*
- *counted by whom?*
- *time-fixed by whom?*

The agent is **always unstated and always Allah**. The passive voice does the apophatic work: it asserts divine action without grammaticalizing the divine subject. To say *"a stated term"* is to imply the Stator without naming Him in the noun phrase. The passive participle is the theology.

This is one of the most important silent features of Quranic Arabic: *whenever you see musamman / mawqūt / maʿlūm / maʿdūd / muʾajjal modifying time, the unstated agent is God*. The grammar embeds the doctrine.

## Negative Space — Can a Term Be Extended?

The Quran is unusually consistent on this. The answer is **no**, with one fascinating exception:

- **[71:4]** explicitly: *inna ajala Allāhi iḏā jāʾa lā yuʾakhkhar* — "the term of Allah, when it comes, cannot be delayed."
- **[63:11]**: *wa-lan yuʾakhkhira Allāhu nafsan iḏā jāʾa ajaluhā* — Allah will never delay a soul when its term comes.
- **[7:34], [10:49], [16:61], [15:5], [23:43]**: the *no-delay-no-advance* formula.

But **[6:128]** is the exception that proves the rule. Here the jinn's allies among humans say to God: *rabbanā istamtaʿa baʿḍunā bi-baʿḍin wa-balaghnā ajalanā allaḏī **ajjalta** lanā* — "Our Lord, we enjoyed one another and we have reached our term **which You appointed for us**." The verb *ajjalta* (You appointed) is the only place in the Quran where God is the **explicit second-person grammatical subject** of the term-setting verb. And the speakers are the damned, naming the appointment that is closing on them. **The agent of *ajl* is named only when the appointment is being collected.**

The other exception worth noting: **[5:1]** — not in the Ajl root but adjacent, treating contractual obligations (*awfū bi-l-ʿuqūd*). The Quran permits *contractual* extension by mutual agreement; it does not permit *cosmic* extension at all. This sharp asymmetry between human term-extension (allowed under contract) and divine term-extension (impossible) is itself a piece of theology.

## Rabbit Holes

1. **The *kitāb–ajal* pairing** ([13:38], [3:145], [2:235], [2:282]). Every *ajl* is paired with or implies a *kitāb*. Trace this pairing as a concept-of-its-own — the **written term** is one of the Quran's deepest infrastructural ideas.

2. **The two-terms ambiguity in [6:2].** Is the *ajalun musamman ʿindahu* (a) the resurrection, (b) the lifespan-of-the-cosmos, (c) the *Hour*? The classical commentaries split. Worth a separate study cross-referenced with sāʿa (السَّاعَة).

3. **Why is the Form II verb suppressed?** Of 56 occurrences, only 2 use the verb form *ajjala* (and both with passive or theological framing). The Quran prefers the noun. Why? Hypothesis: the noun is **reified fate**, the verb would be **act-of-fating** — and the Quran prefers to present *ajl* as an *already-given* feature of creation, not as an ongoing decree. Worth comparing to qadar.

4. **The asymmetric deadline.** *Lā yastaʾkhirūn wa-lā yastaqdimūn* is morphologically symmetric but theologically asymmetric: most readers focus on "no delay," but the impossibility of "no advance" is the philosophically harder claim. Why can't a soul *die early*? (Because *istiqdām* is impossible.) This refuses suicide-as-acceleration, refuses martyrdom-as-acceleration. The term is fixed *both* ways.

5. **[5:32] *min ajli ḏālika* as the lexical fossil.** What does it mean that the *only* causal-prepositional use of the root anchors the most-cited "kill one soul / save one soul" verse? Is "for the sake of" semantically continuous with "for the term of"? The English "*for*" preserves both senses. Worth a comparative-Semitic check (Hebrew *l-* / Aramaic).

6. **The Moses-Shuʿayb dual *ajalayn* in [28:28–29].** This is the only place *ajl* takes the dual. It is also a marriage-contract narrative. The dual marks a *human* contractual choice (between two terms of service). Compare to [2:282]'s commercial *ajal*. The Quran is using *ajal* as the integrative concept across all forms of human bounded obligation.

7. **The geometric system.** If Ajl = endpoint, wqt = instant, Amd = stretch, what then are *ḥīn* (حِين) and *sāʿa* (سَاعَة)? Two more time-words. **Hypothesis to test: Quranic time is a five-coordinate system** — endpoint, instant, stretch, indefinite-while, hour-of-judgment. Each coordinate has its own root and its own grammar. This would be the next investigation.
