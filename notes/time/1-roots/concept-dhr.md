# دَهْر (dhr): Pre-Islamic Fate Disarmed in Two Verses

## Headline finding: the Quran lets *al-dahr* speak only twice — once to refute it, once to use it as a neutral cosmic backdrop

The root **dhr (د-ه-ر)** is the textual ghost of pre-Islamic Arabian theology. *Al-dahr* — Time-as-fate, the impersonal devourer — was, in jāhilī cosmology, the agent that ate generations and to whom no prayer could be addressed. The Quran admits this word **exactly twice**: once in the mouth of the deniers ([45:24]) and once in its own narrating voice ([76:1]). That is the entire corpus. Two verses, one lemma (`d~ahor`), one root, both definite (*al-dahr*), both metrically and rhetorically heavy.

The scarcity is the thesis. A vocabulary item this culturally loaded does not appear two times by accident in a 77,000-word text. The Quran disarms *al-dahr* by **demoting it from agent to backdrop**: in [45:24] it is quoted as the falsely-accused destroyer (then refuted); in [76:1] it is repurposed as a measure of the cosmic interval before the human existed. In neither verse does the Quran allow *al-dahr* to *do* anything as a subject in its own voice. The grammar of the refutation is the refutation.

## The two verses, in full

### [45:24] — the polemic

> وَقَالُوا۟ مَا هِىَ إِلَّا حَيَاتُنَا ٱلدُّنْيَا نَمُوتُ وَنَحْيَا وَمَا يُهْلِكُنَآ إِلَّا ٱلدَّهْرُ وَمَا لَهُم بِذَٰلِكَ مِنْ عِلْمٍ إِنْ هُمْ إِلَّا يَظُنُّونَ

> "And they say: 'There is nothing but our life of this world; we die and we live, and nothing destroys us but al-dahr.' And they have of that no knowledge; they are only conjecturing."

The verse has a five-part architecture:

1. **Speech-act frame:** *wa-qālū* — "and they say." The position is quoted, not endorsed.
2. **Cosmological claim:** *mā hiya illā ḥayātunā al-dunyā* — there is no life but this worldly one (denial of the afterlife).
3. **Temporal cycle:** *namūtu wa-naḥyā* — "we die and we live" (cyclic naturalism; possibly: "some die and some are born," collective).
4. **Agency claim:** *wa-mā yuhliku-nā illā al-dahr* — "nothing destroys us but al-dahr." This is the **theological pivot**. Al-dahr is named as the only operative agent.
5. **Refutation:** *wa-mā lahum bi-ḏālika min ʿilm in hum illā yaẓunnūn* — "they have no knowledge of that; they are only conjecturing."

The Quran does not refute *al-dahr* by replacing it with another noun. It refutes it by attacking the **epistemic ground** of the claim: you have no *ʿilm* (knowledge); this is *ẓann* (conjecture). The word stays; what is dismantled is the speakers' authority to use it.

But there is a second, surgical move: *uhliku-nā* (destroy us) is the verb. Elsewhere in the Quran, *ahlaka* with God as agent is one of the most common destruction-verbs (~70 occurrences with Allah as subject). The deniers attribute to *al-dahr* the verb that the Quran consistently attributes to Allah. The disagreement is not "is there destruction?" but "**who destroys?**" Allah, not impersonal time. This makes [45:24] one of the cleanest **anti-jāhiliyya theological substitutions** in the corpus.

### [76:1] — the cosmic opening

> هَلْ أَتَىٰ عَلَى ٱلْإِنسَـٰنِ حِينٌۭ مِّنَ ٱلدَّهْرِ لَمْ يَكُن شَيْـًۭٔا مَّذْكُورًا

> "Has there come upon man a *ḥīn min al-dahr* in which he was not a thing mentioned?"

Here *al-dahr* is **inert**. It is the genitive substrate from which a *ḥīn* (a stretch, a while) is carved out. The structure is *ḥīn min al-dahr* — "a while *of* time." Al-dahr is reduced from agent to **partitive container**. Compare the syntactic demotion: at 45:24 it stood as nominative subject of *yuhliku* ("destroys"); at 76:1 it is a genitive object of the preposition *min* ("from"). The grammar enacts the doctrine.

The verse opens Sūrat al-Insān, and its rhetorical force is creational — it reminds the human being that there was once a "while" when he did not exist, was not even mentioned. This is the Quran taking the most dangerous word in pre-Islamic Arabian metaphysics and using it to teach the most basic Quranic anthropology: **you came from non-being into being by Allah's act, not by your own continuity in time**.

## Translation divergence

### 45:24 — *al-dahr*

| Translator | Rendering |
|---|---|
| Saheeh | "time" |
| Pickthall | "time" |
| Khattab | "˹the passage of˺ time" |
| Arberry | "Time" (capital T) |

**Arberry alone capitalizes Time** — the only one to mark the deification implicit in the Arabic definite article. Khattab inserts the bracket *˹the passage of˺*, defusing the deity-suggestion and naturalizing it as a process. Saheeh and Pickthall give the bare lower-case noun, neither personifying nor de-personifying. The four English renderings span the full interpretive range: deity (Arberry), agent (Saheeh, Pickthall), process (Khattab). **No translator dares to write "Fate" or "al-Dahr" as a proper noun**, even though that is what the speakers in the verse mean.

### 76:1 — *ḥīn min al-dahr*

| Translator | Rendering |
|---|---|
| Saheeh | "a period of time" |
| Pickthall | "any period of time" |
| Khattab | "a period of time when each human is nothing yet worth mentioning" (outlier) |
| Arberry | "a while of time" |

Khattab is flagged as the divergence outlier (combined score 0.6529, jaccard 0.3782). His move: he **paraphrases the rhetorical question as a declarative anthropological observation**, swapping the past-tense *lam yakun* ("was not") for the present-tense "is nothing." This collapses the cosmological backdrop into a generic statement about every human. Arberry alone keeps the doublet ("a while of time") that mirrors the Arabic *ḥīn min al-dahr* — the only one to preserve the grammatical layering of two time-words.

## Negative space

What the Quran *does not* do with *dahr* is the analytical core.

1. **No verbal forms.** No *tadahhara* (to grow old), no *dāhara* (to outlast). Arabic morphology generates these freely from any triliteral root; the Quran uses none.
2. **No adjective *dahrī*.** The word *dahriyyūn*, "fatalists/eternalists," is a post-Quranic kalām category. Its absence from the corpus is a textual fact, not a translational artifact.
3. **No oath formula on *al-dahr*.** Compare *wa-l-ʿaṣr*, *wa-l-fajr*, *wa-l-layl*, *wa-l-ḍuḥā*, *wa-l-shams*, *wa-l-ʿādiyāt* — the Quran swears by every other temporal measure. It refuses to swear by *al-dahr*. Absence of this oath is structurally significant: God will not make the deniers' deity into His own oath-witness.
4. **No co-occurrence with Allah.** Across both verses, *al-dahr* and *Allāh* never appear in the same clause. The Quran keeps them lexically apart. This is unusual for a quasi-temporal noun; *yawm* co-occurs with *Allāh* hundreds of times.
5. **No second occurrence in the same surah.** The two verses are 31 surahs apart and never echo each other — there is no *dahr*-formula being built.

The negative space is the engineering. *Al-dahr* is admitted into the corpus only under quarantine: once as quoted error, once as inert backdrop. **Programmatic scarcity.**

## Cross-references

- **The hadith qudsī on *al-dahr*** (post-Quranic, but indispensable context): "The son of Adam reviles me — he insults the Time (*yasubbu al-dahr*) — and I am the Time (*wa-anā al-dahr*); in My hand is the Command, I turn the night and the day." This hadith makes explicit what [45:24] only implies: *al-dahr* is a name (or function) of God, not an independent agent. The Quran refuses to do this work itself; the prophetic tradition does. **The Quran's silence on the identification is theologically louder than its making the identification would be.**
- **Sibling time-roots** ([concept-Hyn](concept-Hyn.md), [concept-Esr](concept-Esr.md), [concept-Ajl](concept-Ajl.md), [concept-ywm](concept-ywm.md)) — together these form the Quran's working time-vocabulary. *Dahr* is the conspicuous outsider: the word the Quran lets in only to dismantle.
- The 76:1 *ḥīn min al-dahr* construction is the **only place in the entire Quran** where two indefinite/measure time-words are stacked. This is itself a hapax syntactic move.

## Verses for direct study

- **[45:24]** — the anti-fate polemic. Read against the surrounding 45:21–26 for the full eschatological frame.
- **[76:1]** — the only positive-narration use of *al-dahr*. Read with 76:2 (*innā khalaqnā al-insāna min nuṭfatin amshājin*) — the verse immediately answers the rhetorical question with creation-from-mingled-drop.
- Compare with **[6:29]**, **[23:37]**, **[44:35]** — other "deniers' speech" verses where the eternal-world view is voiced and refuted. The 45:24 formula is part of a recurring **quoted-denial pattern**, but only at 45:24 does *al-dahr* surface as the named agent.

## Rabbit holes

1. **Why is [76:1] classified Medinan in our metadata?** Classical scholars are split (most early reciters held it Meccan, given style and content). The metadata field may be following a single tradition; worth a structural check across all "split-classification" surahs.
2. **The *yuhliku* (destroys) verb pattern.** Cross-reference all ~70 instances where *ahlaka* takes Allah as subject vs the one instance ([45:24]) where it takes *al-dahr* as subject. The grammatical substitution is the entire jāhiliyya/Quranic theological dispute in one verb.
3. **The absent oath.** Build the full inventory of Quranic oath-objects (every *wa-* + cosmic noun). *Dahr* is the most conspicuous missing item. What would the rhetorical effect have been if Sūrat al-ʿAṣr had instead opened *wa-l-dahr*? The oath-economy is doing theological work.
4. **The *ʿilm/ẓann* contrast.** [45:24]'s refutation works via epistemology, not metaphysics: "you have no *ʿilm*, you only *yaẓunnūn*." Trace this pair across the corpus — it is one of the Quran's most compact anti-skepticism formulas.
5. **Pair with [Esr](concept-Esr.md) — surah 103.** Both surahs (76 and 103) bracket the corpus's small-time vocabulary. Sūrat al-ʿAṣr swears by *al-ʿaṣr*; Sūrat al-Insān opens with *ḥīn min al-dahr*. Two surahs, two "time" words, two completely different rhetorical postures. **Why was *al-ʿaṣr* chosen as oath-object and *al-dahr* refused?** The answer is in this pair.
