# خلد (xld): Abiding-Forever — Deep Concept Analysis

## Headline Finding: Khulūd is a State of Being, not a Length of Time

The root **xld (خ-ل-د)** is the Quran's main eschatological vocabulary for "eternal residence" — but this is not how the lexicon usually presents it. Three structural facts force a re-reading:

1. **It is overwhelmingly nominal, not verbal.** Of 87 occurrences, 82 are nouns (94%), only 4 are verbs, and the dominant form by a huge margin is the active participle *xālidīn* / *xālidūn* — "the abiding-ones." The Quran does not generally say "they will dwell" (verb of action through time); it says "they are abiders" (an attribute they wear). Khulūd is a *condition*, not a process. The state-noun *xulūd* itself appears exactly once ([50:34], "the Day of Khulūd") — eternity is referenced almost entirely through its inhabitants.

2. **It is bidirectional and almost symmetric.** Of the 74 *xālid* occurrences, roughly **42 describe Paradise inhabitants and 30 describe Hell inhabitants**, with the rest non-eschatological. Eternity is not the property of reward; it is the property of the **final settling**. Heaven's eternity and hell's eternity are described with the same word, in the same grammar, in the same surahs, often in adjacent verses ([10:26]/[10:27], [11:107]/[11:108], [39:72]/[39:73]). This is the Quran's most unflinching parallelism.

3. **It does NOT describe God.** Across 87 occurrences, *xālid* is never predicated of Allah. The "ever-living" attribute of God is carried by entirely different vocabulary (*al-Ḥayy al-Qayyūm*, *al-Bāqī*). The Quran reserves *xld* for **created** durative-existence — the residency that God grants or imposes. Divine eternity and creaturely abiding-forever belong to different lexical fields. This is doctrinally load-bearing and lexically clean.

The root sits in the "Believers' Reward" Louvain cluster (cluster 0) and is structurally a **bridge node**: it connects to 35 other clusters in the network — the highest inter-cluster connectivity score in its band. *xld* is the word that ties paradise-architecture vocabulary (*jnn*, *nhr*, *jry*) to judgement-vocabulary (*Alh*, *nwr*) to faith/work vocabulary (*Amn*).

## The Khālidīna Fīhā Formula — the Most Repeated Eschatological Phrase

The phrase **خَٰلِدِينَ فِيهَا** ("abiding-ones in it") is the Quran's signature eternity-formula. In **66 of 67** plural *xālid* occurrences (98.5%), it is followed immediately by *fīhā* — "in it/her." The single exception is **[39:73]** (*"sa-lām ʿalaykum ṭibtum fa-d-khulū-hā xālidīn"* — "Peace upon you; you have done well; enter it as abiders") where the prior *fa-d-khulū-hā* already supplies the "in it." The formula is essentially fixed.

The pronoun antecedent of *-hā* (feminine singular "it") is one of a small set:
- **al-jannah / jannāt** (the Garden) — overwhelmingly common in reward verses
- **al-nār / jahannam / al-saʿīr** (the Fire / Gehenna / Blaze) — punishment side
- **al-ʿaḏāb** (the punishment) — masculine, hence *fīhi* in [4:14], [4:93], [25:69], [20:101]
- The vague near-feminine antecedent (hādhā: "this") — for the burden ([20:100–101])

This is the Quran's most stripped-down eschatological grammar: **active participle + locative pronoun**. No verb, no time-reference, no duration measurement. Just *they are residers in it*. The English "they will abide forever therein" requires three lexical units the Arabic does not have. *Xālidīn fīhā* IS the eternity statement, in two words.

### The Ratio: Reward vs Punishment

Reading every *xa`lid* occurrence in context:
- **~42 occurrences refer to Paradise** (jannah, naʿīm, firdaws, ʿadn, al-ḥusnā, raḥmat Allāh)
- **~30 occurrences refer to Hell** (jahannam, nār, ʿaḏāb, saʿīr)
- **~2 occurrences refer to "what they desire"** ([21:102], [25:16]) — paradise inhabitants getting their wishes "as abiders"
- **3 non-eschatological:**
  - [21:8], [21:34] — denials: "We did not make them [the prophets] bodies that did not eat food, *nor were they immortal*"; "We have not granted any human before you immortality"
  - [21:99] — false gods enter hell *with* their worshipers; "all of them are abiders"
  - [7:20] — Iblis tempting Adam: "your Lord forbade you this tree only that you not become angels or *of the immortals*"

The reward/punishment ratio is ~58:42. **There is no asymmetry of vocabulary**: the eternity-word is identical for both fates. This is grammatically striking — the Quran does not soften hell with a different word, and it does not lift paradise with a different word. Khulūd is *bivalent*. It marks the irreversibility, not the moral content.

### The Singular Forms — Three Hell Verses

The singular *xālidan fīhā* appears only three times and **always in punishment**:
- **[4:14]** — *yudkhilhu nāran xālidan fīhā wa-lahu ʿaḏābun muhīn* — whoever transgresses Allah's limits, "[He] will admit him into a Fire, abiding therein, and for him is a humiliating punishment."
- **[4:93]** — *wa-man yaqtul muʾminan mutaʿammidan fa-jazāʾuhu jahannamu xālidan fīhā* — whoever kills a believer intentionally, "his recompense is Hell, abiding therein."
- **[9:63]** — *fa-anna lahu nāra jahannama xālidan fīhā* — for whoever opposes Allah and His Messenger, "for him is the Fire of Hell, abiding therein."

That singular-form usage is restricted to hell (and within hell, to **named individual transgressions**: line-crossing, intentional murder, opposition) is one of the more telling syntactic facts in this root. The mass of believers and disbelievers gets the plural; the named singular sin gets the singular.

## The Abadan Reinforcement — When Does Khulūd Get Doubled?

Eleven verses combine *xa`lid* with the adverb *abadan* (from root **Abd**). Why double-mark eternity? The data shows a strong pattern:

| Loc | Frame | Side |
|-----|-------|------|
| 4:57 | jannāt + tajrī + xālidīna fīhā **abadan** | Paradise |
| 4:122 | jannāt + tajrī + xālidīna fīhā **abadan** + *waʿd Allāh ḥaqqan* | Paradise |
| 4:169 | ṭarīqa jahannam + xālidīna fīhā **abadan** | Hell |
| 5:119 | jannāt + xālidīna fīhā **abadan** + *raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhum* | Paradise |
| 9:22 | nā‍ʿīm muqīm + xālidīna fīhā **abadan** | Paradise |
| 9:100 | jannāt + xālidīna fīhā **abadan** + *al-fawz al-ʿaẓīm* | Paradise |
| 33:65 | (saʿīr) + xālidīna fīhā **abadan** | Hell |
| 64:9 | jannāt + xālidīna fīhā **abadan** + *al-fawz al-ʿaẓīm* | Paradise |
| 65:11 | jannāt + xālidīna fīhā **abadan** + *aḥsana llāhu lahu rizqan* | Paradise |
| 72:23 | nāra jahannam + xālidīna fīhā **abadan** | Hell |
| 98:8 | jannāt ʿadn + xālidīna fīhā **abadan** + *raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhum* | Paradise |

**Eight of eleven are paradise; three are hell.** The reinforcement is **not symmetric**. Where *abadan* is added to a hell-formula, it is in maximally severe contexts: 4:169 (the path of Hell for those denying Allah/Messengers), 33:65 (the disbelievers cursed by Allah, prepared a *saʿīr* with no protector or helper), 72:23 (whoever disobeys Allah and His Messenger). These are "categorical disbelief + opposition" verses, not the more general *kāfir* formulas.

Where *abadan* is added to a paradise-formula, it tends to be in **maximally honorific contexts**: 4:122 ("the truthful promise of Allah, and who is more truthful than Allah in speech?"), 5:119 ("Allah is well-pleased with them and they with Him; that is the great triumph"), 9:100 (the *sābiqūn al-awwalūn* — the foremost of the Emigrants and Helpers), 9:22 (*ajrun ʿaẓīm* with Allah), 98:8 (the paradise of *ʿadn*, *raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhum wa-raḍū ʿanh*).

This suggests *abadan* is functioning as a **rhetorical superlative**, not a logical clarifier. Khulūd is already eternity; *abadan* is the emphatic seal — added when the verse is already operating in the highest register. It is closer to "and ever" than to "actually-forever-this-time."

## The Khuld-Noun — Six Construct Forms

The verbal noun *xulod* (خُلْد) appears 6 times, always in construct, naming a thing-of-eternity:

- **[10:52]** *ʿaḏāb al-xuld* — "the punishment of eternity"
- **[20:120]** *shajarat al-xuld* — "the tree of eternity" (Iblis tempting Adam)
- **[21:34]** *al-xuld* — "immortality" (denied to all humans)
- **[25:15]** *jannat al-xuld* — "the Garden of Eternity"
- **[32:14]** *ʿaḏāb al-xuld* — "the punishment of eternity"
- **[41:28]** *dār al-xuld* — "the abode of eternity" (here applied to **the Fire**: "for them therein is the abode of eternity")

Striking observation: *dār al-xuld* — the "abode of eternity" — is applied **to Hell** ([41:28]: *lahum fīhā dār al-xuld jazāʾan bimā kānū bi-āyātinā yajḥadūn*), not to Paradise. *Jannat al-xuld* (Garden of eternity) appears only at [25:15]. The construct phrases divide cleanly: 2× *ʿaḏāb al-xuld* (Hell), 1× *dār al-xuld* (Hell), 1× *jannat al-xuld* (Paradise), 1× *shajarat al-xuld* (the temptation), 1× *al-xuld* (denied to humans). The "abode of eternity," in the Quran's own vocabulary, is the Fire. This cuts against the popular use where *dār al-xuld* often denotes paradise in later devotional Arabic. The Quranic distribution is the opposite.

The single occurrence of the abstract noun *xulūd* is **[50:34]**: *udxulūhā bi-salām, dhālika yawm al-xulūd* — "Enter it in peace; this is the Day of Eternity." This is the inauguration-formula of paradise residence. Note translator divergence: Saheeh/Pickthall/Arberry "Day of Eternity / immortality"; Khattab "Day of eternal life." Khattab adds "life" — supplying a referent the Arabic withholds.

## The Two Verbs — and Why They Are So Rare

That the verbal forms appear only 4 times across the whole Quran (vs. 82 nominal forms) is itself the finding. The Quran prefers to make khulūd a **state-attribute** rather than an **action**. People do not "abide" — they *are* abiders. The few verb occurrences are correspondingly sharp:

**`>axolada` (Form IV, "to make-eternal / to cling-to as if eternal"):**
- **[7:176]** — *wa-lākinnahu **axlada** ilā l-arḍ wa-ttabaʿa hawāhu* — of Balaam (per tradition): "but he **clung-as-if-eternal** to the earth and followed his desire." Here the verb takes *ilā l-arḍ* (to the earth) — making earth one's permanent home in attitude. The verb of khulūd is used **negatively**: to delusionally treat the temporal as eternal.
- **[104:3]** — *yaḥsibu anna mālahu **axladahu*** — "He thinks his wealth has **made-him-eternal**." Same pattern: clinging-to-the-temporary as if it conferred khulūd. Both verbs are condemnations.

**`yaxoludo` (Form I imperfect):**
- **[25:69]** — punishment doubled on Day of Resurrection, *wa-**yaxlud** fīhi muhānan* — "and he abides therein humiliated."
- **[26:129]** — Hud rebukes his people: *wa-tattakhidhūna maṣāniʿa laʿallakum **taxludūn*** — "and you take fortresses, perhaps you will abide [forever]." Again, the delusion of immortality projected onto stone constructions.

**Pattern:** When khulūd is a verb, it is either (a) hell-residency or (b) the **delusion of permanence projected onto the worldly**. The verb is a marker of false-eternity; the participle is the marker of true-eternity. *Akhlada ila l-arḍ* and *taxludūn fī al-maṣāniʿ* are the lexical antitheses of *xālidīna fī l-jannah*.

## The Wildān Mukhalladūn — Eternal Youths

Two verses ([56:17] and [76:19]) describe paradise: *yaṭūfu ʿalayhim wildānun **muxalladūn*** — "circulating among them are youths **made-eternal**." The form is the passive participle of Form II (*xallada* "to immortalize"). These youths are not described as the rewarded believers; they are *attendants*. The morphology — passive, made-eternal — distinguishes them from the active *xālidūn* (the believers themselves who *are* abiders). The believers abide; the youths are *made* to abide. This is one of the few places the Quran grammatically distinguishes two registers of paradise-eternity.

Translation divergence here is significant. Combined divergence score for [56:17] is **0.80** (high regime). Saheeh/Khattab "young boys made eternal / eternal youths"; Pickthall/Arberry "immortal youths." Khattab inserts "they will be waited on by" (active reframe), while Arberry preserves the distancing "shall go about them." The translators are uncertain how literally to render *muxalladūn*: is this "made-not-to-die" or "made-not-to-age"? The Arabic does not specify; classical exegesis (Ṭabarī) reads it as "perpetually young" — never aging *and* never dying. The lexicon of *xld* admits both readings; the Quran does not disambiguate.

## The "Save What Your Lord Wills" Exception — Khulūd Bracketed

Two adjacent verses contain the most theologically discussed *xld* construction in the corpus:

- **[11:107]** (Hell) — *xālidīna fīhā mā dāmati l-samāwātu wa-l-arḍ illā mā shāʾa rabbuk; inna rabbaka faʿʿālun li-mā yurīd* — "abiding therein **as long as the heavens and the earth endure, except what your Lord wills**; surely your Lord is doer of what He intends."
- **[11:108]** (Paradise) — *xālidīna fīhā mā dāmati l-samāwātu wa-l-arḍ illā mā shāʾa rabbuka; ʿaṭāʾan ghayra majdhūdh* — "abiding therein **as long as the heavens and the earth endure, except what your Lord wills**; a gift uninterrupted."

Three things happen here that happen nowhere else:
1. **Khulūd is conditioned on cosmic duration** ("as long as heavens and earth endure"). This is the only place in the Quran where the eternity-formula gets a temporal frame. Both fire and garden are bounded by *mā dāma*.
2. **An exception clause is added** (*illā mā shāʾa rabbuk*). The will of Allah is preserved as a residual reservation against any seemingly-categorical statement.
3. **The two verses are syntactically twinned** but **lexically distinguished at the close**: hell ends *inna rabbaka faʿʿālun li-mā yurīd* (a bare assertion of divine sovereignty); paradise ends *ʿaṭāʾan ghayra majdhūdh* (a positive affirmation of unbroken gift). The exception clause is identical; the closure is asymmetric. The Quran refuses to add a counter-balancing positive affirmation to the hell-eternity. This is the only formal asymmetry in the entire root's treatment of the two fates.

[6:128] supplies the third "save" clause — applied to hell-jinn-residency: *al-nāru mathwākum xālidīna fīhā **illā mā shāʾa Allāh*** — exception again.

These three exception-clauses are the doctrinal seam. They have generated the entire later debate on *fanāʾ al-nār* (annihilation of the Fire) and the universalist undercurrent in classical Sunni and Shi'i theology.

Lift score *xld–nhr* = **15.6**, lift *xld–Abd* = **28.5**. These are not soft co-occurrences; they are formulaic. | 11:107 (mā dāmat exception) | 0.60 | mid | Arberry compressed |
| 25:15 (jannat al-xuld) | 0.60 | mid | "eternity / immortality / eternal life" |

The pattern: **the further the verse is from the standard *khālidīna fīhā* formula, the more translators diverge.** The formula itself is so over-attested it is a translator-anchor. The marginal cases — *muxalladūn*, *axlada*, *xulūd* in unusual frames — split the translators sharply. Pickthall and Arberry tend toward "immortal," Saheeh and Khattab toward "eternal/abiding." The lexical question — does *xld* mean "deathless" (mortality-negation) or "abiding-without-end" (durative-permanence)? — is not settled by the text and is settled differently by different translators.

For the eschatological mass-formula verses, divergence is **uniformly mid** (0.55–0.65). The disagreement is consistent and small: each translator picks a slightly different way to handle the *abadan* intensifier (Saheeh: "forever"; Khattab: "for ever and ever"; Arberry: "forever and ever" or just "forever" depending on rhythm).

## Negative Space — Khulūd is Never God's

Across 87 occurrences, *xld* is **never** predicated of Allah. The verses that affirm divine eternity — and they are central — use entirely different vocabulary:

- *al-Ḥayy al-Qayyūm* (the Living, the Self-Subsistent) — [2:255], [3:2], [20:111] — root **Hyy** (life) + **qwm** (standing). This is the "ever-living self-subsisting" attribute.
- *al-Bāqī* (the Remaining) — [55:27]: *kullu man ʿalayhā fān, wa-yabqā wajhu rabbika* — "everyone on it [the earth] is perishing, and the Face of your Lord remains." Root **bqy**.
- *al-Awwal wa-l-Ākhir* (the First and the Last) — [57:3]. Roots **Awl** and **Axr** for divine temporal boundlessness.

The Quran enforces a **lexical apartheid**: divine eternity uses *Hyy*, *qwm*, *bqy*, *Awl/Axr*. Created abiding uses *xld*, *Abd*, *qym* (in *muqīm* "permanent" of paradise/punishment). They do not cross. This is not casual — it is one of the cleanest lexical partitions in the entire Quran. The eternity-of-residence (*xld*) and the eternity-of-being (*Hyy/bqy*) are categorically different.

The **second negative**: *xld* never names a measurable duration. There is no *khulūd ʿalā mīʿādin* ("eternity to a fixed point") — that would be a contradiction. Khulūd is presented as **categorical** (abiding) not **quantitative** (long-lasting). It is binary, not gradient.

The **third negative**: *xld* never describes the *dunyā*. The Quran does not say earthly life is *xālid* — even when narrating its long durations, it uses *Ajl* (term), *amad* (span), *ʿumr* (lifespan). The two verbs that come closest — *axlada* ([7:176]) and *yatakhludūn* ([26:129]) — are both **rebukes** of those who treat the temporal as if it had khulūd. *Xld* is reserved for the post-judgement settlement; trying to project it onto worldly life is the error these two verbs name.

## The Cross-Cutting Finding — Two Registers of Forever

Comparing **xld** (87 occ.) and **Abd** (28 occ., all *abadan*) reveals a clean partition between two grammatical functions of "forever":

| | xld | Abd |
|--|-----|-----|
| **POS** | nominal/state (94% N) | adverbial (100% T-time) |
| **Stand-alone use?** | Yes — *xālidīna fīhā* is a complete formula | Almost never — *abadan* attaches to a verb or pre-existing predicate |
| **Polarity** | Bivalent (paradise + hell ~58:42) | **Overwhelmingly negative**: 22 of 28 *abadan* are inside a negation (*lan*, *lā*, *mā*) |
| **Domain** | Eschatological residency | Mostly worldly oaths and prohibitions |
| **Reinforces other words?** | No — it is the headword | Yes — it reinforces *xālid* in 11 verses |

**The hypothesis to test:** *xld* is the **noun of abiding-state** ("residency without termination"); *Abd* is the **adverb of temporal absoluteness** ("at no time, ever"). When the Quran wants to say "they reside in paradise eternally," the ground word is *xālid*; *abadan* is decoration. When the Quran wants to say "they will never go out with you" or "the matter will not happen, ever," the ground word is the negated verb; *abadan* is the temporal seal.

The data supports this strongly. *xld* lives in eschatology; *Abd* lives in negation. Their overlap (11 ayahs) is the class of verses where eschatological residency receives a maximal-emphasis seal — the Quran's most rhetorically heightened eternity statements.

A sharper formulation: **xld is *substantive eternity*, Abd is *modal eternity*.** One says *what is eternal*; the other says *that something is/is-not at any time*. They are not synonyms; they are complements. Together they cover the full range — durative state-of-being (*xld*) and quantified-temporal absoluteness (*Abd*).

| Al-Nisāʾ (4) | 6 | Contains 3 of 3 singular-form (xālidan) hell verses |
| Āl ʿImrān (3) | 6 | 3:107 (rahmat Allāh) is one of the rare paradise-as-rahmah formulations |
| Al-Anbiyāʾ (21) | 5 | Carries the **denial-of-immortality** verses (21:8, 21:34) |
| Al-Furqān (25) | 4 | All three lemmas appear: xālid, xulod, yaxlud |
| Al-Aʿrāf (7) | 4 | Includes the Iblis-temptation verse (7:20) and the Balaam akhlada (7:176) |
| Al-Hajj/Hud/Yunus | 3 each | Hud has the *mā dāmat* exception verses |

Surah 21 (Al-Anbiyāʾ) is the most **theologically interesting** for *xld*: it is the only surah where the root is used both eschatologically (21:99, 21:102) AND meta-eschatologically (21:8 — denying that prophets were immortal; 21:34 — denying that any human is granted immortality). The same surah affirms eternity for the inhabitants of Paradise/Hell while denying it categorically to all living humans. The doctrinal frame: khulūd is a post-judgement state, not a pre-judgement attribute.

## Verses for Direct Study

The high-yield set, ordered by analytical density:

1. **[11:107–108]** — the *mā dāmat* + *illā mā shāʾa rabbuk* exception clauses. The most theologically loaded *xld* construction. Twinned hell/paradise.
2. **[39:72–73]** — the consecutive hell-then-paradise gating verses; the formulaic *abwāb jahannam* / *abwāb al-jannah* parallel. [39:73] has the only *xālidīn* without *fīhā*.
3. **[2:25] vs [2:39]** — the first paradise *xālid* and the first hell *xālid* in the Quran, six ayahs apart.
4. **[21:34]** vs **[7:20]** — the explicit denial of human immortality, paired with Iblis offering it as temptation. The grammatical mirror.
5. **[41:28]** — *dār al-xuld* applied to **Hell**. Cuts against later devotional usage.
6. **[50:34]** — the unique *yawm al-xulūd*. Paradise-entry inauguration.
7. **[56:17] / [76:19]** — *wildānun muxalladūn*. The only passive Form-II usage.
8. **[7:176] / [104:3]** — the two *axlada* condemnations of clinging-as-if-eternal.
9. **[25:69]** — the only Form-I *yaxlud* in eschatology; in hell, *muhānan* ("humiliated").
10. **[4:14] / [4:93] / [9:63]** — the three singular *xālidan fīhā*, all hell, all named-individual sins.

## Rabbit Holes Worth Pursuing

1. **The *dār al-xuld* inversion.** The Quran applies "abode of eternity" to Hell ([41:28]); later Sunni devotional Arabic applies it to Paradise. When and why did the inversion happen? This is a tradition-history question, not a Quran-internal one — but the Quran's own usage is the diagnostic baseline.

2. **The exception clauses and *fanāʾ al-nār*.** Three verses ([6:128], [11:107], [11:108]) bracket khulūd with *illā mā shāʾa rabbuka*. Ibn Taymiyya and Ibn Qayyim drew on these to argue Hell is not literally eternal. The Ashʿarī mainstream resists this. The lexical data is settled; what is unsettled is the force of the exception clause. Worth a separate methodology entry: the "save-what-your-Lord-wills" exception in *xld*, *ywm*, *Ajl*, *qDy*.

3. **Khulūd vs *muqīm*.** The participle *muqīm* ("permanent, residing") is used of paradise (*naʿīmun muqīm*, [9:21], [9:22]) and of punishment (*ʿaḏābun muqīm*, [5:37], [11:39]). It overlaps the semantic field of *xālid* but does not replace it. *Muqīm* may carry a slightly different valence — "permanent" as quality of the thing, not of its inhabitants. Worth mapping.

4. **Why is the verb so suppressed?** 82 nominal vs 4 verbal occurrences is an extreme ratio. Compare the verb-heavy roots (*qwl* "to say" — 1722 occurrences mostly verbal). The Quran's khulūd-grammar refuses agency. Believers do not actively *abide*; they passively *are* abiders. This may reflect a deliberate theological grammar — eternity as condition received, not action performed.

5. **The Iblis-Adam temptation pair.** [7:20] (the *xālidīn* offer) and [20:120] (the *shajarat al-xuld* offer) are Iblis's two attempts to dangle khulūd before the human. Both are temptations to a state that humans cannot have. The fact that the Tempter's offer is precisely the eschatological reward — eternity — is one of the Quran's quietest theological moves. The serpent in Genesis offers *knowledge*; Iblis in the Quran offers *immortality*. That difference is worth a dedicated study.

6. **The *mukhalladūn* youths.** Are they youths-not-aging, or youths-not-dying? Or both? The Form-II passive participle is morphologically ambiguous between intensive ("perpetually-y") and causative-passive ("made-Y"). Translators split. Classical exegesis splits. The text does not adjudicate. This is one of the small lexical questions where the text deliberately under-specifies and the tradition over-specifies.

## Methodology note

This analysis began from the lemma table — which immediately revealed the 74:6:2:2:2:1 distribution and the dominance of the active participle. The pivot was noticing that POS distribution is 94% nominal — a stat-driven question ("why so few verbs?") that anchored the whole analysis on khulūd-as-state-not-action. Cross-classification of all 74 *xa`lid* contexts against jannah/nār vocabulary (via grep) gave the ~58:42 reward/punishment ratio, which falsifies any naive expectation of a paradise-bias. The eleven xld+Abd overlap verses were extracted by intersection of the two concept JSONs. The exception-clause cluster ([6:128], [11:107], [11:108]) emerged from reading every hell-context verse for syntactic anomalies. Negative space (no *xld* of God) was confirmed by checking the well-known divine-eternity verses for vocabulary. Logged in `notes/methodology-log.md`.
