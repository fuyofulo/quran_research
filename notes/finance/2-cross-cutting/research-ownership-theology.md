# Ownership Theology — Who Actually Owns Wealth in the Quranic Frame?

## Headline finding (≈100 words)

The Quran runs a deliberate double grammar: Allah is named **absolute owner** of the heavens and the earth, while humans speak constantly of "their wealth" and "your property." The reconciliation is **stewardship**, encoded across the financial vocabulary. The keystone is the **mwl / rzq split**: *amwāl* (humans' wealth, 86×) marks social responsibility; *rizq* (God's provision, 123×, 80% with divine verb-subject) marks source. Qarun's *ʿilm ʿindī* names the failure mode (self-attribution); Solomon's *hādhā min faḍli rabbī* names the corrective. *Kanz* (human hoarding) and *khazāʾin* (divine treasuries) make the same storage act differ by owner. Ḥashr 59:7's *dūla* clause states the social rule. The Quran builds neither absolute private property nor common ownership — it builds **fiduciary tenancy under divine title, auditable on the Last Day**.

---

## 1. The double grammar

Two grammatical patterns run in parallel through the entire corpus.

**Axis A — divine ownership formula.** *Lillāhi mā fī l-samāwāti wa-mā fī l-arḍ* ("to Allah belongs what is in the heavens and the earth") recurs across surahs as a sovereignty refrain ([2:284], [3:109], [3:129], [4:131-132], [4:170-171], [10:55], [10:66], [14:2], [24:64], [42:4], [53:31], among others). The construction is *li-* + Allah: the lām of ownership. The same lām appears in *al-mulk* verses — *lahu al-mulk* ("to Him belongs the dominion," [64:1], [67:1]). Allah is *al-Mālik* in the absolute sense: the verb of holding governs the entire created field.

**Axis B — possessive pronouns on wealth.** Within the same corpus, the Quran uses *amwāluhum* / *amwālakum* / *amwālinā* constantly — 43 of 86 *māl* occurrences carry a possessive suffix (data: wealth-core lemma table). "Their wealth" is the standard register for orphan property [4:2, 4:6], zakat targets [9:103], jihad expenditure [4:95, 8:72], legal prohibitions [2:188, 4:29], even the enemies' wealth [33:27, 59:6].

**The reconciliation.** The Arabic itself supplies it. *Māl* with a possessive is the grammar of **social attribution** — who handles it, who is legally responsible for it, who must account for it. *Mulk* with the lām of Allah is the grammar of **metaphysical title**. The two registers do not compete because they answer different questions. "Whose wealth is it socially?" — the orphan's, the merchant's, the zakat-payer's. "Whose wealth is it ultimately?" — God's. The keystone verse is [4:5], where God commands guardians not to give wealth to the weak-minded — *amwālakum* ("your wealth") that *jaʿala Allāhu lakum qiyāman* ("Allah has made for you a means of sustenance"). In one sentence: yours-to-handle, His-to-have-assigned.

## 2. The mwl / rzq keystone

The wealth-core agent's central finding is that the same physical wealth bears two descriptions depending on the relational frame.

| Term | Count | Verb subject | Frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| *māl* / *amwāl* | 86 | Human possesses, distributes, withholds | Wealth-as-responsibility |
| *rizq* / *razaqa* | 123 | 80% divine (49 of 61 verbs) | Wealth-as-gift |

*Māl* never appears with God as owner in a possessive construct; God does not "have *māl*" the way humans have *māl*. *Rizq* never appears in legal-distribution contexts (inheritance, ribā, contract): the inheritance fractions of [4:11-12] and the ribā prohibition of [2:275-279] use *māl* exclusively. Conversely, the zakat command formula *anfiqū mimmā razaqnākum* ("spend from what We have provided you," [2:3, 2:254, 63:10]) is built on *rizq* — the source-naming verb that subordinates the human act of spending to the prior divine act of giving.

This is not redundant vocabulary. It is the linguistic apparatus of stewardship. The same loaf of bread is *rizq* in the upward gaze (source) and *māl* in the social-legal gaze (handling). To speak of one's *rizq* as *māl* only — without ever returning the description to *rizq* — is to commit Qarun's error.

## 3. The self-attribution failure (Qarun, 28:76-82)

Qarun's confession is one phrase: *innamā ūtītuhu ʿalā ʿilmin ʿindī* — "I was only given it because of knowledge I possess" [28:78]. The construction is precise. The passive *ūtītu* ("I was given") concedes the form of the divine gift, but the *ʿalā ʿilmin ʿindī* clause re-attributes its **cause** to a property internal to Qarun. The wealth came through him because of him. This is the false ownership claim at its most explicit: not "I made this" but "I deserved this on my own merits."

The Quran's rebuttal in the same verse is historical, not metaphysical: *a-wa-lam yaʿlam anna Allāha qad ahlaka min qablihi min al-qurūni man huwa ashaddu minhu quwwatan wa-aktharu jamʿan* — "did he not know that Allah destroyed before him generations stronger than him in power and greater in accumulation?" The verb *jamaʿa* (to gather, accumulate) is the same root the Quran uses for the eschatological hoarders of [104:2-3] (*jamaʿa mālan wa-ʿaddadahū*). Self-attribution is named here as a **civilizational pattern** — connecting Qarun to ʿĀd ("we are mightier in force," [41:15]), Thamūd, and Sabaʾ. The earth swallows him at [28:81]; the formula his admirers retract at [28:82] (*wayka-anna Allāha yabsuṭu al-rizqa li-man yashāʾu*) is the *rizq*-extension formula — the very vocabulary Qarun refused.

## 4. The Solomon corrective formula

Solomon receives more wealth than any human in the Quran — wind, jinn workforce, molten copper, monumental infrastructure, the kingdom of [38:35] "such as will not belong to anyone after me." His vocabulary is the photo-negative of Qarun's.

> **27:40** — *hādhā min faḍli rabbī li-yabluwanī a-ashkuru am akfur*
>
> Saheeh: "This is from the favor of my Lord to test me whether I will be grateful or ungrateful."

Two markers do the theological work. *Faḍl rabbī* names the wealth as **divine bounty**, not earned product (*faḍl* appears with God-as-source ~50× across the Quran; it is the cardinal anti-meritocratic frame for any human good). The *li-yabluwanī* clause names the wealth as **test**, not reward — the same framing used negatively at [8:28], [18:46], [64:15] for *amwāl wa-awlād* as *fitna*.

The Solomon-Qarun comparison is sharpened by the **shared idiom** *ūtiya min kulli shayʾ* ("given from every thing") used of Solomon [27:16] and Sheba's queen [27:23]; both rulers, both wealthy, neither condemned for the wealth itself. The difference is in attribution-grammar. Solomon's first words upon receiving knowledge are *al-ḥamdu lillāh* [27:15] — the opening of al-Fātiḥa, the master formula of correct orientation. Qarun's first words are *innamā ūtītuhu ʿalā ʿilmin ʿindī*.

The Quranic doctrine: wealth is morally neutral matter passing through human hands. **The orientation toward its source — gratitude or self-attribution — determines its moral charge.**

## 5. The kanz / khazāʾin axis: same act, opposite owners

The wealth-core agent surfaced an unusual data point: the noun *kanz* (treasure, 6×) is morally neutral in Meccan contexts — used of the orphans' inheritance [18:82], Pharaoh's stores [26:58], Qarun's wealth [28:76]. The verb *kanaza* (to hoard, 3×) carries all the condemnation, concentrated in [9:34-35]. The verb is the crime, not the noun.

The condemnation in [9:34] is composite: *yaknizūna al-dhahaba wa-l-fiḍḍata wa-lā yunfiqūnahā fī sabīli Allāh* — they hoard gold and silver **and do not spend it in the way of Allah**. Non-circulation is the crime, not possession.

The corrective contrast is *khazāʾin* — divine treasuries. Of 8 *khazāʾin* occurrences, 7 are explicitly God's [6:50, 11:31, 15:21, 17:100, 38:9, 52:37, 63:7]. [15:21] is the load-bearing verse: *wa-in min shayʾin illā ʿindanā khazāʾinuhū wa-mā nunazziluhū illā bi-qadarin maʿlūm* — "there is nothing whose treasuries are not with Us, and We send it down only in a known measure." The next verse [15:22] strips humans of any custodianship of rain: humans are not *khāzinīn* of what God stores.

The two roots resolve the moral asymmetry:

- **Allah stores** because the wealth is His; storage is regulated distribution.
- **Humans store** what was lent to them; storage is appropriation.

The act is the same — wealth held back from circulation. The owner determines whether it is **stewardship of God's reserves** or **theft from God's circulation**. [17:100] makes this a cognitive diagnosis: if humans possessed God's treasuries of mercy, they would withhold them *khashyata al-infāq* (out of fear of spending) — human stinginess is a category error projected onto infinite stores.

## 6. The dūla principle (59:7)

The ownership doctrine has a social-distributive corollary. Al-Ḥashr 59:7 distributes fayʾ (wealth restored without battle) to five recipient categories — Allah, Messenger, kin, orphans, needy, wayfarer — with an explicit purposive clause:

> **59:7** — *kay lā yakūna dūlatan bayna l-aghniyāʾi minkum*
>
> Khattab: "so that wealth may not merely circulate among your rich."

The root *d-w-l* names rotation, alternation, a closed cycle. The grammatical construction is *kay lā yakūna* — purposive negation. This is the Quran's fullest stated rationale for any economic rule. The critique is **structural, not individual**: not "so that the rich do not hoard" but "so that wealth does not become a circuit confined to the wealthy class." Mandated distribution exists to break a self-reinforcing loop.

Connect to the ownership frame: if the wealth is ultimately Allah's, He is its distributor; the human social order must mirror that distribution. A closed circulation among the rich is class-level appropriation — a collective Qarun-claim that wealth is theirs to retain. The *dūla* clause is the **distributive expression of stewardship**: God's wealth must flow through all categories of God's creatures, not loop back to the already-holding.

## 7. The faḍl-Allah frame versus earned property

The phrase *faḍl Allāh* / *min faḍli rabbī* / *min faḍlihi* appears densely in finance contexts (~50× across the Quran, often paired with seeking provision: [4:32], [16:14], [17:66], [62:10], [73:20]). *Faḍl* names wealth as **undeserved bounty** — surplus, grace, what flows beyond what is owed. The Solomon formula [27:40] uses *faḍl*; the trade verse [62:10] commands believers to "seek the *faḍl* of Allah" after Friday prayer.

This frame is structurally **anti-meritocratic**. Modern economic thought defends property by appeal to desert: I produced it, therefore I deserve it. The Quranic *faḍl* frame collapses the desert premise — what you have was bestowed, not earned. This undercuts the moral force of "my wealth, my choice." It does not collapse private possession; it collapses the entitlement narrative that elevates possession into absolute claim.

The Madyan rebuttal at [11:87] (*aw an nafʿala fī amwālinā mā nashāʾu* — "or that we do with our wealth what we please") is the Quran's named libertarian-property objection. Shuʿayb does not refute it discursively; the destruction sequence is the refutation. The narrative form encodes the doctrine: claims to absolute property dominion collide with divine ownership and are annihilated.

## 8. Eschatological audit

The stewardship frame is held in tension by an accounting horizon. [2:281] — the verse classical tradition names the last revealed — closes the long financial section of al-Baqara with *wa-ittaqū yawman turjaʿūna fīhi ilā Allāhi thumma tuwaffā kullu nafsin mā kasabat* ("fear a Day when you will be returned to Allah; then every soul will be paid in full for what it earned"). The verb *tuwaffā* is from *wafāʾ* — full settlement, the discharge of a debt. The last word of the longest financial passage in the Quran frames every transaction as a **debit-credit entry settled on the Last Day**.

[99:7-8] generalizes: *fa-man yaʿmal mithqāla dharratin khayran yarahu wa-man yaʿmal mithqāla dharratin sharran yarahu* — atom-weight accounting. [104:1-3] indicts *humazatin lumazah / alladhī jamaʿa mālan wa-ʿaddadah / yaḥsabu anna mālahū akhladahu* — the wealth-counter who supposes his *māl* will make him eternal. The verb *ʿaddada* (counting) is the human accountancy of wealth; the divine accountancy is what the verse opposes to it.

Humans are tenants. The landlord audits. Every act of *infāq* or *kanz*, every measure given or shaved [11:85, 83:1-3], every orphan's *māl* consumed or preserved [4:2, 4:10], every contract honored or breached [2:282] enters the ledger. The eschatological frame is the **enforcement mechanism for stewardship** — there is no earthly authority empowered to audit ownership claims at this depth; the audit happens elsewhere.

## 9. Naming the position: stewardship, not property or commons

The Quran's negative space is sharp:

- **Not absolute private property.** The Madyan rebuttal "*fī amwālinā mā nashāʾ*" is precisely the doctrine the Quran rejects [11:87]. The orphan must receive his *māl* intact whether or not the guardian "wishes" [4:2]; ribā is forbidden whether or not the contracting parties consent [2:275]; the zakat is owed as *ḥaqq al-sāʾil wa-l-maḥrūm* — the petitioner's **right**, not the wealthy's discretionary gift [51:19, 70:24-25]. Property is bounded by obligations that cut across owner consent.

- **Not common ownership.** The inheritance fractions of [4:11-12] presuppose distinct ownership transferring between distinct heirs. The ribā verses presuppose a *raʾsu māl* (principal) belonging to a specific lender [2:279]. Solomon's wealth is *his*, the queen's wealth is *hers* [27:16, 27:23]. The contract verse [2:282] presupposes private debtors and creditors. There is no socialist collectivization in the Quranic ethic.

- **Stewardship (al-istikhlāf).** The position is **private handling under divine title, bounded by social obligations built into the asset's description**. The English term "stewardship" approximates it; the Quranic vocabulary names humans as *khulafāʾ* — successors, deputies — on the earth [2:30, 6:165, 35:39]. [57:7] makes the economic application explicit: *anfiqū mimmā jaʿalakum mustakhlafīna fīhi* — "spend out of that in which He has made you successors." *Mustakhlafīna* — successors, custodians, those handling on behalf of an absent principal. The verse fuses the doctrine into a single command: spend (the social act) from that in which you are deputies (the metaphysical status).

The Quranic position is fiduciary tenancy. The metaphysical owner is Allah. The legal-social handler is the human. The handling is bound by obligations the handler did not author (zakat, inheritance fractions, fair measure, ribā prohibition, orphan protection, *dūla* principle). The audit is eschatological. The vocabulary — *māl* vs *rizq*, *kanz* vs *khazāʾin*, *faḍl* vs *kasb*, *mustakhlaf* — is the apparatus of the doctrine. The narratives — Qarun's destruction for self-attribution, Solomon's preservation through gratitude, Madyan's annihilation for asserting *mā nashāʾu fī amwālinā* — are the doctrine in story form.

The single sentence that captures it: **everything is Allah's, you handle some of it, you will be asked.**
