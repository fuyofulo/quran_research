# Research: Zakāt vs. Ṣadaqa vs. Infāq — the Give-Vocabulary, the Eight Categories, and the Purification Logic

## Headline finding (100 words)

The Quran operates four distinct give-verbs, not one. **Zakāt** (32 occ.) is the institutionalized purification-alms, locked to ṣalāh in 50% of its ayahs; **ṣadaqa** (~22 occ.) is the broader truth-charity from which zakāt is carved as a formal subset; **infāq** (68 occ.) is the general spending verb that reveals orientation; **qarḍ ḥasan** (6 identical occ.) is the loan-to-Allah formula that uniquely names God as debtor. The 9:60 verse maps eight recipient categories — a full welfare/state-finance schema. Across all four, the rate is absent. The structural design is moral-architectural, not quantitative: who, why, and how — never how much.

## 1. The Four Give-Verbs at a Glance

| Term | Root | Finance occ. | Formula? | Recipient | Frame |
|------|------|--------------|----------|-----------|-------|
| zakāt | zkw | 32 (noun zakawʿp) | Yes — "establish ṣalāh and give zakāt" (28/56 ayahs) | Eight categories (9:60) | Institution / purification |
| ṣadaqa(t) | Sdq | ~22 (Sadaqat 8 + taSad~aqa 6 + Sadaqap 5 + mutaSad~iq 3) | No | Variable — sometimes = zakāt (9:60), sometimes voluntary (2:280) | Truth-charity (cf. ṣidq) |
| infāq | nfq | 68 (verb anfaqa) | No — but addressed via the formulas "in good times and hard" (3:134), "from what you love" (3:92), "secretly and openly" (2:274) | "fī sabīli llāh," family, the eight, jihad budgets | Spending as orientation-revealer |
| qarḍ ḥasan | qrD | 6 (2:245, 5:12, 57:11, 57:18, 64:17, 73:20) | Yes — locked: verb aqraḍa + noun qarḍan + adj ḥasanan + multiplication promise | Allah | Loan / claim / divine debt |

Two patterns are immediately decisive. First, **zakāt and qarḍ ḥasan are the two formulaic verbs**; ṣadaqa and infāq are the two open-field verbs. Second, **the four span a frequency gradient from rare-and-rigid (qarḍ ḥasan, 6) through formal (zakāt, 32) to broad-and-flexible (ṣadaqa ~22, infāq 68)**. The Quran nests them: every qarḍ ḥasan is an infāq; every zakāt is a ṣadaqa; every ṣadaqa is an infāq. Containment, not synonymy.

## 2. The Ṣalāh–Zakāt Formula

Data-grounded: of the 56 ayahs containing zkw, 28 (50.0%) also contain Slw. The construction "aqīmū al-ṣalāh wa-ātū al-zakāh" appears at [2:43], [2:83], [2:110], [2:177], [2:277], [5:12], [5:55], [9:5], [9:11], [9:18], [9:71], [22:41], [24:56], [98:5], and more. The pairing is Meccan and Medinan ([19:31], [19:55], [21:73], [23:4], [41:7] on the early side; the bulk Medinan).

Interpretive: the formula asserts a structural equivalence between **vertical worship and horizontal redistribution**. The two together form one indivisible act of religion. The negative case at [9:67] is the proof: the munāfiqūn are diagnosed by "they close their hands" (yaqbiḍūna aydiyahum) — withholding stands as the opposite of ṣalāh-coupled-zakāt. No other give-verb (ṣadaqa, infāq, qarḍ ḥasan) is paired with ṣalāh at anything close to this rate. The formula is what makes zakāt *the institution*: it is the only give-verb that the Quran ritually couples to prayer.

## 3. The Zakāt/Ṣadaqa Relation — Same Act Under Different Lenses

The terminological flexibility is the key finding. **9:60 calls the eight-category obligation ṣadaqāt**, not zakāt:

> ۞إِنَّمَا ٱلصَّدَقَٰتُ لِلۡفُقَرَآءِ وَٱلۡمَسَٰكِينِ وَٱلۡعَٰمِلِينَ عَلَيۡهَا … فَرِيضَةࣰ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ
> Saheeh: "Zakah expenditures are only for the poor and for the needy and for those employed to collect [zakah]…"
> Khattab: "Indeed, charities are only for the poor and the needy…"

Note the translator divide: Saheeh writes "Zakah expenditures" — interpolating the institutional name; Khattab writes "charities" — preserving the surface ṣadaqāt. The Arabic word is ṣadaqāt. But **9:60 then closes with farīḍatan min Allāh** — "an obligation from Allah" — coding it as obligatory. So a ṣadaqa is here farīḍa (obligatory) and recipient-bounded.

Yet [2:280] uses the same root in a voluntary frame: "if you give it as ṣadaqa (an taṣaddaqū) it is better for you" — about choosing to forgive a distressed debtor. The decision is praised, not commanded.

**Resolution:** ṣadaqa is the *generic* category for ethical giving. Zakāt is the *institutionalized subset* — the ṣadaqa that has become recurring, recipient-fixed, and worship-coupled. Every zakāt is a ṣadaqa; not every ṣadaqa is zakāt. The Quran refuses to lock the distinction lexically the way later jurisprudence does — because the moral act is one, even if the legal classification splits.

Confirming structural fact: the noun zakawʿp is always substantive (32N, 0V) — it *names an institution*. Ṣadaqa lemmas split substantive (Sadaqat, Sadaqap = 13 occ.) and verbal (taSad~aqa = 6 occ.) — the verb form ("to give as ṣadaqa") is alive, the verb form of zakāt-the-institution is not. Institutions get noun-frozen; acts stay verb-living.

## 4. The 9:60 Eight Categories — A Full Welfare Schema

The verse names eight recipients. Unpacked:

1. **al-fuqarāʾ** (the poor) — root fqr (14 occ.). The destitute by absolute lack.
2. **al-masākīn** (the needy) — root skn, miskīn lemma (23 occ.). Distinct from fuqarāʾ in the Quran's lexicon (both are listed separately at [9:60], [2:177], [8:41], [59:7]). Classical fiqh disputed the exact distinction (poorer vs. less poor; visibly poor vs. silently poor); the Quranic data is content that they are *two* categories.
3. **al-ʿāmilīn ʿalayhā** (those who work upon it) — the collectors and administrators. **A budget line for the collection apparatus itself.** This is structurally remarkable: the institution self-funds its own administration; charity is not pure donation but an operating system with payroll.
4. **al-muʾallafa qulūbuhum** (those whose hearts are to be reconciled) — a social-political category. Funds for bringing the wavering, the recently-converted, the strategically-aligned into harmony with the community. This is *soft-power* expenditure.
5. **fī al-riqāb** (in [freeing] necks) — manumission. The category is prepositional ("in" the necks), not personal — meaning the funds go *toward* an action (liberation), not *to* the slaves themselves. Structurally distinct from poverty relief.
6. **al-ghārimīn** (the debtors) — debt relief. Direct overlap with [2:280] (forgive the distressed debtor) — the zakat fund subsidizes what individual creditors might also voluntarily do.
7. **fī sabīli llāh** (in the way of Allah) — the open category. Public-good, defensive, communal infrastructure spending.
8. **ibn al-sabīl** (son of the road / wayfarer) — the traveler stranded away from his resources. Liquidity in a non-monetized world: a traveler may be wealthy at home but destitute en route.

**The breadth is the finding.** Three of the eight (#1, #2, #8) are pure poverty relief. One (#3) funds administration. One (#4) funds social cohesion. One (#5) funds emancipation. One (#6) funds debt relief. One (#7) is open-ended public good. This is a **comprehensive welfare/state-finance schema**, not a poor-tax. The Quran's zakat verse pre-figures a fiscal architecture — collection costs, soft power, manumission, debt restructuring, infrastructure, and traveler-emergency — all in one verse.

Compare 8:41 (khums) and 59:7 (fayʾ): five recipients, partly overlapping (orphans, masākīn, ibn al-sabīl), but adding Allah/Messenger/dhū al-qurbā (Prophet's kin) and *omitting* the muʾallafa, ʿāmilīn, riqāb, ghārimīn, and fī sabīli llāh. The two systems have different logics: khums/fayʾ flows from state revenue (windfall) to a clan-anchored welfare list; zakāt flows from private wealth to a broader administrative/social list. They are **parallel fiscal channels**, not duplicates.

## 5. The Purification Logic — Zakāt as Cleansing of the Giver

The root z-k-w means simultaneously "to grow" and "to purify." The institution name *is* this double meaning. The decisive verse is [9:103]:

> خُذۡ مِنۡ أَمۡوَٰلِهِمۡ صَدَقَةࣰ تُطَهِّرُهُمۡ وَتُزَكِّيهِم بِهَا
> Saheeh: "Take, [O Muhammad], from their wealth a charity by which you purify them and cause them increase…"
> Pickthall: "Take alms of their wealth, wherewith thou mayst purify them and mayst make them grow…"

Two verbs in apposition: **tuṭahhiruhum** (you cleanse them — from the ṭ-h-r purity root) and **tuzakkīhim** (you purify-and-grow them — from zkw itself). The pronominal object is **them** (the givers), not the recipients. The transaction does its theological work on the *giver*. The poor receive a payment; the rich receive cleansing.

This is the inversion that defines the institution. Alms in a purely social frame benefit the receiver. Zakāt in the Quranic frame *also* benefits the giver — not as reward but as purification of what is *retained*. The act of giving cleanses the kept. Hoarded wealth, by contrast, is described at [9:34–35] as branded fuel on the Day of Judgment: unsubtracted wealth physically deforms the soul. Subtraction is sanctification.

This explains why the rate (2.5% in hadith) is small in financial terms but enormous in spiritual mechanics: the function is not redistribution-maximization but **routine ritual extraction** — the periodic act that prevents the binding of self to māl.

## 6. The Ṣadaqa-as-Truth Resonance

Sdq is a 155-occurrence root whose primary semantic load is **truth/truthfulness**: SaAdiq (truthful person, 59 occ.), muSad~iq (one who confirms, 19 occ.), Sidoq (truthfulness noun, 14 occ.). The charity senses (~22 occ.) are a minority cluster *within a root primarily about veracity*.

The shared root is not etymological accident. **To give ṣadaqa is to perform ṣidq.** Charity *authenticates* faith claim. The Quran encodes this directly at [2:177] — the long birr verse — where giving wealth to the eight-fold list is named as constitutive of righteousness, and the verse closes: "those are the ones who have been truthful (*ṣadaqū*), and those are the muttaqūn." Giving and truth-telling are predicated of the same subject in the same sentence.

This is why the Quran can use *ṣadaqa* for what is jurisprudentially zakat (9:60) and for purely voluntary giving (2:280) without contradiction: in both cases the act is **the materialization of inner truth**. A claim to faith without ṣadaqa is by definition not yet ṣidq. The munāfiq's expenditure (9:54, nafaqātihim) is rejected precisely because it is not ṣadaqa — it is infāq without ṣidq.

## 7. The Infāq Spectrum — Spending as Orientation-Revealer

Infāq (anfaqa, 68 occ.) is the broadest spend-verb. It covers:

- zakāt and ṣadaqa themselves (anfaqa is the umbrella; the others are subtypes)
- family maintenance and dowry ([4:34], [65:6–7])
- fī sabīli llāh / war-funding ([2:195], [9:121])
- "in ease and hardship" ([3:134] fī al-sarrāʾi wal-ḍarrāʾi)
- secret and public giving ([2:274] sirran wa-ʿalāniya)
- "from what you love" ([3:92] mimmā tuḥibbūn)

What does infāq add beyond zakāt and ṣadaqa? **Generality and continuity.** Zakāt is periodic; ṣadaqa is occasional; infāq is *continuous orientation*. The hypocrite passages turn on this: [9:54] does not condemn the munāfiqūn for failing to give zakāt — it condemns them for the *manner* of their infāq (kārahūn, unwilling). The believer is defined not by a single payment but by an ongoing economic posture.

The polysemy of the nfq root underlines this. From the same trilateral come anfaqa (spend, 68 occ.) and munāfiqūn (hypocrites, 37 occ.). Spending and hypocrisy share a root. The semantic logic: **infāq reveals what zakāt may hide** — formal payment can be coerced; continuous spending exposes whether the orientation is real. The munāfiq pays zakāt and remains munāfiq; the believer's infāq is the diagnostic.

## 8. The Qarḍ Ḥasan Distinction — Allah as Debtor

Six identical-structure occurrences (2:245, 5:12, 57:11, 57:18, 64:17, 73:20). All use verb aqraḍa + noun qarḍan + adjective ḥasanan + multiplication promise.

> مَّن ذَا ٱلَّذِي يُقۡرِضُ ٱللَّهَ قَرۡضًا حَسَنࣰ ا فَيُضَٰعِفَهُۥ لَهُۥٓ أَضۡعَافࣰ ا كَثِيرَةࣰ (2:245)
> "Who is it that would loan Allah a goodly loan, so He may multiply it for him many times over?"

Why **loan**, not **charity**? Because the loan frame creates a **claim**. Charity does not. The lender has a receivable; the donor does not. By calling charitable giving qarḍ ḥasan, the Quran:

1. **Names Allah as debtor.** Theologically remarkable. The Owner of All takes on, by self-naming, the structural position of one who owes.
2. **Asserts contractual obligation on the divine side.** The promise of multiplication is the repayment schedule. The believer is positioned as **creditor of God** — a stance no other give-verb permits.
3. **Distinguishes from ṣadaqa structurally.** [57:18] places the two side-by-side: "the muṣṣaddiqīn and muṣṣaddiqāt *wa-aqraḍū llāha qarḍan ḥasanan*" — those who give ṣadaqa AND lend Allah a goodly loan. The conjunction wa separates them as distinct acts within the same verse.

The act itself (handing wealth to the poor or to the cause) may be identical to ṣadaqa. The **frame** differs: ṣadaqa frames the act as gift-and-purification; qarḍ ḥasan frames the same act as **divine-secured investment** with explicit return. This is the Quran's single sharpest move against the *psychology* of loss in giving: you have not given, you have lent, and the borrower is the One who multiplies many times over.

## 9. The Hubb al-Māl Correction — Giving Breaks Attachment

The Quran nowhere commands renunciation. It commands **redirection**:

> لَن تَنَالُواْ ٱلۡبِرَّ حَتَّىٰ تُنفِقُواْ مِمَّا تُحِبُّونَ (3:92)
> "You will not attain piety (birr) until you spend from what you love."

The verb is *tunfiqū* (infāq), and the object is *mimmā tuḥibbūn* — what you love. The construction is positive about love (the love itself is permitted) and constructive about its remedy: spend from it. [76:8] mirrors this — "they feed food, in spite of their love for it (ʿalā ḥubbihī)."

The psychological mechanism: **the act of giving severs the binding to wealth**. Zakāt is the periodic severance; ṣadaqa the occasional; infāq the continuous. All three operate on the *attachment*, not the wealth-amount. This is why the institution can function without a rate: the moral target is the binding, not a percentage. A rich man's 2.5% may break no attachment; a poor man's date may break a profound one. The Quran's silence on quantity is conceptually consistent with its theory of giving as attachment-rupture.

## 10. The 2:276 Opposition — Riba Erased, Ṣadaqāt Grown

> يَمۡحَقُ ٱللَّهُ ٱلرِّبَوٰاْ وَيُرۡبِي ٱلصَّدَقَٰتِ (2:276)
> Arberry: "God blots out usury, but freewill offerings He augments with interest."

The verb **yurbī** (grow, multiply) and the noun **al-ribā** share the root r-b-w. The verse weaponizes the root against itself: what humans call "increase" (ribā) Allah erases (yamḥaqu); what humans call "loss" (ṣadaqāt) Allah grows (yurbī). Arberry's rendering preserves the etymological mirror by translating yurbī as "augments with interest" — Allah is the only legitimate ribā-er, and what He charges interest on is charity.

The accounting is inverted. Human-perceived increase = divine loss; human-perceived loss = divine increase. This is the macroeconomic theology underlying every give-verb in the cluster. Zakāt, ṣadaqa, infāq, qarḍ ḥasan all participate in the same hidden ledger: subtraction at the human scale = addition at the divine scale. Riba is the only economic act that runs the ledger backward — visible gain, invisible loss. The give-verbs are inversions of riba not by accident but by structural design.

## 11. The Missing Rate — Moral Architecture Without Quantification

The Quran specifies for zakāt:

- **Who gives** — anyone with means (no threshold-of-wealth verse)
- **Who receives** — the eight categories of [9:60]
- **Why** — purification and growth ([9:103])
- **With what** — ṣalāh, as the coupled obligation
- **In what spirit** — willingly, from what is loved, openly or in secret

The Quran does not specify:

- **Rate** — 2.5% is hadith-derived
- **Niṣāb** (threshold) — hadith-derived
- **Schedule** — hadith-derived (annual ḥawl)
- **Collection mechanism** — left to communal practice
- **Enforcement** — only [9:5] (in a war-context) couples rejection of zakat with belligerency; no civil penalty in the text

This is structurally consistent with the Quran's pattern across financial law: just measure without specified units ([83:1–3]); ribā prohibited without defined threshold; inheritance fractions specified but not values; debt contracts mandated but not standardized forms ([2:282]).

The Quran is **moral architecture, not legal code**. Categories, principles, recipients, and orientations are fixed; quantities are not. This enables the institution to scale across economies (agrarian, commercial, industrial, digital) without textual obsolescence. A specified rate would have **frozen** zakāt to one economic context; the absence keeps it portable.

The negative space is the design feature. The Quran's silence on rate is consistent with its silence on the mechanism of giving's spiritual effect — both belong to the order of moral action, which is principle-specified and instance-derived. The text gives the architecture; the community supplies the engineering.

## 12. Synthesis Summary

The four give-verbs together form a single moral economy with four lenses:

- **Zakāt** — the *institutionalized lens*: periodic, formula-coupled, recipient-specified, self-cleansing.
- **Ṣadaqa** — the *truth lens*: any act of giving that authenticates faith claim; broader than zakāt, narrower than infāq.
- **Infāq** — the *orientation lens*: continuous spending posture that diagnoses sincerity; the most general verb, polysemous with hypocrisy.
- **Qarḍ ḥasan** — the *contract lens*: the same act reframed as divine-secured loan, with Allah as debtor.

The Quran refuses to collapse them into one word because they encode four different relations: institution (zakāt), authenticity (ṣadaqa), continuity (infāq), and divine-contract (qarḍ ḥasan). Every concrete act of giving can be read under any of the four. The 9:60 eight categories specify *where* the wealth flows; the purification logic specifies *why* it is taken; the hubb al-māl correction specifies *what it does to the giver*; the 2:276 inversion specifies *how it appears in the divine ledger*. The missing rate is not an omission — it is the structural signature of a system designed to be moral architecture, not fiscal code.
