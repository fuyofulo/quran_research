# Finance High-Divergence Verses

## Headline Finding

**Finding:** 1,277 finance verses achieve divergence ≥0.5, indicating contestation in English rendering. The top scorers (>0.78) concentrate in three domains: (1) **Inheritance language** (*ytm*, *wSy*, *mwl*): orphans and bequests resist English legal equivalence; (2) **Testimony & witness** (*$hd*, *ktb*): writing and attestation embed procedural assumptions; (3) **Measurement metaphors** (*wzn*, *qsT*, *jzy*): justice and recompense diverge between literal and figurative translation. Riba verses (*rbw*) show moderate divergence—translators converge on "riba," but the mechanism remains contested. Commerce roots (*byE*, *tjr*) show lower divergence, suggesting clearer English mapping.

## Key Observations

**Inheritance language contests.** Verses like 2:180, 4:11, 4:33 cluster around *wrv* (inheritance), *wSy* (bequest), *ytm* (orphans). English has no stable equivalents for classical Islamic inheritance structures; translators oscillate between legal, familial, and ethical framings.

**Riba remains theoretically contested.** High-divergence riba verses (e.g., 2:276, 3:130) show translators choosing between "usury," "interest," "increase," and "illicit gain"—each carries different economic theology.

**Measurement as justice.** Verses 55:9 (qsT + wzn), 55:60 (jzy), 72:14 (qsT) treat measurement as divine justice. Divergence arises from whether to literalize (scales, weights) or abstract (balance, fairness).
