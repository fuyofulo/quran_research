# Narrative: Solomon (Sulaymān) — Legitimate Wealth and the Grateful King

## Headline Finding (100 words)

Solomon is the Quran's structural answer to Qarun: the prophet-king who holds more wealth than any human before or after, and who survives it. The decisive contrast is at the level of attribution and prayer. Where Qarun declared "I earned this by my own knowledge," Solomon's first words upon receiving his kingdom are "praise belongs to God" [27:15], and his first prayer upon a display of power is "inspire me to be grateful" [27:19]. Even his one moment of wealth-attachment ends in repentance and prayer [38:32-35]. Wealth is not renounced; it is reoriented. Gratitude is the structural solution to the Qarun problem.

---

## I. The Wealth Catalogue — What Solomon Owns

The Quran grants Solomon a uniquely encyclopedic endowment. It is useful to catalogue it precisely before analyzing its meaning:

**Inheritance and knowledge [27:15-16]**
Solomon "inherited" (wariṯa) David [27:16]. This is the first word: wealth as succession, not self-made. The language of inheritance immediately removes self-attribution. He announces: "We have been given from every thing (kull shayʾ)" — the same phrase used of Sheba's queen [27:23] — and names this "the manifest bounty (al-faḍl al-mubīn)." Bounty: gift, not wage.

**The wind [34:12, 38:36, 21:81]**
The wind is "subjected" (sakhkharnā) to Solomon: its morning journey covers a month, its evening course another month. The passive causative — God does the subjecting — keeps agency with the divine. Solomon does not "master" the wind; it is placed under him.

**The spring of molten copper [34:12]**
"We caused to flow for him the spring of copper (ʿayn al-qiṭr)" — liquid copper as a productive resource flowing by divine command. The most literal "mineral wealth" image in the Solomonic corpus.

**The jinn workforce [34:12-13, 38:37-38, 21:82, 27:17]**
Jinn "work before him by the permission of his Lord." They are builders, divers, craftsmen. They construct: maḥārīb (lofty chambers / sanctuaries), tamāṯīl (statues), jifān (great basins "like wells"), and qudūr rāsiyāt (cauldrons fixed immovably in the ground) [34:13]. The construction vocabulary is monumental — this is infrastructure, not decoration. Crucially: they work "by his Lord's permission," and deviants face "punishment of the Blaze" [34:12]. Divine oversight is built into the labor arrangement.

**The horses [38:31]**
"Thoroughbred steeds" (al-ṣāfināt al-jiyād) are paraded before him at evening. This will become the pivot of the repentance episode.

**The throne of Sheba [27:38-40]**
Solomon requests the Queen of Sheba's throne be brought before her delegation arrives. A being "with knowledge of the Book" produces it "before the glance returns" — teleportation-speed. Solomon's response to witnessing this: "This is from the favor (faḍl) of my Lord, to test me whether I am grateful or ungrateful" [27:40].

**The kingdom no one after will have [38:35]**
Solomon explicitly requests — through prayer — "a kingdom (mulk) such as will not belong to anyone after me." This is not boasting; it is petition. And God grants it [38:36-39].

## II. The Grammar of Gratitude — 27:15-19

The passage opens with David and Solomon both declaring "al-ḥamdu lillāh" — praise to God — upon being given knowledge [27:15]. This is not a casual phrase. It is the structural opening of al-Fātiḥa, the Quran's master-frame. To begin one's receipt of wealth with this phrase is to locate it correctly: gift from above, not product from below.

**27:19 — the gratitude prayer triggered by a small thing**

---

**Arabic:** فَتَبَسَّمَ ضَاحِكًا مِّن قَوۡلِهَا وَقَالَ رَبِّ أَوۡزِعۡنِيٓ أَنۡ أَشۡكُرَ نِعۡمَتَكَ ٱلَّتِيٓ أَنۡعَمۡتَ عَلَيَّ وَعَلَىٰ وَٰلِدَيَّ وَأَنۡ أَعۡمَلَ صَٰلِحًا تَرۡضَىٰهُ وَأَدۡخِلۡنِي بِرَحۡمَتِكَ فِي عِبَادِكَ ٱلصَّٰلِحِينَ

| Translator | Rendering |
|---|---|
| Saheeh | So [Solomon] smiled, amused at her speech, and said, "My Lord, enable me to be grateful for Your favor which You have bestowed upon me and upon my parents and to do righteousness of which You approve. And admit me by Your mercy into [the ranks of] Your righteous servants." |
| Pickthall | And (Solomon) smiled, laughing at her speech, and said: My Lord, arouse me to be thankful for Thy favour wherewith Thou hast favoured me and my parents, and to do good that shall be pleasing unto Thee, and include me in (the number of) Thy righteous slaves. |
| Khattab | So Solomon smiled in amusement at her words, and prayed, "My Lord! Inspire me to ˹always˺ be thankful for Your favours which You have blessed me and my parents with, and to do good deeds that please you. Admit me, by Your mercy, into ˹the company of˺ Your righteous servants." |
| Arberry | But he smiled, laughing at its words, and he said, 'My Lord, dispose me that I may be thankful for Thy blessing wherewith Thou hast blessed me and my father and mother, and that I may do righteousness well-pleasing to Thee; and do Thou admit me, by Thy mercy, amongst Thy righteous servants.' |

---

What triggers this prayer? An ant warning its colony about Solomon's army [27:18]. Solomon's forces could inadvertently crush a colony. Upon hearing this, he does not assert dominance or ignore it. He smiles — human, not grandiose — and immediately turns to prayer. The prayer's key verb: awziʿnī, from wazaʿa, meaning "inspire me" or "dispose me to." He does not claim gratitude; he asks to be made capable of it. The orientation is supplicatory even at the height of power.

Compare Qarun: when told "do not exult (lā tafraḥ)," he responds by increasing display [28:79]. Solomon, untold, generates a gratitude prayer spontaneously.

## III. The Sheba Encounter — Wealth as Neutral Medium

The Queen of Sheba "has been given of every thing (min kull shayʾ)" [27:23] — the same phrase used of Solomon [27:16]. The Quran uses identical language for two rulers on opposite sides of a theological divide. Wealth does not mark her as cursed; her error is sun-worship, not possession.

Solomon's response when her delegation brings gifts is striking:

---

**Arabic:** فَلَمَّا جَآءَ سُلَيۡمَٰنَ قَالَ أَتُمِدُّونَنِ بِمَالٍ فَمَآ ءَاتَىٰنِۦَ ٱللَّهُ خَيۡرٌ مِّمَّآ ءَاتَىٰكُمۚ بَلۡ أَنتُم بِهَدِيَّتِكُمۡ تَفۡرَحُونَ

| Translator | Rendering |
|---|---|
| Saheeh | "Do you provide me with wealth? But what Allah has given me is better than what He has given you. Rather, it is you who rejoice in your gift." |
| Pickthall | "What! Would ye help me with wealth? But that which Allah hath given me is better than that which He hath given you. Nay it is ye (and not I) who exult in your gift." |
| Khattab | "Do you offer me wealth? What Allah has granted me is far greater than what He has granted you. No! It is you who rejoice in ˹receiving˺ gifts." |
| Arberry | "What, would you succour me with wealth, and what God gave me is better than what He has given you? Nay, but instead you rejoice in your gift!" |

---

The phrase "what Allah has given me" (mā ātāniya llāh) is the formula of correct attribution. Solomon's wealth superiority is named, but named as God's gift — not Solomon's achievement. The contrast with "you who rejoice in your gift (bi-hadiyyatikum tafraḥūn)" echoes precisely Qarun's fate: the crowd in 28:79 said "Indeed, Qarun has a great fortune (laḥẓun ʿaẓīm)" and the crowd "rejoiced" — the same root f-r-ḥ.

## IV. The Horses Episode — Repentance as Correction (38:31-35)

This is the passage that keeps Solomon human. It is load-bearing for the economic theology.

**38:32 — the admission**

---

**Arabic:** فَقَالَ إِنِّيٓ أَحۡبَبۡتُ حُبَّ ٱلۡخَيۡرِ عَن ذِكۡرِ رَبِّي حَتَّىٰ تَوَارَتۡ بِٱلۡحِجَابِ

| Translator | Rendering |
|---|---|
| Saheeh | "Indeed, I gave preference to the love of good [things] over the remembrance of my Lord until the sun disappeared into the curtain." |
| Pickthall | "Lo! I have preferred the good things (of the world) to the remembrance of my Lord; till they were taken out of sight behind the curtain." |
| Khattab | "I am truly in love with ˹these˺ fine things out of remembrance for Allah" — until they went out of sight. |
| Arberry | "Lo, I have loved the love of good things better than the remembrance of my Lord, until the sun was bidden behind the veil." |

---

The phrasing is dense. "Ḥubb al-khayr" — love of good things / love of the good — is ambiguous: khayr means both "good" in the moral sense and "wealth/goods" in the material sense. The classical exegetes split: some read "the horses which are good (khayr)"; others read "love of worldly good." Both readings are textually valid. The Quran does not resolve the ambiguity, which suggests the statement is meant to be general: attachment to things good in themselves, at the cost of dhikr.

**38:33-34 — the action and the test**

The horses are returned (rudūhā ʿalayya). He "began to stroke (masḥ) their shanks and necks." Here the major translation dispute: Pickthall (and earlier commentators) renders this "slashing with his sword," implying Solomon destroyed the horses as an act of relinquishment. Saheeh and Arberry read the gesture as affectionate stroking — inspection, not sacrifice. The Arabic verb masaḥa most literally means to pass the hand over, not to cut. The interpretation of slaughter requires importing the sword from elsewhere. The stroking reading is more lexically defensible — but the ambiguity is exegetically productive.

Then 38:34: "We tested (fatannā) Sulaymān, and cast upon his throne a body (jasad), then he returned (anāba)." The "body on the throne" is one of the Quran's most debated images — a lifeless substitute? an illness? — but the verse's function is clear: Solomon is tested, and he returns (anāba is the verb for penitent return). The test is defined, the return is confirmed.

**38:35 — prayer, not boast**

---

**Arabic:** قَالَ رَبِّ ٱغۡفِرۡ لِي وَهَبۡ لِي مُلۡكًا لَّا يَنۢبَغِي لِأَحَدٍ مِّنۢ بَعۡدِيٓۖ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلۡوَهَّابُ

| Translator | Rendering |
|---|---|
| Saheeh | "My Lord, forgive me and grant me a kingdom such as will not belong to anyone after me. Indeed, You are the Bestower." |
| Pickthall | "My Lord! Forgive me and bestow on me sovereignty such as shall not belong to any after me. Lo! Thou art the Bestower." |
| Khattab | "My Lord! Forgive me, and grant me an authority that will never be matched by anyone after me. You are indeed the Giver ˹of all bounties˺." |
| Arberry | "My Lord, forgive me, and give me a kingdom such as may not befall anyone after me; surely Thou art the All-giver." |

---

Note the structure: FORGIVENESS first, KINGDOM second. This is not ambition — it is the grammar of repentance followed by petition. The kingdom is explicitly named as God's gift (wahb, from wahhāb — the Bestower, a Divine Name). Solomon does not claim the kingdom; he asks for it, naming the One who grants. The contrast with Qarun's "I was given this by my knowledge (ʿilm)" [28:78] is precise: Solomon names God as source even in the prayer for more.

## V. The Gratitude Command — 34:13

---

**Arabic:** يَعۡمَلُونَ لَهُۥ مَا يَشَآءُ مِن مَّحَٰرِيبَ وَتَمَٰثِيلَ وَجِفَانٍ كَٱلۡجَوَابِ وَقُدُورٍ رَّاسِيَٰتٍۚ ٱعۡمَلُوٓاْ ءَالَ دَاوُۥدَ شُكۡرًاۚ وَقَلِيلٌ مِّنۡ عِبَادِيَ ٱلشَّكُورُ

| Translator | Rendering |
|---|---|
| Saheeh | They made for him what he willed of elevated chambers, statues, bowls like reservoirs, and stationary kettles. [We said], "Work, O family of David, in gratitude." And few of My servants are grateful. |
| Pickthall | They made for him what he willed: synagogues and statues, basins like wells and boilers built into the ground. Give thanks, O House of David! Few of My bondmen are thankful. |
| Khattab | They made for him whatever he desired of sanctuaries, statues, basins as large as reservoirs, and cooking pots fixed ˹into the ground˺. ˹We ordered:˺ "Work gratefully, O family of David!" ˹Only˺ a few of My servants are ˹truly˺ grateful. |
| Arberry | fashioning for him whatsoever he would — places of worship, statues, porringers like water-troughs, and anchored cooking-pots. 'Labour, O House of David, in thankfulness; for few indeed are those that are thankful among My servants.' |

---

The command is ʿamalū shukran — "work in thankfulness." Shukr here is not verbal praise; it is enacted through labor. The formula is: wealth received → labor performed → gratitude as the mode of that labor. Renunciation is structurally absent. The command is to WORK, not to give away or abstain. And the divine editorial — "few of My servants are grateful" — marks this as exceptional achievement, not expected behavior. Solomon's household manages it; most do not.

## VI. The 2:102 Exoneration

The verse distinguishes carefully: "the satans disbelieved (kafara), they taught people magic." Solomon "did not disbelieve (mā kafara Sulaymān)." The construction is emphatic negation — wa-mā kafara — before the contrasting affirmation about the satans. Solomon is exonerated not just acquitted; the Quran inserts his defense actively into the text.

The economic-theological implication: enormous wealth and power CREATE CONDITIONS for corruption by others (the satans operating "during the kingdom of Solomon"), but do not automatically corrupt the holder. Wealth is not self-propagating moral decay. The king can remain clean while his kingdom becomes a site of others' corruption. The moral assessment is individuated.

## VII. The Precise Contrast with Qarun

| Axis | Qarun | Solomon |
|---|---|---|
| **Attribution of wealth** | "I was given this by my knowledge (ʿilm)" [28:78] | "This is from the favor (faḍl) of my Lord" [27:40] |
| **Response to receipt** | Exultation, parade | "Praise to God" [27:15], gratitude prayer [27:19] |
| **Use of wealth** | Spectacle for personal status | Service: army, throne, infrastructure |
| **Moment of attachment** | None recorded; destruction follows directly | Horses episode; repentance follows immediately [38:34-35] |
| **Prayer** | None; only self-declaration | Explicit prayer for forgiveness THEN kingdom [38:35] |
| **End state** | Earth swallows him [28:81] | "He has nearness to Us and a beautiful return" [38:40] |
| **Key noun** | Kanz (treasure hoard) | Faḍl (bounty/grace) |

The contrast is not between rich and poor. It is between two wealthy men. The differentiating variable is the heart-state: to whom the wealth is attributed, and in what direction the self moves when confronted with wealth.

## VIII. The Implicit Economic Theology

Solomon and Qarun together constitute the Quran's fullest answer to the question: what is wealth, morally speaking?

**Wealth is neutral.** The Quran does not resolve the question by condemning material abundance. Solomon has more than Qarun — wind, jinn, copper springs, the greatest kingdom ever — and is praised. The identical phrase "given from every thing" describes both Solomon and Sheba's queen in the same passage.

**Orientation is determinative.** The same wealth is blessing (Solomon) or curse (Qarun) depending on: (1) whether it is attributed to God or to self; (2) whether it generates gratitude or pride; (3) whether it is used for mission or spectacle.

**Repentance is available.** Even Solomon, the model, has the horses episode. The Quran does not demand perfection — it demands correction. When wealth-attachment appears, the right move is anāba (return to God), not renunciation of the wealth itself. After the repentance, the kingdom is GIVEN BACK, expanded [38:36-39].

**The formula**: faḍl (gift) → ʿamal (work) → shukr (gratitude). This is 34:13 compressed. Wealth arrives as grace, is expressed through labor, and is oriented through gratitude. The loop is self-reinforcing: grateful work attracts more faḍl.
