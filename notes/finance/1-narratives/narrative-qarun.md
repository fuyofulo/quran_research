# Narrative: Qarun — Wealth-as-Curse (al-Qaṣaṣ 28:76-82)

## Headline Finding (100 words)

The Qarun narrative is the Quran's most concentrated economic-ethics fable. Its decisive finding: the Quran does NOT condemn wealth — it condemns three specific acts that corrupt it: (1) self-attribution ("I earned this by my knowledge"), (2) conspicuous display that generates communal envy, and (3) fasād (corruption) in the land. The counterweight is 28:77, the Quran's most explicit "middle path" statement: seek the hereafter through your wealth, do not forget your worldly share, do good as God has done good to you. Renunciation is never proposed. The sin is mis-orientation and false ownership, not possession itself.

## Passage: al-Qaṣaṣ 28:76-82 — Full Arabic and Translations

### 28:76 — Qarun's identity and the hyperbolic treasury

**Arabic:** إِنَّ قَٰرُونَ كَانَ مِن قَوۡمِ مُوسَىٰ فَبَغَىٰ عَلَيۡهِمۡۖ وَءَاتَيۡنَٰهُ مِنَ ٱلۡكُنُوزِ مَآ إِنَّ مَفَاتِحَهُۥ لَتَنُوٓأُ بِٱلۡعُصۡبَةِ أُوْلِي ٱلۡقُوَّةِ إِذۡ قَالَ لَهُۥ قَوۡمُهُۥ لَا تَفۡرَحۡۖ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يُحِبُّ ٱلۡفَرِحِينَ

| Translator | Rendering |
|---|---|
| Saheeh | Indeed, Qarun was from the people of Moses, but he tyrannized them. And We gave him of treasures whose keys would burden a band of strong men; thereupon his people said to him, "Do not exult. Indeed, Allah does not like the exultant." |
| Pickthall | Now Korah was of Moses' folk, but he oppressed them; and We gave him so much treasure that the stores thereof would verily have been a burden for a troop of mighty men. When his own folk said unto him: Exult not; lo! Allah loveth not the exultant; |
| Khattab | Indeed, Korah was from the people of Moses, but he behaved arrogantly towards them. We had granted him such treasures that even their keys would burden a group of strong men. ˹Some of˺ his people advised him, "Do not be prideful! Surely Allah does not like the prideful." |
| Arberry | Now Korah was of the people of Moses; he became insolent to them, for We had given him treasures such that the very keys of them were too heavy a burden for a company of men endowed with strength. When his people said to him, 'Do not exult; God loves not those that exult.' |

**Notes:** Qarun is *min qawmi Musa* — inside the community, not a foreign oppressor. Wealth is ātaynāhu ("We gave him") — divine gift from the first clause. KUNŪZ (plural of kanz) is the same root condemned in 9:34 for hoarding. The key-hyperbole sets the scale; the communal counsel ("do not exult") follows within the same verse — before Qarun has spoken.

### 28:77 — The Middle-Path Counsel

**Arabic:** وَٱبۡتَغِ فِيمَآ ءَاتَىٰكَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلدَّارَ ٱلۡأٓخِرَةَۖ وَلَا تَنسَ نَصِيبَكَ مِنَ ٱلدُّنۡيَاۖ وَأَحۡسِن كَمَآ أَحۡسَنَ ٱللَّهُ إِلَيۡكَۖ وَلَا تَبۡغِ ٱلۡفَسَادَ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِۖ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يُحِبُّ ٱلۡمُفۡسِدِينَ

| Translator | Rendering |
|---|---|
| Saheeh | But seek, through that which Allah has given you, the home of the Hereafter; and [yet], do not forget your share of the world. And do good as Allah has done good to you. And desire not corruption in the land. Indeed, Allah does not like corrupters. |
| Pickthall | But seek the abode of the Hereafter in that which Allah hath given thee and neglect not thy portion of the world, and be thou kind even as Allah hath been kind to thee, and seek not corruption in the earth; lo! Allah loveth not corrupters. |
| Khattab | Rather, seek the ˹reward˺ of the Hereafter by means of what Allah has granted you, without forgetting your share of this world. And be good ˹to others˺ as Allah has been good to you. Do not seek to spread corruption in the land, for Allah certainly does not like the corruptors. |
| Arberry | but seek, amidst that which God has given thee, the Last Abode, and forget not thy portion of the present world; and do good, as God has been good to thee. And seek not to work corruption in the earth; surely God loves not the workers of corruption. |

**Notes:** The Quran's densest economic-ethics statement. Four clauses: (1) seek the hereafter *through* what God gave you — wealth as means, not end; (2) do not forget your worldly naṣīb (share/allotment — a legal-economic term) — renunciation explicitly forbidden; (3) extend iḥsān (excellence/beneficence) as God extended it to you — generosity modeled on divine generosity; (4) do not seek fasād in the land — the undoing of social-economic right order, the terminal prohibition. The counsel is communal (qawmuhu speaks — not prophet, not angel): an embedded social ethic.

### 28:78 — The Self-Attribution Claim and the Historical Rebuttal

**Arabic:** قَالَ إِنَّمَآ أُوتِيتُهُۥ عَلَىٰ عِلۡمٍ عِندِيٓۚ أَوَلَمۡ يَعۡلَمۡ أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ قَدۡ أَهۡلَكَ مِن قَبۡلِهِۦ مِنَ ٱلۡقُرُونِ مَنۡ هُوَ أَشَدُّ مِنۡهُ قُوَّةࣰ وَأَكۡثَرُ جَمۡعًاۚ وَلَا يُسۡـَٔلُ عَن ذُنُوبِهِمُ ٱلۡمُجۡرِمُونَ

| Translator | Rendering |
|---|---|
| Saheeh | He said, "I was only given it because of knowledge I have." Did he not know that Allah had destroyed before him of generations those who were greater than him in power and greater in accumulation [of wealth]? But the criminals, about their sins, will not be asked. |
| Pickthall | He said: I have been given it only on account of knowledge I possess. Knew he not that Allah had destroyed already of the generations before him men who were mightier than him in strength and greater in respect of following? The guilty are not questioned of their sins. |
| Khattab | He replied, "I have been granted all this because of some knowledge I have." Did he not know that Allah had already destroyed some from the generations before him who were far superior to him in power and greater in accumulating ˹wealth˺? There will be no need for the wicked to be asked about their sins. |
| Arberry | He said, 'What I have been given is only because of a knowledge that is in me.' What, did he not know that God had destroyed before him generations of men stronger than he in might, and more numerous in multitude? And yet the sinners shall not be questioned concerning their sins. |

**Notes:** "ʿilm ʿindī" — not "I worked hard" but wealth given *because of* (ʿalā) knowledge *he possesses*. A theological error about causation, not merely pride. Rebuttal is historical (not moral): prior generations stronger in quwwa, greater in jamʿ (accumulation — cf. 104:2-3), were destroyed. Rhetorical question ("Did he not know?") names historical blindness as the core cognitive failure. Final clause — criminals not questioned about their sins — is interpretively ambiguous; all translators preserve the tension.

### 28:79-80 — The Parade and the Divided Response

**Arabic (79):** فَخَرَجَ عَلَىٰ قَوۡمِهِۦ فِي زِينَتِهِۦۖ قَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ يُرِيدُونَ ٱلۡحَيَوٰةَ ٱلدُّنۡيَا يَٰلَيۡتَ لَنَا مِثۡلَ مَآ أُوتِيَ قَٰرُونُ إِنَّهُۥ لَذُو حَظٍّ عَظِيمٍ

**Arabic (80):** وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ أُوتُواْ ٱلۡعِلۡمَ وَيۡلَكُمۡ ثَوَابُ ٱللَّهِ خَيۡرٌ لِّمَنۡ ءَامَنَ وَعَمِلَ صَٰلِحًاۚ وَلَا يُلَقَّىٰهَآ إِلَّا ٱلصَّٰبِرُونَ

The display is a test that reveals each group's orientation.

### 28:81-82 — Destruction and Communal Learning

**Arabic (81):** فَخَسَفۡنَا بِهِۦ وَبِدَارِهِ ٱلۡأَرۡضَ فَمَا كَانَ لَهُۥ مِن فِئَةٍ يَنصُرُونَهُۥ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ وَمَا كَانَ مِنَ ٱلۡمُنتَصِرِينَ

**Arabic (82):** وَأَصۡبَحَ ٱلَّذِينَ تَمَنَّوۡاْ مَكَانَهُۥ بِٱلۡأَمۡسِ يَقُولُونَ وَيۡكَأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَبۡسُطُ ٱلرِّزۡقَ لِمَن يَشَآءُ مِنۡ عِبَادِهِۦ وَيَقۡدِرُۖ لَوۡلَآ أَن مَّنَّ ٱللَّهُ عَلَيۡنَا لَخَسَفَ بِنَاۖ وَيۡكَأَنَّهُۥ لَا يُفۡلِحُ ٱلۡكَٰفِرُونَ

Translations available above. The khasafa construction — "We caused the earth to swallow him" — is sudden, complete, and irreversible. No intercession, no defense (no fiʾa to help him, no capacity to help himself). Dar (house/estate) goes with him: wealth and its architecture disappear together. 28:82 closes with the formula waykaʾanna (an unusual particle carrying the sense of astonished recognition — "it turns out that..."). The former-enviers now articulate the lesson: God extends (yabsuṭu) and constricts (yaqdir) provision as He wills. Yesterday's envy becomes today's gratitude with the recognition that they too could have been swallowed.

## Research Question Analysis

### 1. What exactly was Qarun's sin? Three strands disentangled.

**(a) Self-attribution** [28:78]: "ʿilm ʿindī" — false causal claim, not merely pride. A theological error about the origin of provision.

**(b) Conspicuous display** [28:79]: parade in adornment triggers communal envy. The social-economic harm: wealth displayed for status generates covetousness.

**(c) Fasād in the land** [28:77]: the broadest category — undoing of social-economic right order. The narrative does not specify what Qarun's fasād was; it functions as the terminal category that encompasses the other two.

Three distinct levels: theological error (attribution), social harm (envy-spectacle), categorical prohibition (fasād). The text foregrounds the first.

### 2. The Middle Path — 28:77 unpacked

Four clauses; what they omit is decisive. The verse does NOT say "give it away," "live simply," or "wealth is dangerous." It says: (1) orient your wealth toward the hereafter, (2) retain your legitimate worldly portion (naṣīb — a legal-economic term, cognate with distribution-rights), (3) extend generosity as God extended it to you, (4) do not corrupt the social order. The frame is orientation, not renunciation. Wealth is a tool whose moral valence depends on what it is aimed at. The prohibition is delivered by the community itself — not prophet or angel — articulating an embedded social ethic.

### 3. The wealth-knowledge linkage (28:78)

"ʿilm ʿindī" — "knowledge in me" — claims wealth as personal achievement. The Quran does not specify what knowledge (commerce, alchemy, administration); it is interested in the structure, not the content. The rebuttal is historical: prior generations were stronger in quwwa and greater in jamʿ and were destroyed. The same pattern recurs in ʿĀd [41:15] ("who is mightier than us?") and Saba [34:15-17] (self-sufficiency before the flood). The Quran constructs a cross-generational law: self-attributed power is always overpowered.

### 4. The Qarun-Pharaoh-Haman Triad [29:39, 40:24]

At 29:39 and 40:24, Qarun appears as one of three figures who rejected Musa: Pharaoh (political power), Haman (bureaucratic/ministerial power), Qarun (economic power). All three "were arrogant in the land" (29:39: *fastakabarū fī al-arḍ*). The triad maps onto three forms of unjust concentrated power:

- **Pharaoh**: sovereignty/coercive power — claims divine status [28:38]
- **Haman**: administrative power — the minister who executes the tyrant's will
- **Qarun**: economic power — wealth that generates independence from communal accountability

The grouping is significant: economic power is not categorically different from political tyranny in the Quran's taxonomy. All three are *mustakabirīn* — those who claim superiority. Qarun's wealth-arrogance is placed in the same moral category as Pharaoh's political-divinity claim: economic power that generates fasād and claims autonomy is a form of tyranny.

### 5. The Solomon Contrast (flag only)

Solomon held comparable or greater wealth [27:36-44, 34:12-13, 38:30-40] but said: "this is from the bounty of my Lord, to test me whether I am grateful or ungrateful" [27:40]. Correct attribution; no fasād; no envy-spectacle. Full Solomon analysis belongs to its own narrative agent. The flag: the Quran holds both simultaneously — vast wealth can be sanctified or condemned, with attribution and social effect as the variables.

### 6. Negative Space — What the Quran Does Not Say

- No wealth ceiling is specified or implied.
- No recommendation to live simply or in poverty.
- No condemnation of commerce, trade, accumulation per se.
- No suggestion that Qarun's wealth should have been redistributed by force.
- The community counsel [28:77] is not an injunction to give everything away.
- The destruction is not framed as redistributive justice — it is simply erasure.

The three variables the text actually targets: **orientation** (toward hereafter or world?), **attribution** (divine gift or personal merit?), and **social display** (generosity or spectacle?). Wealth itself is morally neutral in the Quranic frame — it is the three orientations above that determine whether it becomes a blessing or a destruction-trigger.

## Verses for Further Study

- **9:34** — the KUNŪZ root in the condemnation of hoarding scholars and monks; directly echoes Qarun's treasure-type
- **104:1-4** — the Humazah surah on the wealth-counter (jamaʿa mālan wa ʿaddadahu); accumulation + counting as moral failure
- **34:15-19** — Saba: the wealth-pride-destruction sequence in a collective (the two garden owners)
- **18:32-44** — the two garden owners: the believing and disbelieving man; explicit on attribution error
- **27:40** — Solomon's "this is from the bounty of my Lord" — the correct attribution formula, direct contrast to Qarun's ʿilm ʿindī

## Summary

Seven-verse architecture: (1) divine origin of wealth established, (2) balanced counsel issued, (3) self-attribution error recorded, (4) envy-spectacle staged, (5) knowledge-community counter entered, (6) sudden total destruction, (7) communal learning. The moral closure is pedagogical: the former-enviers speak the lesson. Economic ethics distilled: wealth is divine trust, oriented toward the hereafter without abandoning the world, disposed through iḥsān, corrupted by fasād. Self-attribution and conspicuous display are the two failure mechanisms. No wealth ceiling, no poverty-advocacy, no forced redistribution — the absence of these is as loud as the prohibitions present.
