# Concept: RIBA & GAIN — rbw / rbH / ksb

## Headline Finding

The Quran's riba prohibition is architecturally structured around a single root's double meaning. The root r-b-w covers both "natural growth" (earth swelling, children being raised, land elevated) and "usury/interest." In [30:39]—the only Meccan riba verse—both senses appear in the same ayah: you give ribā hoping it yarbū (grows) in people's wealth, but it does not grow (yarbū) with God. This is not wordplay; it is the theological argument. Meanwhile, the profit-noun rbH occurs exactly once in the Quran—and only as a negative: their commerce "did not profit" [2:16]. The Quran legislates finance without ever naming legitimate profit.

## 30:39 — The Meccan Pivot

The only Meccan riba occurrence is also the most rhetorically precise. Both senses of the root appear in one ayah:

> وَمَآ ءَاتَيۡتُم مِّن رِّبࣰ ا لِّيَرۡبُوَاْ فِيٓ أَمۡوَٰلِ ٱلنَّاسِ فَلَا يَرۡبُواْ عِندَ ٱللَّهِۖ وَمَآ ءَاتَيۡتُم مِّن زَكَوٰةࣲ تُرِيدُونَ وَجۡهَ ٱللَّهِ فَأُوْلَٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلۡمُضۡعِفُونَ
>
> — Saheeh: "And whatever you give for interest to increase within the wealth of people will not increase with Allah. But what you give in zakah, desiring the countenance of Allah — those are the multipliers."
> — Pickthall: "That which ye give in usury in order that it may increase on other people's property hath no increase with Allah; but that which ye give in charity, seeking Allah's Countenance, hath increase manifold."
> — Khattab: "Whatever loans you give, seeking interest at the expense of people's wealth will not increase with Allah. But whatever charity you give, seeking the pleasure of Allah—it is they whose reward will be multiplied"
> — Arberry: "And what you give in usury, that it may increase upon the people's wealth, increases not with God; but what you give in alms, desiring God's Face, those — they receive recompense manifold."

The argument structure: ribā (noun) → li-yarbuw (verb: hoping it grows) → lā yarbuw (it does NOT grow). The Quran uses the root's natural-growth meaning to indict the financial practice: what you call "growth" is not real growth. Then the contrast: zakāt → those are the real "multipliers" (al-muDʿifūn, from DEf = double/multiply — a different root entirely).

**Divergence score at 30:39: 0.72 (mid-high).** The contestation is in how to render the riba noun vs the growth verbs when they share a root. Arberry's "increases not with God" preserves the verbal pun best; Saheeh's "will not increase with Allah" is functional but loses the recursive wordplay.

## The Baqarah Arc (2:275–281)

Seven of the eight usury-noun occurrences cluster in seven ayahs of Surah 2. This is the densest legal-ethical passage in the Quran on any financial topic.

**[2:275] — The core prohibition with its logic:**

> ٱلَّذِينَ يَأۡكُلُونَ ٱلرِّبَوٰاْ لَا يَقُومُونَ إِلَّا كَمَا يَقُومُ ٱلَّذِي يَتَخَبَّطُهُ ٱلشَّيۡطَٰنُ مِنَ ٱلۡمَسِّۚ ... وَأَحَلَّ ٱللَّهُ ٱلۡبَيۡعَ وَحَرَّمَ ٱلرِّبَوٰاْ
>
> — Saheeh: "Those who consume interest cannot stand [on the Day of Resurrection] except as one stands who is being beaten by Satan into insanity... But Allah has permitted trade and has forbidden interest."
> — Pickthall: "Those who swallow usury cannot rise up save as he ariseth whom the devil hath prostrated by (his) touch... Allah permitteth trading and forbiddeth usury."
> — Khattab: "Those who consume interest will stand ˹on Judgment Day˺ like those driven to madness by Satan's touch... But Allah has permitted trading and forbidden interest."
> — Arberry: "Those who devour usury shall not rise again except as he rises, whom Satan of the touch prostrates... God has permitted trafficking, and forbidden usury."

**Divergence score: 0.68 (mid).** The theological argument embedded in the prohibition is key: the people of riba claim "trade is just like interest (ribā)" (inna al-bayʿa mithlu al-ribā). The Quran's counter is a divine fiat, not a derivation: "Allah has permitted trade and forbidden riba." No economic theory is offered; the distinction is asserted categorically. This is not ignorance of economics — it is a deliberate refusal to reduce the prohibition to a quantitative threshold.

**[2:276] — The inversion principle:**

> يَمۡحَقُ ٱللَّهُ ٱلرِّبَوٰاْ وَيُرۡبِي ٱلصَّدَقَٰتِ
>
> — Saheeh: "Allah destroys interest and gives increase for charities."
> — Arberry: "God blots out usury, but freewill offerings He augments with interest."

**Divergence score: 0.78 (HIGH — highest in the riba arc).** The outlier is Arberry, who writes "He augments with interest" — using the financial meaning of the very root being condemned (yurbī al-ṣadaqāt). This is technically defensible (the verb IS from rbw = to grow/increase) but creates an interpretive scandal: Allah "pays interest" on charity? Saheeh avoids this by writing "gives increase," Khattab by "makes fruitful." The high divergence score confirms: this verse's wordplay is untranslatable without a choice about which sense of rbw to privilege.

**[2:278–279] — The ultimatum:**

[2:278]: O believers, fear Allah and abandon what remains of ribā if you are indeed believers.
[2:279]: If you do not, take notice of war (ḥarb) from Allah and His Messenger. But if you repent, you may have your principal (ruʾūs amwālikum) — neither wronging nor being wronged.

The "war from God" language is singular in the Quran — no other economic prohibition uses this framing. The practical resolution offered is also precise: principal is yours, increase is forfeit. This is the closest the Quran comes to a transactional definition, but it still defines riba by negation (you keep the principal, not the excess) without specifying what rate of excess constitutes riba.

**[2:280–281] — The eschatological frame:**

[2:280]: If the debtor is in hardship, grant respite until ease; and giving (it as) charity is better for you, if you knew.
[2:281]: Fear a Day when you will be returned to Allah; then every soul will be compensated for what it earned (kasabat) — and they will not be wronged.

The arc closes with ksb: souls will be paid for "what they earned." Financial dealings fold into a cosmic accountability register.

## rbH: Absence as Argument

**Root rbH — ربح — 1 occurrence in the entire Quran.** Appears once, at [2:16], in a non-financial context: "Those who purchased misguidance for guidance — their commerce (tijāratuhum) did not profit (mā rabiḥat)." The single use of the standard Arabic profit-word is metaphorical (spiritual trade) and negative (did not profit).

This near-total absence is structurally significant. When the Quran describes legitimate material gain, it reaches for:
- **tijāra** (trade/commerce, root tjr) — used positively at [4:29], [9:24], [61:10]
- **ajr** (wage/reward, root Ajr) — the standard word for labor compensation
- **faḍl** (bounty/excess, root fDl) — God's provision, often financial
- **kasb** (earning, root ksb) — the neutral general verb (see below)

The profit-noun ribḥ is avoided as a positive category. The Quran does not build a vocabulary of legitimate profit; it builds a vocabulary of legitimate exchange (bayʿ) and legitimate giving (ṣadaqa, zakāt). Profit-as-goal is structurally invisible.

## ksb: The Polysemy Spectrum

**67 occurrences, 27 surahs, only 2 lemmas** (kasaba x62, iktasaba x5 — the Form VIII intensified/deliberate variant). The POS is always V (verb) — ksb never appears as a standalone noun in the Quran, only as verb forms.

The key insight from the full occurrence list: ksb distributes across THREE distinct semantic registers. Identifying them:

**1. Financial earning (unambiguously material):**
- [2:267]: "Spend from the good things you have earned (kasabtum) and from what We produced from the earth" — context is agricultural + market income, explicitly material
- [111:2]: "His wealth and what he earned (kasaba) will not avail him" — Abu Lahab; māl (wealth) + kasab = material accumulation
- [2:79]: Scribes who write scripture to sell for "a small price" — yaksibūn here means their financial dealings

**2. Deed-earning (moral/spiritual acts, not money):**
- [2:81]: "Whoever earns evil (kasaba sayyiʾatan)" — the object is a sin-type, not an economic product
- [30:41]: "Corruption appeared from what people's hands have earned (kasabat)" — environmental ruin as consequence of sinful deeds
- [42:30]: "Whatever disaster strikes you is from what your hands have earned" — suffering as consequence of moral failures
- [2:281]: "Every soul compensated for what it earned" — eschatological accounting of all acts

**3. Ambiguous boundary cases (where financial and moral earning collapse):**
- [5:38]: "The thief and the thievess — cut their hands for what they earned (kasabā)" — the act (stealing) is financial but the earning is transgression
- [2:79]: Scribes earn money AND earn sin simultaneously — ksb encompasses both

The decisive pattern: **ksb is morally-loaded through object, not through the verb itself.** kasaba sayyiʾatan = earned evil. kasaba khairan = earned good. kasaba mālan = earned money. The verb is identical; the moral valence comes from what follows it. This makes ksb the Quran's most flexible economic/ethical verb — it covers the full range from commodity-market transactions to eschatological accounting.

**iktasaba (Form VIII, 5 occurrences):** [2:286], [4:32(×2)], [24:11], [33:58]. The intensified form carries a sense of deliberate, self-directed acquisition. Notably at [4:32]: men have a share from what they iktasabū (acquired), women a share from what they iktasabna — used here for legitimate inheritance/work entitlement. The intensification marks intentionality more than scale.

The "interest" vs "usury" rendering choice at 2:275 is the most consequential translation decision in Quranic financial ethics. "Usury" implies high/exploitative rates (implying low rates may be permitted); "interest" implies any rate. The Arabic al-ribā has no rate qualifier anywhere in the text — but translators' word choice embeds a position on this contested question.

## Negative Space: What the Quran Does NOT Say

1. **No rate threshold.** The prohibition says "al-ribā is forbidden" without specifying 1%, 10%, or 100%. [3:130]'s "doubled and multiplied" (aḍʿāfan muḍāʿafa) describes a historical practice the addressees engaged in, not a definition of the prohibition's scope. Jurists have debated for 1400 years whether any interest is riba or only excessive interest — the text refuses to settle this.

2. **No mechanism.** The Quran never explains WHY riba is wrong in economic terms (exploitation of need? artificial money-growth? breach of brotherhood?). The only stated reason is the assertion that "Allah has permitted bayʿ and forbidden ribā." The prohibition is grounded in divine authority, not economic theory.

3. **No positive profit vocabulary.** The word ribḥ (profit) appears once, negated. The Quran describes what you earn (ksb), what you trade (tjr), what God gives you (faDl, ajr, rizq) — but it never names "profit" as a legitimate goal. This may be the most significant lexical absence in the finance field.

4. **No quantification of debt terms.** [2:282]'s long debt documentation verse specifies witnesses, writing, and timing — but no interest rate and no compounding formula.

## Synthesis Rabbit Holes

1. **The bayʿ/ribā binary.** The Quran asserts trade (bayʿ) is permitted and ribā forbidden without defining what distinguishes them. The people of riba themselves argued they are equivalent. A synthesis agent should map the bayʿ root (root byE) against rbw to see what the text itself treats as the operative distinction.

2. **DEf (double/multiply) and the 3:130 clause.** The phrase "doubled and multiplied" appears to describe a compounding practice, but DEf is also the root used for God's multiplication of charitable acts (2:261: 700-fold). The same multiplication root applies to both riba-condemnation and charity-reward — synthesis should trace this.

3. **ksb + nfs pairing.** The co-occurrence data shows nfs (soul/self) appears in 15 of 60 kasaba ayahs (25%). "Every soul for what it earned" is a near-formula. This links earning directly to individual eschatological accountability — worth tracing as a structural feature of Quranic moral anthropology.

4. **The Meccan/Medinan split on rbw.** The natural-growth senses are mostly Meccan; the usury-noun is mostly Medinan. The one Meccan riba occurrence (30:39) frames it through the cosmological growth-argument rather than legal prohibition. Suggests the prohibition developed from a moral-cosmological critique (Meccan) into a legal ban (Medinan) — chronologically significant.
