# Wealth Core — mwl · rzq · knz · xzn

## Headline finding (≈100 words)

The Quran distinguishes two conceptually separate things that English collapses into "wealth." **Māl** (mwl, 86× — all noun) is what humans possess: plural amwāl (71%) when embedded in social obligations, singular māl when attached to an individual soul facing judgment. **Rizq** (rzq, 123×) is what God gives: 82% of its verbs have a divine grammatical subject. The pair defines a structural asymmetry — property belongs to humans, provision belongs to God. Against this, **kanz** (hoard, 9×) marks the moral failure of treating māl as rizq: stockpiling what was only ever divinely lent. **Khazāʾin** (divine treasuries, xzn, 13×) is the corrective frame: God's stores are infinite; human stinginess rests on a category error.

## 1. mwl — Māl / Amwāl (86 occurrences, 80 ayahs, 38 surahs)

### Stats
- Single lemma: **maāl** (مَال), exclusively nominal (N = 86/86)
- Medinan: 54 | Meccan: 32 — concentrated in legal surahs
- Top surahs: An-Nisāʾ (14), At-Tawba (12), Al-Baqara (11)

### Lemma / morphological table

| Form type | Count | Examples |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Plural construct + 3rd-person suffix (*amwāluhum*, *amwālahum*) | 21 | 3rd-party property (orphans, enemies) |
| Plural + preposition + 1st-person plural (*bi-amwālinā*) | 10 | Jihad formula: "with their wealth and their lives" |
| Plural + 2nd-person suffix (*amwālakum*, *amwālikum*) | 12 | Zakat, inheritance, do-not-consume verses |
| Plural absolute / indefinite (*amwālan*, *amwāl*) | 11 | General contexts |
| **Singular indefinite (*mālan*)** | **7** | **Eschatological: individual soul at judgment** |
| Singular definite (*al-māl*, *al-mālu*) | 6 | Definitional contexts ("wealth and children") |
| Singular + 3rd-person suffix (*māluhu*) | 7 | Individual's wealth at death [92:11, 111:2, 69:28] |

**Decisive morphological finding:** The plural dominates (71%) and is systematically relational — always someone's wealth in relation to someone else (orphans, community, enemies). The singular clusters in eschatological and definitional contexts. When the Quran describes wealth failing a person at judgment ([92:11], [69:28], [111:2], [104:2-3]), it invariably uses the singular: *māluhu* — this wealth, his, attached to him as he falls.

### Formulas

**amwāl wa-awlād** (wealth and children): 16 ayahs together. A recurrent dyad. [8:28]: "your properties and your children are but a trial." [18:46]: "Wealth and children are adornment of the worldly life. But the enduring good deeds are better." [63:9], [64:15], [3:10], [3:116], [71:21]. The formula is never positive unqualified — wealth paired with children always appears under theological subordination (fitnah, test, adornment-not-substance).

**bi-amwālihim wa-anfusihim** (with their wealth and their lives): jihad formula, 8 ayahs [4:95 ×2, 8:72, 9:20, 9:44, 9:81, 49:15, 61:11]. Wealth is the material analogue of self-sacrifice.

**lā taʾkulū amwālakum** (do not consume your wealth): prohibition formula targeting unjust consumption [2:188, 4:29].

### Finance-context distribution

- **Zakat / sadaqa / infāq**: majority of amwāl occurrences (~35)
- **Inheritance law**: An-Nisāʾ (14 occurrences), almost entirely orphan property and succession
- **Ribā**: [2:279] returns *ra's al-māl* (principal/capital) — the one finance-technical usage
- **Trade**: [2:282] — amwāl implicit in debt-contract framework

### Semantic field summary

Māl is **social property** — wealth embedded in obligations, never private achievement. Orphan amwāl must be returned intact [4:2, 4:6]; the weak-minded must be provided for out of the community's amwāl [4:5]. The theologically decisive verse is [4:5]:

> **4:5** — وَلَا تُؤۡتُواْ ٱلسُّفَهَآءَ أَمۡوَٰلَكُمُ ٱلَّتِي جَعَلَ ٱللَّهُ لَكُمۡ قِيَٰمًا
>
> Saheeh: "And do not give the weak-minded your property, which Allah has made a means of sustenance for you…"
> Pickthall: "Give not unto the foolish (what is in) your (keeping of their) wealth, which Allah hath given you to maintain…"
> Khattab: "Do not entrust the incapable…with your wealth which Allah has made a means of support for you…"
> Arberry: "But do not give to fools their property that God has assigned to you to manage…"

The word *qiyāman* (means of sustenance / support) frames amwāl as social infrastructure assigned by God — not private ownership but divine assignment to a social function.

### Co-roots signal

Top co-occurring roots: Allah (Alh, 44 ayahs), belief (Amn, 18), souls/self (nfs, 17), children (wld, 16), way-of-God (sbl, 16), giving (Aty, 15), spending (nfq, 9), jihad (jhd, 10), reward (Ajr, 8). The co-occurrence profile is entirely moral-legal — no neutral economic terms (price, market, profit, exchange) appear.

### Divergence signal

Highest-divergence mwl verses: [92:11] (0.759, high), [74:12] (0.751), [104:2] (0.740). All Meccan, eschatological, singular-māl. Translators diverge on quantitative/affective vocabulary ("vast," "abundant," "extensive," "ample") for the same Arabic *mālan*. The singular-indefinite form resists English most.

## 2. rzq — Rizq / Razaqa (123 occurrences, 109 ayahs, 44 surahs)

### Stats
- 4 lemmas: **razaqa** (V, 61) | **rizq** (N, 55) | **rāziqīn** (agent N, 6) | **razzāq** (intensive N, 1)
- Medinan: 43 | Meccan: 80 — provision discourse is primarily Meccan
- Top surahs: Al-Baqara (12), An-Nahl (10), Al-Hajj (7), Sabaʾ (6)

### Lemma table

| Lemma | Count | POS | Semantic role |
|-------|-------|-----|---------------|
| razaqa (رَزَقَ) | 61 | V | "provided / provide" |
| rizq (رِزْق) | 55 | N | "provision / sustenance" |
| rāziqīn (رَّٰزِقِين) | 6 | N (agent) | "provider(s)" — always with "best of providers" |
| razzāq (رَّزَّاق) | 1 | N (intensive) | "the continual Provider" [51:58] |

### Who provides rizq? (verb subject analysis)

Of 61 verb occurrences:
- **Divine 1st-person plural ("We provided/provide")**: 23 forms — *razaqnāhum, razaqnākum, narzuqukum*, etc.
- **3rd-person singular (Allah subject)**: 12 forms — *yarzuqu, yarzuqukum*
- **Past 3rd-person (Allah explicit/implicit subject)**: 14 forms — *razaqakum, razaqahum*
- **Passive ("they were provided")**: 5 forms — *yurزaqūn, ruziqū* (subject is humans receiving)
- **Human imperative ("provide for them [out of it]")**: 5 forms

**Result: 49 of 61 verb occurrences (80%) have a divine grammatical subject.** The 5 human-active cases are all imperatives directing humans to distribute what God has already provided — Abraham praying for Mecca's people [2:126], Moses's instructions for guardians [4:5, 4:8], Ibrahim praying [14:37], Jesus's prayer [5:114]. Humans are never originators of rizq; they are conduits.

### Rizq as noun: semantic channels

1. **Worldly provision** (food, rain, sustenance) — majority. Often with *ṭayyib* (pure): *kūlū min ṭayyibāt mā razaqnākum* [2:172, 2:57, 7:160].
2. **Eschatological provision** — [3:169]: martyrs "receive provision from their Lord." Rizq extends past death.
3. **Expansion/contraction formula** (high divergence) [29:62], [13:26]: "Allah extends provision for whom He wills and restricts it." Rizq is not mechanically distributed; it is modulated by divine will. Translators diverge because *yabsuṭ/yaqdir* (expand/constrain) resists clean English.

**Decisive insight:** rzq is structurally asymmetric with mwl. Māl is what you have; rizq is what you are given. When a verse says *wa-anfiqū mimmā razaqnākum* ("spend from what We have provided you") [2:254, 63:10], the grammar encodes an entire theology: the spending is yours; the original giving is God's. Rizq never appears with possessive constructs the way māl does — there is no *rizqī* meaning "my provision" in the sense of "my property"; the form always marks it as flowing from above.

### Co-roots signal

Top: Allah (Alh, 59), belief (Amn, 23), Lord (rbb, 23), knowledge (Elm, 23), thing (shayʾ, 22), people (qwm, 22), spending (nfq, 16), earth (ArD, 16), good/pure (Tyb, 15), sky (smw, 15). The sky-earth (smw + ArD) pair clusters with rizq — provision descends from sky to earth. Māl has no such vertical axis.

### High-divergence rzq verse

[56:82] (score 0.788, high): *wa-tajʿalūna rizqakum annakum tukadhdhibūn* — "And make [the thanks for] your provision that you deny [the Provider]?" The verse turns rizq into wordplay: your *provision* becomes the act of *denial*. Translators split: Pickthall renders "And make denial thereof your livelihood?"; Arberry "and do you make it your living to cry lies?" Arabic allows both readings — the divergence is semantic, not accidental.

## 3. knz — Kanz / Kanaza (9 occurrences, 7 ayahs, 6 surahs)

### Stats
- 2 lemmas: **kanz** (N, 6) | **kanaza** (V, 3)
- Medinan: 3 | Meccan: 6
- All Medinan uses are in At-Tawba (9:34-35) — the condemnation passage

### Lemma table

| Lemma | Count | POS | Context |
|-------|-------|-----|---------|
| kanz (كَنز) | 6 | N | treasure (neutral/positive: 11:12, 18:82 ×2, 25:8, 26:58, 28:76) |
| kanaza (كَنَزَ) | 3 | V | hoard (condemn: 9:34, 9:35 ×2) |

**Morphological finding:** The noun *kanz* (6×) is used without negative valence in Meccan contexts — a treasure the prophet lacks [11:12], orphans' buried inheritance [18:82], Pharaoh's treasuries [26:58], Qarun's wealth [28:76]. The verb *kanaza* (3×) concentrated in 9:34-35 carries all the moral weight. The verb creates the condemnation; the noun is semantically flexible.

### The 9:34-35 hoarding passage

> **9:34** — وَٱلَّذِينَ يَكۡنِزُونَ ٱلذَّهَبَ وَٱلۡفِضَّةَ وَلَا يُنفِقُونَهَا فِي سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ فَبَشِّرۡهُم بِعَذَابٍ أَلِيمٍ
>
> Saheeh: "And those who hoard gold and silver and spend it not in the way of Allah — give them tidings of a painful punishment."
> Pickthall: "They who hoard up gold and silver and spend it not in the way of Allah, unto them give tidings of a painful doom."
> Khattab: "Give good news of a painful torment to those who hoard gold and silver and do not spend it in Allah's cause."
> Arberry: "Those who treasure up gold and silver, and do not expend them in the way of God — give them the good tidings of a painful chastisement."

The condemnation is composite: (1) *yaknizūna* — the hoarding verb; (2) *wa-lā yunfiqūnahā fī sabīl Allāh* — the absence of spending in God's way. Non-circulation is the crime, not possession. 9:35 enacts the reversal: the gold and silver are heated in hellfire and branded on the hoarders' bodies — stored-up harm returning to its owner.

### The neutral-kanz cases (Meccan)

**18:82** (Khidr / orphans): a buried treasure (*kanz*) belongs to two orphan boys; God intends them to reach maturity and "extract their treasure" as divine mercy. Here kanz is righteous inherited property, not condemned hoarding.

**28:76** (Qarun): "We gave him of treasures (*kunūz*) whose keys would burden a band of strong men." The wealth is described as given by God; condemnation comes from Qarun's *baghy* (arrogance) and *faraH* (exultation) — not the possession itself.

**Decisive insight:** kanz is morally determined by *circulation*. Wealth that flows (inheritance for orphans, spending in God's way) is not condemnable kanz. Wealth that stops is. The Quran condemns the blockage, not the holding.

## 4. xzn — Khazāʾin / Khazana (13 occurrences, 13 ayahs, 11 surahs)

### Stats
- 3 lemmas: **khazāʾin** (N, 8) | **khazanat** (agent N, 4) | **khāzinīn** (agent N, 1)
- Meccan: 12 | Medinan: 1 — almost entirely Meccan
- 11 different surahs — wide distribution

### Lemma table

| Lemma | Count | Referent |
|-------|-------|---------|
| khazāʾin (خَزَائِن) | 8 | Divine treasuries (7×) + Joseph's earthly stores (1×) |
| khazana (خَزَنَت) | 4 | Angels as keepers of Hell / Paradise (39:71, 39:73, 40:49, 67:8) |
| khāzinīn (خَٰزِنِين) | 1 | "Retainers" — humans who are NOT keepers of rain [15:22] |

### Two semantic registers

**Register 1 — Divine ownership**: 7 of 8 *khazāʾin* occurrences assert that the treasuries belong to God [6:50, 11:31, 15:21, 17:100, 38:9, 52:37, 63:7]. The rhetorical pattern in 6:50 and 11:31 is prophetic disclaimer: Noah and Muhammad both say "I do not claim to have the treasuries of Allah" — khazāʾin is divine, not prophetic.

**Register 2 — Angelic keepers**: *khazanat* (4×) = angels who administer Hell [39:71, 40:49, 67:8] and Paradise [39:73]. Administrative custodians of eschatological stores.

Exception: [12:55] — Joseph: "Put me in charge of the *khazāʾin* of the land." The only earthly use, referring to Egypt's grain reserves. Administrative infrastructure of survival.

### The moral asymmetry resolved

Why does kanz earn condemnation while khazāʾin is divine?

1. **Owner**: khazāʾin belongs to God or divine custodians — stores are not private.
2. **Measure**: [15:21] — "We do not send it down except according to a known measure" (*bi-qadar maʿlūm*). Divine storage is regulated distribution, not accumulation.
3. **Access**: humans are explicitly told they are *not* khāzinīn of rain [15:22] — you cannot retain what belongs to God.
4. **Stinginess test** [17:100]: "If you possessed the treasuries of the mercy of your Lord, then you would withhold out of fear of spending." Human kanz is a cognitive as much as a moral failure — mistaking finite holding for infinite stores.

### Co-roots signal

khazāʾin co-occurs with: speech/qawl (8×), *ʿinda* (with/at, 6×), Lord/rabb (6×), knowledge/ʿilm (4×), spending/nafaqa (2×). The *ʿinda* cluster is decisive — khazāʾin Allāh always appear as *ʿindī/ʿindahum* in rhetorical denials of human access.

## 5. Cross-cluster comparison: mwl vs rzq in finance contexts

The clearest contrast appears in surahs where both roots appear together (19 shared surahs).

**Pattern A — Zakat verses** [2:3, 2:254, 63:10]: the command is *wa-anfiqū mimmā razaqnākum* (spend from what We have provided) — rizq as the source; the frame attributes the resource to God before commanding the human action.

**Pattern B — Riba verse** [2:279]: uses amwāl (*ru'ūs amwālikum*, your principal) — the one finance-technical term. Rizq does not appear in the riba prohibition. The argument is in property-law terms (amwāl), not provision-theology (rizq).

**Pattern C — Inheritance law** (An-Nisāʾ): amwāl (14×); rizq does not appear in inheritance passages. Legal distribution uses property vocabulary; provision theology is separate.

**Negative space**: The Quran has no economically neutral vocabulary. There is no equivalent of "assets," "income," or "profit" as value-free terms. Every wealth word is morally loaded: māl as social obligation, rizq as divine gift, kanz as condemned blockage, khazāʾin as divine infrastructure.

## 6. Verses for direct study

1. **[4:5]** — māl as *qiyāman* (means of support / social infrastructure) — the definitional verse for property as social function, not private right
2. **[9:34-35]** — the hoarding condemnation — the crime is non-circulation, not possession
3. **[15:21-22]** — divine khazāʾin + human non-custodianship — stores flow downward in measure; humans cannot retain them
4. **[17:100]** — human stinginess as cognitive failure — if humans had divine stores they would hoard them out of *khashya al-infāq* (fear of spending)
5. **[18:46]** — māl and children as *zīna* (adornment) of worldly life — the clearest theological subordination of wealth to a lesser category

## 7. Rabbit holes for synthesis

- **fDD (silver/فضض)**: co-occurs with both knz [9:34] and xzn [63:7]. Silver and gold as the prototype hoardable commodity — why specifically precious metals?
- **nfq (spending)** as the structural opposite of kanz: every knz condemnation has nfq as its antithesis. The spending cluster needs its own root analysis.
- **qdr (measure/qadar)** co-occurs with xzn [15:21] — "in a known measure." The verb of divine provision is the verb of divine decree. Provision and predestination share a root.
- **28:76-82 Qarun narrative**: the Quran's only extended case study in wealth-and-destruction — Qarun given *kunūz*, commits *baghy*, sinks into the earth. Narrative encodes the kanz-to-punishment arc in story form.
- **56:82** high-divergence rizq-as-denial: the wordplay where gratitude for rizq is inverted into denial — connects provision theology to shirk discourse.
