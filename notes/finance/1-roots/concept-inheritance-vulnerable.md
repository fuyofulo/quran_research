# Inheritance & Vulnerable — Root Cluster Analysis

**Roots covered:** wrv (ورث, wirāth), wSy (وصي, waṣiyya), ytm (يتم, yatīm), fqr (فقر, faqīr), skn/miskīn (سكن, miskīn)

---

## Headline Finding (100 words)

The Quranic inheritance system (4:11–12, 4:176) is the most quantitatively dense legislation in the entire corpus: six distinct fractions (1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 2/3, 1/3, 1/6) assigned to specific heirs across multiple contingencies. The formula "after any bequest and any debt" (min baʿdi waṣiyyatin … aw dayn) recurs five times, establishing a priority queue: debt first, bequest second, fractional inheritance third. The orphan (yatīm, 23 occurrences, all one lemma) is the paradigm test case of social justice: consuming their wealth is eating fire. Fuqarāʾ and masākīn appear as co-listed but distinct categories in 9:60, the zakat verse.

**Decisive split:** The causative lemma >aworava (12x) is almost entirely eschatological — God "grants inheritance" of land or paradise as covenant reward (7:43, 7:128, 7:137, 26:59, 33:27, 39:74, 43:72, 44:28). This confirms the polysemy is structurally organized by voice: passive causative = divine gift; simple verb = human legal transfer.

## The Inheritance Fractions — 4:11, 4:12, 4:176

The surah 4 inheritance block is the most arithmetically specific legislation in the Quran. No other topic receives numerical fractions.

**Panel 1 — 4:11 (children and parents)**

> يُوصِيكُمُ ٱللَّهُ فِيٓ أَوۡلَٰدِكُمۡۖ لِلذَّكَرِ مِثۡلُ حَظِّ ٱلۡأُنثَيَيۡنِۚ فَإِن كُنَّ نِسَآءً فَوۡقَ ٱثۡنَتَيۡنِ فَلَهُنَّ ثُلُثَا مَا تَرَكَۖ وَإِن كَانَتۡ وَٰحِدَةً فَلَهَا ٱلنِّصۡفُۚ وَلِأَبَوَيۡهِ لِكُلِّ وَٰحِدٍ مِّنۡهُمَا ٱلسُّدُسُ مِمَّا تَرَكَ إِن كَانَ لَهُۥ وَلَدٌ فَإِن لَّمۡ يَكُن لَّهُۥ وَلَدٌ وَوَرِثَهُۥٓ أَبَوَاهُ فَلِأُمِّهِ ٱلثُّلُثُۚ فَإِن كَانَ لَهُۥٓ إِخۡوَةٌ فَلِأُمِّهِ ٱلسُّدُسُۚ مِنۢ بَعۡدِ وَصِيَّةٍ يُوصِي بِهَآ أَوۡ دَيۡنٍ

The fractions from this verse and 4:12 catalogued:

| Heir | Condition | Fraction |
|------|-----------|----------|
| Sons (vs. daughters) | Mixed siblings | Son gets double daughter's share |
| Daughters (2+) | No sons | **2/3** of estate |
| Single daughter | No sons | **1/2** |
| Each parent | Deceased had children | **1/6** each |
| Mother alone (father inherits) | No children | **1/3** |
| Mother (siblings present) | No children but has siblings | **1/6** |
| Husband (from wife's estate) | Wife left no child | **1/2** |
| Husband | Wife left children | **1/4** |
| Wife/wives (from husband's estate) | Husband left no child | **1/4** |
| Wife/wives | Husband left children | **1/8** |
| Uterine siblings (one each) | Kalāla (no child, no parent) | **1/6** each |
| Uterine siblings (group 2+) | Kalāla | **1/3** shared |
| Full sister | Kalāla (brother inherits from her) | **1/2** [4:176] |
| Two full sisters | Kalāla | **2/3** [4:176] |

**Structural observation (data-grounded):** The fractions are conditional, not unconditional. Each fraction is tied to a specific family configuration. The same heir (e.g., husband) gets two different fractions depending on whether the deceased had children. This creates a responsive, contingency-based system — not fixed amounts but a priority tree with six leaves.

**The female inheritance share (4:11):** Daughters receive a named fractional share: 2/3 (if two or more) or 1/2 (if one) — fractions of the full estate. In pre-Islamic Arabian custom, women generally received nothing from inheritance. The verse does not establish gender parity (son still gets double daughter's share), but it juridically establishes that daughters are heirs with enforceable claims. The formula "for the male the equivalent of the portion of two females" (li-l-dhakari mithlu ḥaẓẓi l-unthayayn) is stated once here and then referenced again in 4:176, confirming it is a general rule, not a one-time provision.

## The Bequest Formula — Priority Queue

**Panel 2 — the repeating formula (min baʿdi waṣiyyatin … aw dayn)**

The phrase "after any bequest bequeathed and any debt" (مِنۢ بَعۡدِ وَصِيَّةٍ يُوصِي بِهَا أَوۡ دَيۡنٍ) appears five times in four verses: 4:11, 4:12 (×3), and implicitly across the inheritance block.

The order is legally significant. Parsing the implied priority queue:

1. **Debt (dayn)** — must be discharged first; mentioned as alternative/conjunction to bequest, not subordinate to it. Classical fiqh established that debt is paid before bequest (the Arabic "or" [aw] here is not exclusive but enumerative — both must clear).
2. **Bequest (waṣiyya)** — voluntary instrument capped by Hadith tradition at 1/3 of estate. The Quran does not state this cap explicitly; 2:180 commands bequest "with fairness" (bil-maʿrūf), leaving the cap unstated. The constraint is structural: the fractional heir shares (assigned by God, farīḍa min Allāh) kick in after the bequest. If the bequest consumed the whole estate, the mandated fractions could not be paid. The cap therefore logically follows from the fractions themselves.
3. **Fractional shares (farāʾiḍ)** — paid to named heirs in the specified proportions.

**Data-grounded finding:** 4:12 uses the phrase "without harm" (ghayra muḍārrin) in describing the bequest — the only ethical qualifier attached to the formula. The bequest must not be used as a weapon to disinherit heirs. This is the Quran's anti-abuse clause. Combined with the structural cap logic above, the text builds in both a mathematical constraint and an ethical constraint on testamentary freedom.

## The Orphan as Paradigm Test Case

**ytm: 23 occurrences, 1 lemma, 12 surahs**

The distribution of yatīm verses is instructive:
- **Surah 4 alone: 8 occurrences** — the inheritance surah concentrates orphan protection tightly with the fractional heir verses. This is not coincidental: orphans were the group most at risk of having their inherited wealth stolen by guardians.
- **Surah 2: 4 occurrences** — charity and right action context
- **Late Meccan surahs (89, 90, 93, 107): 4 occurrences** — ethical denunciation language

The escalating severity of orphan-protection verses:

**Property rules (Medinan, legal):**
- [4:2] — give orphans their property; don't substitute bad for good; don't absorb their wealth into yours — described as "a great sin" (ḥūban kabīran)
- [4:6] — test the orphan's maturity before handing over wealth; don't consume it hastily before they grow up; bring witnesses
- [6:152] / [17:34] — exact parallel wording: "do not approach the orphan's property except in the best manner until they reach full strength"
- [4:10] — "those who consume orphans' wealth unjustly — they eat fire into their bellies and will burn in blazing fire"

**4:10 is the harshest financial crime verdict in the Quran.** Other financial prohibitions (riba, fraud) get warnings; orphan-property theft gets the specific image of ingesting fire.

**Ethical/spiritual protection (Meccan):**
- [89:17] — "No! But you do not honor the orphan" — as evidence of the unjust society
- [93:9] — "So do not oppress the orphan" — direct imperative
- [107:2] — "This is the one who drives away the orphan" — as the defining act of those who deny religion (yukadhdhibu bi-l-dīn)
- [90:15] — feeding "an orphan of near relation" (yatīman dhā maqraba) as a righteous act in the mountain-pass parable

**Structural observation:** The orphan appears at the intersection of inheritance law (surah 4) and ethical discourse (late Meccan). The Medinan verses legislate protection; the Meccan verses call it a matter of fundamental religion vs. denial of religion. The category is thus simultaneously legal and theological.

**Panel 3 — 4:10**

> إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ يَأۡكُلُونَ أَمۡوَٰلَ ٱلۡيَتَٰمَىٰ ظُلۡمًا إِنَّمَا يَأۡكُلُونَ فِي بُطُونِهِمۡ نَارًا وَسَيَصۡلَوۡنَ سَعِيرًا

"Those who consume the wealth of orphans unjustly — they are only consuming fire into their bellies, and they will burn in a blazing fire."

## fuqarāʾ vs. masākīn — 9:60 and the Double Category

**Panel 4 — 9:60 (the zakat distribution verse)**

> إِنَّمَا ٱلصَّدَقَٰتُ لِلۡفُقَرَآءِ وَٱلۡمَسَٰكِينِ وَٱلۡعَٰمِلِينَ عَلَيۡهَا وَٱلۡمُؤَلَّفَةِ قُلُوبُهُمۡ وَفِي ٱلرِّقَابِ وَٱلۡغَٰرِمِينَ وَفِي سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ وَٱبۡنِ ٱلسَّبِيلِ فَرِيضَةً مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ وَٱللَّهُ عَلِيمٌ حَكِيمٌ

"Zakah is only for the fuqarāʾ and the masākīn, and those who administer it, and those whose hearts are to be reconciled, and for freeing slaves, and for those in debt, and for the cause of God, and for the wayfarer — an obligation from God."

**Data-grounded observations on the double category:**

1. **Order:** fuqarāʾ is listed first, masākīn second. Both receive the lām prefix (lil-fuqarāʾi wa-l-masākīni) — syntactically co-beneficiaries but listed separately with waw (and), not wa-humā (and they are the same).

2. **Surah 4:8** shows orphans, masākīn, and relatives at the inheritance distribution — masākīn here is not in a zakat context but at the moment of estate division, as bystanders who should be given something.

3. **Cross-occurrences:** In the fqr concept JSON, skn co-occurs with fqr in only 1 shared ayah (9:60) despite both being 14-23 occurrence roots. This low overlap suggests they are parallel categories rather than overlapping ones — the Quran consistently uses them in the same distribution lists but in distinct roles.

4. **The 2:273 fuqarāʾ verse** adds a distinctive qualification: the poor "who have been restricted in the path of God … who do not beg of people persistently." This describes fuqarāʾ with dignity (hidden poverty, not begging). The miskīn in 18:79 (the boat story) owns something (a boat) but is vulnerable to expropriation. This textual pattern supports the classical distinction: faqīr = destitute, with nothing; miskīn = poor but has something, yet still in need.

5. **The 90:16 miskīn verse** reinforces deprivation without complete destitution: "a miskīn of extreme dust/dirt" (dhā matraba — ground-level poverty). The image is physical degradation, not total emptiness.

**Interpretive claim (marked as such):** The Quranic distinction between fuqarāʾ and masākīn appears to track degree of destitution, with fuqarāʾ deeper in need (receiving first in the list) and masākīn having some residual resources. The Quran does not define this distinction explicitly — it is enacted through the co-listing convention and the contextual portraits.

## wrv — Eschatological vs. Financial Inheritance

The causative lemma >aworava (12x) reveals the theological logic connecting both senses.

**Financial inheritance (surah 4, simple verb wariva):** Human transfer — heirs receive estate from the deceased. The fractions are "an obligation from God" (farīḍa min Allāh, 4:11), so even the human legal system is divinely mandated.

**Eschatological/covenant inheritance:**
- **Paradise** — [7:43]: "This is the Garden you have been made to inherit by what you used to do." [23:11]: "Those who will inherit al-Firdaws." [43:72]: same formula as 7:43.
- **The earth (covenant)** — [7:128]: "God grants the earth to whomever He wills of His servants." [7:137]: God makes the oppressed (Children of Israel) "inherit" the east and west of the land after Pharaoh. [28:5]: "We wanted to favor those who were oppressed in the land and make them the inheritors (al-wārithīn)."

**Panel 5 — 28:5**

> وَنُرِيدُ أَن نَّمُنَّ عَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ ٱسۡتُضۡعِفُواْ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ وَنَجۡعَلَهُمۡ أَئِمَّةً وَنَجۡعَلَهُمُ ٱلۡوَٰرِثِينَ

"We wished to favor those who were oppressed on earth, and to make them leaders and the inheritors."

The connection between the two senses is the moral logic of transfer from the powerful to the deserving. Financial inheritance transfers wealth from the deceased to rightful heirs defined by kinship; eschatological inheritance transfers blessing from the transient world to those who earned it by faith and patience. The word wrv activates both by design: the same root used for the Quranic legal estate is used for paradise and the covenant land. The inheritance of the oppressed (28:5) echoes the inheritance of the orphan (surah 4) — both are structural reversals of unjust hoarding.

**15:23** is the extreme case: "We are the inheritors" (wa-innā la-naḥnu l-wārithūn) — God as the ultimate heir of everything. This closes the loop: humans inherit from each other; God inherits from all.

## Negative Space

1. **No executor (waṣī) institution in the text.** The Quran mandates bequest and witnesses (5:106) but does not establish a named executor role. The concept JSON for wSy shows no "executor" lemma. Classical fiqh developed the concept from the legal context, not from explicit Quranic language.

2. **No explicit 1/3 cap on waṣiyya.** The Quran says "with fairness/appropriateness" (bil-maʿrūf, 2:180) and "without harm" (ghayra muḍārrin, 4:12) but never specifies a fraction. The 1/3 cap is entirely from Hadith. This is theologically significant: the Quran set the fractional framework for forced-share heirs, but left the testamentary cap to prophetic tradition.

3. **No grandfather rule specified.** The verses cover parents, children, siblings, spouses, but the grandfather's share in the absence of a father is not specified. Classical jurisprudence argued extensively about this — it is precisely where the Quran's silence created the biggest fiqh controversy.

4. **miskīn never appears in the inheritance block.** The masākīn appear at 4:8 (present at the division), but the formal inheritance rules (4:11–12, 4:176) never mention them as heirs. They are observers at estate distribution, not legal recipients of fractional shares.

## Synthesis Threads for Phase 2

- **Priority queue (debt → bequest → fractions)** connects wSy and wrv: the order implies that bequest is a limited instrument because the mandatory fractional system constrains what remains.
- **Orphan-wealth/inheritance overlap:** The surah 4 orphan verses (4:2–10) immediately precede the fractional inheritance verses (4:11–12). Orphans are those who have ALREADY inherited but whose wealth is at risk of guardian theft. They are the paradigm case of inheritance-system failure.
- **faqīr / miskīn / yatīm as a triad of vulnerability:** Three different categories of vulnerable recipient — the absolutely poor, the relatively poor, and the parentless minor. The zakat system (9:60) incorporates fuqarāʾ and masākīn; the inheritance system (4:8) incorporates masākīn and yatāmā as observers. The full triad is assembled by taking both passages together.
- **Eschatological/financial wrv unity:** Synthesis should explore whether the spiritual inheritance language (orphan of the earth, the oppressed inheriting) is the Quran's way of encoding an economic justice narrative into eschatology — virtue earns the real estate, not birth.
