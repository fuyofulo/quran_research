# Finance — the Quran on money, wealth, and economic ethics

## Headline finding

The Quran does not legislate an economy; it legislates a relationship between humans and the property that passes through their hands. One architecture holds across all 21 files: everything is Allah's, humans handle some of it under fiduciary tenancy (*istikhlāf*), every transaction is audited at the Day of Return. From this single premise unfolds the mwl/rzq split, the four-fold give-vocabulary, the categorically prohibited riba, the qisṭ/mīzān justice machinery, and the *dūla* principle that wealth must move and not pool [59:7]. The negative space — no rate, no threshold, no ceiling, no court — is the design.

## Open questions

- **Scope of the riba ban**: any interest, or only exploitative compounding? Silence on rate keeps the modern Islamic-finance debate live.
- **The 2:1 male/female inheritance asymmetry**: [4:11] states fractions without arguing them; classical justifications are imported from outside the verse.
- **Modern fiat / inflation**: does qarḍ ḥasan accommodate inflation-adjusted preservation of real principal?
- **Dūla in a global economy**: when wealth pools across national lines but redistribution operates within them, what is the relevant *minkum*?
- **Conscience-architecture without belief**: the system requires divine-accounting belief to function; no fallback for a secularized practitioner-class.
