# The despair words: yaʾs, qunūṭ, iblās, and the distress vocabulary around them

> **Headline finding.** Despair (`yʾs`/`qnṭ`, 16 ayahs) is faulted when it is a verdict about God's side (6 cases) or a reflex tied to circumstance (4 cases). Only one of the four reflex passages names a cure (11:11, patience and good deeds). The unblamed cases are despair of a thing or an outcome (rain, a missing son, the messengers' waiting), and each is followed by an act of God. `Iblās` is the despair after the last warning: sudden, and lasting in the Fire. Grief (`ḥuzn`) and despair never share a verse. Jacob is the only speaker with both words in his mouth: he grieves to God (12:86) and forbids despair of God (12:87). Distress is a ladder, and its lowest rung is not despair.

**Trap.** `hmm` is not "worry": there is no noun *hamm*. The nine tokens are the verb *hamma*, "to intend or be on the verge of" (3:122, 9:13, 12:24 …); only 3:154 (*ahammat-hum anfusuhum*) is worry. `Hmm.json` is *ḥamīm*, boiling water.

## 2. Q1: anatomy of the faulted despair

**Verified.** Of 10 faulted or forbidden cases, 6 have God's side as object: 12:87 (*rawḥ*), 15:55-56, 29:23, 39:53 (mercy), 60:13 (the hereafter). 4 are the "when evil touches him" reflex: 11:9, 17:83, 30:36, 41:49.

| Passage | Trigger | Reaction | Opposite state | Exception or remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11:9-11 | mercy tasted, then withdrawn | `yaʾūs kafūr` (despairing, ungrateful) | after harm lifts: "bad times have left me", `la-farīḥ fakhūr` | **11:11: `ṣabarū wa-ʿamilū ṣ-ṣāliḥāt`; forgiveness and great reward** |
| 17:83-84 | evil touches | `kāna yaʾūsā` | favour: `aʿraḍa wa-naʾā bi-jānibihi` (turns away, withdraws) | none. 17:84: *each acts according to his manner; your Lord knows best who is guided.* |
| 30:33-37 | harm touches | 30:33 they call on their Lord, turning to Him; 30:36 `yaqnaṭūn` | mercy: some associate (30:33); `fariḥū` (30:36) | none named. 30:37: *Allah extends and restricts provision; signs for believers.* |
| 41:49-52 | evil touches | `yaʾūs qanūṭ` | mercy: *"this is mine; I do not think the Hour will come"* (41:50); favour: turns away (41:51) | none. 41:52 turns to the Qurʾān. |

**Is the cure the same?** No. Only 11:11 names one; the others give a *frame* (17:84, 30:37, 41:52). **Cross-check:** 70:19-23 describes the same man (*impatient in evil, withholding in good*) with a different exception: *those who pray and are constant in their prayer*. The exceptions are plural.

Further points. **(a)** 17:83 and 41:51 share a verbatim first clause (*wa-idhā anʿamnā ʿalā l-insāni aʿraḍa wa-naʾā bi-jānibihi, wa-idhā massahu sh-sharru*), then end in *yaʾūsā* and *fa-dhū duʿāʾin ʿarīḍ* (long supplication). The same man despairs and begs in the same sūra (41:49, 41:51). **[Interpretive]** What is faulted is the stance that rises and falls with circumstance, not the begging. **(b)** 30:36 is the only reflex verse with a cause: *bi-mā qaddamat aydīhim*. **(c)** The opposite of despair in 11:9-10 is exultation, `farḥ`, not hope.

## 3. Q2: unblamed despair

- **12:80.** The brothers *istayʾasū* "of him" (Benjamin). The eldest stays *until my father permits me or Allah decides for me, and He is the best of judges*. Despair of a human outcome turns to God as judge; no praise or blame.
- **12:110.** *Until, when the messengers despaired … Our help came to them.* **The Arabic gives no object**; "of their people" is supplied. Who "thought they had been denied" is also open: Saheeh has the messengers, Khattab the people. What follows is `naṣr`, then `fa-nujjiya man nashāʾ` (so whoever We willed was saved), and punishment not turned from the criminals.
- **42:28 and 30:49.** People who `qanaṭū`/were `mublisīn` before rain. After it, *He sends down the rain … and spreads His mercy*. In 30:48 they rejoice; 30:50 moves from rain to the dead being raised. The object is a thing, drought-broken rain. **[Interpretive]** *Raḥma* is the object of faulted despair (15:56, 29:23, 39:53) and the name of the rain in 42:28. Despair of a *thing* is answered; despair of *Whom* is the fault.

## 4. Q3: why `iblās` is end-despair

Five ayahs: 6:44, 23:77 (both *ḥattā idhā* … *idhā hum mublisūn*), 30:12 (the Hour), 43:75 (Hell), 30:49 (rain). **Shape (data-grounded):**
- **Sudden.** 6:44 *akhadhnāhum baghtatan fa-idhā hum mublisūn*; 23:77 *idhā hum fīhi mublisūn*.
- **Lasting.** 43:75 *lā yufattaru ʿanhum, wa-hum fīhi mublisūn*.
- **Not silence.** Khattab's "dumbstruck" (30:12) has no support in the Arabic. In 43:75-77 the `mublisūn` *speak*: *"O Mālik, let your Lord put an end to us!"* They have no exit, not no voice.

The sequence before it is diagnostic. 6:42-43 and 23:76 show a warning sent *la-ʿallahum yataḍarraʿūn* (so they might humble themselves) and the refusal, then abundance, rejoicing, seizure, `iblās`. **[Interpretive]** `Iblās` is the despair after the chances to turn went unused, which is why it clusters at the end. The text names no remedy for it, because the remedy (`taḍarruʿ`) was what was refused.

## 5. Q4: the distress words as a ladder

| Word | Who | Rescue verb |
|---|---|---|
| `whn` (weakening) | believers warned (3:139, 4:104, 47:35); the prophets' devotees praised for *not* weakening (3:146); Zechariah's bones (19:4) | none: it is a thing *not to do* |
| `ḍyq` (the breast or the earth narrows) | the Prophet (11:12, 15:97, 16:127, 27:70), Moses (26:13), Lot (11:77, 29:33), believers at Hunayn (9:25), the three left behind (9:118); deniers (6:125, 25:13) | 9:118 `tāba ʿalayhim`; 15:98 *glorify your Lord and be of those who prostrate*; 29:33 `munajjūka` |
| `ghamm` | the fleeing believers (3:153-154), Moses (20:40), Jonah (21:88); Hell (22:22) | `najjā` (20:40, 21:88); 3:154 `anzala amanatan` |
| `karb` | Noah (21:76, 37:76), Moses and Aaron with their people (37:115), the deniers saved in a storm (6:64) | `najjā`, 4 of 4 |

- **`Karb` and `ghamm` are always what one is saved *from*.** `Najjā` is the rescue verb in 6 of 7 cases; the odd one, 3:154, has security sent down.
- **`Ḍīq` is the widest word**: applied to prophets, believers and deniers, and to the earth itself (9:25, 9:118). **9:118** is the text's deepest description of a believer: the earth closes in, their souls close in, and they are *certain there is no refuge from God except in Him*. Then He turns to them.
- **No single bottom.** 12:110 puts the messengers' `yaʾs` at the edge, just before help. **[Interpretive]** The lowest *named* rung for believers is `ḍīq` at 9:118, where "no refuge except Him" is learned. Below it the road forks: `naṣr` for the messengers, `iblās` for those who refused `taḍarruʿ`.
- Prophets report these states to God in speech and are not blamed: Zechariah (19:4), Moses (26:13), Job and Jonah (21:83, 21:87).

## 6. Q5: grief versus despair

> **12:86** قَالَ إِنَّمَآ أَشۡكُواْ بَثِّي وَحُزۡنِيٓ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ وَأَعۡلَمُ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ مَا لَا تَعۡلَمُونَ
> S: *I only complain of my suffering and my grief to Allah, and I know from Allah that which you do not know.* K: *I complain of my anguish and sorrow only to Allah…*

> **12:87** يَٰبَنِيَّ ٱذۡهَبُواْ فَتَحَسَّسُواْ مِن يُوسُفَ وَأَخِيهِ وَلَا تَاْيۡـَٔسُواْ مِن رَّوۡحِ ٱللَّهِۖ إِنَّهُۥ لَا يَاْيۡـَٔسُ مِن رَّوۡحِ ٱللَّهِ إِلَّا ٱلۡقَوۡمُ ٱلۡكَٰفِرُونَ
> S: *Go and find out about Joseph and his brother and despair not of relief from Allah. Indeed, no one despairs of relief from Allah except the disbelieving people.*

**Data.** `Hzn` and `yʾs`/`qnṭ`/`bls` **never share a verse**. The nearest are 12:84-86 and 12:87, one speaker. Jacob's eyes whiten from `ḥuzn` he suppresses (`kaẓīm`, 12:84); he directs it at God (`ashkū baththī wa-ḥuznī ilā Llāh`); the next breath is a command to act (*go and search*) and a ban on `yaʾs`.

**The line [interpretive, from the data].** Grief is a *state spoken to God*. Despair, as the text defines it, is a *verdict about God's side* (*rawḥ*, mercy, the hereafter). The prohibition is preventive: it is said to those who have not yet despaired (12:87, 15:55, 39:53), and 39:53 says it exactly where despair is most reasonable, to those who have wronged themselves.

**Is grief praised?** Not as such. **9:92** comes closest: eyes overflowing *ḥazanan allā yajidū mā yunfiqūn*, set among those on whom there is no blame. It is excused and shown, not commended; 12:86 likewise. In 35:34 Paradise's people thank God for removing `ḥazan`, so grief is a burden, not a sin.

**Translator trap.** Saheeh renders 57:23 and 12:69 (`taʾsa`/`ibtaʾasa`, grief words) with "despair". The English blurs a line the Arabic keeps.

## 7. Q6: `lā tahinū wa-lā taḥzanū`

> **3:139** وَلَا تَهِنُواْ وَلَا تَحۡزَنُواْ وَأَنتُمُ ٱلۡأَعۡلَوۡنَ إِن كُنتُم مُّؤۡمِنِينَ
> S: *So do not weaken and do not grieve, and you will be superior if you are [true] believers.*

| Ref | Reason given |
|---|---|
| 3:139 (with 3:140) | You are `al-aʿlawn` *if you are believers*, and a wound like yours has touched the other side, since *We alternate these days among the people*. |
| 4:104 | The other side suffers as you suffer, **but you hope from God what they do not hope.** Same pain, different object. |
| 47:35 | *You are the superior, Allah is with you, and He will never deprive you of your deeds.* |

The reasons differ; none is "because the outcome is certain", and 3:139 is conditional. 3:146-147 adds the model: prophets' companions *did not weaken*, and said *forgive our sins … plant our feet firmly*. Weakening in 47:35 is to *call for peace*. `Wahn` is an act; `ḥuzn` is not.

## 8. Phase-0 corrections

1. **57:23 is not despair.** The verb is `taʾsawā` (grief, `Asw`), and Saheeh's "despair" produced the pairing "despair + exultation" cited in Phase 0 and in the regret synthesis. That pairing is genuine only for 11:9-10, 30:36 and 41:49-50. 57:23 belongs with grief.
2. **12:110 has no stated object** in the Arabic. "Of their people" is an interpretation.
3. **17:83's opposite state** is turning away, not exultation, and Saheeh's "disbeliever" is added (Arabic: `al-insān`).
4. **The 11:11 exception is unique** among the four reflex passages (§2).

## 9. Negative space

No verse tells anyone to feel hope in place of despair; the ban comes with a ground (39:53: God forgives all). Grief is never called a sin, and no verse says how long it lasts. `Faraj` is absent, so Jacob's relief word is `rawḥ`.

## For the one losing hope

- Say it to God, and say it as it is. Jacob did, in the plainest words: *I complain of my suffering and my grief to Allah* (12:86). Grief spoken to Him is not despair.
- Despair is a conclusion about *Him*, not a feeling about the situation. The text bans the conclusion (12:87) and never the pain.
- The ban is addressed even to those who have sinned most (39:53).
- Keep doing what is in your hands: *go and search* (12:87); patience *and* good work (11:11).
- If strength is going: God is with you (47:35), and the other side hurts too (4:104).
- The Quran does not say the wish will be granted. It says despair of Him is the wrong conclusion, and that help came at the edge for the messengers (12:110).
