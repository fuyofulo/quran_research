# The lifting: ʿusr/yusr, kashf, najāt, rawḥ, makhraj — and the word that is not there

## Headline finding

*Faraj* never occurs in the Quran. Its relief words are *yusr*, said to be **with** hardship (94:5-6) and **after** it (65:7); *kashf* (God removing harm, with no other remover: 6:17, 10:107); *najāt* (rescue, with a self-binding *ḥaqqan ʿalaynā*, 10:103); *makhraj* (65:2); and *rawḥ* (3 tokens). Ease is attached to conduct (taqwā, giving, reliance). The relieved often relapse. Relief is **never dated**: it is *near*, *with* or *after*, and the only durations given are of the waiting, told afterwards.

## 1. ʿUsr and yusr: the grammar (data-grounded)

`ʿsr` has 12 ayahs and `ysr` 40. They share six: 2:185, 2:280, 65:7, 92:10, 94:5, 94:6.

**94:5-6**
فَإِنَّ مَعَ ٱلْعُسْرِ يُسْرًا ۝ إِنَّ مَعَ ٱلْعُسْرِ يُسْرًا
*For indeed, with hardship will be ease. Indeed, with hardship will be ease.* (Saheeh)

- The sentence is repeated verbatim, once with *fa-* and once without.
- *ʿusr* takes the article both times (**al-ʿusr**). *yusr* is indefinite both times (**yusran**).
- The preposition is ***maʿa*** ("with, together with"), not *baʿda*.
- Khattab inserts "[more]" at 94:6. The Arabic has none.

**65:7**
سَيَجْعَلُ ٱللَّهُ بَعْدَ عُسْرٍ يُسْرًا
*God will bring about, after hardship, ease.*

- Here *ʿusr* is **indefinite** (*ʿusrin*), *yusr* is indefinite, and the preposition is ***baʿda***.

**What the text supports.** *al-ʿusr* (definite) and *yusr* (indefinite) in two identical sentences; *maʿa* in one place and *baʿda* in another. Ease is **God's making** in 65:7; in 94 it is stated with no agent.

**What is commentary.**
- The well-known "one hardship, two eases" argument is a tafsīr/grammatical rule. A repeated definite noun means the same referent, and a repeated indefinite means a new one. It is **outside the evidence base** here. The text does not say how many eases or hardships there are.
- Whether *al-ʿusr* means "this hardship" or "hardship as a class" the text does not settle.

**What the surrounding verses attach the ease to.**
- **94:1-8.** The frame is what God has already done: He expanded the breast, removed the burden, raised the repute (94:1-4). Then the pair, then **94:7 *fa-idhā faraghta fa-nṣab*** (when you finish, exert yourself), and **94:8 *wa-ilā rabbika fa-rghab*** (to your Lord direct your longing). So the ease is followed by an instruction to work and to direct desire to God. Neither is stated as a condition; the order is what the text gives.
- **65:2-7.** Three consecutive promises open with ***wa-man yattaqi llāh***. 65:2 gives "a way out" (*makhraj*). 65:3 gives provision from where he does not reckon, and adds *wa-man yatawakkal ʿalā llāh fa-huwa ḥasbuh* (whoever relies on God, He is enough for him). 65:4 gives "ease from his affair". 65:7 gives ease after hardship, to a man whose provision is restricted and who is told to spend from what he has been given. **In 65 the ease is tied to taqwā and reliance.**
- **92:5-10.** *We will ease him toward the easy* is for the one who gives, fears God and believes in the best; *toward the hard* for the one who withholds. 87:8 says it to the Prophet. In 92 ease is **a direction one is eased along**, not an event.
- **2:185.** *God wants ease for you and does not want hardship.* **74:9-10:** the Day is *ʿasīr, ghayru yasīr* for the deniers, so the pair appears negated. In 2:280 the creditor is told to wait for the debtor's ease.

## 2. Kashf al-ḍurr: who removes harm, and what follows (data-grounded)

`k$f` has 20 tokens, in 3 groups.
- **16 remove harm.** 6:17, 6:41, 7:134, 7:135, 10:12, 10:98, 10:107, 16:54, 17:56, 21:84, 23:75, 27:62, 39:38, 43:50, 44:12, 44:15.
- **3 uncover.** 27:44, 50:22, 68:42.
- **1 is ambiguous.** 53:58.
- **God is the only affirmed remover.** The negated removers are the idols, who have no power to remove adversity (17:56, 39:38). 6:17 and 10:107 each say that if God touches you with harm, **no one removes it but Him**.
  - 10:107 adds: *if He wills good for you, none can repel His bounty* (*rādd li-faḍlih*), and closes on *al-Ghafūr al-Raḥīm*.
  - 6:17 closes on His power over all things.
- **27:62** asks: ***amman yujību l-muḍṭarra idhā daʿāhu wa-yakshifu l-sūʾ***: *who answers the one in need/desperate when he calls and removes the evil?* The verse names no condition on the one called. It is an exclusivity claim, framed as a question expecting "God".
- **6:41 qualifies it**: *He removes that for which you call on Him, **if He wills*** (*in shāʾa*). So the Quran states both: removal is God's alone (27:62), and removal is not stated as automatic (6:41).

**After the lifting.** Of the 16, the relapse cases (6 of 16) are 7:135, 10:12, 16:54, 23:75, 43:50 and 44:15.
- *At once they broke their word* (7:135, 43:50); *as if he had never called Us* (10:12); *a party associates others* (16:54, 30:33). 39:8 and 39:49 show it with *niʿma*; 17:67 and 29:65 at sea.
- **The exceptions are narrated.**
  - **21:84**: Job calls (21:83 states the harm and *you are the most merciful*, without asking for anything), and God answers, removes it, restores the family and a like number with it, ***raḥmatan min ʿindinā wa-dhikrā li-l-ʿābidīn***.
  - **10:98**: of all the cities, only Jonah's people believed and benefited, and the punishment was removed.
- **[interpretive]** The relapse verses warn about the one lifted; the harm is lifted in each.

## 3. Najāt: how strong, and what is it? (data-grounded)

`njw` has 84 tokens. **18 are not rescue** (*najwā*, secret talk, and *najiyy*, confidant: e.g. 58:7-13, 12:80, 19:52). **66 are rescue**, across 61 ayahs.

**The language.**
- **21:88**: وَكَذَٰلِكَ نُـۨجِي ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ — *and thus do We rescue the believers.*
- **10:103**: ثُمَّ نُنَجِّي رُسُلَنَا وَٱلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كَذَٰلِكَ **حَقًّا عَلَيْنَا** نُنجِ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ — *then We save Our messengers and those who believed; thus, it is binding upon Us to save the believers.*
- *Ḥaqqan ʿalaynā* occurs in only two ayahs (10:103, and 30:47 for *naṣr al-muʾminīn*). In 21:88 the imperfect with *kadhālika* reads as a pattern, not a one-off.
- The object is "the believers" as a class. No timing is given.

**What the rescue consists of.**
- It is mostly **removal from**, with the danger falling on others: in roughly 38 of 61 ayahs the rescue sits within two ayahs of punishment of the rest (crude keyword window). Examples:
  - Noah: *We saved him... and drowned the rest* (26:119-120, 7:64).
  - Lot's family *before dawn* (54:34), **except his wife** (7:83, 29:32-33, 15:60, 37:135).
  - Israel (26:65-66).
  - Hud, Ṣāliḥ, Shuʿayb *by mercy from Us* (11:58, 66, 94).
- **Danger removed in place** is rarer: Abraham's fire (21:69 *be cool and safe*; called *najāt* at 29:24) and the *ghamm* of Jonah (21:88) and Moses (20:40, which Saheeh renders "retaliation").
- Kinship does not guarantee it: Noah's and Lot's wives (66:10).
- **66:11**, Pharaoh's wife: *build for me a house near You in Paradise and save me from Pharaoh.* No outcome is narrated. Prayers for *najāt*: 10:86, 26:118, 26:169, 28:21, 66:11.
- 19:72, 39:61, 40:41 and 61:10 place *najāt* in the hereafter.
- **[interpretive]** 10:103 promises **rescue**, not freedom from trial (2:214).

## 4. The relief vocabulary as a whole (data-grounded)

| Word | What it lifts | Who receives | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| *yusr* (94:5-6, 65:4, 65:7) | *ʿusr* | the Prophet (94); *man yattaqi* (65); the creditor-debtor case (2:280) | promised |
| *yassara* (20:26, 87:8, 92:7) | difficulty | Moses; the Prophet; the giver | **prayed** (20:26), promised |
| *kashf* (16 tokens) | *ḍurr*, *sūʾ*, *ʿadhāb* | Job; Jonah's people; deniers (7:135, 44:15); "the desperate" (27:62) | mostly narrated; prayed 7:134, 44:12; asserted 6:17, 10:107, 27:62 |
| *najāt* (66 tokens) | being in danger | prophets and "those with them"; believers | narrated most; promised 10:103, 21:88; **prayed** 5 times |
| *makhraj* (65:2, once) | an impasse | *man yattaqi llāh* | promised |
| *rawḥ* (12:87 ×2, 56:89) | despair; death | Jacob's sons (commanded); the near-ones (56:88) | commanded / promised |
| *naṣr* / *fatḥ qarīb* (2:214, 61:13, 48:18, 48:27) | being overpowered | the believers | said to be near |

**Nearness.** `qarīb` has 26 tokens; the rest concern the Hour, punishment or travel. God is *near* (2:186, 11:61, 34:50), His mercy is *near to the doers of good* (7:56), and help/conquest is near (2:214, 61:13, 48:18, 48:27). 2:186 pairs it with *I answer the call of the caller when he calls*, and a request: *so let them respond to Me and believe in Me.*

**The absence of *faraj*** (verified). Scanning normalized Arabic for فرج (tanwīn rejoined) gives 8 string hits, none relief. Five are other words (2:239, 2:282, 20:40, 20:86, 21:64). Three are *farj* (21:91, 66:12, *furijat* 77:9).

**What "relief after hardship" maps to.** **Baʿda ʿusrin yusran (65:7)**, *maʿa l-ʿusri yusran* (94:5-6), *makhraj* (65:2) and *kashf* (the lifting of *ḍurr*). Translators have supplied "relief" for *rawḥ* (Saheeh at 12:87) and "relief" for *kashf* (Khattab at 27:62). The word "relief" is the translators', not the text's.

## 5. Rawḥ (12:87 ×2, 56:89) (data-grounded, then [interpretive])

- The root `rwḥ` has 57 tokens: *rīḥ* (wind) 29, *rūḥ* (spirit) 21, *rawḥ* 3, *rayḥān* 2, *rawāḥ* 1 (34:12, an evening journey), *turīḥūn* 1 (16:6, bringing cattle home in the evening).
- **Divergence** at 12:87 / 56:89 (0.657 / 0.694): Saheeh "relief" / "rest"; Khattab "mercy" / "serenity"; Pickthall "the Spirit of Allah" / "breath of life"; Arberry "comfort" / "repose".
- **12:87** is Jacob's speech: *go and search out Joseph... and do not despair of God's rawḥ; none despairs of it but the faithless.* It comes with a command to go and search. The object of *yaʾs* is *rawḥ* here, as it is *raḥma* at 15:56 and 39:53. The text lets the two stand in the same slot. It does not say they mean the same.
- **56:89** is promised at death to those brought near: *fa-rawḥun wa-rayḥānun wa-jannatu naʿīm*.
- **[interpretive]** The shared root with wind and breath makes "a breath, room to breathe" a plausible image. It is a lexical association, not a definition the Quran gives. The verses do not unpack *rawḥ*. The safe statement: it is what Jacob forbids despair of, while himself still grieving (12:84-86).

## 6. The time of relief (data-grounded)

Across the ease, kashf and najāt verses, relief is **never given a date**.
- **Prepositions, not dates**: *maʿa* (94), *baʿda* (65:7), *qarīb* (2:214, 61:13). "When is God's help?" (2:214) is answered with nearness, no date. Parallel "when?" questions about the Hour get "perhaps near" (17:51, 33:63) and "I do not know whether near or far" (72:25).
- **Retrospective durations** of the wait only: Noah *a thousand years less fifty* (29:14), Joseph *several years* (12:42), Moses *years in Madyan... then you came at the decreed time (ʿalā qadar)* (20:40). Cf. 65:3 *qadran*.
- **Dated only inside punishment stories**: leave *by night* (11:81, 15:65), rescued *before dawn* (54:34). Removal can carry a term: 7:135 *until a term*; 44:15 *for a little*.
- ***Ḥattā idhā*** (12:110, 2:214) places help after despair has nearly arrived, with no schedule.

## For the one losing hope

- The Quran promises no end-date and no granted wish. It does say ease is **with** hardship (94:5-6) and **after** it (65:7), and that it is God's to make.
- Say what Jacob said: bring the grief to God (12:86), and do not despair of His *rawḥ* (12:87). Job's prayer (21:83) only states the harm and who God is.
- Do what 65 and 94 attach the ease to: *taqwā*, reliance, and after finishing a task, work again and direct your longing to Him (94:7-8). The text gives the order. It does not make them a formula.
- When the weight lifts, do not be the one who forgets he called (10:12).
- If relief is slow, the text has no date, only *near*; even messengers asked *when* (2:214).
