# The hope words: rajā, ʿasā, ṭamaʿ, raghba, amal

## Headline finding

The hope words divide by *who speaks*. **`rajā` is never first person (0 of 23)**: it describes others, anchored in God's side and in action. **`ʿasā` ("perhaps") is how people voice hope aloud** in hard moments, and in 6 of 7 such quotes it names *my/our/your Lord*. **`ṭamaʿ` and `raghba` are directions**: toward forgiveness, toward God. `amal` is the long hope, faulted at 15:3 and redeemed at 18:46 by its object. The seven prophet-and-believer `ʿasā` sentences follow patience, prayer or confession, and ask for the lost thing in only two; five ask for guidance or an unnamed "better".

## 1. Who, for what, in what grammar (data-grounded)

| Word | Form | Who | For what |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rajā` (`rjw`) | 22 verb + 1 participle; 12 affirmed, 11 negated | third person or "you"; **no first-person form** | God's side (below); 29:36 is the lone imperative |
| `ʿasā` | fixed verb-particle + `an` + subjunctive; 28 ayahs | first person in 7 quoted speeches; God's own voice in 8 more | worldly outcomes: guidance, a lost person, an enemy gone, "better" |
| `ṭamaʿ` | 8 verbs, 4 nouns | **all 3 first-person verbs are praised** (5:84, 26:51, 26:82); the 3 faulted are third person | forgiveness or entry to the Garden; faulted: greed or "Garden without deeds" |
| `raghba` (`rgb`) | 4 verbs, 3 participles, 1 noun | humans speaking or addressed | **direction depends on the preposition** |
| `amal` (`ʾml`) | noun, 2 tokens | the heedless (15:3); anyone (18:46) | the long worldly hope vs the lasting good deed |

**`raghba` is the only one whose preposition matters.** Toward God it appears 4 times: 21:90 (*raghaban*), 9:59 and 68:32 (*rāghibūn*), and the command **94:8 *fa-rghab*** (*to your Lord direct your longing*). It also means aversion *ʿan*: 2:130, 9:120, 19:46. At 4:127 it is desire for orphan women.

**`ṭamaʿ`.** The noun appears only in *khawf wa-ṭamaʿ*, 4 of 4: 7:56, 13:12, 30:24, 32:16. Its verb praises the first person (Abraham *aṭmaʿu an yaghfira lī*, 26:82) and faults the third: 33:32, 70:38 (*does every one of them aspire to a Garden of delight?*), 74:15.

**`amal`: one noun, two verdicts.** 15:3: *let them eat and enjoy, and hope (`al-amal`) distracts them.* 18:46: *wealth and children are the adornment of this life, but the lasting good deeds are better in reward and `khayrun amalan`* (better in hope). The word is the same; what the hope is fastened to is what changes.

## 2. Testing the Phase-0 `rajā` claim

I re-read all 23 tokens against the census.

**Result: "19 of 23 on God's side" is right by the census's definition, but needs three refinements.**

- The 19 are: 2:218, 4:104 (×2), 10:7, 10:11, 10:15, 17:28, 17:57, 18:110, 25:21, 25:40, 29:5, 29:36, 33:21, 39:9, 45:14, 60:6, 71:13, 78:27.
- The four others are **11:62** (marjuww), **24:60**, **28:86** and **35:29**.
  - 11:62 is *you were a man of promise among us*: Ṣāliḥ is the hoped-for one, not the hoper's object.
  - 24:60 is marriage, the only truly worldly object.
  - 28:86 and 35:29 are God-given anyway: *a mercy from your Lord*; *a trade that will not perish*.
  - So genuinely worldly hope with `rajā` is **1 verse (24:60)**, plus 11:62 in a passive.
- **71:13 sits in the 19 on a disputed reading** (section 6). Without it: 18 of 22.
- **"Those who do not hope for the meeting with Us", described as deniers 7×, overstates it.** The phrase *liqāʾanā* is **4×** (10:7, 10:11, 10:15, 25:21). The other negations: resurrection (25:40), a reckoning (78:27), *the days of God* (45:14).
  - 45:14 is said of people believers should *forgive*; no denial label is attached.
  - **Of the 11 negations, two are not a fault**: 24:60 (no blame) and 28:86 (not hoping, yet given).

**Hope-sense verses without any of the five words** (verified by root search):

- **`ẓann` with `mulāqī`**: 2:46, 2:249 and 69:20 (*I was certain I would meet my account*). It is the mirror of the negated `rajā`: the same object, the meeting, held rather than denied.
- **`laʿalla`** ("so that perhaps"): 123 tokens, mostly God's purposes. Human first-person uses seen (scan not exhaustive): 12:46, 20:10, 28:29, 23:100, 40:36: a torch from a fire, a return, a tower.
- **`tarqub`** is not hope: `rqb` verbs mean watch, heed or await (11:93, 20:94, 44:10). **28:18 and 28:21 *yataraqqabu* is Moses "fearful, anticipating"**, which is expectation coloured by fear, and 28:21 sits directly before the `ʿasā` of 28:22.

## 3. Who never hopes: the profile (data-grounded)

Negated `rajā`: 10:7, 10:11, 10:15, 25:21, 25:40, 45:14, 78:27, 71:13. What else the text says of the same people:

| Ref | Co-description |
|---|---|
| 10:7 | *satisfied (`raḍū`) with the life of this world, secure (`iṭmaʾannū`) in it, heedless of Our signs* |
| 10:11 | *in their transgression, wandering blindly*; they hasten for harm, in the same ayah |
| 10:15 | *when clear verses are recited, they say: bring another Quran or change it* |
| 25:21 | demand angels or to see the Lord; *arrogant within themselves, great insolence* |
| 25:40 | passed the town rained on with evil, *have they not seen it?* |
| 78:27-28 | *they denied Our verses with denial* |

**Profile:** the one who does not hope is **content, safe, heedless, demanding and arrogant**. Nothing in these ayahs describes sadness, exhaustion or pain. **[interpretive]** The Quran's "no hope" is a settled satisfaction, not a wounded heart; a person who is hurting, still calling and still asking is the opposite of this profile. Despair is another file's subject.

## 4. The seven `ʿasā` sentences (data-grounded, then [interpretive])

> فَصَبۡرࣱ جَمِيلٌۖ عَسَى ٱللَّهُ أَن يَأۡتِيَنِي بِهِمۡ جَمِيعًا [12:83]
> *Saheeh:* So patience is most fitting. Perhaps Allah will bring them to me all together.
> *Khattab:* So (I am left with nothing but) beautiful patience! I trust Allah will return them all to me.

> عَسَىٰ رَبُّنَآ أَن يُبۡدِلَنَا خَيۡرࣰا مِّنۡهَآ إِنَّآ إِلَىٰ رَبِّنَا رَٰغِبُونَ [68:32]
> *Saheeh:* Perhaps our Lord will substitute for us one better than it. Indeed, we are toward our Lord desirous.
> *Pickthall:* ...Lo! we beseech our Lord.

**Grammar they share.**

- `ʿasā` + subject + `an` + subjunctive. The subject is God in six; at 19:48 the speaker is the subject: *ʿasā allā akūna ... shaqiyyan*.
- **Six of seven say *rabb* ("my/our/your Lord")**, not *Allāh*. Only 12:83 says *Allāh*. 28:22, 18:40, 19:48 and 18:24 say *rabbī*; 68:32 says *rabbunā*; 7:129 says *rabbukum*.
- The verb is an act of God done *to the speaker* (bring, guide, give, substitute); five carry *-nī* or *-nā*.

**What comes right before** (all verified in the ayahs around them):

| Ref | Hardship | Act just before |
|---|---|---|
| 12:83 | two sons lost, a third taken | *fa-ṣabrun jamīl* in the same breath |
| 28:22 | killed a man, fled, *fearful, anticipating* (28:21) | *my Lord, save me from the wrongdoing people* |
| 7:129 | oppressed, harmed before and after | Moses's 7:128: *seek help through God and be patient* |
| 18:24 | the Prophet told to say "if God wills" | *remember your Lord when you forget* |
| 18:40 | outranked in wealth and children | 18:38: *He is Allah, my Lord* |
| 19:48 | threatened with stoning and exile by his father | leaving them and calling on his Lord |
| 68:32 | garden destroyed | confession and exalting: *Exalted is our Lord, we were wrongdoers* (68:29-31) |

**[interpretive]** Hope is never the first move. In all seven it comes after a patience, a prayer, a confession or an act of leaving. None of these sentences is a complaint.

**What they ask for.** *Specific thing*: 12:83 (the lost sons), 7:129 (enemy destroyed, succession). *Guidance*: 28:22, 18:24. *"Better"*: 18:40, 68:32, and neither names it. *Not to be wretched in calling*: 19:48. So two of seven ask for the lost thing itself; **five do not name the thing**.

**Does the text show the answer?** 19:49 follows at once (*We gave him Isaac and Jacob*); 28:23 takes Moses to the well of Madyan. For 68:32 the next ayah (68:33) is a warning, not a gift; for 18:40 the narration reports the *rival's* ruin (18:42) and no gift to the speaker; 7:130 turns to Pharaoh's people's seizing. **The text does not report the "better" in 68:32 or 18:40.** 12:83 is followed at 12:87 by *do not despair of the relief of God* (reunion falls later in the sūrah and I did not re-check it here).

**Translator note.** Khattab renders `ʿasā` as **"I trust"** at 12:83, 28:22, 18:24, 19:48 and 68:32, turning "perhaps" into confidence. Saheeh and Arberry keep "perhaps / haply".

## 5. Hope paired with fear (data-grounded)

| Ref | Order | Actions around it |
|---|---|---|
| 7:56 | fear, hope | *do not corrupt the earth after its reform*; mercy is *near to the doers of good* |
| 32:16 | fear, hope | leave beds, call on their Lord, spend |
| 13:12, 30:24 | fear, hope | lightning; then clouds / rain that revives dead earth |
| 21:90 | hope, fear (*raghaban wa-rahaban*) | hastening to good deeds, calling, humble submission; follows Zechariah's answer |
| 17:57 | hope, fear | those invoked seek nearness to their Lord, each trying to be nearest |
| 39:9 | fear, hope | devout in the night, prostrating and standing |

**Order.** The `ṭamaʿ` pairings are fixed, **fear then hope, 4 of 4**. With `rajā` and `raghba` the order flips (17:57, 21:90 hope first; 39:9 fear first). 
**Actions.** All six come with doing: avoiding corruption, calling, spending, praying at night, hastening, or seeking nearness. The lightning pair is the exception: there fear-and-hope is an *experience* the sign produces, then the rain comes (30:24). **[interpretive]** Hope rides with action; it does not replace it.

## 6. Translator divergence (data-grounded)

**71:13** (*mā lakum lā tarjūna li-llāhi waqārā*; combined 0.732; lexical disagreement saturated):

- Pickthall: *hope not toward Allah for dignity*.
- Arberry: *look not for majesty in God*.
- Saheeh: *do not attribute to Allah due grandeur*.
- Khattab: *are not in awe of the Majesty of Allah*.

The split: `rajā` as **expecting or hoping** (Pickthall, Arberry) vs **ascribing or fearing** (Saheeh, Khattab). I count the verse apart and rest nothing on it.

**53:24** (*am lil-insāni mā tamannā*; 0.740; Khattab is the outlier at jaccard 0.158):

- Saheeh: *whatever he wishes*; Pickthall: *what he coveteth*; Arberry: *whatever he fancies*.
- Khattab: *whatever **intercessors** they desire*, importing a bracket from the context.

The Arabic gives `mā tamannā`: "whatever he wishes". **The split is between a bare wish and a wish with an assumed object.** Whatever the resolution, the verse denies that wishing guarantees receiving. It uses `tamannā`, not one of the five hope words.

## Negative space

- No one says "I hope for His mercy" with `rajā`; that is voiced with `ṭamaʿ` or `ʿasā`.
- No hope word is paired with a promise that a *specific* wish comes true.

## For the one losing hope

1. **Hope is not the first thing the prophets do.** Before "perhaps" come patience, asking, confessing, and leaving. Start there; the "perhaps" follows.
2. **A usable sentence:** *`ʿasā rabbī an yahdiyanī`* (perhaps my Lord will guide me, 28:22), said in flight. It asks for guidance, not a result.
3. **Ask "better" without naming it** (18:40, 68:32). The text shows people doing this; it does not say the "better" always arrives in that form.
4. **Turn the longing toward God**: *fa-rghab* (94:8). The same root is used by the people who lost a garden (68:32).
5. **Hope may be spoken with fear** (7:56); neither cancels the other.
6. **Not hoping is described as contentment and arrogance, not as pain.** A person who is hurting and still turning to God is not in that portrait.
