# When the wish is not given

## Headline finding

**No one in the Quran is rebuked for asking long, and no one is told to stop wanting.** Of 13 named asks, 9 are granted, one narrowed, three refused. Rebukes target *what* is asked (11:46, 2:200, 2:61), never how long. *"Perhaps you love a thing and it is bad for you"* (2:216, 4:19) speaks of things commanded or present; applying it to a withheld wish is analogy. Hearts are God's to join: in 3 of 4 *allafa*-heart tokens God joins, and the fourth is a human who could not. No prayer asks God to remove a love.

---

## 1. May they keep asking for the specific thing? (data-grounded)

**Named asks and what met them:**
- **Granted (9):** an heir (Zechariah, 19:5-6 → 21:90); a righteous child (Abraham, 37:100 → 37:101); a good child (7:189; the later shirk is faulted, not the asking); fruits for Mecca (2:126, *widened* to disbelievers); ease and Aaron (20:25-32 → *you have been granted your request*, 20:36); water (2:60); a kingdom (38:35); a table (5:114); the Israelites' onions and lentils (2:61).
- **Narrowed (1):** Abraham, leadership for his descendants (2:124): *not the wrongdoers*.
- **Refused (3):** Noah's son (11:45-46); Abraham's father's forgiveness (9:114, 60:4); Moses's *show me Yourself* (7:143), followed by *take what I have given you and be among the grateful* (7:144).

**Rebukes concern the object, and give a reason:**
- asking against a ruling God has made known: *ask Me not of what you have no knowledge* (11:46); *even if they were relatives* (9:113);
- asking for this world *alone* (2:200: *no share in the Hereafter*). 2:201 asks *ḥasana* in both worlds and is the model;
- preferring the lesser: *would you exchange the better for the less?* (2:61, Moses, and granted anyway);
- demanding to see God (4:153).

**No rebuke concerns the length or repetition of asking.** 17:11 (*man prays for evil as for good; man is ever hasty*) describes the asker, not God's answer or its timing.

**Duration.** The text never says Zechariah asked "for years". It gives old age and a barren wife, and a *history* of asking:

> **19:4** قَالَ رَبِّ إِنِّي وَهَنَ ٱلۡعَظۡمُ مِنِّي وَٱشۡتَعَلَ ٱلرَّأۡسُ شَيۡبࣰا وَلَمۡ أَكُنۢ بِدُعَآئِكَ رَبِّ شَقِيࣰّا
> Saheeh: "…and never have I been in my supplication to You, my Lord, unhappy." Khattab: "…but I have never been disappointed in my prayer to You."

- **His ground for asking again** is that past asking never left him *shaqiyy*, wretched or disappointed.
- **When Zechariah asked.** It was *hunālika*, "there" (3:38): right after he found Maryam's provision and heard *God provides for whom He wills without account* (3:37). **[interpretive]** Seeing God give to someone else, he asked for his own. This is 4:32 acted out.
- **21:89 pairs the thing with God's sufficiency**: *rabbi lā tadharnī fardan wa-anta khayru l-wārithīn*, "do not leave me alone, and You are the best of inheritors".

**[interpretive]** So: yes, by name.

## 2. What the text says a person cannot see

**Data-grounded.** The *ʿasā an tuḥibbū shayʾan wa-huwa sharrun lakum* formula occurs once (2:216), and it concerns a **command** (fighting, *kutiba*). 4:19 concerns a **present** spouse; 24:11 (*do not think it bad for you*) a harm that **happened**; 18:79-81 **losses** (the ship, the boy). Only three passages concern something **withheld**:
- **18:82**: the orphans' treasure, kept *until they come of age, as a mercy from your Lord*. They never learn it.
- **28:79-82**: Qārūn's fortune, wished for and not given. Next day the wishers call it grace: *had God not favoured us, He would have swallowed us*. The wish was for another's portion (cf. 4:32; 20:131).
- **42:27**: provision held *in measure*, lest *they transgress*. Said of "His servants" in general.

**None says of a believer's own unanswered prayer that it was withheld because it would harm.** Applying "perhaps it is bad for you" to one's withheld wish is analogy: reasonable, but analogy. **[interpretive]** The Kahf reasons are told to Moses, never to those they concern.

## 3. Hearts are God's to join

**Data-grounded.**
- **Joining hearts (`Alf`, *allafa*).** There are 4 heart-tokens (3:103; 8:63 three times). God is the subject in 3. The one human subject is negated: *mā allafta*. (24:43 is clouds.)
- **Affection placed.** God is the agent in all three cases: 30:21 (*jaʿala baynakum mawaddatan wa-raḥma*), 60:7 (*ʿasā*), and 19:96 (*sa-yajʿalu lahumu r-raḥmānu wuddā*).
- **Also:** *God comes between a man and his heart* (8:24); *you do not guide whom you love* (28:56).

> **8:63** وَأَلَّفَ بَيۡنَ قُلُوبِهِمۡۚ لَوۡ أَنفَقۡتَ مَا فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ جَمِيعࣰا مَّآ أَلَّفۡتَ بَيۡنَ قُلُوبِهِمۡ وَلَٰكِنَّ ٱللَّهَ أَلَّفَ بَيۡنَهُمۡۚ إِنَّهُۥ عَزِيزٌ حَكِيمࣱ
> Saheeh: "If you had spent all that is in the earth, you could not have brought their hearts together; but Allah brought them together." Khattab: "…could not have united their hearts."

**Human means exist, framed as given.**
- *Repel with what is better*, and the enemy becomes *as though* a devoted friend (41:34). But *none is granted it except the patient* (41:35).
- *If they both desire reconciliation, God will bring about agreement between them* (4:35).
- **Force is ruled out:** no inheriting women *karhan* (4:19); remarriage *if they agree between themselves* (2:232); *would you compel people?* (10:99).

**What it gives the asker.** One prayer asks God about others' hearts: Abraham's *make hearts among people incline toward them* (14:37). Two verses speak to a bond ended or not begun:
- *if they separate, God will enrich each from His abundance* (4:130);
- *let those who find no means to marry keep chaste until God enriches them from His bounty* (24:33). The *ḥattā* is open, with no date.

**[interpretive]** Conduct and asking are in a person's hands. Another's heart is God's to join. Ask Him for affection and for hearts to incline. The text gives no warrant for forcing a heart, by pressure, by money (8:63) or by insistence.

## 4. Holding on and letting go (data-grounded)

**Jacob (12:83-96).**
- **Says:** *beautiful patience; perhaps God will bring them all* (12:83); *my sorrow over Joseph* (12:84); *do not despair of God's rawḥ* (12:87).
- **Does:** holds his grief in (*kaẓīm*), and sends his sons to search.
- **Told to stop:** only by his sons (*you will not cease remembering Joseph until you perish*, 12:85; *your old error*, 12:95). Never by God or the narrator.
- **Ending:** *Did I not tell you I know from God what you do not know?* (12:96).

> **12:86** قَالَ إِنَّمَآ أَشۡكُواْ بَثِّي وَحُزۡنِيٓ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ وَأَعۡلَمُ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ مَا لَا تَعۡلَمُونَ
> Saheeh: "I only complain of my suffering and my grief to Allah, and I know from Allah that which you do not know." Khattab: "I complain of my anguish and sorrow only to Allah."

**Moses's mother (28:7-13).**
- **Says:** nothing to God is recorded; to his sister, *follow him* (28:11).
- **Does:** casts him into the river as told. Her heart was "empty", and she nearly disclosed him until God *bound her heart* (28:10).
- **Told:** *do not fear, do not grieve; We will return him* (28:7). She is not told to stop wanting him.
- **Ending:** returned *so her eye be cooled* (28:13).

**Abraham (37:100-107).**
- **He asks:** *grant me one of the righteous* (37:100).
- **Then:** a vision, *see what you think* (37:102). Both submit (37:103).
- **Ending:** *you have fulfilled the vision… the clear trial*, and the son is ransomed (37:105-107). He is not told to stop loving.

**Findings.**
- **None of the three is told to stop wanting.** The only "stop" in the text comes from Jacob's sons, and the story proves them wrong.
- **Each letting-go answers a specific command**: a revelation with a promise (28:7), or a vision (37:102). **[interpretive]** Neither is a general rule to release a wish. Even letting go, the mother had her son followed.

**Negative space.** **No prayer in the Quran asks God to take a love out of the heart.** I searched every prayer (*rabbi/rabbanā/allāhumma*) naming a heart, a breast or love. It found: hearts kept from swerving after guidance (3:8), no rancour toward believers (59:10), an expanded breast (20:25), a heart at rest (2:260), others' hearts inclined (14:37) or hardened (10:88).

Near, but not that prayer:
- **12:33.** Joseph asks that *their plan* be turned away, *or I will incline to them*. The occasion is averted, not a love removed, and the inclination is to a forbidden act.
- **7:43, 15:47.** God removes *ghill* (rancour), not love, in Paradise.
- **9:24.** It ranks loves and empties none.
- **38:32-33** (Solomon): translators split completely. I build nothing on it.

## 5. What to ask when the thing is uncertain

> **28:24** فَسَقَىٰ لَهُمَا ثُمَّ تَوَلَّىٰٓ إِلَى ٱلظِّلِّ فَقَالَ رَبِّ إِنِّي لِمَآ أَنزَلۡتَ إِلَيَّ مِنۡ خَيۡرࣲ فَقِيرࣱ
> Saheeh: "My Lord, indeed I am, for whatever good You would send down to me, in need." Khattab: "…in [desperate] need of whatever provision You may have in store for me."

*Anzalta* is perfect in form. All four translators render it future or present (divergence 0.589). **[interpretive]** The Arabic also allows *whatever good You have sent down to me*. Moses names nothing. What follows (28:25-28) is an invitation, *you have escaped*, work, and a marriage.

**A small repertoire, all from the text:**

**For the thing**
- Name it, with its quality: *grant me from Yourself good offspring* (3:38); *make him, my Lord, pleasing* (19:6).
- Name it, and name God as enough: *do not leave me alone, and You are the best of inheritors* (21:89).
- Turn from another's share to His bounty: *ask God of His bounty* (4:32).
- *Our Lord, give us good in this world and good in the Hereafter* (2:201).
- Hope it as "perhaps", toward Him: *perhaps our Lord will give us better than it; to our Lord we turn in longing* (68:32).

**For oneself**
- *Our Lord, grant us mercy from Yourself and set right our affair for us* (18:10).
- *Perhaps my Lord will guide me nearer than this to what is right* (18:24).
- *My Lord, I am in need of whatever good You send down to me* (28:24).
- *Pour patience upon us* (2:250); *do not burden us with what we cannot bear* (2:286).

**For the heart**
- *Expand my breast* (20:25); *that my heart may be at rest* (2:260), asked and granted.
- *Do not let our hearts swerve after You guided us* (3:8).
- *Put no rancour in our hearts* (59:10). **[interpretive]** This is the prayer for when a door opened for others.
- Say the pain without a demand: *I complain of my anguish and grief only to God* (12:86); *harm has touched me, and You are the most merciful of the merciful* (21:83).
- For what is lost: *we belong to God, and to Him we return* (2:156).
- Remembrance, by which *hearts are at rest* (13:28); *to your Lord turn your longing* (94:8). 64:11 states God's act: *whoever believes, He guides his heart*.

## 6. What the text does not promise

- **The specific thing.** Named promises go to named people in the stories (28:7, 3:39). The general promises name no content: *I respond* (2:186, 40:60); provision *from where he does not expect* (65:3); ease *with* hardship (94:5-6). One is qualified: He *would remove that for which you invoked Him, if He willed* (6:41).
- **A timeline.** None is given. *God has set for everything a measure* (65:3); *for every term a decree* (13:38). Moses *came at a decreed time* (20:40), and that is said after "years".
- **That "better" will be visible.** The "better than it" sentences never show it arriving. Neither the parents (18:81) nor the orphans (18:82) are told.
- **That unanswered means unloved.** The text names this inference and rejects it:

> **89:16** وَأَمَّآ إِذَا مَا ٱبۡتَلَىٰهُ فَقَدَرَ عَلَيۡهِ رِزۡقَهُۥ فَيَقُولُ رَبِّيٓ أَهَٰنَنِ
> Saheeh: "But when He tries him and restricts his provision, he says, 'My Lord has humiliated me.'" Khattab: "…'My Lord has [undeservedly] humiliated me!'"

  - **89:17 opens *kallā*, "No!"** It rejects both readings: that wealth is honour (89:15), and that restriction is disgrace. Both are *ibtilāʾ*.
  - 93:3 denies *abandoned, detested*.
  - The messenger's *when is God's help?* (2:214) is answered, not rebuked.
  - Love attaches to the response: *God loves the patient* (3:146) and *those who rely* (3:159).
- **"If it's meant for you it will come"** is not in the Quran. Its nearest verses (9:51, 57:22-23) concern what *befalls* or *has passed*, not what is wished. Istikhāra is a hadith practice, outside this evidence.

## 7. Corrections

- **CORRECTED** (`narrative-wishes-and-answers.md` §6), *"they ask for the good, not the specific form (28:24)"*: naming the thing is the more frequent pattern (§1), mostly granted. Open-handed asking (28:24, 2:201) is a second pattern.
- **CORRECTED** (`narrative-wishes-and-answers.md` §6), on 25:74: *hab lanā min azwājinā wa-dhurriyyātinā* ("from our spouses and offspring") presupposes them and asks comfort *in* them. Khattab's *"Bless us with [pious] spouses"* makes it a prayer for spouses (divergence 0.685). It is no model for a spouse not yet given.
- **CORRECTED** (`narrative-wishes-and-answers.md` §4), *"Each says a person cannot see… from inside the loss or the want"*: 2:216 is a command, 4:19 a present spouse, and 18:79-81 are losses. Only 18:82, 28:79-82 and 42:27 concern a withheld thing, and none applies "perhaps it is bad for you" to a believer's unanswered prayer.
- **CORRECTED** (`narrative-wishes-and-answers.md` §3), *"not every unanswered or slow prayer is a refusal: haste asks for what would harm"*: 17:11 is silent on God's answer or delay, and 10:11 concerns deniers. The inference is [interpretive].
- **CORRECTED** (`narrative-wishes-and-answers.md`, For the one losing hope), *"The text rebukes wishing for another's share, not asking God for yours"*: 2:61 rebukes a named worldly ask (and grants it), and 2:200 faults asking for this world *alone*.
- **CORRECTED** (`narrative-those-who-waited.md` §3), *"The only refused request is Noah's"*: it holds only within the group. 9:114 and 7:143 are refused, and 2:124 is narrowed. None is rebuked for persistence.

## For the one losing hope

- You may keep asking for the thing, by name. No one in the Quran is rebuked for how long they asked; Zechariah's reason to ask again was that asking had never left him wretched (19:4).
- Ask as he did: *do not leave me alone, and You are the best of inheritors* (21:89). Name the thing, and name God as enough.
- If it concerns another person's heart, God joins hearts (8:63). Ask Him to incline them (14:37). Do not try to force them.
- You are not told to stop wanting. Jacob's sons told him to stop. He took his grief to God instead (12:86) and kept sending people to search.
- Ask for yourself too: mercy and a right course in your affair (18:10), patience poured on you (2:250), a breast expanded (20:25).
- The text does not promise the thing, or a date. It does deny that being kept short means being disgraced (89:16-17).
