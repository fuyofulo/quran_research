# Hope and despair: one model, three attacks

## Headline finding

**The model.** Hope is a turning toward God that may name what it wants. `rajā` describes it, never in the first person; people voice it as `ʿasā` + *my Lord*, with action and fear. Despair is faulted only as a closed sentence about God (*never*, *He does not know*, *only delusion*), and the same grammar carries presumption. Grief is consoled, never faulted. **Verdicts:** (1) "Hope aims at God, not outcomes" is *narrowed*: two specific hopes are granted (12:83, 7:129). (2) "Help at the edge" is the text's grammar, under its declared selection (11:120), not a law. (3) Grief: 14 commands, none blaming.

---

## 1. The model (data-grounded unless marked)

**Hope.**
- *Object.* 19 of 23 hope-sense `rajā` tokens are on God's side (18 without the disputed 71:13). The only plainly worldly object is marriage (24:60), negated and unblamed.
- *Speaker.* `rajā` has **no first-person form**; it describes hope. People voice hope with **`ʿasā` + God**, and 6 of the 7 quoted sentences say *rabb*. First-person `ṭamaʿ` is praised 3 of 3 times (5:84, 26:51, 26:82); `raghba` turns *ilā* God (94:8).
- *Ground.* People hope out of a history with God:
  - Zechariah: *wa-lam akun bi-duʿāʾika rabbi shaqiyyā*, "never have I been, in calling You, unhappy" (19:4).
  - In the same sūra Abraham turns that phrase into a hope: *ʿasā allā akūna bi-duʿāʾi rabbī shaqiyyā* (19:48).
  - Jacob: *I know from God what you do not know* (12:86, repeated at 12:96).
- *Action.* Affirmed `rajā` comes with doing (18:110, 29:5, 33:21, 39:9), and `ʿasā` follows patience, prayer or confession.
- *Fear.* Hope shares 7 ayahs with fear (7:56, 32:16, 21:90, 17:57, 39:9, 13:12, 30:24).
- *No hope* is pictured as contentment (10:7), not as pain.

**Despair.** 16 ayahs, 10 faulted.
- 6 have God's side as the object (12:87, 15:55-56, 29:23, 39:53, 60:13).
- 4 are the reflex *when harm touches him* (11:9, 17:83, 30:36, 41:49). At 41:49-50 the same man says, once eased, *I do not think the Hour will come*.
- Unblamed: despair of a thing (12:80; rain at 42:28 and 30:49), and 12:110, whose object is unstated.
- `iblās` follows a refusal to humble oneself, *taḍarruʿ* (6:43, 23:76).
- [interpretive, argued in §5] The faulted kind is a settled sentence that closes off God's side. Presumption is its twin.

**Grief.** 42 ayahs, never sharing a verse with a despair word. Jacob grieves *to* God (12:86) and forbids despair *of* God (12:87) in consecutive verses.

**What the text gives at breaking point.**

| Gift | Count | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Speech to God | 9 of 11 waiters pray; complaint and confession are unrebuked | 12:86; 21:83 (no request); 28:24 |
| An act | 5 commands given *inside* answers | *search* 12:87; *shake the palm* 19:25; *build* 11:37; *exert yourself, then long for your Lord* 94:7-8 |
| A reframe | 4 of the Prophet's 27 consolations | 6:33; 2:216; 89:15-17 |
| A limit on responsibility | 6 of 27, the largest class | 11:12, 35:8, 31:23 |
| Past care, presence | 5 of 27 | 93:3, 93:6-8, 15:97 |
| A promise | 2 of 27 (93:4-5, 94:5-6); 6 of 11 waiters, before the outcome | 28:7; 10:103 |

[interpretive] Promise is the rarest gift; the text mostly lightens and orients, seldom forecasts.

**Relief.** *faraj* is absent. Relief is **never dated**: *maʿa* (with, 94:5-6), *baʿda* (after, 65:7), *qarīb* (near, 2:214, 61:13). God alone removes harm (10:107), *if He wills* (6:41).

## 2. Stress test 1: "Quranic hope is directed at God, not at outcomes"

| Case | God named? | Specific? | Outcome in the text |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jacob, *perhaps God will bring them all to me* (12:83) | yes | yes | granted (12:96, 12:99-100) |
| Moses, *perhaps your Lord will destroy your enemy and make you successors* (7:129) | yes | yes | granted in both halves, *bimā ṣabarū* (7:136-137) |
| Zechariah's heir (21:89); *comfort of our eyes in spouses and offspring* (25:74); *another you love: help from God and a near opening* (61:13) | yes | yes | answered, praised, promised |
| *Perhaps he will benefit us, or we adopt him* (12:21 = 28:9) | **no** | yes | unfaulted; the narrator frames it in God's plan (*God prevails over His affair*, 12:21) |
| Moses, *perhaps I will bring you a brand, or find guidance at the fire* (`laʿalla`, 20:10, 28:29) | **no** | yes | unfaulted; he is called by name (20:11) |
| *Women who do not hope for marriage* (24:60) | no | yes | no blame |
| `tamannī`; `amal` (15:3); *would that I had been with them* (4:73); Qārūn's envious onlookers (28:79) | no | yes | faulted (`tamannī` in 8 of 15 ayahs), or reversed |
| *You wished the unarmed party were yours* (8:7) | no | yes | overruled by God's intent |

> **12:83** فَصَبۡرࣱ جَمِيلٌۖ عَسَى ٱللَّهُ أَن يَأۡتِيَنِي بِهِمۡ جَمِيعًاۚ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلۡعَلِيمُ ٱلۡحَكِيمُ
> *…so patience is most fitting. Perhaps Allah will bring them to me all together. Indeed it is He who is the Knowing, the Wise.* (Saheeh)

Jacob's sentence is both: specific (*them*, *all*), routed through God, spoken in grief (12:84), and confirmed. At the reunion Joseph closes on the same words, *innahu huwa l-ʿalīmu l-ḥakīm* (12:100).

**Verdict: NARROWED.**
- The strong form holds only for `rajā`.
- Hope may name outcomes, including specific ones, and two are shown granted.
- What is constant is that the outcome is **routed through God**: `ʿasā` + God + a verb done *to the speaker*.
- Hope voiced without God is not faulted in itself (12:21, 28:9, 20:10).
- [interpretive] What is faulted is desire that **stands in for** something: deed (4:123), proof (2:111), God's distribution (4:32), or the hereafter (15:3).

Revised: *Quranic hope is addressed to God; its content may be the outcome.*

## 3. Stress test 2: "Help comes at the edge of despair"

**Extremity → relief.**

| Ref | The edge, in the text's words | First arrival | Said or done at the edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12:110 | *ḥattā idhā stayʾasa r-rusul*; *thought they had been denied* | *naṣr*; *whom We willed was saved* | nothing |
| 2:214 | shaken *ḥattā* the Messenger says *when?* | a word, *near*; not shown arriving | "When is God's help?" |
| 9:118 | *ḥattā idhā* earth and souls closed in | *tāba ʿalayhim* (inward) | certain: no refuge but Him |
| 42:28 | *min baʿdi mā qanaṭū* | rain (habitual) | nothing |
| 30:49 | *mublisīn* before it | rain | nothing |
| 21:87-88 | *in the darknesses* | rescue: *thus We rescue the believers* | confession, glorification |
| 26:61-62 | *we will be overtaken* | Moses's *kallā*, then the sea | "my Lord is with me" |
| 33:9-25 | hearts at the throats, a severe shaking | wind and armies, **told first** (33:9) | "this is what God promised" |
| 3:154 | *min baʿdi l-ghamm* | security, **to one party only** | nothing |
| 28:10-13 | heart *fārigh*; *in kādat* (she nearly) | heart bound (inward), then return | nothing |

- **7 of 10 use explicit edge grammar** (*ḥattā idhā*, *min baʿdi mā*, *min qabl*, *kādat*). The edge is the text's own phrasing.
- **In 5 of 10 nothing right is recorded as said or done**, three of them despairing groups (12:110, 42:28, 30:49). No formula earns relief. In 5, what arrives first is inward, or a word.

**Counter-cases.**

| Kind | Ref | Shown |
|---|---|---|
| Edge → seizure | 6:42-44 | Hardship came *that they might humble themselves*; they did not (6:43). Doors were opened *ḥattā idhā fariḥū*, until they rejoiced, and they were seized. The turning point is the **top**. |
| | 23:75-77 | Gripped by punishment, *they did not humble themselves* (23:76); a door of severe punishment followed, then *iblās*. |
| Relief, no edge | 19:4; 11:69-73; 28:86; 10:12 | Zechariah asks out of a history of answered calls; Abraham's tidings come unasked; the Book *you were not hoping for*; harm is lifted for people who then relapse. |
| Edge, no narrated relief | 11:42-43 | Noah's son: *the waves came between them*. |
| | 85:4-11 | Believers *persecuted* (`fatanū`) by fire; the *aṣḥāb al-ukhdūd* are the persecutors, and no death is narrated. Gardens for believers, Hell for unrepentant persecutors. |
| | 7:126; 36:26-27; 66:11 | The magicians: *pour patience on us and take us as Muslims*, outcome untold; the man told *enter the Garden*; Pharaoh's wife's prayer for *najāt*, no outcome. |

**The forks are separated by something the text states.** Both seizures follow a refusal to humble oneself (6:43, 23:76). No relief case states one. Depth is not the variable.

> **11:120** وَكُلࣰّا نَّقُصُّ عَلَيۡكَ مِنۡ أَنۢبَآءِ ٱلرُّسُلِ مَا نُثَبِّتُ بِهِۦ فُؤَادَكَۚ
> *And each [story] We relate to you from the news of the messengers is that by which We make firm your heart.* (Saheeh)

With 40:78 (*some We have related to you, and some We have not*), the text declares that its stories are chosen, and for what.

**Verdict: a pattern of the text, by its own declared selection, not a law of events.** The edge is in the grammar seven times but never stated as a rule. The key verse limits itself (*whom We willed was saved*, 12:110). The same edge ends in seizure where turning was refused, and relief also comes with no edge. Extremity without worldly relief is told plainly, with the help placed in the hereafter or in patience (7:126).

[interpretive] At the edge, God's side is still open. *How* and *when* it moves is not promised.

## 4. Stress test 3: "Grief is allowed; despair of God is not"

**The commands.** Phase 0's 18 are 14 imperatives (3:139, 3:176, 5:41, 9:40, 10:65, 15:88, 16:127, 19:24, 27:70, 28:7, 29:33, 31:23, 36:76, 41:30), 3 purpose clauses (3:153, 20:40, 28:13) and 1 statement (21:103). **13 of 14 imperatives give a reason or gift in the same verse** (27:70's comes at 27:72). **None blames or threatens.**

**God acts so that someone will not grieve:** Moses restored *so that she be content and not grieve* (20:40, 28:13); *distress upon distress so that you do not grieve* (3:153). 57:23 uses the same frame, *li-kaylā taʾsaw ʿalā mā fātakum*. How distress prevents grief, the text does not say.

> **3:153** فَأَثَٰبَكُمۡ غَمَّۢا بِغَمࣲّ لِّكَيۡلَا تَحۡزَنُواْ عَلَىٰ مَا فَاتَكُمۡ وَلَا مَآ أَصَٰبَكُمۡ
> *So Allah repaid you with distress upon distress so you would not grieve for that which had escaped you or [for] that which had befallen you.* (Saheeh)

**Grief shown without blame:** Jacob's whitened eyes (12:84); the tears of 9:92, among those with *no blame* (9:91); *We know it grieves you* (6:33); Maryam's *I wish I had died* (19:23), answered with a stream.

**The grief that is cautioned** consumes the self over others' choices: *let not your soul go out over them in ḥasarāt* (35:8), and *perhaps you would destroy yourself* (`bākhiʿ`, 18:6, 26:3). Its words are not `ḥzn`. The remedy is a limit on responsibility, *God knows what they do* (35:8), not a rebuke.

**Grief is a burden.** The Garden's people thank God *who removed al-ḥazan from us* (35:34). Despair of God, by contrast, is labelled (*kāfirūn* 12:87, *ḍāllūn* 15:56) and threatened (29:23).

**Verdict: holds, but "allowed" is the wrong word. Grief is *consoled*.** It is never faulted and never left alone: God lifts it by word, by act, and at the end. *Lā taḥzan* is a prohibition in grammar and a comfort in function. The one grief cautioned destroys the self over what is not one's charge, and its cure is a lighter load.

## 5. The fault in despair, precisely

**What the faulted sentence says about God.**

| Sentence | Ref | What it closes |
|---|---|---|
| *thinks God will never help him* (`an lan`) | 22:15. "Him" is disputed: Arberry reads the thinker; Saheeh, Khattab and Pickthall read the Prophet. | future help |
| *they will never return, ever*, *made attractive in your hearts* | 48:12 (`ẓann al-sawʾ`) | an outcome, as a thought about God |
| *God does not know much of what you do* | 41:22 (cf. 90:7) | knowledge |
| *God and His Messenger promised us only delusion* | 33:12 | truthfulness |
| *Do we have any say in the matter?* | 3:154, answered *the matter is wholly God's* | command |
| *My Lord has humiliated me* | 89:16, answered *kallā* | regard, read off one's provision |
| *the bad has gone from me*; *this is mine* | 11:10, 41:50 | His presence, in both states |
| implied by the ban: *my wrongs are beyond Him* | 39:53, answered *God forgives all sins* | forgiveness |

**The same grammar carries presumption.** `ẓanna`/`ḥasiba an lan`, "thought that … would never", occurs 7 times about God's side.
- 2 are despair: 22:15 and 48:12.
- 4 are presumption:
  - *We will never assemble his bones* (75:3);
  - *he would never return* (84:14);
  - *no one would ever overpower him* (90:5);
  - *God would never send anyone* (72:7).
- 1 is Jonah: *he thought We would never constrain him* (21:87). Translators split three ways on this verse (divergence 0.688).

[interpretive] The fault lies in the closing, in either direction. Despair and presumption are twins, and the reflex man shows both (41:49-50). That is why hope is paired with fear (7:56): each keeps God's side open against one of them.

**What is not faulted.** Sentences about *oneself* at breaking point are answered, never rebuked: *we will be overtaken* (26:61), *we have no strength today* (2:249), *I wish I had died* (19:23), *adversity has touched me* (21:83), *I am in need* (28:24), *when is God's help?* (2:214). **"When?" is answered with *near*; "never" is the fault.**

**What the faithful say instead.** The 9 sentences (2:156, 2:214, 2:249, 2:250, 3:147, 3:173, 33:22, 9:40, 26:62) set God in relation to the speaker (*to Him*, *enough for us*, *with me*, *promised us*, *our Lord*) or ask Him, inward things first. In 26:61-62 and 2:249 the frightened sentence comes from the company and the God-sentence from someone else in it.

> **33:12** مَّا وَعَدَنَا ٱللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُۥٓ إِلَّا غُرُورࣰا
> **33:22** هَٰذَا مَا وَعَدَنَا ٱللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُۥ وَصَدَقَ ٱللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُۥ
> *"Allah and His Messenger did not promise us except delusion."* / *"This is what Allah and His Messenger had promised us, and Allah and His Messenger spoke the truth."* (Saheeh)

It is the same siege, with the same words *waʿadanā llāhu wa-rasūluhu*, and opposite verdicts. [interpretive] The faithful do not deny the extremity. They read it as inside the promise, since 2:214 had promised the shaking.

**A closed sentence can be taken back.** Jonah held one and went into the dark:

> **21:87** فَنَادَىٰ فِي ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ أَن لَّآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّآ أَنتَ سُبۡحَٰنَكَ إِنِّي كُنتُ مِنَ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ
> *And he called out within the darknesses, "There is no deity except You; exalted are You. Indeed, I have been of the wrongdoers."* (Saheeh)

*So We answered him… and thus do We rescue the believers* (21:88). 39:53 forbids despair to exactly those *who have wronged themselves*.

## For the one losing hope

- You may name what you want and ask God for it. Jacob did, *perhaps God will bring them all to me*, and grieved while he waited. The Quran does not promise that yours will come as his did.
- Say how it is with you: *I am weak*, *I am afraid*, *when?* None of these is despair, and none is rebuked.
- The sentence to refuse is a closed one about God: *He will never*, *He does not see*, *He did not mean it*.
- If you have already said it, Jonah's words are there: *There is no god but You, glory be to You; I have been of the wrongdoers* (21:87).
- Do one thing that is in your hands, and set down what is not yours to carry (35:8).
- Help is called *near*, and it is never dated. Some in the Quran received it in this life and some did not, and the text tells both plainly.
