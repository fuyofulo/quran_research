# When hope is running low: what the Quran says

*For anyone in a long trial, or with a wish that has not come. [12:86] means chapter 12, verse 86. Where something is our own reading, we say so.*

## The short answer

In the Quran, hope means turning toward God. You are allowed to grieve, to be afraid, to ask "when?", and to name what you want. None of that is blamed. What the Quran warns against is a closed sentence about God: "He will never", "He doesn't know", "He has humiliated me". Help is called near, and ease is said to come with hardship. But the Quran never gives a date, and it never promises the exact thing you are asking for.

## 1. What hope is

- **Hope is turning toward God.** It is not a feeling you have to force. It is a direction you face.
- **You may name what you want.** Zechariah asked for a child by name, more than once [3:38, 21:89]. In the Quran's stories, most requests that named a thing were granted: nine of the thirteen the research found. Three were refused, each with a reason. None of this is a promise about yours.
- **The Quran's way of saying hope out loud is "perhaps my Lord will…".** Jacob said it about his lost sons [12:83]. Moses, on the run: "Perhaps my Lord will guide me" [28:22]. Some translations say "I trust God will…". The Arabic word, *'asa*, means "perhaps".
- **Hope usually comes after something**: patience, a prayer, an admission of wrong [12:83, 28:21-22, 68:29-32]. You don't have to feel hopeful first. Start with patience or a prayer, and let the "perhaps" come after.
- **Hope and fear can sit together.** "Call on Him in fear and in hope" [7:56]. Being afraid does not cancel your hope.
- **The Quran's picture of someone with no hope is not someone in pain.** It is someone comfortable with this world and not paying attention [10:7]. *Our reading:* if you are hurting and still turning to God, you are the opposite of that picture.

## 2. What the Quran blames, and what it doesn't

**It does not blame these:**

- **Grief.** Jacob's eyes turned white from sorrow [12:84]. No verse blames him. Only his sons told him to stop [12:85], and the story shows they were wrong [12:96].
- **Tears.** People who wept because they had nothing to give are named among those with "no blame" [9:91-92].
- **Fear.** "We will be caught!" [26:61] and "We have no strength today" [2:249] were answered, not scolded.
- **"When is God's help?"** The Messenger and the believers said this after being shaken. The reply: "God's help is near" [2:214].
- **"I wish I had died."** Maryam said this, alone, in labour [19:23]. She was answered with "Do not grieve", water and fresh dates [19:24-25].
- **Asking for years.** No one in the Quran is told off for asking too long or too often. When a request is turned down, the reason is about *what* was asked, never how long.

Fourteen times the Quran says "do not grieve". In thirteen of them the same verse gives a reason or a gift. These words are comfort, not a scolding.

**What it does blame: a closed sentence about God.** Not a feeling: a conclusion about Him that shuts the door.

- "God will never help." "It will never, ever happen." [22:15, 48:12]
- "God doesn't know what is happening." [41:22]
- "God's promise was nothing but a delusion." [33:12]
- "My Lord has humiliated me", said when life is tight. The Quran answers with a direct "No!" Having plenty and having little are both tests, and neither is honour or disgrace [89:15-17].
- "My wrongs are too much for Him." To exactly the people who have wronged themselves, the Quran says: "Do not despair of God's mercy. God forgives all sins" [39:53].

**The difference fits in one word. "When?" is allowed. "Never" is the fault** [2:214; 22:15].

The Quran also faults the opposite mistake: being sure nothing will ever catch up with you [75:3, 90:5]. *Our reading:* both close the door on God. Hope keeps it open.

**A "never" can be taken back.** Jonah left in anger, "thinking We would never constrain him". In the dark he took it back (words 5 below), and was rescued: "That is how We rescue the believers" [21:87-88].

**Is hardship a sign that God is displeased with you?** The Quran never says so. When it links a hardship to what people did, it adds "and He pardons much" [42:30]. To the Prophet in a hard time: "Your Lord has not left you, and does not dislike you" [93:3]. Nor does it say hardship proves His love; His love is for the patient and those who rely on Him [3:146, 3:159]. *Our reading:* the Quran turns your attention from what the hardship "means" to how you meet it.

## 3. People who waited

The Quran says it tells these stories "to make your heart firm" [11:120], and that it has not told every story [40:78]. They are chosen to strengthen you, not a promise that every story ends the same way.

**How long did they wait?** The Quran almost never says. There is no number for Jacob's grief, Job's illness, or Zechariah's asking, only old age. The only spans given are vague and told afterwards: Joseph in prison "several years" [12:42], Moses "years" in Madyan [20:40].

**Jacob** lost Joseph, and later two more sons [12:80]. "Beautiful patience. Perhaps God will bring them all back to me" [12:83]. His eyes turned white from grief [12:84]. He took the grief to God [12:86], and told his sons, "Go and search, and do not despair of God's relief" [12:87] (his word, *rawḥ*, is close to "breath"). All of them came back [12:93-100].

**Zechariah** was old, his wife unable to have children [19:4-5]. He asked right after seeing God provide for Maryam [3:37-38]. His reason to ask again was his past: "I have never been disappointed in calling You" [19:4]. He even asked "How?" [19:8], and was not scolded. He was given a son, John [21:90].

**Job** lost his health and his family. He asked for nothing, and only said, "Harm has touched me, and You are the most merciful" [21:83]. The harm was lifted and his family given back, "and as many again with them" [21:84]. He was also told to strike the ground with his foot, and a spring came [38:42].

**Jonah** left his people in anger. The Quran blames him for leaving [37:142], not for his distress. In the darkness he admitted his wrong and praised God, and was rescued [21:87-88].

**Maryam**, in labour and alone by a palm tree, said, "I wish I had died before this and been forgotten" [19:23]. She was not blamed. A voice told her: "Do not grieve. Your Lord has put a stream beneath you. Shake the trunk of the palm toward you, and fresh dates will fall" [19:24-25]. The comfort came with a small task.

**Moses's mother** was told to put her baby in the river, with a promise first: "Do not fear, do not grieve. We will bring him back to you" [28:7]. Her heart still emptied with worry, until God steadied it [28:10]. She sent his sister to follow him [28:11], and he came back [28:13]. The Quran records no prayer from her. God's care did not wait for her words.

**Moses in Madyan** was on the run, afraid and alone [28:21]. He helped two women water their animals, then sat in the shade and said, "My Lord, I am in need of whatever good You send down to me" [28:24]. He named nothing. What came was safety, work and a marriage [28:25-28].

**Moses at the sea**, with the army behind him, heard his people cry, "We will be caught!" He said, "No! My Lord is with me. He will guide me" [26:61-62].

**What they share.** Most spoke to God, and most also did something. In at least six of the eleven stories, what came was not exactly what was asked: sometimes more, sometimes different, sometimes only the lifting of the weight.

## 4. Words to say

The Quran's own sentences, said by real people in hard moments. Say them in Arabic or in your own words.

### When you are hurting

**1. "I only complain of my grief to God"**

> إِنَّمَآ أَشۡكُواْ بَثِّي وَحُزۡنِيٓ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ وَأَعۡلَمُ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ مَا لَا تَعۡلَمُونَ

*Innama ashku baththi wa huzni ila-llah, wa a'lamu mina-llahi ma la ta'lamun.*
**Means:** I only complain of my sorrow and my grief to God, and I know from God what you do not know.
**Who:** Jacob, after losing his sons [12:86].

**2. "Do not load us with more than we can carry"**

> رَبَّنَا وَلَا تُحَمِّلۡنَا مَا لَا طَاقَةَ لَنَا بِهِۦۖ وَٱعۡفُ عَنَّا وَٱغۡفِرۡ لَنَا وَٱرۡحَمۡنَآۚ

*Rabbana wa la tuhammilna ma la taqata lana bih, wa'fu 'anna, waghfir lana, warhamna.*
**Means:** Our Lord, do not load us with what we have no strength for. Pardon us, forgive us, and have mercy on us.
**Who:** The believers, in the prayer that ends the second chapter [2:285-286]. The same verse begins: "God does not burden any soul with more than it can bear."

**3. "Pour patience on us"**

> رَبَّنَآ أَفۡرِغۡ عَلَيۡنَا صَبۡرࣰا وَثَبِّتۡ أَقۡدَامَنَا

*Rabbana afrigh 'alayna sabran wa thabbit aqdamana.*
**Means:** Our Lord, pour patience on us, and make our feet firm.
**Who:** Saul's (Talut's) small army, facing Goliath [2:249-250]. They asked for patience first, then victory.

### When you have no words

**4. "Harm has touched me"**

> أَنِّي مَسَّنِيَ ٱلضُّرُّ وَأَنتَ أَرۡحَمُ ٱلرَّٰحِمِينَ

*Anni massaniyad-durru wa anta arhamur-rahimin.*
**Means:** Harm has touched me, and You are the most merciful of all who show mercy.
**Who:** Job [21:83]. He asked for nothing: he named the harm, and who God is. He was answered [21:84].

**5. "There is no god but You"**

> لَّآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّآ أَنتَ سُبۡحَٰنَكَ إِنِّي كُنتُ مِنَ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ

*La ilaha illa anta, subhanaka, inni kuntu minaz-zalimin.*
**Means:** There is no god but You. Glory be to You. I have been one of the wrongdoers.
**Who:** Jonah, in the darkness [21:87]. He was answered and rescued [21:88]. (The doubling mark on the first letter comes from the word before it in the verse.)

### When you want something specific

**6. "Perhaps God will bring them all back to me"**

> فَصَبۡرࣱ جَمِيلٌۖ عَسَى ٱللَّهُ أَن يَأۡتِيَنِي بِهِمۡ جَمِيعًاۚ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلۡعَلِيمُ ٱلۡحَكِيمُ

*Fa-sabrun jamil. 'Asallahu an ya'tiyani bihim jami'an. Innahu huwal-'alimul-hakim.*
**Means:** So: beautiful patience. Perhaps God will bring them all back to me. He is the All-Knowing, the All-Wise.
**Who:** Jacob, about his lost sons [12:83]. It came true [12:99-100]. You can borrow its shape, "Perhaps my Lord will…", and name your own thing.

**7. "I have never been disappointed in calling You"**

> رَبِّ إِنِّي وَهَنَ ٱلۡعَظۡمُ مِنِّي وَٱشۡتَعَلَ ٱلرَّأۡسُ شَيۡبࣰا وَلَمۡ أَكُنۢ بِدُعَآئِكَ رَبِّ شَقِيࣰّا

*Rabbi inni wahanal-'azmu minni washta'alar-ra'su shayba, wa lam akun bi-du'a'ika rabbi shaqiyya.*
**Means:** My Lord, my bones have grown weak and my head is white with age, and never, my Lord, have I been disappointed in calling You.
**Who:** Zechariah, asking for a child in old age [19:4]. His reason to keep asking was his history with God.

**8. "Do not leave me alone"**

> رَبِّ لَا تَذَرۡنِي فَرۡدࣰا وَأَنتَ خَيۡرُ ٱلۡوَٰرِثِينَ

*Rabbi la tadharni fardan wa anta khayrul-warithin.*
**Means:** My Lord, do not leave me alone, and You are the best of inheritors.
**Who:** Zechariah [21:89]. He named what he wanted, and in the same breath named God as enough. He was answered [21:90].

### When you don't know what is good

**9. "I am in need of whatever good You send"**

> رَبِّ إِنِّي لِمَآ أَنزَلۡتَ إِلَيَّ مِنۡ خَيۡرࣲ فَقِيرࣱ

*Rabbi inni lima anzalta ilayya min khayrin faqir.*
**Means:** My Lord, I am in need of whatever good You send down to me. (It can also be read "whatever good You have sent me".)
**Who:** Moses, a refugee, resting in the shade after helping strangers [28:24].

**10. "Set our situation right"**

> رَبَّنَآ ءَاتِنَا مِن لَّدُنكَ رَحۡمَةࣰ وَهَيِّئۡ لَنَا مِنۡ أَمۡرِنَا رَشَدࣰا

*Rabbana atina min ladunka rahmatan wa hayyi' lana min amrina rashada.*
**Means:** Our Lord, give us mercy from Yourself, and guide us to what is right in our situation.
**Who:** The young people who took shelter in the cave [18:10].

### For your heart

**11. "Open my chest"**

> رَبِّ ٱشۡرَحۡ لِي صَدۡرِي
> وَيَسِّرۡ لِيٓ أَمۡرِي

*Rabbish-rah li sadri, wa yassir li amri.*
**Means:** My Lord, open up my chest for me, and make my task easy for me.
**Who:** Moses, when he was sent to Pharaoh [20:25-26]. The answer: "You have been given what you asked, Moses" [20:36].

**12. "Do not let our hearts turn away"**

> رَبَّنَا لَا تُزِغۡ قُلُوبَنَا بَعۡدَ إِذۡ هَدَيۡتَنَا وَهَبۡ لَنَا مِن لَّدُنكَ رَحۡمَةًۚ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلۡوَهَّابُ

*Rabbana la tuzigh qulubana ba'da idh hadaytana, wa hab lana min ladunka rahmah, innaka antal-wahhab.*
**Means:** Our Lord, do not let our hearts turn away after You have guided us. Give us mercy from Yourself. You are the Giver.
**Who:** Those "firm in knowledge" [3:7-8].

### When something is lost

**13. "We belong to God"**

> إِنَّا لِلَّهِ وَإِنَّآ إِلَيۡهِ رَٰجِعُونَ

*Inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi raji'un.*
**Means:** We belong to God, and to Him we are returning.
**Who:** The patient, "when disaster strikes them". Blessings and mercy are on them [2:155-157].

## 5. What the Quran promises, and what it doesn't

**It promises, or says plainly:**

- **Nearness and an answer.** "I am near. I answer the call of the one who calls Me" [2:186]. "Call on Me; I will answer you" [40:60].
- **Ease with hardship.** "With hardship comes ease. With hardship comes ease" [94:5-6]. The word is "with", not only "after". Elsewhere: "God will bring ease after hardship" [65:7].
- **God's attention.** To the Prophet, when his chest felt tight: "We know" [15:97].
- **That having little is not disgrace** [89:16-17], and **that no soul is burdened beyond what it can bear** [2:286].

**It does not promise:**

- **The specific thing.** Promises of a named thing go to named people in the stories, like Moses's mother [28:7]. "I answer" does not say what the answer will be.
- **A date.** Help is "near" [2:214]. Ease is "with" and "after". There is never a day or a year.
- **That you will see the "better".** Hopes like "perhaps our Lord will give us better than it" [68:32] are never shown coming true. Job is shown getting back more than he lost [21:84], but his story doesn't use that phrase.
- **That every story ends well in this life.** For some faithful people, like believers persecuted with fire, no rescue in this world is told. Their help is spoken of in the next life [85:4-11].
- **That an unanswered prayer was bad for you.** "Perhaps you love a thing and it is bad for you" [2:216] was said about a duty people disliked, not about a prayer that wasn't granted. Using it that way is a fair comparison, not the verse's own words.

**Often said, but not in the Quran:** "If it's meant for you, it will come." "I am as My servant thinks of Me" and "relief comes with distress" (both hadith). "Every hardship has two eases" (a scholars' argument). Some come from other respected sources, but none is the Quran's own words, so this guide does not lean on them. Even the usual word for relief, *faraj*, is not in the Quran.

## 6. What to do, not only feel

- **Hope goes with doing.** "Whoever hopes to meet his Lord, let him do good work" [18:110].
- **"When you have finished, strive on. And to your Lord turn your longing"** [94:7-8].
- **Take small steps.** Maryam was told to shake the palm tree [19:25]. Noah was told to build [11:37]. Jacob grieved and still said "go and search" [12:87]. *Our reading:* the task asked of Maryam was small, and what came was far more than the task.
- **Put down what is not yours to carry.** When the Prophet was wearing himself out over people who would not listen, God said: "Do not let your soul waste away over them" [35:8].
- **If your wish involves another person's heart:** hearts are God's to join [8:63]. Abraham asked God to make people's hearts lean toward his family [14:37]. You can ask the same way, but the Quran gives no permission to force a heart [4:19, 10:99]. And no one in it is told by God to stop wanting.

**Getting help from people.** Moses asked God for his brother Aaron as a helper, "strengthen me through him", and was given what he asked [20:29-36]. *Our reading, not a rule from the Quran:* reaching out to family, a friend you trust, or a counsellor can be like Maryam shaking the palm tree. It is a small act on your side, alongside the asking.

## 7. A simple practice when hope is low

*This is our suggestion, drawn from the research. It is not a Quranic rule or a set ritual.*

1. **Say it as it is, to God**, in Jacob's or Job's words, or your own [12:86, 21:83].
2. **Name what you want, the Quran's way:** "Perhaps my Lord will…" [12:83].
3. **Catch the closed sentence.** If you hear "never" or "He doesn't care" in your head, turn it into a question: "When, my Lord?" [2:214].
4. **Ask for your heart too:** patience, an open chest [2:250, 20:25].
5. **Do one small thing that is in your hands today** [19:25, 94:7].
6. **Put down one thing that is not yours to carry** [35:8].
7. **Turn your longing to your Lord** [94:8].

The Quran does not promise that your wish will come the way Jacob's did. It does say that God is near [2:186], and that ease comes with hardship [94:5-6].
