# Those who waited: eleven people in long pain or longing

## Headline finding

**There is no single template, but two regularities hold.** Of eleven waiters, only 6 get the verb *fa-stajabnā* (or *ujībat*): Zechariah, Job, Jonah, Joseph, Noah, Moses and Aaron. In the other five the answer is narrated with other verbs or not named. **The Quran gives a duration for almost none of them**: only old age (Zechariah, Abraham, Sarah), "several years" (Joseph, 12:42), "years" in Midian (Moses) and the counterfactual of Jonah. The words *do not fear / do not grieve / do not despair / do not be distressed* are spoken to the waiter in 5 stories, and in 3 of those the promise comes *before* the outcome. Grief, laughter, "I wish I had died" and white eyes are reported; only Jonah's departure is censured.

**Key counts (11 rows).**
- A prayer in the text: 8. No prayer recorded: 3 (Moses's mother, Maryam, Abraham and Sarah).
- Asking *and* naming the wanted thing: 5 (Zechariah, Joseph, Noah, Moses and Aaron, Jacob). Naming and asking something open: Moses. Describing the harm or confessing without any request: 2 (Job, Jonah).
- Durations: **0 given in years for the pain itself.** Old age or white hair: 3 (Zechariah, Abraham, Sarah). "Several years": Joseph. "Years": Moses.

## 2. Is there a common shape?

Order of what the person first says to God: request 5 (Zechariah, Moses in flight, Moses and Aaron, Joseph, Noah); description of harm with praise 2 (Job, Jacob); confession 1 (Jonah); nothing recorded 3. **Praise or the divine names come with the speech in Job (*arḥamu al-rāḥimīn*), Zechariah (*khayru al-wārithīn*, 21:89), Jonah (*subḥānaka*), Abraham (14:39).**

*ʿasā* is voiced by 2 of 11 (Jacob 12:83, Moses 28:22). Moses at the sea instead says *sayahdīn*, flat certainty (26:62). Both *ʿasā* sentences follow a cost: Jacob's "beautiful patience", Moses's flight.

An act beside prayer: 7 acted before the answer (Jacob, Zechariah, the mother, Moses, Abraham, Maryam, Joseph). 5 were handed a command *inside* the answer: Job (38:42), Noah (11:37), Maryam (19:25), Moses at the sea (26:63), Moses and Aaron (10:89). Jonah's tasbīḥ is the only action.

Answer verb: *istajāba* 6; other verbs or none 5. **The answer differs from the request in at least 6 of 11 stories**: more (Zechariah's wife, Job's double), different (Moses: asked guidance and "good", received a household; Joseph: asked the plan be turned, stayed in prison), or relief only (Jonah).

## 3. Did anyone stop asking?

**Not in this group.** No one in the 119 verses is reported to cease. Jacob never petitions in the text; he complains to God (*ashkū*, 12:86) and then tells his sons *do not despair of God's rawḥ* (12:87). Abraham says the same in near-identical form (15:56). **So two of the waiters speak the sentence that forbids despair**, in both cases as a sentence about who despairs: only the *kāfirūn* (12:87), only the *ḍāllūn* (15:56).

The only refused request is Noah's for his son (11:45-47): "ask Me not about what you have no knowledge of". Noah answers with refuge and a plea for forgiveness (11:47). **[interpretive]** These stories are told because they ended in an outcome; another file covers unanswered prayers. The brothers who despaired (12:80) are not waiters.

## 4. The inner states the text allows

| State | Who | Rebuked? |
|---|---|---|
| White eyes from grief, suppressed (*kaẓīm*) | Jacob | **No.** Others (the sons) reproach; the narrator doesn't. |
| "I wish I had died... forgotten" | Maryam | **No.** Answered *lā taḥzanī*. |
| "How will I have a boy?" (twice) | Zechariah | **No.** Answered with explanation (19:9) and a sign. |
| Smile, "woe to me", striking the face | Sarah | **Mild at most:** *ataʿjabīna min amri Allāh?* (11:73) is a question, then blessing. |
| Fear | Abraham, Moses, Moses's mother (heart *fārigh*, 28:10) | **No.** Her heart was "bound" by God. |
| Anger, flight | Jonah | **Yes**, and it is the *leaving*: *malīm* (37:142). Not his distress. |

*Kaẓīm* (12:84) and *makẓūm* (68:48) share a root. **[interpretive]** The fault attaches to Jonah's act, not to the feeling.

## 5. What returns

- **The same thing returned: 3.** Jacob's son and sight (12:93-100); Moses to his mother (28:13, *radadnāhu*); Job's family *and the like thereof* (21:84, 38:43), which is return plus a second set.
- **Something given that was not lost: 3.** John to Zechariah, with the wife "amended" (21:90); Isaac, and Jacob after him (11:71), to Abraham and Sarah; for Moses, a household and work in Madyan (28:27) in place of the "good" he could not name.
- **Relief, not the object: 4.** Jonah's rescue from *ghamm* (21:88); Joseph's turning away of the plan, with prison left (12:34); Maryam's stream (19:24); Noah's escape (54:13-14). Jonah is the clearest case: he asked for nothing.
- **Abraham** does not ask in these verses; 14:39 later calls the gift an answer: "my Lord is the Hearer of supplication."

## 6. What God says *to* the waiter, and when

| Phrase | Said to | Before or after the outcome |
|---|---|---|
| *lā takhāfī wa-lā taḥzanī, innā rāddūhu ilayki* (28:7) | the mother | **Before**, with the return named |
| *lā takhaf* (11:70, 15:53, 51:28); *lā takun mina al-qāniṭīn* (15:55) | Abraham | **Before**, with the tidings |
| *lā taḥzanī, qad jaʿala rabbuki taḥtaki sariyyā* (19:24) | Maryam | During the pain; stream at once; the child was announced earlier (19:19-21) |
| *falā tabtaʾis* (11:36); *do not address Me* (11:37) | Noah | **Before** the flood |
| *lā takhaf, najawta* (28:25) | Moses | After; spoken by a man in Madyan, not by God |
| *bushrā* of John (3:39) | Zechariah | After his prayer, before the child |
| *qad ujībat daʿwatukumā* (10:89) | Moses and Aaron | **Before** the outcome, with a command |
| *nothing said to them* | Jacob, Job, Jonah, Joseph | The outcome comes without a prior promise in these verses |

Count: of 11, **6 get a promise or an answer-announcement before the outcome** (mother, Abraham and Sarah, Zechariah, Maryam, Noah, Moses and Aaron). Jacob, Job, Jonah, Joseph and Moses in flight get none from God beforehand. **Where the promise is given, a command to act is part of it** (cast him; build; stay straight). 

## For the one losing hope

- Say what you have, even without a request. Job and Jonah asked for nothing by name; both were answered with *fa-stajabnā*.
- Naming the wish is allowed. Zechariah named his, twice, and said "how?" and "make me a sign" and was not rebuked.
- Grief that turns the eyes white is described, not condemned. The thing to avoid is the one Jacob also told his sons: not despairing of God's *rawḥ* (12:87).
- Do something small alongside: shake the palm, hire out, build the ship. The Quran never gives a duration for the wait.
- The Quran does not promise that the specific thing returns. It records people for whom it did, and Noah, who was told no, and Moses, who got something he had not named.

## Orchestrator addendum (verified 2026-10-22)

- **Abraham did pray for a son — outside this file's verse group.** *Rabbi hab lī mina ṣ-ṣāliḥīn* — "My Lord, grant me [one] of the righteous" (37:100), answered at once: *fa-bashsharnāhu bi-ghulāmin ḥalīm* (37:101). So the "no prayer recorded" row is true only of 11:69-73, 15:51-56 and 51:24-30. Corrected counts: **a prayer in the text 9 of 11; no prayer recorded 2 (Moses's mother, Maryam).** Abraham's request names the thing (a righteous child), so "asked and named the thing" becomes 6.
- **Noah's duration** is given elsewhere: *a thousand years less fifty* among his people (29:14) — a duration of mission, not of a wait for an answer.
