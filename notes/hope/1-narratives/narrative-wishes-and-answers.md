# Wishes and answers: what the Quran does with a wish

## Headline finding

The Quran's wishing words (`tamannī`/`amāniyy`, 15 ayahs after removing 3 *semen*) are never a prayer: in none does a speaker wish *to God*. Eight are faulted or forbidden, because a wish stands in place of deed or proof; four are a test the wishers refuse; three are met, contested or reversed. The same desire is redirected, not abolished: *do not wish for what God favoured others with — ask God of His bounty* (4:32). Answers carry conditions in some verses and none in others, and never promise the asked content; refusals (11:46, 9:80) are reasoned. "Better than it" is a promise or a hope, and the text never shows it arriving.

## 1. Wishing vs hoping (data-grounded)

`mny`: 18 ayahs; 53:46, 56:58, 75:37 are semen. 15 remain:

| Verdict | Ayahs | Who wishes, for what |
|---|---|---|
| Faulted: wish as substitute for deed | 2:78, 2:111, 4:123, 57:14, 53:24 | scripture-people and hypocrites; Paradise or standing, without proof or work |
| Faulted: Satan's instrument | 4:119-120 | "I will arouse wishes in them"; "Satan promises only delusion" |
| Forbidden | 4:32 | craving what God gave another |
| A test they refuse | 2:94-95, 62:6-7 | "wish for death if you are truthful"; "they will never wish it" |
| Met by reality | 3:143 | believers wished for martyrdom, then "saw it" |
| Contested | 22:52 | the messenger's own *tamannā* |
| Reversed next day | 28:82 | those who wished Qārūn's position |

**3:143 is the only wish that comes true**, recorded as observation (*you wished… now you have seen it*), neither praised nor blamed in the verse.

**22:52, translator split** (data): Saheeh "he spoke [or recited]", Pickthall "recited", Khattab "recited"; Arberry "Satan cast into his fancy, when he was fancying". Wished vs recited both stand in print; I build nothing on it.

**4:123 and 53:24-25**: the wish is rebutted by God's ownership (*to God belong the last and the first*) and by deed-linked reward (*whoever does wrong is recompensed*).

**28:79-82.** Those who "desired the worldly life" said *would that we had like what Qārūn was given* (28:79). Those with knowledge answered: *God's reward is better… none are granted it except the patient* (28:80). Qārūn is swallowed. The wishers then say *had God not favoured us, He would have swallowed us* (28:82). The wish is withdrawn after they saw its end.

### Where is the line? [interpretive, from data]

- **Address.** No `mny` token is addressed to God or has "my Lord" in it. By contrast, in the roots headlines, 6 of the 7 quoted `ʿasā` sentences say *my/our/your Lord*. A wish is spoken about a thing; a hope is spoken toward God.
- **Deed.** `rajā` is affirmed with action (18:110, 29:5, 2:218). The faulted wish has none: 2:111 gets "produce your proof".
- **4:32** gives the line within one verse:

وَلَا تَتَمَنَّوۡاْ مَا فَضَّلَ ٱللَّهُ بِهِۦ بَعۡضَكُمۡ عَلَىٰ بَعۡضࣲۚ … وَسۡـَٔلُواْ ٱللَّهَ مِن فَضۡلِهِۦٓ
*And do not wish for that by which Allah has made some of you exceed others… And ask Allah of his bounty.* (Saheeh) — *Rather, ask Allah for His bounties.* (Khattab)

The desire survives; its direction changes (`tamannā` of another's thing → `sʾl` of God's *faḍl*).

## 2. The prayer God answers (data-grounded)

| Ref | Condition attached | Content of answer |
|---|---|---|
| 2:186 | *let them respond to Me and believe* (after "I respond") | none stated |
| 40:60 | *call upon Me, I will respond*; arrogance → Hell | none |
| 27:62 | the *desperate one* (`muḍṭarr`) when he calls; no other condition | *removes evil* |
| 42:26 | those who believed and did righteous deeds | "increases for them from His bounty" |
| 11:61 | *ask forgiveness, then repent*; "my Lord is near, responsive" | none |
| 12:34 | (Joseph had prayed, 12:33) | their plan averted |
| 3:195 | (they had asked, 3:191-194) | work not lost; misdeeds removed; gardens |
| 8:9 | asked help | a thousand angels |
| 10:88-89 | Moses asked for Pharaoh's wealth destroyed and hearts hardened | "answered" — content unstated |

Conditions are **mixed**: 27:62 has none; 2:186, 42:26, 11:61 tie to belief, righteousness, repentance. The content is **specified in 12:34, 8:9 and 3:195, and not in 2:186, 40:60, 11:61 or 10:89**. "Answered" is not "everything asked": after 12:34 Joseph is still imprisoned (12:35); after 8:9, the angels are *good tidings and reassurance* (8:10).

10:89 is the key verse (dual, Moses and Aaron):

قَالَ قَدۡ أُجِيبَت دَّعۡوَتُكُمَا فَٱسۡتَقِيمَا وَلَا تَتَّبِعَآنِّ سَبِيلَ ٱلَّذِينَ لَا يَعۡلَمُونَ
*[Allah] said, "Your supplication has been answered." So remain on a right course and follow not the way of those who do not know.* (Saheeh)

The answer is announced and the next instruction is **stay straight**, with nothing shown arriving. **[Interpretive]** "Answered" is paired with "stay on the path".

## 3. Prayers not granted as asked

**Noah and his son (11:45-47).** Noah builds on the promise (*my son is of my family; Your promise is true*). The reply, in the vocative:

قَالَ يَٰنُوحُ إِنَّهُۥ لَيۡسَ مِنۡ أَهۡلِكَۖ إِنَّهُۥ عَمَلٌ غَيۡرُ صَٰلِحࣲۖ فَلَا تَسۡـَٔلۡنِ مَا لَيۡسَ لَكَ بِهِۦ عِلۡمٌۖ إِنِّيٓ أَعِظُكَ أَن تَكُونَ مِنَ ٱلۡجَٰهِلِينَ
*"O Noah, he is not of your family; he is [one whose] work was other than righteous, so ask Me not for that about which you have no knowledge. Indeed, I advise you, lest you be among the ignorant."* (Saheeh) — *I warn you…* (Khattab)

Tone: vocative, a reason, and *aʿiẓuka* (I counsel). Noah afterwards: *I seek refuge in You from asking what I have no knowledge of; unless You forgive me and have mercy, I will be among the losers* (11:47).

**Abraham and his father (9:113-114).** The prayer is explained as keeping a promise (*only because of a promise*). Once the father's enmity "became apparent", Abraham *disassociated himself*. The text's verdict on him is *awwāh ḥalīm* ("compassionate and patient/forbearing"). 9:113 states the rule: no forgiveness-asking for those clearly bound for the Fire, "even relatives".

**The hypocrites (9:80).** *Ask forgiveness for them or do not… seventy times — never will Allah forgive them. That is because they disbelieved.* Tone: flat, a stated reason, no rebuke to the asker. The asker's next act is not recorded here.

**Pattern.** The refusal is **reasoned** (his work, clear enmity, disbelief), and where the asker's response is told (Noah, Abraham) it is submission and withdrawal. **[Interpretive]** The asking exceeded what the asker knew.

**17:11 and 10:11.** *Man prays for evil as he prays for good — man is ever hasty* (17:11). *If Allah hastened evil for people as He hastens good, their term would be ended* (10:11; said of those who "do not hope for the meeting"). So not every unanswered or slow prayer is a refusal: haste asks for what would harm. The verses do not say any given delay is this.

## 4. The wish that would have harmed

- **2:216**, said of fighting: *perhaps you hate a thing and it is good for you; perhaps you love a thing and it is bad for you. Allah knows and you do not.*
- **4:19**, said of a disliked spouse: *perhaps you dislike a thing and Allah places much good in it.*
- **18:79-82.** Each act looked like harm to Moses: the ship holed (to escape a king seizing ships), the boy killed (parents would be burdened by his "transgression and disbelief"), the wall rebuilt (a treasure for orphans). Al-Khidr gives the reason after the fact, adding *I did it not of my own accord*. The owners are not told in the text.
- **28:79-82**: the splendour the wishers saw did not include the ending.

Each says a person **cannot see the end, or the hidden protection, from inside the loss or the want**. **Limits:** 2:216 says "perhaps", not "always"; the Quran does not say every dislike conceals good. The Kahf explanations go to Moses, not to the parents.

(The istikhāra prayer, from hadith, is a traditional practice here and outside the evidence.)

## 5. "Better than it" (data-grounded)

| Ref | Who speaks | Replacement of | Conditional on | Shown? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8:70 | God, to captives | what was taken | *if God knows good in your hearts* | no |
| 18:40 | the believer, `ʿasā` | his poorer garden | none; spoken before ruin | the rival's ruin is shown (18:42); his own "better" is not |
| 18:81 | al-Khidr: *we intended that their Lord substitute* | a son | none | no |
| 66:5 | God, `ʿasā` | wives | *if he divorced you all* | no |
| 68:32 | the garden-owners, `ʿasā` | their garden | after repentance in 68:29-31 | no; ends with *we turn in longing to our Lord* |
| 24:55 | God promising | fear → security | belief and righteous deeds | no |
| 25:70 | God | evil deeds → good | repent, believe, do right | no |
| 7:95 | God | bad → good, then seizure | (warning) | yes, and it is a trap |

- **What is offered:** a **better** thing (`khayran min`), not the same (the son is "better", the garden is "better"), and in 24:55 and 25:70 a **different** thing (fear → security, evil → good). "The same thing back" is never offered.
- **Who speaks:** God promises (8:70, 24:55, 25:70); humans hope with `ʿasā` (18:40, 68:32); al-Khidr voices an intention (18:81); 66:5 is God's hypothetical `ʿasā`.
- **Shown?** In none of the seven does the replacement arrive in the text. The one shown case, 7:95, is a warning.

## 6. What to want (data-grounded)

فَسَقَىٰ لَهُمَا ثُمَّ تَوَلَّىٰٓ إِلَى ٱلظِّلِّ فَقَالَ رَبِّ إِنِّي لِمَآ أَنزَلۡتَ إِلَيَّ مِنۡ خَيۡرࣲ فَقِيرࣱ
*So he watered [their flocks] for them; then he went back to the shade and said, "My Lord, indeed I am, for whatever good You would send down to me, in need."* (Saheeh) — *…in [desperate] need of whatever provision You may have in store for me.* (Khattab)

Moses, a fugitive, names nothing: *whatever good*.

وَإِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ فَٱرۡغَب
*And to your Lord direct [your] longing.* (Saheeh) — *turning to your Lord [alone] with hope.* (Khattab) [94:8]

- **9:59:** *if only they had been content with what God gave them and said, "God is sufficient for us; God will give of His bounty; to God we turn in longing"* — content, then sufficiency, then expected bounty, then a turning.
- **25:74** asks for a worldly thing, with a qualifier: *comfort of our eyes in our spouses and offspring, and make us an example for the righteous.*
- **3:26-27:** *in Your hand is all good*; provision "without account". No petition, only address to the Giver.
- **Pattern [interpretive]:** when a person in the text asks for a worldly thing, they ask for **the good, not the specific form** (28:24), or for the thing joined to its quality (25:74), and they ask *God* instead of looking at another's share (4:32).

## For the one losing hope

- A wish is not hope. Quranic hope is spoken *to* God and carried with action.
- You can **ask** for what you want. The text rebukes wishing for another's share, not asking God for yours.
- "Answered" in 10:89 is followed by *stay straight*. The text does not promise your asked content, and it does not date it.
- When you cannot see why, 2:216 says only that you do not know and God does. It promises no outcome.
- If you want to say something: *My Lord, I am in need of whatever good You send down to me* (28:24), or *to You I turn in longing* (94:8).
- "Perhaps our Lord will give better" (68:32) is a hope, not a promise, and the Quran leaves it so.

## Orchestrator addendum (verified 2026-10-22)

- **12:34 was answered exactly as asked.** Joseph's request (12:33) was *if You do not avert their plan from me…* — and 12:34 says *He averted from him their plan*. Prison was not refused to him; it was what he himself said he preferred (*prison is dearer to me*). So 12:34-35 is **not** a case of "answered but not everything asked"; the §2 sentence citing it should be read with this correction. 8:9-10 stands.
- **"Better than it" never shown arriving — true only of the seven `khayran min` / `bdl` verses.** A return *with more* than was lost **is** shown elsewhere: Job is given *his family and the like of them with them* (21:84, 38:43); Joseph is given rule and reunion (12:100-101). See `narrative-those-who-waited.md`. The precise finding is: **the Quran's explicit "better than it" sentences are promises or hopes; its narrated returns-with-more do not use that phrase.**
