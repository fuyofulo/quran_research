# Hope and despair in the Quran: synthesis

> Phase 4 synthesis of the nine files in this folder. Later files override earlier ones (corrections: §10 and `README.md`). Counts and "the text says" lines are **data-grounded**; readings are tagged **[interpretive]**. Hadith, tafsīr and fiqh are outside the evidence. Arabic is from `data/arabic/quran.json`; translations are Saheeh unless named.

## Headline

In the Quran, hope is a turning toward God that may name what it wants. People voice it as *ʿasā*, "perhaps my Lord will…", after patience, prayer or confession, with action and with fear; *rajā* only describes it. Grief is consoled fourteen times by command and never faulted, and "when is God's help?" is answered "near". Despair is faulted only as a closed sentence about God ("never", "He does not know", "He has humiliated me"), and the same grammar carries presumption. Relief is never dated, *faraj* never occurs, no one is rebuked for asking long, and no one is promised the specific thing.

## 1. The vocabulary (data-grounded)

| Family | Word | What the data shows |
|---|---|---|
| Hope | *rajā*, 23 hope-sense tokens | Object on God's side in 19 (18 without the disputed 71:13); the one worldly object, marriage (24:60), is unblamed. 11 are negated; *lā yarjūna liqāʾanā* describes deniers 4× (10:7, 10:11, 10:15, 25:21). **No first-person form.** |
| | *ʿasā*, "perhaps", 28 ayahs | God is the subject in 15. Of the 7 hopes voiced in hard moments (12:83, 28:22, 7:129, 18:24, 18:40, 19:48, 68:32), 6 say *rabb*. |
| | *ṭamaʿ* 12 · *raghba* 8 · *amal* 2 | First-person *ṭamaʿ* is praised 3 of 3 (26:82); *raghba* turns toward God 4× (94:8 *fa-rghab*); *amal* is faulted (15:3) and redeemed by its object (18:46). |
| Despair | *yaʾs* + *qunūṭ*, 16 ayahs · *iblās*, 5 | 10 faulted, 6 unblamed (§4). *Iblās* is in punishment 4 of 5 times. |
| Wishing | *tamannī*, 15 ayahs | Never addressed to God (§7). |
| Distress | *ḥuzn* 42 · *ḍīq* 13 · *ghamm* 6 · *karb* 4 | *Karb* and *ghamm* are always what one is rescued *from*. `hmm` is "intend"; it is worry only at 3:154. |
| Relief | *yusr*; *kashf* (16 harm-removals); *najāt* (66 rescues); *makhraj* (65:2); *rawḥ* (3) | §9. ***Faraj* never occurs**: every `frj` token means chastity or rifts, and "relief" is the translators' word. |

**[interpretive]** The words divide the work: *rajā* describes hope, *ʿasā* says it, *raghba* points it. No one in the Quran says "I hope". They say "perhaps my Lord will…".

## 2. The model of hope

**Hope is a turning toward God that may name what it wants.**

1. **Addressed to God.** *ʿAsā* + *my/our Lord* + a verb done to the speaker: *bring them to me* (12:83), *guide me* (28:22), *give us better* (68:32).
2. **Grounded in a history with God.** Zechariah asks because asking never left him wretched (19:4); Abraham turns that phrase into a hope (19:48); Jacob says *I know from God what you do not know* (12:86, 12:96).
3. **Never the first move** [interpretive, on data]. All seven voiced *ʿasā* hopes follow a patience, a prayer, a confession or a leaving (12:83, 28:21, 68:29-31, 19:48). None is a complaint.
4. **Tied to action.** *Whoever hopes for the meeting with his Lord, let him do righteous work* (18:110; also 29:5, 33:21, 39:9).
5. **Held with fear.** They share 7 ayahs, in prayer (7:56, 32:16, 21:90), in the devotee (17:57, 39:9) and in lightning (13:12, 30:24).
6. **Its absence is contentment, not pain.** Those who do not hope are *satisfied with the life of this world and secure in it* (10:7). 4:104: *they suffer as you suffer, but you hope from God what they do not hope.* **[interpretive]** A person still calling while hurting is the opposite of that portrait.

Khattab renders *ʿasā* "I trust" in five places; the Arabic says "perhaps". The traditional view that God's *ʿasā* is certain is tafsīr, and nothing here rests on it.

## 3. Three verdicts

### 3a. "Hope aims at God, not at outcomes": NARROWED

> **12:83** فَصَبۡرࣱ جَمِيلٌۖ عَسَى ٱللَّهُ أَن يَأۡتِيَنِي بِهِمۡ جَمِيعًاۚ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلۡعَلِيمُ ٱلۡحَكِيمُ
> *…so patience is most fitting. Perhaps Allah will bring them to me all together. Indeed it is He who is the Knowing, the Wise.* (Saheeh)

Jacob's hope is specific (*all*: three sons, since the eldest stayed behind, 12:80), routed through God, spoken in grief, and **granted** (12:99-100). Moses's *perhaps your Lord will destroy your enemy* (7:129) is granted in both halves, *because they were patient* (7:137).

- The strong form holds only for *rajā*. What is constant is the address to God.
- Worldly hope voiced *without* God is not faulted (12:21, 28:9, 20:10).
- What is faulted is desire that **stands in for** something: deed (4:123), proof (2:111), another's share (4:32), the hereafter (15:3).

**Revised:** Quranic hope is addressed to God; its content may be the outcome.

### 3b. "Help comes at the edge of despair": a declared selection, not a law

Ten passages move from extremity to relief (12:110, 2:214, 9:118, 42:28, 30:49, 21:87-88, 26:61-62, 33:9-25, 3:154, 28:10-13).
- **7 use explicit edge grammar** (*ḥattā idhā*, *min baʿdi mā*, *kādat*).
- **In 5 nothing right is recorded as said or done**, three of them despairing groups (12:110, 42:28, 30:49). No formula earns the relief.
- **In 5 what arrives first is inward or a word**: *near* (2:214), a turning (9:118), a bound heart (28:10).

Against it:
- **Edge, then seizure** (6:42-44, 23:75-77). Both follow a stated refusal to humble oneself (6:43, 23:76), and 6:44 strikes at the **top**, *ḥattā idhā fariḥū*. The forks differ by refused humbling, not by depth.
- **Relief with no edge** (19:4, 28:86, 10:12), and **edge with no worldly relief** narrated (11:43, 85:4-11, 7:126, 36:26-27, 66:11).

The text names its selection: *each story We relate to you is that by which We make firm your heart* (11:120); *some We have related, and some We have not* (40:78). The key verse limits itself: *whoever We willed was saved* (12:110). **[interpretive]** At the edge God's side is still open; how and when it moves is not promised.

### 3c. "Grief is allowed, despair is not": HOLDS, but grief is *consoled*

> **12:86** إِنَّمَآ أَشۡكُواْ بَثِّي وَحُزۡنِيٓ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ وَأَعۡلَمُ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ مَا لَا تَعۡلَمُونَ
> **12:87** يَٰبَنِيَّ ٱذۡهَبُواْ فَتَحَسَّسُواْ مِن يُوسُفَ وَأَخِيهِ وَلَا تَاْيۡـَٔسُواْ مِن رَّوۡحِ ٱللَّهِۖ إِنَّهُۥ لَا يَاْيۡـَٔسُ مِن رَّوۡحِ ٱللَّهِ إِلَّا ٱلۡقَوۡمُ ٱلۡكَٰفِرُونَ
> *"I only complain of my suffering and my grief to Allah, and I know from Allah that which you do not know. O my sons, go and find out about Joseph and his brother and despair not of relief from Allah. Indeed, no one despairs of relief from Allah except the disbelieving people."* (Saheeh)

- The "18 commands not to grieve" are **14 imperatives, 3 purpose clauses and 1 statement**. 13 of the 14 give a reason or gift in the same verse (27:70's comes at 27:72). **None blames.**
- In the purpose clauses God acts so that someone will not grieve (20:40, 28:13, 3:153).
- Grief is shown without blame: Jacob's white eyes (12:84), the tears of 9:92 among those with *no blame*, Maryam's *I wish I had died* (19:23), answered with a stream.
- **Grief and despair never share a verse.** Jacob grieves *to* God, then forbids despair *of* God with a command to search.
- The one grief cautioned consumes the self over others' choices (35:8, 18:6), and its cure is a lighter load: *God knows what they do* (35:8).
- Grief is a burden, not a sin: the Garden's people thank God *who removed al-ḥazan from us* (35:34).

**[interpretive]** *Lā taḥzan* is a prohibition in grammar and a comfort in function.

## 4. The fault in despair

**Data.** 6 faulted cases have God's side as object: His *rawḥ* (12:87), mercy and glad tidings (15:55-56, 29:23, 39:53), the hereafter (60:13). 4 are the reflex *when harm touches him* (11:9, 17:83, 30:36, 41:49), with no object, swinging into exultation or turning away once eased; only 11:11 names a cure, *those who are patient and do righteous deeds*. Unblamed: despair of a thing (12:80; rain, 42:28, 30:49) and the messengers' despair, object unstated (12:110), each followed by an act of God. *Iblās* follows refused humbling (6:43-44, 23:76-77).

**The closed sentence**, in the faulted thinker's words:

| Sentence | Ref | Closes |
|---|---|---|
| *God will never help him* (*an lan*) | 22:15 ("him" disputed) | future help |
| *they will never return, ever* | 48:12 | an outcome, faulted as a thought about God |
| *God does not know much of what you do* | 41:22 | His knowledge |
| *God and His Messenger promised us only delusion* | 33:12 | His truthfulness |
| *My Lord has humiliated me* | 89:16 | His regard |
| implied by the ban: *my wrongs are beyond Him* | 39:53 | His forgiveness |

**Presumption is the twin.** *Ẓanna/ḥasiba an lan*, "thought that … would never", occurs **7 times** about God's side: 2 despair (22:15, 48:12), 4 presumption (75:3, 84:14, 90:5, 72:7), and Jonah (21:87, translators split three ways). The reflex man shows both: despair in harm (41:49), *I do not think the Hour will come* in ease (41:50). **[interpretive]** The fault is the closing, in either direction; hope is paired with fear (7:56) because each keeps God's side open against one of them.

**Not faulted: sentences about oneself.** *We will be overtaken* (26:61); *we have no strength today* (2:249); *I wish I had died* (19:23); *adversity has touched me* (21:83); *I am in need* (28:24). And the question:

> **2:214** وَزُلۡزِلُواْ حَتَّىٰ يَقُولَ ٱلرَّسُولُ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ مَعَهُۥ مَتَىٰ نَصۡرُ ٱللَّهِۗ أَلَآ إِنَّ نَصۡرَ ٱللَّهِ قَرِيبࣱ
> *…and were shaken until [even their] messenger and those who believed with him said, "When is the help of Allah?" Unquestionably, the help of Allah is near.* (Saheeh)

**"When?" is answered with *near*. "Never" is the fault.**

At the siege, hypocrites and believers use the same words, *waʿadanā llāhu wa-rasūluhu*: *promised us only delusion* (33:12) against *this is what God and His Messenger promised us* (33:22). **[interpretive]** The faithful read the extremity as inside the promise, since 2:214 had promised the shaking.

**A closed sentence can be taken back.** Jonah *thought We would never constrain him* (21:87), and in the dark said *lā ilāha illā anta subḥānaka innī kuntu mina ẓ-ẓālimīn*. *So We answered him… thus do We rescue the believers* (21:88). The ban on despair is addressed to *those who have wronged themselves* (39:53).

The Quran never commands "think well of God"; only *ẓann al-sawʾ* appears (48:6, 48:12). No verse commands a feeling of hope; the ban on despair comes with a ground (39:53).

## 5. Those who waited

Eleven waiters (versepack `those-who-waited`, plus 37:100):

| Who | What they said | What came |
|---|---|---|
| Jacob | *perhaps God will bring them all* (12:83); *I complain of my grief to God* (12:86) | all three sons, and his sight (12:93-100) |
| Zechariah | *grant me good offspring* (3:38); *do not leave me alone* (21:89) | *fa-stajabnā*: John, his wife made well (21:90) |
| Abraham and Sarah | *grant me one of the righteous* (37:100) | tidings (37:101) |
| Job | *adversity has touched me* (21:83), **no request** | harm removed; family *and the like with them* (21:84) |
| Jonah | confession, glorification (21:87), **no request** | rescue from *ghamm* (21:88) |
| Moses's mother | **no prayer recorded** | promise first (28:7); son returned (28:13) |
| Maryam | *I wish I had died* (19:23); **no prayer recorded** | a voice, a stream, dates (19:24-25) |
| Moses, flight and sea | *save me*; *perhaps my Lord will guide me*; *I am in need*; *my Lord is with me* (28:21-24, 26:62) | the sea parted; a household and work (28:25-28) |
| Moses and Aaron | *obliterate their wealth* (10:88) | *your prayer is answered, so stay straight* (10:89) |
| Joseph | *unless You avert their plan* (12:33) | averted, exactly as asked (12:34) |
| Noah | *I am overpowered, so help* (54:10) | the ship (21:76, 54:11-14) |

- **9 of 11 pray**; Moses's mother and Maryam say nothing to God and still receive His care. **6 name the thing; 2 ask nothing** (Job, Jonah) and both get *fa-stajabnā*.
- **No exact length is given for any waiter's pain.** Old age is named for three; the only spans in years are vague and told afterwards: Joseph's "several years" in prison (12:42), Moses's "years" in Madyan (20:40). Noah's 950 years (29:14) is his mission.
- **The answer differs from the request in at least 6**: more (Job, Zechariah), different (Moses), relief only (Jonah).
- **6 get a promise before the outcome** (28:7, 11:70-71, 3:39, 19:24, 11:36, 10:89); in most a command comes with it (cast him, build, stay straight).
- **Inner states go unrebuked**: white eyes (12:84), *how will I have a boy?* (19:8), *I wish I had died*, an empty heart (28:10). Only Jonah is censured (37:142), for leaving, not for distress.
- **No one stops asking.** Only Jacob's sons tell him to stop (12:85), and the story proves them wrong (12:96).

## 6. At breaking point

**The Prophet consoled** (27 units): limits on his responsibility 6, instruction 5, reframe 4, precedent 3, past care 3, presence 2, **promise 2** (93:4-5, 94:5-6), naming 2. Seven commands not to grieve each give a reason about God, and **none says the outcome will change**. 93:3 denies the feared inference: *your Lord has not taken leave of you, nor detested you.* 15:97-98 pairs *We know that your breast is constrained* with an act: glorify, prostrate. **[interpretive]** The text mostly lightens and orients; it seldom forecasts.

**The believers shaken.** Their 9 sentences (2:156, 2:214, 2:249, 2:250, 3:147, 3:173, 33:22, 9:40, 26:62) set God in relation to the speaker (*to Him*, *enough for us*, *with me*) or ask Him, **inward first**: patience or forgiveness, then firm feet, then victory. Frightened sentences without God in the same scenes (26:61, 2:249) are answered, not rebuked.

**What trial is for**: to make known who strives, is patient or is truthful (3:142, 9:16, 29:3, 47:31), to purify (3:141), that people might return (7:168) or humble themselves (6:42), *so that you would not grieve* (3:153), *which of you is best in deed* (67:2). Ease tests too (21:35).

**Displeasure or love?** No verse calls a believer's trial a sign of either. Some disasters are tied to people's own doing, each time with pardon beside it (3:152; *for what your hands have earned, but He pardons much*, 42:30). Love attaches to the response: *God loves the patient* (3:146), *those who rely* (3:159).

> **89:16** وَأَمَّآ إِذَا مَا ٱبۡتَلَىٰهُ فَقَدَرَ عَلَيۡهِ رِزۡقَهُۥ فَيَقُولُ رَبِّيٓ أَهَٰنَنِ
> **89:17** كَلَّاۖ
> *But when He tries him and restricts his provision, he says, "My Lord has humiliated me." No!* (Saheeh)

*Kallā* rejects both readings, wealth as honour (89:15) and restriction as disgrace. **[interpretive]** Trial is a question, not a verdict.

## 7. Wishes, answers and refusals

**Wishing is not hoping.** The wishing words are **never addressed to God**: 8 of 15 are faulted or forbidden (a wish in place of deed or proof, 2:111, 4:123; another's share, 4:32), 4 are a test refused, 3 are met, contested or reversed. **4:32 redirects the desire**: *do not wish for what God favoured others with… ask God of His bounty.*

**Answers.** God answers in 14 ayahs, including four consecutive *fa-stajabnā lahu* (21:76, 84, 88, 90). Conditions are mixed: none for *the desperate one when he calls* (27:62); belief or repentance elsewhere (2:186, 11:61). The general promises (2:186, 40:60) name no content. 10:89 says *your prayer has been answered, so stay straight*.

**Named asks (13).** **9 granted**: Zechariah's heir, Abraham's righteous son (37:100-101), a good child (7:189), fruits for Mecca (2:126), Moses's ease and Aaron (20:36), water (2:60), a kingdom (38:35), a table (5:114), onions and lentils (2:61). **1 narrowed**: *not the wrongdoers* (2:124). **3 refused**: Noah's son (11:46), Abraham's father (9:114), *show me Yourself* (7:143).
- **Rebukes concern the object, never the length**, and give reasons: asking against a known ruling (11:46, 9:113), this world *alone* (2:200), the lesser for the better (2:61, granted anyway).
- **Refusals are met with submission**: Noah seeks refuge from *asking what I have no knowledge of* (11:47); Moses is told *take what I have given you* (7:144).

**"Better than it."** Seven sentences promise or hope for something better (8:70, 18:40, 18:81, 66:5, 68:32, 24:55, 25:70). **None is shown arriving.** Returns *with more* are narrated without that phrase: Job (21:84, 38:43), Joseph (12:100-101).

## 8. When the wish is not given

**Keep asking, by name.** No one is rebuked for asking long. 17:11 (*man is ever hasty*) describes the asker and says nothing about God's delay.

> **19:4** رَبِّ إِنِّي وَهَنَ ٱلۡعَظۡمُ مِنِّي وَٱشۡتَعَلَ ٱلرَّأۡسُ شَيۡبࣰا وَلَمۡ أَكُنۢ بِدُعَآئِكَ رَبِّ شَقِيࣰّا
> *"My Lord, indeed my bones have weakened, and my head has filled with white, and never have I been in my supplication to You, my Lord, unhappy."* (Saheeh); *"…never been disappointed in my prayer to You."* (Khattab)

- The ground is a history: asking never left him *shaqiyy*. No number of years is given.
- He asked *hunālika* (3:38), just after hearing *God provides for whom He wills without account* (3:37). **[interpretive]** Seeing God give to another, he asked for his own: 4:32 acted out.
- 21:89 names the thing and God's sufficiency together: *do not leave me alone, and You are the best of inheritors.*
- **Two patterns**: naming the thing (more frequent, mostly granted) and open-handed asking (*whatever good You send down*, 28:24; 2:201).

**"Perhaps it is bad for you" is not said of a withheld wish.** 2:216 concerns a command, 4:19 a present spouse, 18:79-81 losses. The three passages about something withheld (18:82, 28:79-82, 42:27) never say a believer's prayer was refused because it would harm. **Applying 2:216 to an unanswered prayer is analogy**: reasonable, but analogy.

**Hearts are God's to join.** God joins hearts in 3 of 4 *allafa* heart-tokens; the human subject is negated: *if you had spent all that is on earth, you could not have joined their hearts* (8:63). Affection is God's placing (30:21, 60:7, 19:96); force is ruled out (4:19, 10:99). The prayer is Abraham's: *make hearts incline toward them* (14:37).

**Holding on, letting go.** Jacob, Moses's mother and Abraham are never told to stop wanting; each letting-go answers a specific command (28:7, 37:102). **[interpretive]** Neither is a general rule to release a wish.

**No prayer in the Quran asks God to take a love out of the heart.** The heart-prayers ask that it not swerve (3:8), hold no rancour (59:10), be expanded (20:25), be at rest (2:260).

## 9. Relief: with, after, near, never dated

> **94:5** فَإِنَّ مَعَ ٱلۡعُسۡرِ يُسۡرًا
> **94:6** إِنَّ مَعَ ٱلۡعُسۡرِ يُسۡرࣰا
> *For indeed, with hardship [will be] ease. Indeed, with hardship [will be] ease.* (Saheeh)

- ***Maʿa***, *with*, twice; *al-ʿusr* definite, *yusr* indefinite. At 65:7 it is ***baʿda***, *after*, with both nouns indefinite. Khattab's "[more]" at 94:6 is not in the Arabic, and "one hardship, two eases" is a tafsīr argument; the text does not count.
- The ease of 94 is followed by instruction: *exert yourself, and to your Lord turn your longing* (94:7-8). In 65:2-7 it is tied to *taqwā* and reliance (65:3).
- ***Kashf***: God alone removes harm (6:17, 10:107), answers *the desperate one* (27:62), *if He wills* (6:41). In 6 of 16 removals the relieved relapse (10:12).
- ***Najāt***: *it is binding upon Us to save the believers* (10:103). **[interpretive]** Rescue, not exemption from trial (2:214).
- **Time comes as prepositions, never dates**: *with*, *after*, *near* (2:214, 61:13).

## 10. Negative space, and how the investigation corrected itself

**Absent.** *Faraj*. Any dated relief, or exact length of a wait. A promise of the specific thing to the general reader (named promises go to named people, 28:7, 3:39). "Better than it" shown arriving. Trial called displeasure or love. A prayer to remove a love. A rebuke for asking long. First-person *rajā*. Grief called a sin.

**Not in the Quran, though often attributed to it**: "I am as My servant thinks of Me" and "relief comes with distress" (hadith); "if it is meant for you it will come" (nowhere; 9:51 and 57:22-23 concern what befalls, not what is wished); "one hardship, two eases" and "*ʿasā* from God is certain" (tafsīr); istikhāra (a hadith practice).

**Self-corrections** (full log in `README.md`). The ones that changed the picture: hope may name the thing (12:83 and 7:129 shown fulfilled; naming is the more frequent pattern); the fault is narrower than "despair" (57:23 is grief; 12:110's object unstated; *an lan* mostly presumption); the edge is not depth (6:44 strikes at the top); grief is consoled (14 + 3 + 1, none blaming); comfort was trimmed where it overreached (2:216 never applied to a withheld thing; 17:11 silent on delay; 25:74 presupposes spouses).

**Resolved here.** (a) "No duration in years for any pain" becomes *no exact length*: 12:42 and 20:40 give vague spans, told afterwards. (b) "Despair is faulted only when its object is God (10 of 16)" becomes 6 with God's side as object plus 4 object-less reflexes. (c) "Abraham's tidings came unasked" holds only for 11:69-73 and 51:24-30.

## Open questions

1. **71:13**: is *lā tarjūna li-llāhi waqārā* hope for God's dignity, or awe of it? Translators split.
2. **22:15**: whose help does the thinker deny, his own or the Prophet's?
3. **21:87**: is Jonah's *an lan naqdira ʿalayh* "constrain", "decree" or "restrict"? It decides whether his was a closed sentence about God.
4. **3:153**: how does *distress upon distress* keep someone from grieving? The text gives the purpose, not the mechanism.
5. ***Rawḥ* and *raḥma*** fill the same slot (12:87, 39:53). One meaning, or neighbours?
6. **The untold stories** (40:78): does the selection of 11:120 imply others ended differently? The text does not say.

## For the one losing hope

- You may name what you want and say it the Quran's way: *perhaps my Lord will…* (12:83, 28:22). Jacob did, and grieved while he waited.
- Grief, fear, tears, *when?* and *I wish I had died* are shown without blame, and often answered (2:214, 9:92, 19:23-24).
- The sentence to refuse is a closed one about God: *He will never*, *He does not know*, *He has humiliated me*. If you have said it, Jonah's words take it back (21:87).
- Keep asking. No one in the Quran is told off for asking long, and no one is told by God to stop wanting.
- Do one thing in your hands, and set down what is not yours to carry (19:25, 94:7-8, 35:8).
- Help is called *near*, and ease comes *with* hardship. Neither is dated, and the specific thing is not promised.
