# Hope and despair — Phase-0 structurals (orchestrator)

> Census: `scripts/build_hope_census.py` → `data/structural/hope-census.json`.
> Verses: `scripts/build_hope_versepack.py` → `data/structural/hope-versepack.json` (525 verses, 14 groups).

## Headline

**The Quran's hope verb (`rajā`, 23 hope-sense tokens) almost never has a worldly object.** In 19 of 23 the object is God, His mercy, the meeting with Him or the Last Day; and 11 of the 23 are **negated** — *"those who do not hope for the meeting with Us"* describes deniers 4× (10:7, 10:11, 10:15, 25:21); 7 of the 11 negations concern the hereafter *(CORRECTED from "7×" by the hope-words agent)*. Worldly hope is carried by a different word: **`ʿasā` ("perhaps")**, and in 15 of its 28 ayahs **God is the subject** — *perhaps God will…*. Despair is faulted only when its object is God (10 of 16); despair of people or outcomes is left unblamed, and twice the rain falls exactly *after* people despaired (42:28, 30:49).

**Polysemy traps.**
- **`rjw` has 5 non-hope tokens**: *postpone* (7:111, 26:36, 33:51), *deferred* (9:106) and *edges* (69:17). Count hope by occurrence, never by root.
- **`frj` has no "relief" sense at all.** All 9 tokens mean *chastity, private parts* or *rifts, openings* (50:6, 77:9). **The popular word for relief, *faraj*, does not occur in the Quran.** The Quran's relief words are `rawḥ`, `yusr`, `kashf al-ḍurr`, `najāt` and `makhraj` (65:2).
- **`ghmm`**: 4 of 11 tokens are `ghamām`, *clouds*.
- **`yʾs` at 13:31 is "have they not known"**, and at 65:4 it is legal (women past menstruation). Neither is despair.
- **`mny`**: 3 tokens are `manī`/`yumnā`, *semen* (53:46, 56:58, 75:37).
- **`ṭmʿ` is two-valued**: praised in 8 cases (aspiring to God's forgiveness and mercy) and faulted in 3 (greed: 33:32, 70:38, 74:15).
- **Grief has near-synonyms**: `taʾsa` (`Asw`, 4×: 5:26, 5:68, 7:93, 57:23) and `ibtaʾasa` (`bAs`, 2×: 11:36, 12:69). Both are in the versepack's `grief-synonyms` group.

## 2. What `rajā` hopes for (data-grounded; annotation in the census)

| Object | Affirmed | Negated |
|---|---|---|
| The meeting with God / the Last Day / resurrection / reckoning / the days of God | 5 | **7** |
| God's mercy / from God / God's grandeur | 5 | 2 |
| A trade that will not perish (35:29) | 1 | — |
| Worldly: a person (11:62), marriage (24:60), the Book being sent (28:86) | 1 | 2 |

- **Affirmed hope is conditional on action** in its key verses: *whoever hopes for the meeting with his Lord — let him do righteous work* (18:110). The same holds at 29:5, 33:21, 60:6, 2:218 (believed, emigrated, strove), 35:29 (recite, pray, spend) and 39:9 (standing in the night).
- **4:104 holds both polarities in one verse**: *if you are suffering, they suffer as you suffer — but you hope from God what they do not hope.* Same pain, different hope.
- **28:86, the one thing not hoped for that was given**: *you were not hoping that the Book would be cast to you — except as a mercy from your Lord.* **[interpretive]** It is the Quran's single case of an un-hoped-for gift, and the gift is the revelation itself.

## 3. `ʿasā`: the grammar of worldly hope (data-grounded)

Of 28 ayahs, **15 make God the subject**: *perhaps God / your Lord will…*. Another is Abraham's *perhaps I will not be unhappy in calling my Lord* (19:48). The rest are general (6: *perhaps you hate a thing…*), human (3) or rebukes (2).

The prophets' and believers' own hopes in a hard moment are almost all `ʿasā` + God:

| Ref | Speaker | Hope |
|---|---|---|
| 12:83 | Jacob | *beautiful patience — perhaps God will bring them all to me* |
| 28:22 | Moses, fleeing | *perhaps my Lord will guide me to the right way* |
| 7:129 | Moses, to his oppressed people | *perhaps your Lord will destroy your enemy* |
| 18:24 | the Prophet, told | *perhaps my Lord will guide me nearer than this* |
| 18:40 | the believer with the poorer garden | *perhaps my Lord will give me better than your garden* |
| 68:32 | the garden-owners, after losing it | *perhaps our Lord will give us better than it; we turn in longing to our Lord* |
| 60:7 | God | *perhaps God will put affection between you and those you were enemies with* |

The word also carries the Quran's warning about one's own judgement of outcomes: **2:216** (*perhaps you hate a thing and it is good for you, and perhaps you love a thing and it is bad for you*) and **4:19**, said of a wife one dislikes (*perhaps you dislike a thing and God places much good in it*).

## 4. Despair: whose is faulted (data-grounded)

- **`yʾs`/`qnṭ`, 16 ayahs.** 10 are faulted or forbidden. 6 are unblamed or neutral.
- **Six faulted cases have God's side as the object**: His mercy (15:56, 29:23, 39:53), His *rawḥ* (12:87), His glad tidings (15:55) or the hereafter (60:13). **The other four are the human reflex "when harm touches him"**, with no object named: 11:9, 17:83, 30:36, 41:49.
- **Every reflex case pairs despair with its opposite**: exultation or ingratitude in ease (11:10, 41:50, 30:36). **11:11 names the exception: *except those who are patient and do righteous deeds.*** (The regret investigation found the same pairing at 57:23.)
- **Unblamed despair**: the messengers despairing of their people (12:110), people before rain (42:28), the brothers (12:80).
- **`bls` (`iblās`, 5 ayahs) is the despair of the end**: 4 of 5 are in punishment (6:44, 23:77, 30:12, 43:75), and the fifth is people before rain (30:49).
- **"Until, when…" (*ḥattā idhā*) runs both ways.**
  - For the messengers, the edge of despair is where help arrives (12:110; cf. 2:214 *until the Messenger said: when is God's help?*), and the three left behind are turned to once the earth closes in on them (9:118).
  - For the heedless, the same construction ends in sudden seizure and `iblās` (6:44, 23:77).
  - **This is the main stress test for "help comes at the edge".**

## 5. Hope with fear (data-grounded)

Hope and fear share an ayah 7 times (the census's 8th, 10:15, is incidental):
- **in prayer, commanded or praised**: 7:56, 32:16 (*call on Him in fear and aspiration*), 21:90 (*raghaban wa rahaban*);
- **in the devotee**: 17:57, 39:9;
- **in the sign of lightning**: 13:12, 30:24. The same flash brings fear and hope.

**7:56 continues: *surely God's mercy is near to those who do good.*** Fear of God is often commanded alone (e.g. 3:175); hope as such is never commanded with `rajā` — Shuʿayb's *urjū l-yawma l-ākhir* (29:36) is the only imperative of the verb.

## 6. Answers, replacement, ease (data-grounded)

- **God answers** (`istajāba`, `ajāba`, `mujīb`, God as subject): 14 ayahs. They include the promises 2:186 and 40:60, *who answers the desperate one when he calls* (27:62), and **four consecutive "So We answered him" in Sūrat al-Anbiyāʾ (21:76, 84, 88, 90)**: Noah, Job, Jonah, Zechariah. **10:89 says *your prayer has been answered* — then *so stay straight*.** The text shows no fulfilment at that point.
- **Replacement, "better than it"** (`khayran min` with a giving verb, or `bdl`, God as giver): 8:70 (*better than what was taken from you*), 18:40, 18:81 (al-Khidr: *a better son*), 66:5, 68:32, 24:55 (*He will replace their fear with security*), 25:70 (*bad deeds into good*). 70:41 is punitive. 7:95 is the warning case: good in place of bad, then seized.
- **Hardship and ease share 6 ayahs**: 2:185, 2:280, 65:7 (*God will bring ease after hardship*), 92:10, and **94:5-6, where ease is *with* hardship (`maʿa`), stated twice.** Sūrat al-Sharḥ then closes on ***and to your Lord turn your longing*** (`fa-rghab`, 94:8). **`rgb` + *to God* occurs 3×, and each time follows loss or contentment: 9:59, 68:32, 94:8.**

## 7. Grief (data-grounded)

- **`ḥzn`, 42 ayahs.**
  - **18 are commands not to grieve** (or *let it not grieve you*). Most are said to the Prophet about people's rejection. The others go to the believers at Uḥud (3:139, 3:153), Abū Bakr in the cave (9:40), Maryam (19:24), Moses's mother (20:40, 28:7, 28:13) and Lot (29:33).
  - **13 are the formula *no fear on them, nor will they grieve*.**
- **What thinking of God does**: there are 5 *ẓann bi-llāh* ayahs. At 3:154, 48:6 and 41:23 the thought is ill. At 33:10 it is the trench, hearts at the throats. At 37:87 Abraham asks *what do you think of the Lord of the worlds?*

## 8. What Phase 1 should test

1. Is Quranic hope fundamentally **God-directed** (`rajā`), with worldly hope only ever spoken as *perhaps God will* (`ʿasā`)? Look for counter-cases.
2. **Help at the edge**: does the text place relief at the point of despair, systematically or only in some stories? What separates the messengers' *ḥattā idhā* from the heedless one?
3. **The replacement pattern**: when something wanted is not given, or is taken, does the Quran promise *it*, or *better than it*, or neither?
4. **Wishing vs hoping**: `amāniyy` and `tamannī` are faulted (4:123, 57:14, 53:24). Where is the line between a wish and a hope?
5. **Grief is allowed, despair of God is not.** Jacob grieves and is never blamed (12:84-86). Test it.

Divergence (top of core set): 57:23 (0.765), 29:5, 17:83, **53:24 (0.740: *or shall man have whatever he wishes?*)**, 71:13 (*rajā* as hope or as fear?), 78:27, 37:75.
