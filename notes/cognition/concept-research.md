# Concept profile: research / inquiry / cognition in the Quran

> Multi-root analysis. The English word "research" has no single Quranic equivalent — instead the Quran deploys a constellation of cognitive verbs. This note documents the constellation, then deep-dives the closest functional analog: ف-ك-ر (fkr, "to reflect").
> Sources: `data/concepts/bHv.json`, `data/concepts/fkr.json`, root survey across `data/morphology/roots.json`.

## The headline finding

**The modern Arabic word for "research" — بحث (b-ḥ-th) — appears exactly once in the entire Quran.**

That single occurrence is at **5:31 (Al-Maaida)**, the verse about Cain and Abel. After Cain murders his brother Abel, Allah sends a crow that **يَبۡحَثُ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ** ("scratches/digs/searches in the earth") to teach Cain how to bury his brother's body.

| Source | How يَبْحَثُ is rendered |
|---|---|
| Literal word-by-word | "it (was) scratching" |
| Saheeh | "searching" |
| Pickthall | "scratching up" |
| Khattab | "digging" |
| Arberry | "scratching into" |

The etymology is exposed by the translator disagreement: the original Arabic verb means **a bird clawing the soil** to uncover or hide what lies beneath. The metaphorical sense — "to investigate, to research" — is downstream of this physical image. The modern academic activity we call "research" descends from a Quranic image of a crow teaching a murderer how to bury his crime.

This single verse already tells us something about the Quran's epistemic landscape: the Quran does **not have a noun-concept for "research"** as a settled academic activity. What it has instead is a **verbal vocabulary of cognitive acts**, each describing a different mode of inquiry.

Two structural facts jump out from this table:

1. **The Quran's cognitive vocabulary is plural and process-oriented.** Where modern English collapses many activities under "research," the Quran distinguishes them: knowing, remembering, judging, seeing-with-insight, observing, asking, finding, seeking, reading, reasoning, pondering, understanding, reflecting, taking-a-moral. These are not synonyms — each carries a different cognitive mode.

2. **The most common roots are about *knowing-as-state* (ʿilm, dhikr, ḥikm) rather than *seeking-as-action* (baḥth, ṭalab).** ʿilm appears 854 times; baḥth, 1. The Quran's epistemic emphasis is *receiving* and *perceiving* knowledge much more than *constructing* it through systematic search. This is a profoundly different epistemology than the modern one.

## Deep dive: ف-ك-ر (fkr, "to reflect")

Of all the roots in the constellation, **fkr** is the closest functional analog to "research" as an *active cognitive verb* (rather than knowledge-as-state). It always denotes the act, never the state. It is what a person *does* with material before them.

### Stats

| | |
|---|---|
| Root | ف-ك-ر (`fkr`) |
| Total occurrences | 18 |
| Lemmas | 2 |
| Ayahs | 18 |
| Surahs | 13 / 114 |
| Revelation split | 13 Meccan · 5 Medinan |

### The morphology already tells us something

Two lemmas, but their distribution is striking:

| Lemma (Arabic) | Pattern | Count | What the form means |
|---|---|---|---|
| يَتَفَكَّرُ (`yatafak~aru`) | Form V (tafaʿʿala) | **17** | reflexive-intensive: "to reflect repeatedly, to turn over in one's mind" |
| فَكَّرَ (`fak~ara`) | Form II (faʿʿala) | 1 | causative-intensive: "to make think, to consider" |

**17 of 18 occurrences are in the reflexive-intensive (Form V).** In Arabic morphology, Form V (tafaʿʿala) marks an action turned back upon the actor — the doer is also the object. *Tafakkur* is therefore not "thinking once" but "thinking-and-being-changed-by-thinking, repeatedly." The grammatical pattern itself encodes that real reflection is iterative and self-modifying.

### The signature formula

**8 of 18 ayahs (44%) follow a recurring structural formula:**

> "...إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَءَايَٰتࣲ لِّقَوۡمࣲ يَتَفَكَّرُونَ"
>
> "Truly in that are signs for a people who reflect."

These 8 ayahs span: **10:24, 13:3, 16:11, 16:69, 30:21, 39:42, 45:13, 7:176**.

The pattern places three concepts in fixed structural relationship:
- **āyāt** (signs / verses) — the things to be reflected upon
- **qawm** (a people) — reflection is communal, not solitary
- **yatafakkarūn** (they reflect) — the active verbal mode

The "signs" in these formulas are almost always natural phenomena: rain, vegetation, the cycle of day and night, bees and honey, marriage and procreation, the heavens and the earth. The Quran is repeatedly placing the natural world in the role of "research material" — and prescribing communal reflection as the appropriate response.

Three things to note here:
- The **people-and-signs cluster** (qwm + Ayy at 56% each) confirms the formulaic pattern.
- The **clarity cluster** (byn at 39%) — reflection follows from things being *made clear* (yubayyinu llāhu lakum al-āyāt). The data is presented; reflection is the expected response.
- The **earth-heavens-fruits cluster** (ArD + smw + vmr) — the prescribed objects of reflection are concrete, observable, natural.

### Illustrative ayahs — the four-translator panel

**3:191** — perhaps the closest the Quran comes to defining reflective practice:

| | |
|---|---|
| Arabic word | وَيَتَفَكَّرُونَ ("and they reflect") |
| Literal | "and they reflect" |
| Saheeh | "Who remember Allah while standing or sitting or [lying] on their sides and give thought to the creation of the heavens and the earth, [saying], 'Our Lord, You did not create this aimlessly…'" |
| Pickthall | "Such as remember Allah, standing, sitting, and reclining, and consider the creation of the heavens and the earth…" |
| Khattab | (similar wording, contemplative posture) |
| Arberry | "who remember God, standing and sitting and on their sides, and reflect upon the creation of the heavens and the earth: 'Our Lord, Thou hast not created this for vanity…'" |

Reflection here is: (a) tied to remembrance of God, (b) practiced in any bodily posture (it's continuous, not ritual-bound), (c) directed at creation, (d) issuing in a verbal response that affirms purpose ("not in vain"). All four translators converge — when the meaning is well-bounded, English agrees.

**6:50** — the rhetorical-challenge mode:

> "Are the blind and the seeing equal? **Will you not then give thought?**" (Saheeh)
> "Will ye not then take thought?" (Pickthall)

*Tafakkur* here is positioned as the response that distinguishes belief from disbelief. Refusing to reflect is not neutral — it is structurally aligned with refusing to see.

**2:219** — reflection as response to ethical teaching:

The verse on wine and gambling concludes "*…thus Allah makes clear to you the verses [āyāt] that you might give thought*." Reflection isn't reserved for cosmology; it's the appropriate response to ethical revelation as well.

## What the data tells us about "research" in the Quran

A first-principles reading from the structure alone — not from commentary:

1. **There is no "research" as such.** The activity Westerners call research — solo, library-bound, citation-accumulating, hypothesis-testing — has no single Quranic word. The closest etymological match (baḥth) appears once and means a bird scratching dirt.

2. **Cognition is plural, not unified.** The Quran distinguishes at least 14 cognitive activities by giving each its own root. ʿilm (knowing-as-state), naẓar (looking-as-observation), tafakkur (reflecting-as-iteration), tadabbur (pondering-deeply-on-text), fiqh (deep-grasp), dhikr (remembrance) — each names a different cognitive operation.

3. **The center of gravity is on receiving, not constructing.** The most-frequent cognitive roots (*ʿilm* 854, *dhikr* 292, *ḥikma* 210) all describe knowledge as something received and held. Even "thinking" — *tafakkur* — is reflexive: the thinker is changed by the act, not the thinker constructing the object. The Quranic epistemology is fundamentally **responsive** to a world that is already a sign-system.

4. **"Research" in the Quran is communal.** The signature formula is *li-qawmin yatafakkarūn* — "for a people who reflect." Not a person who reflects. The collective frame shapes the entire epistemic stance: knowledge-seeking is a community practice, not a solitary scholar's labor.

5. **The objects of inquiry are natural phenomena.** The signs to be reflected upon are rain, fruit, day and night, the heavens and earth, marriage, the bee and honey. The Quran's prescribed "research material" is *the world as it presents itself*. Books, texts, and prior scholarship are not the primary site.

6. **The Form V (reflexive-intensive) morphology of *tafakkur* signals iteration and self-modification.** Real reflection is not arriving at a conclusion once. The morphology itself encodes that the thinker must keep turning the material over, and is changed by the turning.

## How this contrasts with modern academic research

(This section is more interpretive than the data alone supports — flagged so the reader can take or leave it.)

| Modern academic research | Quranic *tafakkur* |
|---|---|
| Solo scholar | Communal — *qawm* who reflect |
| Archival, citation-based | Observational — natural signs |
| Builds knowledge cumulatively | Recognizes signs already present |
| Hypothesis → test → conclude | Turn-over → be-changed → turn-over again |
| Conclusion as goal | Recognition of purpose ("not in vain") as goal |
| The knower acts on the object | The knower is acted upon by what is reflected upon |

These are not necessarily opposed — modern research can be communal, can engage natural observation, can be iterative. But the Quranic framing makes a different set of choices about what counts as the *core* of cognitive work.
