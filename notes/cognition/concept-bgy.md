# Concept profile: ب-غ-ي (`bgy`) — to seek / desire / aim for / transgress

> **Source:** Parallel-agent deep-read, deep "research" investigation, 2026-04-26.
> **Data:** `data/concepts/bgy.json` (96 occurrences, 8 lemmas, 90 ayahs, 41 surahs, 55 Meccan / 41 Medinan)
> **Critical feature:** the root is BIVALENT — same root means both "seek/desire" (positive) AND "transgress/wrong" (negative).

## The bivalent root

The root **ب-غ-ي** is structurally unique among the Quran's cognitive vocabulary because it is **morally bipolar by design**. The same three consonants that generate the Quran's most reverent verb of pursuit — *ابتغاء وجه الله* ("seeking the face of God") — also generate its sharpest noun for tyranny: *البَغْي* ("oppression," which God *forbids* alongside *fahsha'* and *munkar* in 16:90). 96 occurrences across 90 ayahs and 41 surahs, distributed 55 Meccan / 41 Medinan, span from chaste petition to political revolt.

The pivot is etymological: bgy denotes **directed wanting that exceeds**. To desire is to reach out; to transgress is to reach past one's right. The Arabic root collapses pursuit and overreach into one phonetic identity. The Quran does not soften this — it exploits it.

## Sense distribution (96 occurrences, 8 lemmas)

| Sense | Count | Primary lemmas |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive — "seek / desire / aim for"** | ~57 | `ابتغى` ibtaghaa (34), `ابتغاء` ibtighaa' (14), and 9 of 25 `بغى` baghaa |
| **Negative — "transgress / oppress / rebel / be insolent"** | ~30 | `بغْي` baghy (11), `باغ` baagh (3), and 16 of 25 `بغى` baghaa |
| **Modal — "is fitting / befits"** | 6 | `يَنبغي` yanbaghi (always negated: "it does not befit") |
| **Sexual transgression — "unchaste / prostitution"** | 3 | `بغيّ` baghiyy (2, of Mary in 19:20, 19:28), `بغاء` bighaa' (1, in 24:33) |

Note the distribution by **lemma morphology**: Form VIII (*ابتغى*) is **exclusively positive** in all 34 occurrences — the reflexive/intensive stem domesticates the root's energy into focused pursuit. Form I (*بغى*) is **the bivalent form** — 9 positive vs 16 negative — and is where the Quran's moral knife-edge lives. The verbal noun *بغْي* is **uniformly negative** (11/11). The participle *باغ* is uniformly negative (3/3, in the dietary-emergency formula "neither desiring nor transgressing the limit," where *باغ* is paired with *عاد* — already a violation).

## The "seeking the face of Allah" formula

The signature positive construction is **بغي + وجه الله/ربّه** ("seeking the face/countenance of God"). It is rarer than expected — only **4 ayahs** explicitly conjoin bgy with wjh: **2:272, 13:22, 48:29, 92:20**. But the *full* class of "seeking-something-divine" is much larger: bgy + *fadl Allah* (bounty: 2:198, 17:12, 28:73, 30:46, 35:12, 45:12, 62:10), bgy + *mardatihi* (His pleasure: 2:207, 2:265, 4:114, 60:1), bgy + *al-dar al-akhira* (the Hereafter abode: 28:77), bgy + *wajh rabbihi* (92:20). Together these constitute the **pious-pursuit formula** — roughly 18-20 occurrences where the *target* of bgy is divine. This is where ibtighaa'/ibtaghaa cluster.

Co-occurring roots confirm it: **`fDl` (bounty)** appears in 18 ayahs (20%), **`rDw` (pleasure)** in 11, **`wjh` (face)** in 4. These are the *objects* of seeking, not adjacent ideas.

## The transgression cluster — what gets transgressed against?

The negative occurrences fall into five clean buckets:

1. **Against truth/revelation through envy** (5: 2:90, 2:213, 3:19, 42:14, 45:17) — *baghyan baynahum*, "out of jealous animosity between themselves." This is the most theologically loaded use: people split over scripture **not** from ignorance but **after knowledge came**, *baghyan* — out of overreach. Knowledge plus baghy = sectarianism.
2. **Against other people / political tyranny** (38:22, 38:24, 49:9 ×2, 28:76 [Qarun], 10:90 [Pharaoh])
3. **Against the path of Allah** (3:99, 7:45, 7:86, 11:19, 14:3) — the *yabghunaha 'iwajan* formula: "seeking to make it crooked." Note: here bgy is *grammatically* "seeking" but morally negative — the target is corruption.
4. **Against the earth itself** (42:27, 28:77, 10:23) — *al-baghy fi'l-ard*, cosmic/ecological overreach.
5. **Against the sexual/marital order** (19:20, 19:28, 24:33) — *baghiyy* and *bighaa'*.

Co-occurring roots for negatives: **`Zlm` (oppression: 4 ayahs)**, **`Edw` (enmity: 8)**, **`Hqq` (right/truth: 10)** — and Hqq appears specifically in the formula *yabghuna fi'l-ard bi-ghayri'l-haqq* ("transgress in the earth without right," 10:23, 42:42). The Quran's structural answer is in **16:90**, the only ayah pairing `bgy` with `Edl` (justice): God commands *al-'adl* and forbids *al-baghy*. They are explicit antonyms.

## The moral-psychological insight

The Quran exploits the bivalence with surgical clarity. **28:77** uses bgy *twice in one ayah*, in opposite valences:

> *وَٱبۡتَغِ فِيمَآ ءَاتَىٰكَ ٱللَّهُ ٱلدَّارَ ٱلۡأٓخِرَةَ ... وَلَا تَبۡغِ ٱلۡفَسَادَ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ*
> "**Seek** (*ibtaghi*) the home of the Hereafter through what God has given you ... and **seek not** (*la tabghi*) corruption in the earth."

Same verbal energy; opposite objects; opposite moral signs. The lesson is encoded in the grammar: *what you seek is what you become*. The Form VIII intensive pulls bgy upward toward God; the bare Form I, with the wrong object, tips into transgression. **42:27** makes the diagnosis explicit: *"Had God expanded provision for His servants, they would have transgressed (la-baghaw) in the earth"* — abundance, undisciplined, *converts* the seeking-faculty into oppressive overreach. Same energy. Different ceiling.

This is why bgy's modal *ينبغي* always appears **negated** in the Quran (6/6): "it does not befit the Most Merciful to take a son" (19:92), "it is not befitting" for the sun to overtake the moon (36:40). The modal sense — what is "proper" — is the regulator of what may be sought without becoming transgression.

## Five illustrative ayahs (translator panel)

**(1) 92:20 — Pure positive: face of the Lord**
- Saheeh: "But only **seeking the countenance** of his Lord, Most High."
- Pickthall: "Except as **seeking (to fulfil) the purpose** of his Lord Most High."
- Khattab: "but **seeking the pleasure** of their Lord, the Most High."
- Arberry: "only **seeking the Face** of his Lord the Most High."
- *Divergence:* face / purpose / pleasure / Face. Same word, four theologies.

**(2) 16:90 — Pure negative: baghy as antonym of justice**
- Saheeh: "...forbids immorality and bad conduct and **oppression**."
- Pickthall: "...forbiddeth lewdness and abomination and **wickedness**."
- Khattab: "...forbids indecency, wickedness, and **aggression**."
- Arberry: "...He forbids indecency, dishonour, and **insolence**."

**(3) 28:77 — The bivalence in one verse**
> *"And **seek** (ibtaghi) the home of the Hereafter ... and **seek not** (la tabghi) corruption."* — Both bgy. Both imperatives. Inverse valence.

**(4) 4:34 — Genuinely contested middle case**
- Saheeh: "**seek no means** against them"
- Pickthall: "**seek not a way** against them"
- Khattab: "**do not be unjust** to them"
- Arberry: "**look not for any way** against them"
- *Divergence:* Khattab alone reads it as *transgress*, not *seek*. The Arabic *fa-la tabghu 'alayhinna sabilan* hovers — "do not seek a way against them" or "do not transgress against them"? The preposition *'ala* tilts toward transgression. **Khattab disambiguates morally; the others preserve the bivalence.**

**(5) 49:9 — The political-jurisprudential pivot**
- Saheeh / Pickthall / Khattab: "**oppresses / does wrong / transgresses**" — the *baghi* faction must be fought *until it returns to God's command*.
- Arberry: "**insolent**"
- *Note:* This ayah established the Islamic legal category of *al-baghy* (rebellion against legitimate authority). The verb here is technical.

**(6) 19:20 — Mary's denial: bagiyy**
> Mary: *"How can I have a boy ... and I have not been **baghiyyan**?"*
All four translators converge on "**unchaste**." The same root that elsewhere means "seek the face of God" here means sexual overreach. The bivalence is at its sharpest: in one root, the chastest woman in scripture and the verb of her possible transgression.

**(7) 42:27 — The diagnostic verse**
> *"Had God expanded provision, they would have **transgressed** in the earth."*
- Saheeh "committed tyranny," Pickthall "rebel," Khattab "transgressed," Arberry "been **insolent**." All converge on the negative — *but the verb is bare bagha*. The same verb that, with the right object, would be devotional pursuit. Provision without measure flips seeking into transgression.

## Translator divergence — high, as predicted

bgy produces **higher inter-translator divergence than typical roots**, and the pattern is structural: **Khattab tends to *interpret*** the moral valence (rendering ambiguous bgy as "be unjust" in 4:34, "strive to make crooked" everywhere else), while **Arberry tends to *preserve*** the bivalence by reaching for a single English word that holds both senses ("insolent" — overreach with attitude). Pickthall and Saheeh fall in between. The cleanest split: **4:34** (Khattab alone moralizes), **3:99 / 7:45 / 11:19 / 14:3** (Khattab unique with "striving to make crooked"), **42:27** (Arberry alone with "insolent"; others with "rebel/transgress/tyranny").

## Semantic field

Top co-occurring roots: **`Alh`** (Allah, 55) and **`rbb`** (Lord, 19) — bgy is overwhelmingly oriented toward or against the divine. **`fDl`** (bounty, 18) and **`rDw`** (pleasure, 11) anchor the positive pole. **`Zlm`** (oppression, 4), **`Edw`** (enmity, 8), **`Hqq`** (right, 10), and **`Edl`** (justice, 2) anchor the negative. **`sbl`** (path, 13) is the contested object — people seek God's *sabil* and others seek to bend it. **`wjh`** (face, 4) is small but iconic.

Notably *absent* from the top 25: ʿ-q-l, f-k-r, d-b-r — the cognitive-reflection roots. **bgy is not a thinking verb. It is a wanting verb.** It denotes the directional vector of desire, which is morally inert until you specify the target.

## What surprised me

1. **The yanbaghi modal is always negated** (6/6). The Quran never says "this is fitting"; it only says "this does not befit." The modal of propriety enters the text only as a check on overreach. The grammar is theological.
2. **Form VIII is morally rehabilitated.** The reflexive stem *ابتغى* never carries the negative sense. The act of *internalizing* the seeking — taking it on yourself, intensively — is precisely what disciplines it. Bare Form I is dangerous; the reflexive is safe. The Arabic morphology encodes a moral psychology of self-direction.
3. **Mary's denial** (19:20) lands the bivalence most sharply: the same root naming devout seeking is the root she must explicitly disclaim. The Quran knows what it is doing.
4. **The *baghyan baynahum* refrain** (5 occurrences, almost verbatim): people receive knowledge and *then* divide *baghyan* — out of overreach. The Quran's diagnosis of sectarianism is not stupidity but **mis-aimed seeking**.

## One-sentence summary

**bgy is the Quran's verb of directed wanting — a morally neutral vector that becomes worship when aimed at God's face and tyranny when aimed at anything else, and whose Arabic root encodes the doctrine that seeking and overreaching are the same act distinguished only by their object.**
