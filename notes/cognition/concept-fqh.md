# Concept profile: ف-ق-ه (`fqh`) — deep comprehension / "to grasp"

> **Source:** Parallel-agent deep-read, deep "research" investigation, 2026-04-26.
> **Data:** `data/concepts/fqh.json` (20 occurrences, 2 lemmas, 12 surahs, 10 Meccan / 10 Medinan)
> **Critical context:** In modern Islamic discourse, *fiqh* means JURISPRUDENCE — Islamic law. This is a profound semantic narrowing of the Quranic root, which originally meant deep cognitive comprehension. **VERB-ONLY in the Quran (20/20).**

## The distinctive role of fqh

The root ف-ق-ه denotes **comprehension that penetrates beneath the surface** — grasping the inner sense, the gist, the *what-is-actually-meant*. It is cognitively distinct from its neighbors: where **ʿ-l-m** (`Elm`) is the possession of information ("knowing that"), and **ف-ك-ر** (`fkr`) is the *process* of mental turning-over (reflection), **ف-ق-ه** is the *moment of grasp* — the cognitive act of "getting it." When the people of Madyan tell Shuʿayb "**مَا نَفْقَهُ كَثِيرًا مِّمَّا تَقُولُ**" (11:91 — "we do not *fqh* much of what you say"), they are not denying that they hear his words; they are denying that the words *land*, that the meaning becomes graspable. Likewise 17:44 — the heavens and earth glorify God "**وَلَٰكِن لَّا تَفْقَهُونَ تَسْبِيحَهُمْ**" — "but you do not *fqh* their glorification": you can perceive the phenomenon, you cannot penetrate its sense.

Quranically, fqh appears **only as a verb** (20/20 occurrences are POS=V): it is an action of the mind, never a body of doctrine. The noun *fiqh* — and the agent noun *faqīh / fuqahāʾ* — **do not appear in the Quran at all**. This is decisive for the institutional gap discussed below.

The two lemmas are sharply asymmetric:
- **يَفْقَهُ (yafqahu)** — Form I, basic verb "to comprehend": **19 of 20 occurrences**
- **يَتَفَقَّهُ (yatafaqqahu)** — Form V, "to seek to gain deep understanding": **1 occurrence — 9:122**

That single Form V occurrence carries the entire weight of later Islamic technical vocabulary. We return to it.

## The negation pattern — fqh as failure indicator

Working through all 20 occurrences, the distribution is:

| Mode | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Negation ("they do not / hardly / will not comprehend") | **15** | 75% |
| Purpose ("so that they may comprehend" — 6:65, 9:122, 20:28) | 3 | 15% |
| Positive ("a people who comprehend") | 2 (6:98, and the implicit 9:122 outcome) | 10% |
| Counterfactual wish (9:81 — "if only they would *fqh*") | 1 | 5% |

This is striking: **fqh is overwhelmingly an accusation, not a description**. The Quran almost never says "people do comprehend"; it says, again and again, "they do not comprehend." The morphology of the data confirms the hypothesis — `Elm` (knowing) is widely affirmed of believers; `fqh` (deep grasp) is mostly *denied* of opponents. fqh is the cognitive faculty whose *absence* the Quran most insistently diagnoses.

## The hypocrites / disbelievers cluster

The negative cases cluster tightly around two character-groups:

**Hypocrites (munāfiqūn)** — 4:78, 9:81, 9:87, 9:127, 48:15, 59:13, 63:3, 63:7 — fully **8 of 15** negation cases. Note that Surah 63 (al-Munāfiqūn) is named for them; 2 of its occurrences sit there. The diagnostic formula is fixed: **"بِأَنَّهُمْ قَوْمٌ لَّا يَفْقَهُونَ"** — "*because they are a people who do not comprehend*" (8:65, 9:127, 59:13). Hypocrisy in the Quran is therefore not principally moral but **cognitive**: the hypocrite is the one whose tongue and heart are misaligned (63:3 — "they believed, then disbelieved, *so their hearts were sealed* — and they do not *fqh*"), and the symptom of that misalignment is the inability to grasp meaning.

**Sealed-heart unbelievers** — 6:25, 7:179, 17:46, 18:57. Here the recurring image is the **"akinna"** (أَكِنَّة, "coverings") on the heart and "**waqr**" (وَقْر, deafness/heaviness) in the ear: "**وَجَعَلْنَا عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ أَكِنَّةً أَن يَفْقَهُوهُ وَفِي آذَانِهِمْ وَقْرًا**" (6:25, 17:46, 18:57 — near-verbatim repetitions). The co-occurring roots confirm this: **qlb (heart) shares 7/20 ayahs (35%)**; the rare roots `wqr` (heaviness) and `knn` (covering) each appear in 3/20 — they exist almost solely as fqh's antonyms.

The 7:179 vision is the most haunting: people with hearts that do not *fqh*, eyes that do not see, ears that do not hear — "**أُولَٰئِكَ كَالْأَنْعَامِ بَلْ هُمْ أَضَلُّ**" — "they are like cattle; rather, they are more astray." To lack fqh is to fall below the animal floor.

## The 9:122 outlier — the seed verse for fiqh-as-jurisprudence

This is the **single** Quranic verse where fqh is paired with religion:

> **فَلَوْلَا نَفَرَ مِن كُلِّ فِرْقَةٍ مِّنْهُمْ طَائِفَةٌ لِّيَتَفَقَّهُوا فِي الدِّينِ وَلِيُنذِرُوا قَوْمَهُمْ**

The translators read it cautiously — and divergently:

| Translator | Rendering of *liyatafaqqahū fī d-dīn* |
|---|---|
| Saheeh | "to obtain understanding **in the religion**" |
| Pickthall | "may gain sound knowledge **in religion**" |
| Khattab | "to gain **religious knowledge**" |
| Arberry | "to become **learned in religion**" |

Three things matter here:

1. The verb is the **only Form V** in the entire fqh dataset — *tafaqquh* is reflexive/intensive: not just "comprehend" but "**apply oneself to deep comprehension**." This is the morphology that later Islamic vocabulary annexes wholesale.
2. The object is **fī d-dīn** ("in the religion") — *not* "in the law" (fī l-fiqh / fī sh-sharīʿa). The Quran knows no such genitive construction.
3. The **purpose** is *liyundhirū qawmahum* — "to **warn** their people." The function of *tafaqquh* here is pastoral and prophetic, not juridical: the returning party warns, it does not adjudicate.

The semantic narrowing — from "deep comprehension of religion broadly" to "Islamic positive law" — happens centuries later, primarily through hadith literature where the Prophet's prayer "**اللَّهُمَّ فَقِّهْهُ فِي الدِّينِ**" ("O God, give him deep understanding in the religion") was lodged in the technical lexicon, and through the codification movement of Abū Ḥanīfa, al-Shāfiʿī, and their schools. 9:122 is the textual seed; the harvest is medieval. **None** of the other 19 fqh occurrences shows even a hint of legal-jurisprudential content. Test passed: the Quranic data does not support reading fqh as "law."

## Five illustrative ayahs across the spectrum

**6:98 — fqh as positive cognitive faculty (rare)**
- Saheeh: "We have detailed the signs for **a people who understand**."
- Khattab: "for **people who comprehend**."
- Arberry: "for **a people who understand**."

**7:179 — the cognitive failure that defines hellfire-bound humans**
- Saheeh: "They have hearts with which they **do not understand**."
- Pickthall: "having hearts wherewith they **understand not**."
- Khattab: "hearts they **do not understand with**."

**8:65 — disbelievers as cognitive deficiency**
- Saheeh: "**they are a people who do not understand**."
- Pickthall: "they are **a folk without intelligence**." *(Pickthall paraphrases — fqh becomes "intelligence" as a stable trait.)*
- Khattab: "**a people who do not comprehend**."

**9:122 — the outlier (full panel above)**

**63:7 — the hypocrites' opacity to political-economic reality**
- Saheeh: "the **hypocrites do not understand**."
- Pickthall: "the **hypocrites comprehend not**."
- Khattab: "the **hypocrites do not comprehend**."

**17:44 — fqh of cosmic praise**
- Saheeh: "you **do not understand** their [way of] exalting."
- Khattab: "you ˹simply˺ **cannot comprehend** their glorification."

## Translator divergence

Translators converge on "understand / comprehend" for the Form I verb and split on intensity. The pattern:
- **Saheeh, Arberry**: consistently render fqh as plain "understand" — flattening the depth dimension.
- **Khattab**: alone uses "**comprehend**" repeatedly (6:98, 8:65, 17:44, 63:7), preserving the depth nuance.
- **Pickthall**: shifts toward trait language at 8:65 ("a folk **without intelligence**") and toward technical register at 9:122 ("**sound knowledge**") — he is most willing to interpret rather than transliterate.
- For Form V (9:122), **Arberry's "become learned in religion"** is the only rendering that gestures toward the institutional sense — and even he resists writing "jurists."

The supporting cast: **Amn** (faith, 4) and **kfr** (disbelief, 3) — fqh tracks the faith/disbelief axis. **wqr** (heaviness in ear, 3) and **knn** (coverings on heart, 3) — fqh's antonym vocabulary. **nfr** (going forth, 3) — appears with the 9:122 cluster (the seekers of *tafaqquh* are those who *march forth*). Notably **absent**: **fqh never co-occurs with `Hkm` (judgment/wisdom) or any explicitly legal vocabulary** — exactly what the jurisprudence-narrowing thesis predicts.

## The institutional vs Quranic gap

| Quran (610–632 CE) | Medieval Islam (9th c. onward) |
|---|---|
| **Verb only** (20/20 = V) — fqh is an *act* | **Noun *fiqh*** — fqh is a *body of doctrine* |
| **0 agent nouns** — no *faqīh* in the Quran | **Fuqahāʾ** — a defined scholar class |
| **General**: comprehension of speech, signs, cosmic praise, divine warning | **Narrowed**: positive law (ʿibādāt + muʿāmalāt) |
| **75% negative** — the *failure* of comprehension | **Positive**: a system of rulings to be mastered |
| **One** verse links fqh to *dīn* (9:122) | **Entire discipline** built on that one verse |

The parallel with `Elm` is exact and instructive. Just as **ʿulamāʾ** (Islamic scholars) is a class barely countenanced by the Quran's `Elm` vocabulary, **fuqahāʾ** is a class wholly absent from the Quran's fqh vocabulary. In both cases, an institution of trained legal scholars retroactively claims a Quranic word whose Quranic meaning was wider, more cognitive, and less professional. **The medieval scholar inhabits Quranic vocabulary that did not originally describe him.**

## What surprised me

Three things, in order of magnitude:

1. **fqh is the Quran's chief diagnostic of hypocrisy.** I expected `nfq` (the hypocrisy root itself) to dominate the munāfiqūn passages. Instead the Quran reaches repeatedly for **fqh** — making cognitive failure, not moral duplicity, the deep description of hypocrisy. The hypocrite is the one who *does not get it*.

2. **The 95% verb-form / 5% Form V split.** That a single occurrence (9:122) of a single morphological form (*tafaqquh*) generated an entire civilizational discipline is one of the most consequential semantic narrowings in religious history. The whole superstructure of *fiqh* sits on one Medinan verse about a wartime educational dispatch.

3. **fqh has no positive Quranic adjective.** Where `Elm` gives `ʿulamāʾ`, `Hkm` gives `ḥakīm`, `bṣr` gives `baṣīr`, fqh produces no agent noun in the Quranic text at all. The Quran gives us only *people who fail to fqh* — never *the one who fqh's*. The very category "deep comprehender" exists Quranically only as a missing role.

## One-sentence summary

**fqh is the Quran's word for cognition-as-grasping — the moment when meaning lands inside a heart — and it appears overwhelmingly in the negative, as the Quran's chief diagnostic of hypocrisy and disbelief, with one Medinan verse (9:122) on wartime religious education serving as the slender textual hinge on which medieval Islam swung the entire institution of Islamic jurisprudence.**
