# Concept profile: ذ-ك-ر (`*kr` / dhikr) — remember / mention / The Reminder

> **Source:** Parallel-agent deep-read, deep "research" investigation, 2026-04-26.
> **Data:** `data/concepts/*kr.json` (292 occurrences, 14 lemmas, 71 surahs, 216 Meccan / 76 Medinan)
> **Filename note:** Buckwalter root key is `*kr` (asterisk encodes ذ); file uses transliterated `dhikr` for safe filename.

## The distinctive role of ذكر in Quranic epistemology

ذكر is the only Quranic cognitive root that simultaneously names (a) a *psychological act* (recalling, being mindful), (b) a *speech act* (mentioning aloud, naming, narrating), and (c) the *Qur'an itself* — الذِّكْر, "the Reminder." No other root in the cognitive constellation does all three at once. Where `Elm` (knowledge) names a stored *state* and `fkr` / `dbr` name *generative* operations on data, ذكر names *retrieval and re-presentation* — and the same Arabic verb does that retrieval whether it surfaces silently in the heart or audibly on the tongue. The Quran weaponizes that ambiguity: revelation is called الذكر because, in the Quranic theory, it is not delivering new content but *recalling* something already latent in the human soul (the primordial covenant of 7:172, etc.). The verb you are commanded to do (اذكر, "remember Allah") and the noun naming what is given to you (الذكر, "the Reminder sent down") are the same root — the human act and the divine gift mirror each other.

## Formulaic patterns (counts)

- **اذكر / اذكروا (imperative Form I)** — 39 total. Singular اذكر (addressed to Muhammad) = 11; plural اذكروا (addressed to the community) = 28.
- **وَٱذْكُرْ في ٱلْكِتَاب** ("And mention in the Book…") — the Maryam-formula opens 5 consecutive prophet biographies in Sūrah 19 (19:16, 41, 51, 54, 56) and recurs in 38:17, 38:41, 38:45, etc. This is the Quran's *biographical opener* — and it is a *speech-act*: Muhammad is told to mention, not to remember.
- **فَذَكِّرْ / وَذَكِّرْ ("Remind!" to Muhammad, Form II imperative)** — 7 occurrences (51:55, 52:29, 87:9, 88:21, etc.). The Prophet's office reduces in late-Meccan surahs to "you are only a reminder" (إنما أنت مذكر, 88:21).
- **هَلْ مِن مُّدَّكِر؟ ("Is there any who will take heed?")** — 6 occurrences, all in Sūrah 54 (Al-Qamar), forming a refrain after each destruction-story; this is the Quran's most concentrated rhetorical-challenge formula.
- **لَعَلَّكُمْ / لَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَذَكَّرُونَ ("perhaps you will take heed")** — Form V hortatory closer, 13 occurrences. Form V (تَذَكَّرَ) total = 51, distributed across 50 distinct ayahs — this is the *internalization* mode (not just remember, but "let it sink in").
- **أَفَلَا تَذَكَّرُونَ?** ("will you not take heed?") — preceder analysis shows أَفَلَا occurs immediately before the plural Form V verb in **9 ayahs** — the exact rhetorical-challenge formula the brief asked about.
- **Form V vs Form I imperative** — Form V (51 occ., mostly indicative/jussive) is the *cognitive-uptake* mode; Form I imperative (39 occ.) is the *commanded act*. The Quran consistently *commands* in Form I and *hopes-for* in Form V.

## Subject and addressee analysis

Of the 39 Form I imperatives plus 7 Form II imperatives (46 total): **18 are 2nd-person-singular** (addressed to Muhammad — "mention X to them," "remind!") versus **28 are 2nd-person-plural** (addressed to the believing community — "remember My favor," "remember Allah much"). The split is meaningful: when the addressee is *Muhammad alone*, ذكر almost always means **mention/narrate aloud** (the prophet biographies of Sūrah 19, the David/Job/Abraham narratives of Sūrah 38, the imperative "Remind!"). When the addressee is *plural*, ذكر means **remember Allah / His favor** internally and ritually. The same root, but the singular form fronts the *speech* sense and the plural form fronts the *cognition* sense.

## Five illustrative ayahs (translator panel)

**1. 2:152 — mutual remembrance, the cognition sense at its purest** (فَٱذْكُرُونِى)
- Saheeh: "So remember Me; I will remember you."
- Pickthall: "Therefore remember Me, I will remember you."
- Khattab: "remember Me; I will remember you."
- Arberry: "So remember Me, and I will remember you."
*All four converge — pure cognitive sense, no ambiguity.*

**2. 19:16 — biographical formula, the speech sense** (وَٱذْكُرْ فِى ٱلْكِتَٰبِ مَرْيَمَ)
- Saheeh: "And mention, [O Muhammad], in the Book [the story of] Mary…"
- Pickthall: "And make mention of Mary in the Scripture…"
- Khattab: "And mention in the Book…"
- Arberry: "And mention in the Book Mary…"
*Total convergence on "mention" — but the Arabic is the same verb as 2:152.*

**3. 15:9 — Quran-as-Reminder, the divine sense** (إِنَّا نَحْنُ نَزَّلْنَا ٱلذِّكْرَ)
- Saheeh: "Indeed, it is We who sent down the **Qur'an**…"
- Pickthall: "Lo! We, even We, reveal the **Reminder**…"
- Khattab: "It is certainly We Who have revealed the **Reminder**…"
- Arberry: "It is We who have sent down the **Remembrance**…"
*Saheeh paraphrases الذكر as "Qur'an"; the others preserve the literal Reminder/Remembrance — a translator-divergence flag for the self-reference claim.*

**4. 54:17 — the rhetorical refrain** (وَلَقَدْ يَسَّرْنَا ٱلْقُرْءَانَ لِلذِّكْرِ فَهَلْ مِن مُّدَّكِرٍ)
- Saheeh: "And We have certainly made the Qur'an easy **for remembrance**, so is there any who will remember?"
- Pickthall: "And in truth We have made the Qur'an easy to remember; but is there any that remembereth?"
- Khattab: "…easy to remember. So is there anyone who will be **mindful**"
- Arberry: "Now We have made the Koran easy for **Remembrance**. Is there any that will remember?"
*Crucial theological hinge: the Quran (al-Qur'ān, "the Recitation") was made easy *for the dhikr* — i.e. revelation packages itself for retrievability.*

**5. 39:9 — Form V tied to those of insight** (إِنَّمَا يَتَذَكَّرُ أُو۟لُوا۟ ٱلْأَلْبَٰبِ)
- Saheeh: "Only they will remember [who are] **people of understanding**."
- Pickthall: "But only **men of understanding** will pay heed."
- Khattab: "None will be **mindful** ˹of this˺ except people of reason"
- Arberry: "Only men possessed of minds **remember**."
*Form V tadhakkara is repeatedly attached to ulū al-albāb (people of insight) — uptake of dhikr is gated by lubb, the inner core.*

**6. 12:45 — recall after time, the only ٱدَّكَر occurrence** (وَٱدَّكَرَ بَعْدَ أُمَّةٍ)
- Saheeh: "But the one who was freed and **remembered after a time**…"
- Pickthall: "And he…(now) at length remembered…"
- Khattab: "the surviving ex-prisoner remembered ˹Joseph˺ **after a long time**…"
- Arberry: "Then said the one who had been delivered, **remembering after a time**…"
*The Form VIII iddakara — used uniquely here for memory recovered from latency. Mirrors the divine theory: dhikr is what surfaces what was always there.*

**7. 21:10 — extreme translator divergence** (لَقَدْ أَنزَلْنَآ إِلَيْكُمْ كِتَٰبًا فِيهِ ذِكْرُكُمْ)
- Saheeh: "…a Book in which is your **mention**."
- Pickthall: "…a Scripture wherein is your **Reminder**."
- Khattab: "…a Book, in which there is **glory** for you."
- Arberry: "…a Book wherein is your **Remembrance**."
*Four translators, four senses (mention / reminder / glory / remembrance) for a single ذكركم — the cleanest illustration that the root's polysemy is irreducible.*

## Semantic field

The top co-occurring roots: **اله Allah** (91 ayahs, 34%), **ربب Lord** (48), **علم knowledge** (44), **قول speech** (48), **قلب heart** (19), **نعم blessing** (18). Three theologically targeted patterns:
- **ذكر + الله / ربك** — by far the dominant collocation: dhikr is overwhelmingly *of* Allah/the Lord. Remembrance is intentional and directed.
- **ذكر + قلب** (heart) — 19 ayahs. dhikr is the activity that "makes hearts find rest" (13:28: أَلَا بِذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ تَطْمَئِنُّ ٱلْقُلُوبُ). It locates the act in the heart, not the head.
- **ذكر + نعمة** (blessing) — 18 ayahs. The most frequent *object* of remembering is past divine favor (the Children-of-Israel formulas اذكروا نعمتي).

Notable **near-absences**: ذكر co-occurs with **فكر** in only **2 ayahs** and with **دبر** in only **3**. This is striking — for a root so central to cognition, it rarely appears alongside *reflective* roots. ذكر is positioned as the *prerequisite operation*, not a partner to reflection.

## Comparison with Elm, fkr, dbr

- **علم (Elm)** — the *holding* of knowledge as a state, a property of mind/God. It is timeless and propositional.
- **فكر (fkr)** — *generative* thinking that constructs new conclusions from given premises.
- **دبر (dbr)** — *sequential* tracing-through, following implications to the end.
- **ذكر (*kr)** — *retrieval and presentation* of something already deposited. It is temporal (something stored, now surfaced) and bidirectional (silently in the heart or audibly on the tongue).

The data supports the brief's hypothesis: ذكر is about *retrieving stored content*, where Elm is about *holding* it. This is why dhikr can be intensified by repetition (ذِكْرًا كَثِيرًا, 33:41) — you don't repeat knowledge, you repeat retrieval. And it is why revelation is الذكر: the Quran's claim is that nothing new is being taught, only retrieved.

## What surprised me

**Three findings I did not expect:**

1. **Sūrah 54 is the dhikr-density anomaly** — 11 occurrences in 55 verses, six of which are the refrain هل من مدكر ("any who will take heed?"). The surah uses dhikr as a *liturgical hammer*, repeating the same line after each historical destruction (ʿĀd, Thamūd, Lot's people, Pharaoh). No other surah uses the root this rhythmically.

2. **The fkr/dbr near-zero co-occurrence.** I expected ذكر to cluster with reflective roots — it doesn't. The Quran appears to position dhikr *upstream* of reflection: you must first re-present the data before you can think over it.

3. **The Form-I vs Form-V division of cognitive labor.** Form I (اذكر) is what Allah *commands* — 39 imperatives. Form V (تذكر) is what Allah *hopes for* — appearing 13 times in لعل constructions ("perhaps they will…"). The grammar itself encodes that the surface act of dhikr is enjoined, but the deeper internal uptake (تذكر) cannot be commanded, only invited.

## One-sentence summary

**ذكر is the Quran's name for the single bridging operation of revelation and worship — the retrieval of latent content into present awareness, equally valid as a silent act of the heart (ذكر الله), an audible act of speech (وٱذكر في ٱلكتاب), and a gift from God (الذكر) — and the Quran's self-naming as "the Reminder" makes the entire revelation a claim about anamnesis rather than instruction.**
