# Concept profile: ع-ل-م (`Elm`) — knowledge / to know

> **Source:** Parallel-agent deep-read, deep "research" investigation, 2026-04-26.
> **Data:** `data/concepts/Elm.json` (854 occurrences, 14 lemmas, 728 ayahs, 85 surahs, 495 Meccan / 359 Medinan)
> **Context:** Most frequent cognitive root in the Quran.

## The distinctive role of Elm in Quranic epistemology

`Elm` is not just the most frequent cognitive root — it is the **ontological backbone** of the Quran's epistemic universe. Where `bSr` (perception, 148) and `Eql` (reason, 49) describe *human cognitive faculties*, `Elm` operates at a higher level: it names the **content and possession** of knowledge as a metaphysical attribute. It is the only cognitive root that names both:
- (a) a divine attribute (`Ealiym`, "All-Knowing", 163×)
- (b) the act (`Ealima`, "to know", 382×)
- (c) the object/possession (`Eilom`, "knowledge" as noun, 105×)
- (d) a transitive causative ("to teach", `Eal~ama`, 41× — `bSr` and `Eql` cannot be taught)
- (e) the receptive passive (`muEal~am` / `m~uEoluwm`, "taught" / "fixed/known", 12×)

The lemma table itself is the structural finding: **knowledge in the Quran is something Allah *has*, *gives*, *teaches*, *withholds*, and *measures humans against*.** Other cognitive roots describe how mind moves; `Elm` describes the asymmetry between divine omniscience and human poverty.

Note one important quirk: `Ea`lamiyn` (عَٰلَمِين, 73 occ., "worlds/universes" as in *Rabb al-ʿālamīn*) is grouped under root `Elm` morphologically but semantically belongs to a homonymous sense ("worlds"). Excluding it, the genuinely "knowledge"-meaning corpus is ~781 occurrences — still by far the largest.

## Formulaic patterns

Three dominant formulas account for a huge share of usage:

1. **The divine-attribute closing formula.** `Ealiym` appears 163× — and **119 of those (73%)** sit in the same ayah as `Allah` (root `Alh`). Of those, **39 ayahs end with the binomial `ٱلْعَلِيمُ ٱلْحَكِيمُ` (al-ʿalīm al-ḥakīm, "All-Knowing, All-Wise")** and **33 with `ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ` (al-samīʿ al-ʿalīm, "All-Hearing, All-Knowing")**. This is liturgical scaffolding — a doxological coda that ties any legal, moral, or narrative passage back to the divine epistemic horizon.

2. **The imperative `iʿlamū` ("Know that…!") — 31 occurrences.** A directive speech-act, usually in Medinan legal contexts (Surah 2 dominates). Knowledge here is not curiosity but obligation: *be advised that Allah is X.*

3. **The "He knows / they (or you) do not know" contrast.** Of all 382 `Ealima` verbs, **110 (28.8%) are syntactically negated** (preceded by *lā / mā / lam / lan*). Cross-tabulated by subject: when the subject is human (2nd person), negation occurs **34/123 = 28%**; when the subject is 3rd-plural ("they"), **71/228 = 31%**; when the subject is 1st person ("I/We" — almost always Allah speaking), only **5/31 = 16%**. The pattern is unmistakable: **negation tracks human subjects.** Eleven ayahs explicitly stage the contrast within a single verse (e.g. 2:216, 2:232, 3:66, 6:59, 16:74, 22:70).

## Subject and addressee analysis (382 `Ealima` verbs)

| Subject | Count | % |
|---|---|---|
| 3rd-singular ("He [Allah] knows") | 129 | 33.8 |
| 2nd-person ("you [humans] know / do not know") | 123 | 32.2 |
| 3rd-plural ("they [people, often disbelievers] know / do not know") | 99 | 25.9 |
| 1st-person ("I / We [Allah] know") | 31 | 8.1 |

Aspect: **316 imperfective (ongoing/timeless), 35 perfective, 31 imperative.** The overwhelming present-tense usage reinforces the timeless-divine quality: Allah's knowing is not an event, it is a state. Crucially, **66% of all knowing-verbs have a human subject** (2nd + 3rd person) — but a third of those are *negated*. The Quran is not so much describing what humans know as **drawing a line around what they do not.**

## Five illustrative ayahs (four-translator panel)

**(1) 96:5 — Al-ʿAlaq (Meccan, the first revelation)** عَلَّمَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ مَا لَمْ يَعْلَمْ
- Saheeh: "Taught man that which he knew not."
- Pickthall: "Teacheth man that which he knew not."
- Khattab: "taught humanity what they knew not"
- Arberry: "taught Man that he knew not."

The Quran's *first* explicit theological statement after "Read!" is an epistemological one: knowledge originates as divine teaching of human ignorance. Both `Eal~ama` (causative) and `yaʿlam` (negated) appear in five Arabic words.

**(2) 2:31–32 — Adam, the angels, the names** (Medinan)
"He taught Adam the names… They [angels] said, 'We have no knowledge except what You have taught us. You are the All-Knowing, the All-Wise.'" Three distinct lemmas (`Eal~ama`, `Eilom`, `Ealiym`) collide in two ayahs — establishing the entire vertical hierarchy: Allah-knows → Allah-teaches → human (Adam) receives → angels confess limit.

**(3) 2:216 — the canonical contrast** وَٱللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ وَأَنتُمْ لَا تَعْلَمُونَ
- Saheeh: "Allah Knows, while you know not."
- Pickthall: "Allah knoweth, ye know not."
- Khattab: "Allah knows and you do not know"
- Arberry: "God knows, and you know not."

All four translators converge — the formula is too sharp to vary. It is the contrast pattern in pure form.

**(4) 39:9 — the rhetorical question** قُلْ هَلْ يَسْتَوِى ٱلَّذِينَ يَعْلَمُونَ وَٱلَّذِينَ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ
- "Are those who know equal to those who do not know?" (all four agree).

The only ayah to assert that the *human* possession of knowledge confers ontological rank — and even here the verb is intransitive: knowledge as a state of being, not knowledge *of* anything specified.

**(5) 6:59 — the unseen** وَعِندَهُۥ مَفَاتِحُ ٱلْغَيْبِ لَا يَعْلَمُهَآ إِلَّا هُوَ
"With Him are the keys of the unseen; none knows them except Him… Not a leaf falls but He knows it." The leaf-falling image. `yaʿlam` repeats three times in one ayah; the entire universe is recast as the object of a single uninterrupted divine perception.

**(6) 35:28 — the rare human "knowers"** إِنَّمَا يَخْشَى ٱللَّهَ مِنْ عِبَادِهِ ٱلْعُلَمَٰٓؤُاْ
- Saheeh: "Only those…who have knowledge [fear Allah]."
- Pickthall: "**The erudite** among His bondmen fear Allah alone."
- Khattab: "only the knowledgeable [of His might]"
- Arberry: "only those of His servants…who have knowledge"

The plural `ʿulamāʾ` — the word that today means "Islamic scholars" — appears only **2 times in 854 occurrences** (here and 26:197, the scholars of Israel). The Quran almost never gives this title to humans. Pickthall's "erudite" leans toward formal scholarship; the others keep it abstract. **This is a quiet but striking finding: the institutional vocabulary of later Islam barely exists in the Quran.**

**(7) 3:7 — translator divergence on the limits of knowing**
"…وَمَا يَعْلَمُ تَأْوِيلَهُۥٓ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ ۗ وَٱلرَّٰسِخُونَ فِى ٱلْعِلْمِ يَقُولُونَ…"
The famous *mutashābihāt* verse. The translation question is whether to put a full stop after "except Allah" (Saheeh, Khattab, Arberry — making "those firm in knowledge" a *new* sentence that only believes) or to read continuity (Pickthall: "those who are of sound instruction say"). Same Arabic text, but a punctuation choice decides whether deep knowledge of scripture's interpretation is **closed to humans** or **shared with the elite**. This single ayah encapsulates the centuries-long Sunni vs. Mu'tazili / Shi'i debate, and it pivots on one root: `Eilom`.

## Semantic field (top co-occurrences)

The top co-occurring roots cluster into three families:
- **Theological frame** — `Alh` (Allah, 408 ayahs together, 56%), `rbb` (Lord, 18%), `Hkm` (wisdom, 10%).
- **Discourse / revelation** — `qwl` (speech, 32%), `ktb` (book, 8%), `nzl` (sending-down, 6%), `rsl` (messenger, 8%), `*kr` (remembrance, 6%).
- **Cosmological scope** — `smw` (heavens, 10%), `ArD` (earth, 12%), `gyb` (the unseen, 34 ayahs), `kll` (every/all, 10%), `$yA` (thing, 13%).

Translation: knowledge in the Quran is bound to **(a) God, (b) speech/revelation, and (c) totality** (everything, heaven-and-earth, the unseen). It is almost never bound to *observation, experience, or evidence* — those belong to `bSr`, `nZr`, `smE`. `Elm` clusters with `Hkm` (wisdom) far more than with any perceptual root: knowledge here is judicial / ordering / final, not investigative.

## Comparison to other cognitive roots

- **vs. `*kr` (292, "remember/mention")** — `*kr` requires a prior content; it is *retrieval*. `Elm` is *possession*. The Quran asks humans to *remember* (`*kr`) what Allah has *taught* (`Elm`).
- **vs. `Hkm` (210, "wisdom/judgment")** — `Hkm` is the application of `Elm`. The pair `ʿalīm ḥakīm` shows them as dyad: knowledge → judgment.
- **vs. `bSr` (148, "see/perceive")** — `bSr` is sensory; `Elm` is propositional. A human can *see* (`bSr`) and still *not know* (`lā taʿlamūn`). Hence 17:36 chains `samʿ + baṣar + fuʾād` together as faculties accountable for what they take in — but the *content* is `Eilom`.
- **vs. `nZr` (129, "look/observe")** — `nZr` is the act of investigation ("Have they not looked at the heavens…?"). `Elm` is what one is supposed to *arrive at* through such looking. They are two ends of the same epistemic arc.
- **vs. `Eql` (49, "reason")** — surprisingly small. `Eql` always appears as a verb ("do you not reason?"). The Quran chastises non-reasoning, but reasons less often than it asserts knowing. **Knowledge outweighs reason 17:1 in raw frequency.** The Quranic epistemic ideal is not the philosopher who reasons but the witness who knows-because-told.

## What surprised me

Two things, in tension. **First**, the human plural "scholars" (`ʿulamāʾ`) — the very word that names the Islamic clerical class — appears **only twice** in 854 occurrences. The Quran's vocabulary of `Elm` does not authorize a learned caste; it authorizes only Allah and those He directly teaches. The institutional vocabulary is nearly absent.

**Second**, the negation pattern is **stronger in Meccan** (33% of verbs negated) **than Medinan** (24%), while the divine epithet `Ealiym` more than doubles its proportional share in Medinan (12.5% → 28.1%). This is a real shift in mode: early Meccan rhetoric *destabilizes* the addressee ("you do not know!") to open them to revelation; Medinan rhetoric *stabilizes* the legal community by repeatedly **closing** ayahs with the divine-attribute formula ("…and Allah is All-Knowing, All-Wise"). The same root, with a moved center of gravity: from epistemic challenge to juridical guarantee.

## One-sentence summary

`Elm` is the Quran's vocabulary for the **vertical axis of knowing** — Allah possesses, teaches, and conceals; humans receive, miss, or are commanded — and it appears 854 times not because it describes inquiry but because the asymmetry it names is the precondition for everything else the Quran does.
