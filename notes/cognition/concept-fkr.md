# Concept profile: ف-ك-ر (`fkr`) — to think / reflect / ponder

> **Source:** Initial analysis from `notes/concept-research.md` (the first pass at the cognitive constellation), 2026-04-26.
> **Data:** `data/concepts/fkr.json` (18 occurrences, 2 lemmas, 13 surahs, 13 Meccan / 5 Medinan)

## Why fkr is the closest functional analog to "research" in the Quran

Of all the roots in the cognitive constellation, **fkr** is the closest functional analog to "research" as an *active cognitive verb* (rather than knowledge-as-state). It always denotes the act, never the state. It is what a person *does* with material before them.

## The morphology already tells us something

Two lemmas, but their distribution is striking:

| Lemma (Arabic) | Pattern | Count | What the form means |
|---|---|---|---|
| يَتَفَكَّرُ (`yatafak~aru`) | Form V (tafaʿʿala) | **17** | reflexive-intensive: "to reflect repeatedly, to turn over in one's mind" |
| فَكَّرَ (`fak~ara`) | Form II (faʿʿala) | 1 | causative-intensive: "to make think, to consider" |

**17 of 18 occurrences are in the reflexive-intensive (Form V).** In Arabic morphology, Form V (tafaʿʿala) marks an action turned back upon the actor — the doer is also the object. *Tafakkur* is therefore not "thinking once" but "thinking-and-being-changed-by-thinking, repeatedly." The grammatical pattern itself encodes that real reflection is iterative and self-modifying.

## The signature formula

**8 of 18 ayahs (44%) follow a recurring structural formula:**

> "...إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَءَايَٰتࣲ لِّقَوۡمࣲ يَتَفَكَّرُونَ"
>
> "Truly in that are signs for a people who reflect."

These 8 ayahs span: **10:24, 13:3, 16:11, 16:69, 30:21, 39:42, 45:13, 7:176**.

The pattern places three concepts in fixed structural relationship:
- **āyāt** (signs / verses) — the things to be reflected upon
- **qawm** (a people) — reflection is communal, not solitary
- **yatafakkarūn** (they reflect) — the active verbal mode

The "signs" in these formulas are almost always natural phenomena: rain, vegetation, the cycle of day and night, bees and honey, marriage and procreation, the heavens and the earth. The Quran is repeatedly placing the natural world in the role of "research material" — and prescribing communal reflection as the appropriate response.

## Illustrative ayahs — the four-translator panel

**3:191** — perhaps the closest the Quran comes to defining reflective practice:

| | |
|---|---|
| Arabic word | وَيَتَفَكَّرُونَ ("and they reflect") |
| Literal | "and they reflect" |
| Saheeh | "Who remember Allah while standing or sitting or [lying] on their sides and give thought to the creation of the heavens and the earth, [saying], 'Our Lord, You did not create this aimlessly…'" |
| Pickthall | "Such as remember Allah, standing, sitting, and reclining, and consider the creation of the heavens and the earth…" |
| Khattab | (similar wording, contemplative posture) |
| Arberry | "who remember God, standing and sitting and on their sides, and reflect upon the creation of the heavens and the earth: 'Our Lord, Thou hast not created this for vanity…'" |

Reflection here is: (a) tied to remembrance of God, (b) practiced in any bodily posture (it's continuous, not ritual-bound), (c) directed at creation, (d) issuing in a verbal response that affirms purpose ("not in vain"). All four translators converge — when the meaning is well-bounded, English agrees.

**6:50** — the rhetorical-challenge mode:

> "Are the blind and the seeing equal? **Will you not then give thought?**" (Saheeh)
> "Will ye not then take thought?" (Pickthall)

*Tafakkur* here is positioned as the response that distinguishes belief from disbelief. Refusing to reflect is not neutral — it is structurally aligned with refusing to see.

**2:219** — reflection as response to ethical teaching:

The verse on wine and gambling concludes "*…thus Allah makes clear to you the verses [āyāt] that you might give thought*." Reflection isn't reserved for cosmology; it's the appropriate response to ethical revelation as well.

## One-sentence summary

**fkr is the Quran's verb for iterative, communal, self-modifying reflection — encoded morphologically as Form V (the reflexive-intensive) and structurally as the *li-qawmin yatafakkarūn* formula in which a community is invited to turn over signs already disclosed by the Lord, again and again, and be changed by the turning.**
