# Concept profile: ح-ك-م (`Hkm`) — wisdom / judgment / governance

> **Source:** Parallel-agent deep-read, deep "research" investigation, 2026-04-26.
> **Data:** `data/concepts/Hkm.json` (210 occurrences, 13 lemmas, 57 surahs, 93 Meccan / **117 Medinan** — the only Medinan-leaning major cognitive root)

## The distinctive role of Hkm in Quranic epistemology

Across the 14 cognitive roots in this study, **Hkm is the sole exception to the Meccan rule**. Every other cognition root I checked clusters Meccan — Elm 495/359, nZr 99/30, *kr 216/76, qrA 78/10, Eql 30/19, fkr 13/5. Hkm inverts the pattern: **93 Meccan vs 117 Medinan (44% / 56%)**. The hypothesis holds: Meccan revelation builds a theological epistemology of perceiving, reflecting, remembering, knowing; Medinan revelation operationalises it into an *applied* epistemology — judging disputes, ruling between people, and recognising the wisdom embedded in revealed law. Hkm is the hinge where cognition turns into governance.

The clearest period-shift signals:
- **Ḥukm** ("judgment / authority") is the *Meccan* face of the root — 22/8 — but in Mecca it overwhelmingly describes prophetic gift (Joseph 12:22, Moses 28:14, John 19:12, Lot 21:74, Solomon 21:79). It is **prophetic discernment**, not yet civic adjudication.
- **Ḥikma** ("wisdom") more than doubles Medinan-ward — 6/14 — and becomes the technical companion of *al-Kitāb*.
- **Yuḥakkimū / yataḥākamū / ḥukkām / muḥkamāt** — every juridical-procedural derivation is exclusively Medinan. The vocabulary of *adjudication itself* arrives only after the community has something to adjudicate.

## The Wisdom vs Judgment split

Bucketing the 210 tokens:

| Bucket | Count | Meccan | Medinan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Divine attribute (al-Ḥakīm / aḥkam) | 99 | 39 | 60 |
| Judgment / rule / arbitrate (verbs + ḥukm + judges) | 87 | 47 | 40 |
| Wisdom-as-quality (ḥikma) | 20 | 6 | 14 |
| Precise / clear (muḥkamāt etc.) | 4 | 1 | 3 |

Cognitive ḥikma is a small (~10%) minority. The root's centre of gravity is **divine attribute (47%) + governance (41%)**, with theoretical "wisdom" a marginal third. This is striking: Hkm is far less about *being* wise and far more about *Allah being wise* and *humans being judged*.

## The Ḥakīm–ʿAlīm pairing

- Hkm and Elm co-occur in **71 of 189** Hkm-bearing ayahs (37.6%) — by far the strongest cognitive-root link.
- The fixed divine-name pair (al-ʿAlīm + al-Ḥakīm or its indefinite ʿalīm + ḥakīm, adjacent within a few words) appears in **36 ayahs** — Medinan 26, Meccan 10.
- But the *most frequent* divine-attribute pair with Ḥakīm is actually **al-ʿAzīz al-Ḥakīm** ("the Almighty, the All-Wise"): 42 closing pairs, vs 25 for ʿAlīm-Ḥakīm. Power + wisdom edges out knowledge + wisdom. The theological signal: divine wisdom is paired more with *sovereignty* than with *omniscience*.

## Five illustrative ayahs (translator panels condensed)

1. **5:44 (Medinan, Ḥakama)** — *governance*: "...and whoever does not **judge** by what Allah has revealed — they are the disbelievers." All four translators: judge / judgeth / judge / judges.
2. **4:65 (Medinan, yuḥakkimū)** — *arbitration*: "...they will not believe until they make you **judge** in their disputes." Saheeh "make you judge"; Pickthall "make thee judge"; Khattab "accept you as the judge"; Arberry "make thee the judge."
3. **2:269 (Medinan, Ḥikma)** — *wisdom as gift*: "He gives **wisdom** to whom He wills..." Unanimously "wisdom" — but Arberry capitalises *the Wisdom*, hinting at a substantive entity.
4. **2:32 (Medinan, Ḥakīm)** — *divine attribute*: "...You are the All-Knowing, the **All-Wise**." All four: Wise / Wise / All-Wise / All-wise.
5. **3:7 (Medinan, muḥkamāt)** — *epistemic precision*: "...in it are verses **precise** (muḥkamāt) — they are the foundation of the Book — and others ambiguous (mutashābihāt)." Saheeh "precise"; Pickthall "clear"; Khattab "precise"; Arberry "clear." Here the root denotes textual unambiguity — the *epistemological* sense.
6. **31:12 (Meccan, Ḥikma)** — *practical reason*: "We gave Luqmān **wisdom**: be grateful to God." Wisdom as ethical discernment.
7. **11:1 (Meccan, uḥkimat)** — *the Book itself made-precise*: "A Book whose verses are **perfected** [uḥkimat] then expounded, from One Wise [Ḥakīm], Aware." The same root governs both the text's precision and its Author's wisdom — an internal claim of self-authentication.

## Translator divergence

The cleanest single-word disagreement is **12:22** (young Yūsuf reaching maturity): the Arabic is *ḥukm-an wa-ʿilm-an*. **Saheeh and Arberry: "judgment and knowledge." Pickthall and Khattab: "wisdom and knowledge."** Identical pattern at **19:12** (John as a child, *ḥukm-an ṣabiyy-an*) and **28:14** (Moses). Arabic *ḥukm* legitimately covers both senses; the translator has to pick, and the split runs along an axis of how juridically vs sapientially they read prophetic maturation. This *is* the entire interpretive question of the root in miniature.

The semantic field is *theological-revelatory* (Allah / knowledge / Book / sent-down / messenger / between-people), not abstractly ethical. Hkm sits at the intersection of *Allah's wisdom — Book sent down — judgment between people* — a tight Medinan triangle.

## The "Wisdom" question (Kitāb wa-Ḥikma)

Of 20 *ḥikma* tokens, **9 are explicitly paired with "the Book"** (kitāb): 2:129, 2:151, 2:231, 3:48, 3:81, 3:164, 4:54, 4:113, 5:110, 62:2. The pattern is consistently Medinan and consistently bound to the messenger's pedagogical mission: "...reciting to them His verses, purifying them, **and teaching them the Book and the Wisdom**" (2:151, 3:164, 62:2 — near-verbatim refrain).

What the data shows: ḥikma is *not* a synonym for kitāb (always conjoined with *wa-*, never identified). It is something **transmitted alongside** the Book by the same messenger. The Quran refuses to define it further, but the *unpaired* ḥikma verses constrain the answer — Luqmān's ḥikma (31:12) is moral-practical, ʿĪsā's (43:63) clarifies disagreement, Dāwūd's (38:20, 2:251) is paired with kingship, and 16:125 instructs *daʿwah* "with wisdom and good admonition." Ḥikma is therefore **applied prophetic discernment** — the practical, instructional, dispute-resolving counterpart of textual revelation. The classical sunnah-as-ḥikma reading is not forced by the data, but it is consistent with it; the more cautious reading is "the Book gives the law, ḥikma gives the application."

## Comparison with Elm / Eql / fqh

- **Elm** (854) — what Allah knows and what is taught/learned. Static knowledge.
- **Eql** (49) — discursive reasoning; almost always interrogative ("do you not reason?").
- **fqh** (20) — deep grasp/comprehension; usually negated of disbelievers.
- **Hkm** (210) — *cognition that issues in a verdict*. It is the only root where the cognitive act has a juridical output. If Elm is *seeing the truth* and Eql is *thinking through it*, Hkm is *ruling on it*.

## What surprised me

1. **Ḥukm is more Meccan than Medinan** (22/8) — counter to the headline narrative. The civic-law turn of the Medinan period is carried by the *verb* (Ḥakama 19/26) and the *abstract noun* (Ḥikma 6/14), not by Ḥukm itself. In Mecca, *ḥukm* names the prophets' inner discernment; in Medina, the action shifts to verbs of adjudication. The same root, redeployed.
2. **Al-ʿAzīz al-Ḥakīm beats al-ʿAlīm al-Ḥakīm** (42 vs 25 closing pairs). The dominant theological pairing is power+wisdom, not knowledge+wisdom. Hkm leans more sovereign than epistemic even at the divine level.
3. **Every juridical-procedural derivation (yuḥakkimū, yataḥākamū, ḥukkām, muḥkamāt) is exclusively Medinan, zero Meccan**. The grammar of arbitration itself is a Medinan invention.
4. **muḥkamāt (3:7)** ties textual epistemology to the same root as judgment: a "precise" verse is etymologically a "judged-firm" verse. The Quran's own theory of its clear vs ambiguous verses is built on Hkm.

## One-sentence summary

**Hkm is the Quran's applied-cognition root — the unique pivot where divine wisdom, prophetic discernment, textual precision, and human adjudication converge — and its Medinan dominance marks the moment Quranic epistemology stops asking "do you understand?" and starts asking "by what do you judge?"**
