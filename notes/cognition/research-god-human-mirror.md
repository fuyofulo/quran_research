# Cross-cutting investigation: The God-Human Cognitive Mirror

> **Source:** Phase 2 rabbit-hole agent, deep "research" investigation, 2026-04-26.
> **Hypothesis tested:** The God-human cognitive mirror identified in dbr (`yudabbir al-amr` / `yatadabbarūn al-Qur'ān`) is a STRUCTURAL pattern that runs through Quranic epistemology — multiple cognitive roots have this dual-voice structure where divine and human cognitive activity share a root but with different morphological forms and different objects.
> **Triggered by:** The dbr concept-deep-read finding (4 divine `tadbīr` of cosmos + 4 human `tadabbur` of Book).
> **New concept JSONs built:** smE, xlq, rzq, hdy, mlk, rbb.

## The motivating example: `dbr` (د-ب-ر)

Root `dbr` (44x) splits cleanly along a divine/human seam by **morphological form** and by **object**:

- **Form II `yudabbir` (يُدَبِّرُ)**, 4x — subject is *always* Allah, object is always *al-amr* (the cosmic affair/decree): 10:3, 10:31, 13:2, 32:5. The masc.pl. participle `mudabbirāt` (79:5) extends this to His angelic deputies.
- **Form V `yatadabbarūn` (يَتَدَبَّرُ)**, 4x — subject is *always* humans, object is always *al-Qurʾān*: 4:82, 23:68, 38:29, 47:24.

Same root, two voices, two scopes. The Form II is *causative arrangement*; the Form V is *reflexive tracing-through*. God arranges the cosmos; humans trace the Book. The structural symmetry is too tight to be accidental.

## Systematic check across 10 candidate roots

For each root I report (i) divine lemma + count, (ii) human lemma + count, (iii) the morphological signal, and (iv) the object scope.

**1. `Elm` — knowledge (854x). MIRROR CONFIRMED.**
- Divine: `ʿAlīm` (عَلِيم) 163x + `ʿAllām` (عَلَّام) 4x; Form II `ʿallama` (عَلَّمَ) 41x — God *teaches* (2:31 Adam the names; 2:32 "we have no knowledge except what You taught us").
- Human: Form I `ʿalima` (عَلِمَ) 382x — overwhelmingly negated or restricted ("they know not", "you know not"); Form V `yataʿallamu` (يَتَعَلَّمُ) only 2x, and both at 2:102 about *learning sorcery* — a marked, almost pejorative usage.
- **Signal:** Form II is the divine causative ("makes-known"); Form I is the human stative ("comes to know, often partially"). The masdar `ʿilm` (105x) is what God has fully and humans receive in fragments.

**2. `Hkm` — wisdom/judgment (210x). MIRROR CONFIRMED.**
- Divine: `Ḥakīm` (حَكِيم) 97x — divine name; `ḥukm` (حُكْم) belongs to Allah ("the decision is only for Allah", 6:57; 5:50).
- Human: `ḥakama` (حَكَمَ) 45x — humans/prophets judge, but the verb is overwhelmingly *commanded* ("when you judge, judge with justice", 4:58) or *deferred* ("Allah will judge between them", 2:113). Form V `yataḥākamū` (4:60) is humans seeking judgment — and the verse condemns their seeking it from *ṭāghūt* instead of God.
- **Signal:** the divine name is the intensive adjective; the human verb is contingent and almost always tied back to a divine norm.

**3. `bSr` — sight/insight (148x). MIRROR CONFIRMED, with a twist.**
- Divine: `Baṣīr` (بَصِير) 51x — divine name in the doublet *al-Samīʿ al-Baṣīr*.
- Human: `baṣar` (بَصَر) 48x — the physical eye, often described as veiled or sealed (2:7); Form IV `abṣara` (أَبْصَرَ) 29x — humans *try to see* but commonly *fail* (7:179: "eyes with which they do not see").
- **Signal:** the same morphological root; God is `Baṣīr` (intensive adjective, total perception); humans have `baṣar` (the organ) and the act `abṣara` (Form IV — causative-of-self, "to bring sight to bear"). Critically, *baṣīrah* (بَصِيرَة) — inner vision/insight — is the scaled-up human variant, granted *from* God (6:104, 12:108).

**4. `*kr` — remember/Reminder (292x). MIRROR CONFIRMED — the cleanest case after `dbr`.**
- Divine: `al-Dhikr` (الذِّكْر), 76x — *the noun* is the Quran/Reminder *sent down by* God; passive `dhukkira` (ذُكِّرَ) 18x is humans being-reminded-by-God; `tadhkirah` (تَذْكِرَة) 9x is the Book itself.
- Human: Form I `dhakara` (ذَكَرَ) 84x and Form V `tadhakkara` (تَذَكَّرَ) 51x — humans *remember/recall*. Form V is again the reflexive-internal mode: "let them ponder/recall to themselves."
- **Signal:** Allah PROVIDES the *dhikr* (noun); humans PERFORM the *dhikr* (verb). The mirror is structurally identical to `dbr`: God supplies the object, humans do the cognitive operation upon it. Form V `tadhakkara` is the exact morphological cognate of `tadabbara`.

**5. `smE` — hearing (185x). MIRROR CONFIRMED.**
- Divine: `Samīʿ` (سَمِيع) 47x — divine name, almost always paired with `ʿAlīm` or `Baṣīr`.
- Human: `samiʿa` (سَمِعَ) 78x — humans hear, but again largely negated or qualified ("they have ears with which they do not hear", 7:179); Form VIII `istamaʿa` (ٱسْتَمَعَ) 16x is *active listening*, often to the Quran (7:204, 17:47) — and this is the human counterpart to divine hearing: an effortful, reflexive form (Form VIII, not Form V here).
- **Signal:** God is the intensive `Samīʿ`; humans must do the Form VIII work `istamaʿa` to truly hear.

**6. `xlq` — creation (261x). MIRROR PARTIAL — the exception that confirms the rule.**
- Divine: `Khāliq` / `Khallāq` (خَالِق / خَلَّاق) 14x — divine name; `khalaqa` (خَلَقَ) 184x — almost exclusively Allah's act.
- Human: `khalaqa` is used of humans only at the *boundary* — Jesus *creates* a bird from clay "by Allah's permission" (3:49, 5:110, marked twice with *bi-idhnī*); 23:14 closes with "blessed is Allah, *the best of creators*" (`aḥsanu l-khāliqīn`), which grammatically *implies* lesser creators (37:125 likewise). But there is no Form V or Form VIII reflexive-human counterpart. Humans don't have a derived form for "trying to create."
- **Signal: NO MIRROR.** Creation is uniquely divine; the rare human uses are explicitly licensed and miraculous. The expected dual-voice does not appear morphologically.

**7. `rzq` — provision (123x). MIRROR PARTIAL.**
- Divine: `Razzāq` (رَزَّاق) 1x at 51:58 — the divine name; `rāziqīn` (رَازِقِين) 6x — *plural* "providers", and 5/6 occurrences are Allah being "the best of providers" (`khayr al-rāziqīn`). Verb `razaqa` (رَزَقَ) 61x — almost always Allah ("We have provided you...").
- Human: 15:20 marks the limit — "We made for you means of living and *for those for whom you are not providers*." The Quran explicitly *denies* humans the role of *rāziq* in any meaningful sense. Humans only `anfaqa` (spend) what they were given.
- **Signal: NO MIRROR.** Like `xlq`, *rizq* is unilateral. Same structural class as creation: ontologically exclusive to God.

**8. `hdy` — guidance (316x). MIRROR CONFIRMED with a sharp restriction.**
- Divine: `hadā` (هَدَى) 144x — Allah guides; `Hādī` (هَادِي) 2x as direct attribute (22:54, 25:31).
- Human: Form I `hādin` (هَادٍ) 7x — every people has a *guide* (13:7), prophets are guides — but the human "guide" is always derivative. Form VIII `ihtadā` (ٱهْتَدَىٰ) 40x + `muhtadūn` 17x — *humans being guided / finding their way*. This is the structural mirror: Form I (causative — God guides) vs Form VIII (reflexive — humans get-guided).
- **Signal:** sharp restriction at 28:56 ("you do not guide whom you love, but Allah guides whom He wills") — even the Prophet does not *truly* guide; he only conveys. Same root; divine causation, human reception.

**9. `mlk` — sovereignty (206x). MIRROR CONFIRMED.**
- Divine: `Mālik` (مَالِك) 3x as divine attribute (3:26 *Mālik al-Mulk*, 1:4 *Māliki yawmi-d-dīn*); `mulk` (مُلْك) 48x — universal dominion *belongs to Allah*; `malakūt` (مَلَكُوت) 4x — the cosmic kingship.
- Human: `malik` (مَلِك) 15x — earthly kings (Saul, the king of Egypt, Solomon); verb `malakat` (مَلَكَتْ) 44x — overwhelmingly in the formula *mā malakat aymānukum* ("what your right hands possess") — slaves, marriage rights, household property.
- **Signal:** divine kingship is `mulk` / `malakūt` (cosmic, total); human possession is `malakat aymānukum` (local, household). Same root, scaled scope.

**10. `rbb` — Lord/sustainer (980x). MIRROR CONFIRMED — the rarest but most precise.**
- Divine: `rabb` (رَبّ) 975x — overwhelmingly Allah as Lord of the worlds, of the heavens and earth, of all things.
- Human: ~6 verses in Sūrat Yūsuf (12:23, 12:41, 12:42 ×2, 12:50 ×2) use *rabb* for human masters — the Egyptian master of Joseph, the king. The same lemma, same form, scaled drastically down. The mirror is striking precisely because the lexical item is *unchanged*.
- **Signal:** identical lemma, divine vs. household scope. This is mirror-without-morphological-shift — the noun itself does the work.

## The pattern that emerges

Of 10 candidate cognitive/relational roots, **8 confirm the mirror** (`Elm, Hkm, bSr, *kr, smE, hdy, mlk, rbb`); **2 partially fail** (`xlq, rzq`). Three things are now clear:

1. **The mirror is the rule, not the exception.** It runs through nearly every cognitive and relational root. `dbr` is a particularly clean instance, not a unique one.

2. **The morphological signal is consistent — but not always Form II/Form V.** Different roots use different morphological strategies to mark the divine/human asymmetry:
   - **Form II divine causative vs. Form V human reflexive:** `dabbara/tadabbara`, `dhakkara/tadhakkara`, `ʿallama/yataʿallamu`.
   - **Intensive adjective divine name vs. Form I human verb:** `ʿAlīm/ʿalima`, `Ḥakīm/ḥakama`, `Samīʿ/samiʿa`, `Baṣīr/abṣara`.
   - **Form I divine causative vs. Form VIII human reflexive:** `hadā/ihtadā`.
   - **Same lemma, scaled scope:** `rabb` (cosmic Lord vs. household master), `malik / mulk` (divine dominion vs. earthly kingship), `rabb`.
   The unifying principle: divine activity is **intensive, total, causative**; human activity is **reflexive, partial, often-negated, contingent**.

3. **The object difference is consistent.** God's verb takes a *cosmic* or *total* object (al-amr, al-samawāt wa-l-arḍ, kull shayʾ, al-mulk, al-ghayb). The human verb takes a *local* or *scriptural* object (al-Qurʾān, the verses, the right-hand-possessed, the household, the people-of-the-people, "between people").

## Counter-evidence: where the mirror does NOT hold

`xlq` (creation) and `rzq` (provision) are the clear exceptions, and they fail in the *same* way. Both are roots where the act is **ontological**, not cognitive — bringing-into-being, sustaining-in-being. The Quran refuses to grant humans a true reflexive form here:
- 23:14 must hedge "best of creators" with the implication that the others are merely figurative.
- 15:20 explicitly says "you are not providers."
- Jesus' creating (3:49, 5:110) is doubly marked with *bi-idhnī* ("by My permission").

This counter-evidence is not noise — it sharpens the principle. **The mirror operates over cognitive/relational acts (knowing, judging, hearing, seeing, remembering, pondering, guiding, ruling). It refuses to operate over acts of being (creating, providing).** Cognition can be participated; existence cannot.

## Implication for Quranic theology of cognition

The data supports the deep reading: **human cognitive activity in the Quran is structured as a scaled-down, reflexive participation in divine cognitive activity.** The same root, the same root-meaning, but a derived (reflexive, partial, contingent) form for humans, applied to a smaller (scriptural, household, local) object.

This is why the Book is the human's cosmos: `tadabbur` of the Quran is the structurally exact human analogue of `tadbīr` of the universe. `Tadhakkur` of the verses mirrors *al-Dhikr* sent down. `Ihtidāʾ* by guidance mirrors Allah's `hidāyah`. `Istimāʿ` of the recitation mirrors the divine `samʿ`. The epistemology is *deliberately fractal*: the Book is given because humans cannot read the Cosmos — but the cognitive operations they are commanded to perform on the Book are the *same operations* God performs on the Cosmos, only one octave down.

The exceptions (creation, provision) reveal the boundary: humans can mirror divine cognition, but not divine ontology.

## One-sentence summary

**The Quran encodes a systematic God-human cognitive mirror: the same Arabic roots furnish a divine intensive/causative form acting on cosmic objects and a human reflexive/derived form acting on scriptural or local objects, making `tadabbur` of the Book to humans what `tadbīr` of the cosmos is to God — same operation, scaled scope — across at least eight cognitive roots, with creation and provision as the deliberate ontological exceptions.**
