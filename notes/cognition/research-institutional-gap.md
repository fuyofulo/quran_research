# Cross-cutting investigation: The Institutional Vocabulary Gap

> **Source:** Phase 2 rabbit-hole agent, deep "research" investigation, 2026-04-26.
> **Hypothesis tested:** Medieval Islam built scholarly/institutional categories on Quranic vocabulary that the Quran itself does not authorize.
> **Triggered by:** Findings in concept-Elm.md (`ʿulamāʾ` only 2× in 854) and concept-fqh.md (no noun *fiqh*, no agent *faqīh* in 20 occurrences).

## Headline finding

Of the ~25 institutional/scholarly Arabic terms that define classical and modern Islamic life — *mufti, qāḍī, faqīh, mujtahid, mufassir, muhaddith, madhhab, madrasa, ijtihād, ijmāʿ, qiyās, fatwā, tarbiya, tarjamah, khānqāh, ṭarīqa* — **not a single one appears in the Quran in its medieval-technical sense**. About **half do not appear at all** as lemmas. The other half appear only as verbs or general nouns describing ordinary human activity (asking, judging, studying, gathering, going-out, narrating). The entire architecture of Islamic professional religion is built on roots the Quran uses, but in lexical forms the Quran never coins.

## Synthesis

Counting strictly: of ~25 surveyed terms,

- **~12 lemmas are entirely absent** from the Quran (mufti, fatwā as nouns, faqīh, fiqh, mujtahid, ijtihād, mufassir, muhaddith, madhhab, madrasa, khānqāh, ijmāʿ, qiyās, amīr, tarbiya, tarjamah, madhhab) — and three of these (qiyās, amīr, tarjamah) lack even the root.
- **~10 appear in a non-technical, pre-institutional sense**: shaykh = old man, ḥadīth = "any speech," sunnah = God's pattern, ṭarīqa = "way," tafsīr = "explanation" of an argument, dirāsa = "studying" (often mocking), jihād = "exertion (with the Quran)," sulṭān = "authority/proof," khalīfa = "successor on earth," imām = "exemplar/record."
- **Only ~1–2 carry anything resembling later institutional content**: *jumuʿah* (62:9, the Friday assembly) and arguably *masjid* as a place of worship — and even *masjid* is generic ("place of prostration"), not a defined juridical site.

## Conclusion

The pattern is now unambiguous and extends what was already documented for *ʿulamāʾ* and *fiqh*: **the entire professional, institutional, juridical, mystical, and educational apparatus of historical Islam is named with vocabulary the Quran either does not use, or uses in a fundamentally different sense.**

Three sub-patterns emerge:

1. **Total invention.** *Qiyās*, *amīr*, *tarjamah*, *madrasa*, *khānqāh*, *madhhab*, *fiqh* (noun), *faqīh*, *mufti*, *fatwā*, *mujtahid*, *ijtihād*, *ijmāʿ*, *mufassir*, *muhaddith*, *tarbiya* — these noun-forms simply do not exist in revelation. They were minted later to label professions and disciplines.

2. **Lexical capture.** *Sunnah* in the Quran is exclusively *sunnat Allāh* (God's unchanging pattern with prior peoples — 33:62). Medieval Islam transferred the word from God's sunnah to the Prophet's sunnah. *Ḥadīth* in the Quran asks "*fa-bi-ayyi ḥadīthin baʿdahu yuʾminūn?*" ("In what *ḥadīth* after [the Quran] will they believe?", 77:50, polemical against extra-Quranic discourse) — yet the term became the name of precisely the extra-Quranic corpus the Quran questioned. *Jihād* meant "striving with the Quran" (25:52); it became a doctrine of war. *Sulṭān* meant "evidentiary authority/proof"; it became a king's title. *Khalīfa* meant Adam, David, or any people succeeding another; it became a political office.

3. **Generic words promoted to titles.** *Shaykh* simply means an old man (an old husband at 11:72, a feeble father at 28:23). *Imām* means a leader, an exemplar — even "a clear record" (36:12). These ordinary words were elevated into religious offices.

The implication is sharp: **medieval Islamic institutional vocabulary is not authorized by the Quran.** The Quran has roots for knowing, judging, comprehending, gathering, narrating, exemplifying — but it conspicuously refuses to coin agent-nouns and abstract institutional nouns from those roots. A book that says "*li-yatafaqqahū fī l-dīn*" (let them deeply comprehend in the religion, 9:122) does not produce *the fuqahāʾ*. A book that says "*yastaftūnaka*" (they ask you for a ruling, 4:127) does not produce *the muftis*. A book that calls God *al-mawlā* (8:40) does not produce a class of human *mawālī* with juridical status.

**The vocabulary gap is the institution-shaped hole at the center of Quranic Arabic. Medieval Islam filled it; the Quran left it open.**
