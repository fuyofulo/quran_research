# Cluster 75 — Witnessing the Visible Sign

> **Size:** 18 roots | **Source:** Parallel-agent analysis, 2026-04-26
> **Top members:** راي · خرج · صوب · كود · بلد · خلل · طمع · سحب
> **Full data:** `data/full-quran-graph/clusters.json` key "75"
> **CRITICAL:** This cluster contains rAy (see/think) — the third "seeing" root I identified as missed from the original 14 cognitive roots. It clusters HERE, NOT with cognition. This is structurally significant.

## Cluster name
**Witnessing the Visible Sign — Sky, Storm, Land, and the Eye that Beholds**

## Semantic territory

This cluster is built around the *empirically perceivable cosmos as evidentiary theater*. Its center of mass is رأي (rAy, 328) — the Quranic verb of *ocular witnessing* — surrounded by a constellation of natural-phenomenon roots (سحب clouds, برق lightning, ودق rain, ركم cumulus piling, ضغن swirling, ثور stirring up, كوكب stars, افق horizons, بلد land/town) and human/cosmic-event roots that complete the perceptual scene (خرج emerging/expelling, صوب striking/befalling, جدث graves opening, وقف being made-to-stand, فوت escape/elude, كاد almost/nearly, خلال interstices, طمع yearning to see, خليل intimate). The unifying gesture is the imperative "أَلَمْ تَرَ" — *Do you not see?* — answered by a moving picture of weather, geography, history, and eschatology.

## Sub-themes

1. **The atmospheric sign-system (سحب · برق · ودق · ركم · ضغن · ثور · صوب · سحب · افق · كوكب · بلد).** The Quran's argumentative weather: clouds driven, piled (مَّرْكُوم), made into mountains, then rain emerging (يَخْرُج), lightning striking (يُصِيب), winds stirring (أَثَارَ) — a self-contained meteorological proof-loop centered on 24:43, 30:48, 13:12.

2. **The ocular imperative — "ألم تر".** rAy here is overwhelmingly the *evidential* "have you not seen" (يَرَى, تَرَ, رَأَوْا), not the cognitive "consider/opine." It points the addressee outward to a phenomenon, not inward to thought.

3. **Emergence and expulsion (خرج · جدث · بلد).** Things coming *out* of containers: rain out of cloud, dead out of graves (أَجْدَاث), people expelled from cities (بلد), Banu al-Nadir from their homes (59:2), plants from dead land. خرج is the cluster's verb of revealing-by-extraction.

4. **Striking, missing, almost (صوب · فوت · كاد · طمع).** The drama of contingency: calamity *strikes* (أصاب), evildoers cannot *escape* (يفوتنا), the believer was *almost* swayed (كاد يركن), the heart *yearns* (يطمع) — modal verbs for the moment a sign almost reaches its target.

5. **Eschatological seeing (وقف · جدث · خرج · صوب · رأي).** The Day when they are *made to stand* (وُقِفُوا), graves *expel* their contents, and "if you could see when they are terrified" (لو ترى) — the same rAy that watches clouds now watches resurrection.

## Top 7 structurally important roots

1. **رأي (rAy)** — hub; co-occurs with everything; supplies the verb of perception that the rest illustrate.
2. **خرج (xrj)** — second hub; the action verb that animates rain, graves, expulsion, plants.
3. **صوب (Swb)** — bridges weather (لِيُصِيبَ بِهِ) to moral calamity (مصيبة) to correctness (صواب).
4. **كاد (kwd)** — modal; binds "almost happened" scenes which always invoke a witnessed sign.
5. **سحب (sHb)** — strongest weather node; 55% co-occurrence with rAy.
6. **بلد (bld)** — geographical anchor: the dead land revived, the secure city, expulsion-from.
7. **برق (brq)** — lightning: the prototype of fearful/hopeful sign (خَوْفًا وَطَمَعًا, 13:12 / 30:24).

## Surprises (especially the rAy positioning)

The biggest surprise — and the project-relevant finding — is *why rAy did not cluster with the cognition family (عقل, فقه, فكر, ذكر, بصر, نظر).* The data answers cleanly: **Quranic رأي is overwhelmingly deictic-evidential, not deliberative.** Its dominant idiom is "أَلَمْ تَرَ كَيْفَ" / "أَلَمْ تَرَ إِلَى" — *look at this thing*, where the object is a cloud, an army, a city, a grave, a mountain, lightning. Co-occurrence with weather/geography roots (sHb 0.55, brq 0.50, wdq 1.0, kwkb 0.40, Afq 0.67) outweighs co-occurrence with cognitive verbs. By contrast, بصر/نظر/فكر cluster around the *interior* act of considering. So **rAy belongs to the *phenomenology of the displayed sign*, while the cognition cluster owns the *interior processing of meaning*.** The graph is making a real semantic distinction the lexica blur.

Other surprises: (a) خليل (Abrahamic friendship) sitting with خلال (the gaps/interstices through which rain emerges) — the Louvain method correctly groups them as homographic root خلل but the *semantics* are split; worth flagging. (b) ضغن (rancor, only 2 occurrences, both 47:29-37) lands here only via co-occurrence with خرج ("expose their hatreds") — a fragile edge. (c) طمع clusters with brq via the formula خوفًا وطمعًا — fear/hope as the affect-pair triggered by lightning.

## Suggested research directions

1. **Quantify the evidential vs. cognitive split for rAy.** Tag every rAy occurrence as (a) "ألم تر" deictic, (b) eschatological "لو ترى", (c) dream/vision (رؤيا), (d) ostentation (رياء), (e) opinion. Predict cluster membership from the distribution.
2. **The weather-proof template.** Map the recurring 5-step rhetorical structure (drives clouds → piles → rains → strikes → some rejoice / some grieve) across 24:43, 30:48, 13:12, 7:57, 35:9, 78:14. Is there a stable syntactic skeleton?
3. **خرج as the "revelation operator."** Tabulate the source→target arguments of أخرج: dead-from-living, plants-from-earth, people-from-darkness, ore-from-stone, Banu Nadir-from-homes. Test whether خرج functions as the Quran's general verb of *unveiling*.
4. **Bridging back to cognition.** Build a small co-occurrence subgraph across cluster 75 + the cognition cluster to see what edges *almost* pulled rAy across — likely بصر, آية, كيف. This may identify the "perception interface" between displayed-sign and processed-meaning.

## One-sentence summary

Cluster 75 is the Quran's *theater of the visible sign* — the eye (رأي) trained on storms, stars, cities, graves, and battlefields, where God displays evidence that strikes (صوب), emerges (خرج), and almost (كاد) escapes the heedless beholder.
