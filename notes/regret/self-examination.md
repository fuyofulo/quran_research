# Looking at my own life: a self-examination on regret

*First draft, for us to work on together. Every fact comes from the research in this folder. The numbers in brackets are verses (surah:ayah), so you can check each one.*

## What regret is in the Quran

The Quran shows people regretting what they did in 56 verses. Most of these moments (34) are on the Day of Judgement. Ten come at death, or as punishment arrives. Twelve happen in this life.

Regret almost always arrives when a person finally *sees* what was true all along: "We have removed your cover, so your sight today is sharp" (50:22).

And one thing surprised us. **The Quran never asks you to feel regret.** No verse tells anyone to regret. What it asks, again and again, is that you *turn back* to God (39:54, 66:8). Regret happens to a person. Turning back is something you do.

## How to use this

Each regret below has two kinds of signs, kept apart on purpose:

- **The Quran names this**: the word or phrase is in the verse.
- **Our reading**: what we think the verses point to, though they don't say it. Treat these as questions, not facts.

One thing first. When people in the Quran describe their own failure, they say "we were heedless" (21:97) or "our misery overpowered us" (23:106). They never say "I wanted," "I loved," or "I preferred." Those words come from God, or from people looking on. So don't wait to *feel* the wanting. Ask: what did I want, prefer, assume?

## The regrets, one by one

### 1. Not taking the warning seriously (12 verses, the largest group)

**What the Quran shows.** "If only we had been listening, or using our reason, we would not be among the people of the Fire" (67:10). Pharaoh says "I believe" only as he drowns (10:90).

**The Quran names this:** assuming it would never end: "Had you not sworn before that there would be no end for you?" (14:44). Forgetting the meeting with God (32:14). Being "diverted by hope" (15:3). Luxury (23:33).

**Our reading:** ears that close slowly. For a believer who denies nothing, the nearer danger may be quieter: people "whose effort is lost… while they think that they are doing well" (18:104).

**Ask yourself:** Which reminder have I heard so often that I no longer hear it? Who, apart from me, could tell me whether I am doing well?

### 2. Whom I followed (6 verses)

**What the Quran shows.** "Woe to me! I wish I had not taken that one as a friend. He led me away from the reminder after it had come to me" (25:28-29). Others wish they had obeyed God instead of "our leaders and our great ones" (33:66-67).

**The Quran names this:** turning a blind eye to the remembrance of the Most Merciful, after which a devil is assigned as companion (43:36). Companions who make things look good (41:25). Thinking you are guided when you are not (43:37).

**Notice:** in these arguments on the Day, no one says "I was forced." Even Satan says: "I had no authority over you except that I invited you, and you responded to me" (14:22).

**Our reading:** going along to keep a friendship. Deferring to people above you.

**Ask yourself:** After time with this person, do I remember God more, or less? Whose opinion do I follow without checking it?

### 3. What I neglected toward God (5 verses)

**What the Quran shows.** "Alas for me, over what I neglected in my duty to God, and I was one of the mockers" (39:56). The word for *neglected*, farraṭa, means falling short of something you owed. Both times someone cries "alas!" (yā ḥasratā), they name what they neglected (6:31, 39:56).

**The Quran names this:** heedlessness, ghafla (21:97, 19:39). Forgetting (7:53). Mockery (39:56). Arrogance (39:59). One verse makes a chain: a heart "made heedless of Our remembrance," that "follows his desire," and "whose affair is ever in neglect" (18:28).

**Our reading:** not one bad act but a slow fraying.

**Ask yourself:** Which duty do I keep meaning to come back to? Where in my life are things fraying?

### 4. Wealth: trusted, hoarded, or kept from the poor (5 verses)

**What the Quran shows.** The dying man: "My Lord, if only You would delay me a little, so I could give charity" (63:10). "I wish I had sent ahead for my life" (89:24). And the garden owners who planned, "No poor person will enter it today" (68:24), then found their garden destroyed.

**The Quran names this:** wealth and children distracting from remembering God (63:9). "You love wealth with immense love" (89:20). Not honouring the orphan, not urging one another to feed the poor (89:17-18). "Competing for more distracts you, until you visit the graves" (102:1-2).

**Worth noticing:** in 19 passages that list causes, failing to feed the poor comes up 4 times, missing prayer once (74:43-44). Twice the fault is *not urging others* (69:34, 89:18).

**Our reading:** greed. Relying on yourself. Feeling entitled.

**Ask yourself:** Whom did I not feed this month, or not encourage others to feed? Name the person, or the excuse.

### 5. Putting something beside God (4 verses)

**What the Quran shows.** In his own words, the man with two gardens never denied God; he even spoke of "my Lord" (18:36). But when his garden was destroyed, he turned his hands over what he had spent on it and said: "I wish I had not associated anyone with my Lord" (18:42).

**The Quran names this:** "I am greater than you in wealth and in men" (18:34). "I do not think this will ever perish" (18:35). His friend asked: when you entered your garden, why didn't you say *mā shāʾa Allāh*, "what God willed" (18:39)?

**Our reading:** feeling safe because of what you own.

**Ask yourself:** When something I built goes well, what is my first sentence?

### 6. Wronging my own family (4 verses)

**What the Quran shows.** Cain, after killing his brother, "became one of the regretful" (5:31). Joseph's brothers, years later: "We have been sinners" (12:91).

**The Quran names this:** of Cain, "his soul permitted to him the murder of his brother" (5:30). The brothers, in their own words, before the act: "Joseph and his brother are more beloved to our father than we" (12:8).

**Our reading:** envy. But the word never appears in either story. For Cain the text gives no motive at all, only that his offering was rejected (5:27). And his words of regret are about failing to hide the body, not about the killing (5:31).

**What repair looked like:** the brothers named their wrong and asked their father to ask forgiveness for them (12:97). Joseph said, "No blame on you today" (12:92).

**Ask yourself:** Is there someone in my family I resent for being preferred? Has that feeling moved into anything I do?

### 7. One rash act against others (2 verses)

Both are warnings given *before* the act. "If a wicked person brings you news, check it, so you do not harm people out of ignorance and then become regretful over what you did" (49:6). And people who rush into alliances saying "we are afraid a misfortune may strike us" (5:52).

**The Quran names this:** acting in ignorance (49:6). Fear, and "disease in their hearts" (5:52).

**Our reading:** haste. Believing things too easily. Hedging your bets.

**Ask yourself:** What did I pass on this week without checking? Which alliance do I keep only out of fear?

### 8. Regrets with no name (18 verses)

Some regrets point at nothing specific. **The whole life** (10): "We were wrongdoers" (7:5); "My Lord, send me back" (23:99). **Wishing never to have existed** (6): "I wish that I were dust" (78:40). **Facing the record** (2): "What is this book that leaves nothing small or great?" (18:49). The one feeling named there is fear.

**What to notice:** the vague promise is the cheap one. When people beg to go back, only 2 of 13 name an actual deed (63:10, 2:167). God's answer points to the past: "Did We not give you life enough for whoever would remember to remember?" (35:37).

**For you:** a vague unease is not yet the Quran's shape of regret. A named thing is. Turn the unease into one specific item.

### Regrets aimed at the wrong thing (3 verses)

- **A missed gain.** A man who stayed behind thanks God when the others are hurt, then wishes he had been with them when they win (4:72-73).
- **Someone else's fortune.** "If only we had what Qārūn was given" (28:79).
- **The "if only" about fate.** "If they had stayed with us, they would not have died." God makes that very thought a grief in their hearts (3:156).

**Ask yourself:** Is my regret about something I did, or about something I missed?

## Three kinds of regret, and what to do with each

### The kind that turns you back

The Quran shows 12 regrets that worked, among them Adam (7:23), Moses (28:16), Yūnus (21:87) and the three men left behind (9:118). What they share:

- **None of them says "I regret" or "if only."** They turn.
- **All 12 turn toward God.** Some speak to Him directly; the brothers went through their father (12:97).
- **Most name their own wrong plainly**: "I have wronged myself" (28:16). The subject is *I*, not "they" or "circumstances".
- **When they ask, they ask for forgiveness or mercy**, never for the loss to be undone.
- **God moves first**: "He turned to them so they could repent" (9:118).
- **An act follows.** In 17 of the 32 verses where someone repents, a second action comes straight after: setting things right, believing, doing good. Moses, forgiven, says: "I will never be an assistant to the criminals" (28:17). They "do not persist" (3:135), and they turn "soon after" (4:17).

**Ask yourself:** After "I turned back," what is my second verb?

### The kind that comes too late

Pharaoh, drowning: "I believe." The answer: "Now? And you had disobeyed before?" (10:90-91). The dying man's "I have repented now" is not accepted (4:18). What decides is what came *before*.

The same words can land on either side. "We were wrongdoers," said in time, is followed by hope (68:29-32). Said as punishment falls, it was all they could say (7:5).

There is a this-life version too: people at sea cry out to God, are rescued, and at once go back (10:22-23).

**Ask yourself:** Think of something you turned to God about under pressure. If the pressure were gone, which part would stay?

### The kind the Quran tells you not to carry

- **Grief over what slipped away**: "So that you do not despair over what has eluded you" (57:23), because it was written before it happened (57:22).
- **Grief over what others chose.** To the Prophet: "Do not let yourself perish over them in regret" (35:8).
- **The "if only" about fate** (3:156, above).

The Quran forbids grief 16 times, and not once is it grief over one's own sin. So the line is this: regret about **your own act** can lead somewhere. Grief about **an outcome you did not control** is what you are told to put down.

Some grief is not blamed: men who wept because they could not afford to join (9:92). And Jacob: "I only complain of my suffering and my grief to God" (12:86). His grief went to God, kept hope, and ended in action: "Go and find out about Joseph" (12:87).

### A guard against despair

Just before three regrets of the Day (39:56-58), the Quran says:

> "O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of the mercy of God. Indeed, God forgives all sins" (39:53).

Hope comes first. Then turn back (39:54). Then follow (39:55). Only then the regrets, as a warning, not a verdict. Losing hope in *people* is human; even the messengers felt it (12:110). Losing hope in *God's mercy* is the fault (12:87, 15:56).

## Two exercises

### 1. The death-bed wish, read backwards

63:10 opens with a command: "Spend from what We have provided you, before death comes to one of you." Then it gives the dying person's words: "My Lord, if only You would delay me a little, so I could give charity and be among the righteous."

The wish *is* the command. What he regrets is exactly what he was told to do.

So finish this sentence: *"My Lord, if only You would delay me, so I could ______."* Whatever goes in the blank, you can still do.

Now read 9:75-76. Some people made almost the same promise in this life: "If He gives us from His bounty, we will surely give charity and be among the righteous." When He gave, "they were stingy with it and turned away." Their promise had a condition. The Quran shows four promises of this shape (rescue me, or give me, and I will…), and every one is broken in the very next verse (6:63-64, 7:189-190, 9:75-76, 10:22-23).

Does your blank hide a condition, like "when I have more"? Remove it. Make it small. Do it this week.

### 2. What have I sent ahead?

"Let every soul look to what it has put forth for tomorrow" (59:18).

On the Day, "a man will observe what his hands have put forth" (78:40). 59:18 asks for that look *now*, while it can still change something.

Take one page with two columns. Left: what I sent ahead this month (prayer, giving, kindness, duties met). Right: what I held back. Then show it to one person you trust, because people can be sure they are doing well when they are not (18:104), and the saved "advise each other to truth" (103:3).

## Where we go next together

The research opened these and left them for you. The text does not settle them; your own life might.

1. **Blank, or looking away?** "That is what you were trying to avoid" (50:19), then "you were in unmindfulness of this" (50:22). Which comes first in you?
2. **The silences.** Nine regret scenes give no reason at all, such as Cain (5:31) and Thamūd (26:157). What would you put there?
3. **Moving a resentment.** Joseph's brothers first said "our father loves him more" (12:8), and later, "God has preferred you over us" (12:91). Is that move the real repair?
4. **The quiet danger.** Is the real risk for a believer "thinking I'm doing well" (18:104)? What would count as evidence against it?
5. **One feeling under three?** "Sure they were able" (68:25). "I do not think this will ever perish" (18:35). "No end for you" (14:44).
6. **The bad friend: cause or result?** "He led me away" (25:29), or "whoever turns from remembrance, We appoint for him a devil" (43:36)?

Bring back what you find, and we will build the next version together.
