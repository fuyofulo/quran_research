# The speech of regret: what people say, and what God says back

## Headline finding

Regret speech has three grammars, not one. **Soliloquy** (`yā laytanī`, 14×) is quoted, addressed to no one, mostly singular (8 of 10 self-regret), and names a *specific past item* in the perfect or `lam`+jussive: a way with the Messenger, a friend, an obedience, shirk, "what I sent ahead". **Petition** (`rabbanā`/`rabbi` + imperative) is mostly plural and promises *generic* righteousness (6 of 13) or identity (4 of 13); only 2 of 13 name an act. **God's replies never answer the hypothetical on its merits** (one exception, 6:28). They re-narrate the past: time given, a warner came, your own words. The specific wish is the diagnostic; the generic promise is the cheap one.

**9 of 10 look backward** (perfect or negated-past); only 6:27 looks forward; only 43:38 is nominal. Nothing in `layta` is a bare feeling: the wish always takes a clause with a verb or a place.

**Test of Phase 0's four shapes.**
- *Undo* mixes two things: a **named deed** (18:42, 25:27, 25:28, 33:66, 89:24, plus 15:2 `law kānū muslimīn`) and **self-annulment** (69:25, 69:27, 78:40, 4:42). Split them.
- *Return* is three asks, by verb: **back** (`irjiʿ/ruddā/karra`: 2:167, 6:27, 7:53, 23:99, 26:102, 32:12, 39:58, 42:44 = 8), **out** (`akhrij`: 23:107, 35:37, 40:11 = 3), **later** (`akhkhir`: 14:44, 63:10 = 2). 6:27 is filed under *undo* in Phase 0 but is a return.
- *Separate* holds (43:38; 2:167 `natabarraʾa`; 3:30), but 70:11 is **substitution** (ransom by children, wife, brother, all `law yaftadī`): a fifth shape.
- Form is not diagnostic of guilt: 19:23 (Maryam, innocent) and 69:27 are the same wish in the same grammar (`mittu qabla hādhā` / `kānat al-qāḍiya`). Only context separates them.

**Woe-cries (10).** Own-conduct with a stated fault: 5:31, 21:14, 21:46, 21:97, 25:28, 68:31 (6). Recognition or shock with no stated fault: 18:49, 36:52, 37:20 (3). Control: 11:72. The only *named* inner state in any woe-cry is `ghafla` (21:97 *kunnā fī ghaflatin min hādhā*); 68:31 names `ṭughyān`. `ẓālimīn` is a verdict word, not a feeling.

**`wadda … law` (3:30, 4:42, 15:2, 70:11).** Unlike `layta`, these are *narrated* wishes. The Quran names the wishing (`yawaddu`) only in narration; the quoted speeches stay bare. Shapes: separate, annul, undo, substitute.

## 2. The return-requests

### 2a. What they promise (13 refs; scripted tally)

| Promise | Refs | n |
|---|---|---|
| General righteous action (`naʿmal ṣāliḥan`, `ghayra alladhī kunnā naʿmal`, `min al-muḥsinīn`, `min al-ṣāliḥīn`) | 23:100, 32:12, 35:37, 7:53, 39:58, 63:10 | 6 |
| Identity / belief (`nakūna min al-muʾminīn`, `lā nukadhdhiba`, answer the call, follow the messengers) | 6:27, 26:102, 14:44 | 3 |
| One specific act | 63:10 `aṣṣaddaqa` (the only before-death request; it names charity); 2:167 `natabarraʾa minhum` (disown the leaders) | 2 |
| No promise: pledge of penalty / confession / question | 23:107, 40:11 (`iʿtarafnā`), 42:44 | 3 |

**[interpretive]** What people promise to do differently is vague and flattering; what they wish *undone* in `layta` is concrete. For introspection, trust the specific wish over the generic vow. The Quran's own pre-emption (63:10, `anfiqū min mā razaqnākum min qabli an yaʾtiya`) names the one concrete deed.

Tally: **time/means already given** 3 (35:37, 39:59, 14:45); **own earlier words or conduct** 5 (6:28, 14:44, 23:110, 32:14, 40:12); **flat refusal/closure** 4 (23:100, 23:108, 2:167, 63:11); **no in-scene reply** 3 (26:102, 7:53, 42:44).

**Fixed shape?** Not a template, but a pattern: replies to *address* (`rabbanā` + imperative) turn into **retrospective accounts** opening `a-wa-lam` / `balā` / `dhālikum bi-`, then close with `fa-dhūqū` (32:14, 35:37; also 6:30). Replies to *soliloquy* are narrated, third-person (6:28, 26:103). Only 23:100 and 63:11 refuse the request in so many words. **[interpretive]** The reply to "send me back" is "look at what you were given," not "no."

Panels (the five load-bearing):

## 3. The hands

| Ref | Idiom | What the text gives |
|---|---|---|
| 25:27 | `yaʿaḍḍu ʿalā yadayhi` | self-directed bite; **no emotion word** (the "[in regret]" is Saheeh's bracket) |
| 18:42 | `yuqallibu kaffayhi ʿalā mā anfaqa fīhā` | palms turned **over what he spent**; the gesture's object is the lost outlay, the speech's object (shirk) is different |
| 7:149 | `suqiṭa fī aydīhim` + `raʾaw annahum qad ḍallū` | passive, agentless; realisation by sight. The speech is a conditional plea (`la-in lam yarḥamnā … la-nakūnanna min al-khāsirīn`) |
| 3:119 (control) | `ʿaḍḍū ʿalaykumu al-anāmila mina al-ghayẓ` | same verb, tool `ʿaḍḍa`, but aimed at *others* (`ʿalaykum`) and the emotion is **named**: `ghayẓ` rage |

So "bite the hands" is not intrinsically regret; context assigns it. The idiom **hold: partly**. All three regret gestures use hands (data), but the reading "regret is staged on the hands" overreaches: other bodies appear too. Faces are turned over in the Fire (`tuqallabu wujūhuhum` 33:66, the same root `qlb` as 18:42's palm-turning, immediately before `yā laytanā aṭaʿnā`), heads hang (32:12), eyes fix (21:97). **[interpretive]** Hands carry the *deed* (`qaddamat aydīhim` 78:40; 89:24 `qaddamtu`); faces carry the *stance*. Regret is staged on whatever did the thing.

## 4. Singular vs plural (data)

`layta`: singular 11 (self-regret 8: 18:42, 25:27, 25:28, 43:38, 69:25, 69:27, 78:40, 89:24), plural 3 (6:27, 28:79, 33:66). Return-requests: singular 3 (23:99, 39:58, 63:10), plural 10. Woe-cries: singular 2 (5:31, 25:28), plural 7. So Phase 0's plural dominance is carried by petitions and woe-cries; **`layta` is singular, and its singular content clusters**: personal ledger (record, death, dust: 69:25, 69:27, 78:40); the companion (25:27-28, 43:38); own property or life (18:42, 89:24). Plural `layta` is the *followers'* voice (33:66 → 33:67 *we obeyed our masters*; 2:167).

## Negative space

No `layta` is addressed to God. No reply to a return request answers the vow itself except 6:28. No inner-state word appears in the speech of the self-regret `layta` (the states come from nearby verses: `ṭughyān` 68:31, `ghafla` 21:97, `istikbār` 39:59, `ḥubb` of wealth 89:20, `ẓann` 18:35-36).

## For introspection

1. Name one concrete thing you could say today in the form `yā laytanī lam…` or `…ittakhadhtu`. If you can only produce a vague unease, the Quranic shape is not yet there.
2. List the vows you make (to be better, more regular, more charitable). How many name a specific act? Compare to 63:10, which names charity.
3. Which person, if you could audit the friendship from the end, would you "have not taken"? (25:28, 43:38)
4. Who are your `sādat` and `kubarāʾ`, the ones whose obedience would later be called your regret? (33:67)
5. Check 35:37 against yourself: what warner has already come, and what time has been given?
6. Does your expenditure (`anfaqa`) go to something whose loss you would turn your palms over? (18:42)
