# ndm / Hsr / frT — the two words, ḥasra, and what was neglected

## Headline finding

The Phase-0 claim "`nādim` = *woke up to find himself* regretful" **does not survive**. Of the 28 `aṣbaḥa` ayahs, only 2 are literal morning (30:17, 68:20); the five regret uses are plain *became* (4 of 5 after `fa-`; 3 of 5 imperfect, i.e. **forecasts**). Reframe: this-life regret is a **state one passes into after the act, and which God announces beforehand**. `nadāma` is concealed in the hereafter; `ḥasra` is the *spoken* cry. `ḥasra` is feeling, object and outcome at once — and every human `yā ḥasratā` (2/2) names the same object: `ʿalā mā farraṭ-`, *what I neglected*. 17:29 `maḥsūr` is *depleted*, not regretful.

---

## 1. Test of the `aṣbaḥa` claim (Q1) — data-grounded

Lemma `>aSobaHa` (root `SbH`), computed from `words.jsonl`: **28 words in 28 ayahs**. My classification (reading each ayah; the sort is interpretive, the list is counted):

| Class | n | Refs |
|---|---|---|
| Literal time of day | 2 | 30:17 (`tuṣbiḥūn` paired with `tumsūn`); 68:20 (68:17, 68:21 say `muṣbiḥīn` — morning stated) |
| Ambiguous: follows a sudden/overnight event or a "yesterday" contrast; morning possible, never required | 9 | 7:78, 7:91, 11:67, 11:94, 29:37 (`jāthimīn`); 46:25; 28:18, 28:82 (`bi-l-amsi`); 18:42 |
| "Became" — no time sense | 17 | 3:103, 5:30, **5:31**, **5:52**, 5:53, 5:102, 18:40, 18:41, 18:45, 22:63, **23:40**, **26:157**, 28:10, 41:23, **49:6**, 61:14, 67:30 |

Bold = the five `nādim` uses. **Verdict: "woke up to find himself" is not defensible for any of the five.** 18:42 (garden-owner turning his palms) is the strongest morning candidate and is still ambiguous; Arberry's "in the morning" is the translator's import. The "SbH ×15 lift" double-counts: 5 of its 6 regret ayahs are the `nādim` ayahs.

What survives, and is more interesting:

- **Never `kāna`, always `aṣbaḥa`**: 5/5. Regret is spoken of as an **inchoative state** — something entered, not something one is.
- **Tense**: perfect 2 (5:31, 26:157); imperfect **3** (5:52 `fa-yuṣbiḥū`, 23:40 `la-yuṣbiḥunna`, 49:6 `fa-tuṣbiḥū`). Three of five are God's forecast or warning, not report.
- **`fa-` consequence marker** 4/5 (all but 23:40, which has `ʿammā qalīlin`, *after a little*).
- **Companion state-predicates**: `aṣbaḥa` takes `khāsirīn` 3× (5:30, 5:53, 41:23) and `jāthimīn` 5×. In sūrat al-Māʾida the two states alternate in adjacent ayahs: 5:30 *loser* → 5:31 *regretful*; 5:52 *regretful* → 5:53 *losers*. **[interpretive]** Loss (`khusr`) is the objective ledger; regret is the later awareness of it.

**[interpretive]** Reframe for the catalogue: *regret is a resultative — the state you pass into once the act's result is visible.* The "morning" metaphor adds nothing the grammar supports; "what will I become?" is the question the text actually hands over.

## 2. The two words (Q2)

`nādim` ×5 (5:31, 5:52, 23:40, 26:157, 49:6), `nadāma` ×2 (10:54, 34:33) — counted; `nadāma` is both times the same clause.

| | `nādim` | `nadāma` |
|---|---|---|
| World | L | H |
| Grammar | `aṣbaḥa` + participle (state) | `asarrū` + noun, trigger `lammā raʾaw al-ʿadhāb` |
| Spoken? | 5:31 voiced (`yā waylatā`); others not | **never voiced** |
| Object | `ʿalā mā asarrū` (5:52), `ʿalā mā faʿaltum` (49:6) | none |

Hereafter, the **spoken** regrets are different words: `ḥasra` (6:31, 39:56), `layta`, woe-cries. In 34:31-33 the two camps speak at length — and every line is blame (`lawlā antum`, `bal kuntum mujrimīn`, `bal makr al-layl`) — while `nadāma` is *hidden*. **Data**: concealed regret sits beside deflected blame in 34, beside a failed ransom (`laftadat bihi`) in 10:54. **The text states no reason for the concealment** — the seam to push. [inferred] Admitting regret would concede the blame each camp is assigning elsewhere.

> 5:52 فَتَرَى ٱلَّذِينَ فِي قُلُوبِهِم مَّرَضࣱ يُسَٰرِعُونَ فِيهِمۡ يَقُولُونَ نَخۡشَىٰٓ أَن تُصِيبَنَا دَآئِرَةࣱ … فَيُصۡبِحُواْ عَلَىٰ مَآ أَسَرُّواْ فِيٓ أَنفُسِهِمۡ نَٰدِمِينَ
> Saheeh: "…hastening into [association with] them, saying 'We are afraid a misfortune may strike us'… they will become, over what they have been concealing within themselves, regretful."

> 10:54 … وَأَسَرُّواْ ٱلنَّدَامَةَ لَمَّا رَأَوُاْ ٱلۡعَذَابَۖ
> Saheeh: "they will confide regret when they see the punishment." (Arberry: "secretly remorseful"; Pickthall: "feel remorse within them".)

**The inversion** (`asarra` 19 ayahs in the corpus; it meets `ndm` in exactly three: 5:52, 10:54, 34:33): in life, the *concealed thing* becomes the regret (5:52: hidden hedging, then regret over it). In the hereafter, the *regret* is the concealed thing. That is as far as the text goes. Counter-text: 6:28 (what they used to hide becomes apparent) — hiding is not stable at that Day either.

## 3. `ḥasra` (Q3) — lemma/POS data

`Hasorap` N ×7 (2:167, 3:156, 8:36, 19:39, 35:8, 36:30, 69:50) + `Hasorat` (6:31 `yā ḥasratanā`) + `HasorataY` (39:56) = **9 nouns**; excluded: `yastaḥsirūn` 21:19 V, `ḥasīr` 67:4 N, `maḥsūr` 17:29 (below). **7 of 9 are construed with `ʿalā`** (2:167, 6:31, 8:36, 35:8, 36:30, 39:56, 69:50) — *upon* them, as 6:31's burdens are carried `ʿalā ẓuhūrihim`. **[interpretive]** ḥasra falls on, it is not merely felt in.

| Ref | Who feels | Who/what causes | Form |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6:31, 39:56 | the speaker (cry) | own `farraṭ-` | feeling, voiced |
| 3:156 | the hypocrites, in hearts | **God makes** (`li-yajʿala Allāhu dhālika ḥasratan`) their "if only they'd stayed" | feeling, installed |
| 2:167 | followers | **God shows** (`yurīhimu … aʿmālahum ḥasarātin ʿalayhim`) | **object: deeds *are* the ḥasarāt** |
| 8:36 | spenders of wealth | the wealth *becomes* (`takūnu ʿalayhim ḥasratan`), then `yughlabūn` | outcome |
| 19:39 | — | a Day named for it; `idh quḍiya al-amr`, they in `ghafla` | **time** |
| 69:50 | disbelievers | the Quran itself (`inna-hu la-ḥasra`) | **object** |
| 35:8 | the Prophet, forbidden | `lā tadhhab nafsuka ʿalayhim ḥasarātin` | lament over others |
| 36:30 | unmarked | — | lament over others |

**Is it a feeling, an object or an outcome? All three, and God is the grammatical agent wherever the agent is stated (3:156, 2:167).** The one human-caused case is 8:36, where the act itself (spending to block) turns into the regret. **[interpretive]** ḥasra is less an emotion one *chooses* than a state the deed becomes once exposed.

**36:30 speaker.** No `qāla`. The ground clause (`mā yaʾtīhim … illā kānū bihi yastahziʾūn`) is third-person, so the servants themselves are not speaking; 36:28-29 is God's "We" voice; the vocative is bare (`yā ḥasratan`, no possessive, unlike 6:31/39:56). Text allows: God's/narrator's voice, or a general address to ḥasra. It does **not** allow the Prophet to be the speaker without importing — 35:8 forbids his soul to go out in ḥasarāt over them. **Tension worth flagging**: the lament is forbidden to the Prophet (35:8) and unattributed at 36:30.

> 36:30 يَٰحَسۡرَةً عَلَى ٱلۡعِبَادِۚ مَا يَأۡتِيهِم مِّن رَّسُولٍ إِلَّا كَانُواْ بِهِۦ يَسۡتَهۡزِءُونَ
> Saheeh: "How regretful for the servants. There did not come to them any messenger except that they used to ridicule him."

## 4. `ḥasra` + `farraṭa` (Q4)

> 6:31 قَالُواْ يَٰحَسۡرَتَنَا عَلَىٰ مَا فَرَّطۡنَا فِيهَا … 39:56 يَٰحَسۡرَتَىٰ عَلَىٰ مَا فَرَّطتُ فِي جَنۢبِ ٱللَّهِ وَإِن كُنتُ لَمِنَ ٱلسَّٰخِرِينَ
> Saheeh: "Oh, [how great is] our regret over what we neglected concerning it…" / "…my regret over what I neglected in regard to Allah and that I was among the mockers."

**Verified by the verse text: 2/2** human `yā ḥasra` cries take exactly `ʿalā mā farraṭ-` (6:31, 39:56). Conversely `farraṭa` (human) is 3 (6:31, 39:56, 12:80); controls 6:38, 6:61 are God/angels (the angels "do not neglect" — `lā yufarriṭūn`, a duty done). Other frT: `furuṭ` 18:28, `yafruṭa` 20:45 (form I: *act hastily/exceed*), `mufraṭūn` 16:62.

- **Object grammar**: `farraṭa fī` + the thing owed: *it* (the Hour, 6:31), *the side of God* (39:56), *Joseph* (12:80). Always a **party to whom something was due**.
- **12:80** is not a lament: the elder brother cites `mā farraṭtum fī Yūsuf` as a reason not to fail the *next* trust (`mawthiq min Allāh`) — a regret that **binds future conduct** (`fa-lan abraḥa`). It also covers a deliberate act (throwing Joseph), so **`farraṭa` is not "omission" as against "commission"**: it is the *speaker's own frame* — failure measured against a duty.
- **18:28**: `wa-kāna amruhu furuṭan` describes the life that precedes such regret, with two named states: `aghfalnā qalbahu ʿan dhikrinā` (heedlessness) and `ittabaʿa hawāhu` (desire). 19:39 pairs the Day of Ḥasra with `ghafla`. So **named chain: `ghafla` + `hawā` → `furuṭ` → ḥasra**.
- In all three human uses `farraṭa` co-occurs with active offence (denying the meeting 6:31; mockery 39:56, 39:59 adds denial and `istakbara`). **[interpretive]** The people *describe* their wrong as neglect of a debt, not as an act. Neither cry names a deed (no "I killed", "I followed"). Cain (5:31) did the opposite: he names an act he failed to perform (hiding), not the killing.

## 5. 17:29 `maḥsūr` (Q5)

Root `Hsr` other senses: 21:19 `lā yastaḥsirūn` (angels *do not tire*), 67:4 `huwa ḥasīr` (sight returns *exhausted*). Both are **depletion of a faculty**; neither takes an object or `ʿalā`. 17:29's frame is a purse: hand wide open → `malūman maḥsūran`. **Four translators in the pack: 0 say "regretful"** (Saheeh "insolvent", Khattab "poverty", Arberry/Pickthall "denuded"). **My call: depleted/denuded.** `malūm` = blame from others; `maḥsūr` = emptied. Recommend it be **dropped from the regret catalogue**, kept as a prophylactic verse about the hand.

## 7. Negative space

No `nādim` is followed by repentance in-verse (23:41, 26:158: seizure next); `nadāma` never has an object; no `farraṭa` cry names a specific act.

## For introspection

1. Where, in an important relationship, are you describing a failure to yourself as something done *to* you (circumstance) rather than as something *owed* and unpaid (`farraṭa`)? Which framing is true?
2. 5:52: what are you hedging toward out of fear (`nakhshā`) that you would be ashamed to name? Write it; that is the thing the text says turns into regret.
3. 49:6: name one report you acted on this month that you did not check. What would `tabayyun` have cost?
4. 34:33: is there something you already regret that you are hiding by blaming? Who is the `lawlā antum`?
5. 18:28: where do you see `ghafla` + `hawā` right now — an area where your own conduct is `furuṭ` (frayed, unattended)?
