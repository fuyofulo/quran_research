# Narrative — regret inside this life

## Headline finding

In this life the Quran shows regret that converts and regret that does not, and the dividing line is **what the regretter does with God, not how much he feels.** Every converting case (calf 7:149, garden-people 68:29-32, brothers 12:91-98, the three 9:118) contains a **petition or act directed to God** (`la-in lam yarḥamnā rabbunā`, `subḥāna rabbinā`, `illā ilayhi`) Both narrated `nādim` cases (Cain 5:31, Thamūd 26:157) contain **no address to God at all**; the garden-owner *mentions* God but does not ask him. `nādim` never co-occurs with a turn to God.

---

## 1. Cain 5:27-31

**Five fields.** (1) Cain, L, narrated. (2) `yā waylatā a-ʿajaztu an akūna mithla hādhā al-ghurābi fa-uwāriya sawʾata akhī`. (3) Cause stated: none for the killing; the regret clause itself states only the inability to cover. (4) Offering rejected → threat → `ṭawwaʿat lahu nafsuhu` → killed → `aṣbaḥa mina al-khāsirīn` (5:30) → crow → regret. (5) Named: `nafs` that "made it easy" (5:30). Not named: envy. The text gives the rejected offering and the threat; envy is **[inferred]**.

**Panel — 5:31:** فَأَصۡبَحَ مِنَ ٱلنَّٰدِمِينَ ← قَالَ يَٰوَيۡلَتَىٰٓ أَعَجَزۡتُ أَنۡ أَكُونَ مِثۡلَ هَٰذَا ٱلۡغُرَابِ فَأُوَٰرِيَ سَوۡءَةَ أَخِيۖ
*"Woe to me! Have I failed to be like this crow and hide my brother's body?" (Saheeh)*

**Clause order (data-grounded).** The interrogative `a-ʿajaztu` takes `an akūna mithla al-ghurāb` as its object, followed by `fa-uwāriya` (so that I might cover). The grammatical object of the lament is **incapacity to conceal**. The killing is not the object of any clause; `nādim` follows after. The traditional reading takes his regret as being about the burial, not the deed; the clause order is consistent with that reading, and also with a narrow one in which the crow shows him *what the deed has made of the body*. **[interpretive]** The text does not show him regretting the *act* in any clause. Third party spoke **before** the act, at 5:28-29 (`innī akhāfu Allāh`), and was not heard.

## 2. The garden-owner 18:32-44

**Five fields.** (1) A man, L. (2) 18:42 `yā laytanī lam ushrik bi-rabbī aḥadā`; hands turned `ʿalā mā anfaqa fīhā`. (3) The cause the text states: **none in his own mouth**; the friend states it at 18:37 as `a-kafarta` and 18:39 as a missing phrase. (4) Boast → entering the garden → denial of perishing and the Hour → friend's counsel → ruin → regret. (5) **Named:** `ẓālimun li-nafsihi` (18:35); `mā aẓunnu` ×2 (`an tabīda hādhihi abadā`, `al-sāʿata qāʾima`); comparison `anā akthar minka mālan wa-aʿazzu nafarā` (18:34); entitlement `la-ajidanna khayran minhā munqalabā` (18:36). **[inferred]:** pride, security in ownership.

**Panel — 18:42:** وَأُحِيطَ بِثَمَرِهِۦ فَأَصۡبَحَ يُقَلِّبُ كَفَّيۡهِ عَلَىٰ مَآ أَنفَقَ فِيهَا وَهِيَ خَاوِيَةٌ عَلَىٰ عُرُوشِهَا وَيَقُولُ يَٰلَيۡتَنِي لَمۡ أُشۡرِكۡ بِرَبِّيٓ أَحَدࣰا

**Observations (data-grounded).** (a) His spoken words never denied God (he says `rabbī` at 18:36); the regret names **shirk**, a diagnosis he did not utter earlier. The text, not the man, supplies the category. (b) The hands turn over **what he spent** (`anfaqa`), so the grief object is the investment. Contrast 9:92 below. (c) The friend's advice opens with `law-lā idh dakhalta jannataka qulta mā shāʾa Allāh` (18:39). The middle brother in 68:28 opens `law-lā tusabbiḥūn`. Both reproaches name **a missing acknowledgement at the moment of entering the garden**. (d) He boasted in `nafar` (18:34); the end is `lam takun lahu fiʾatun yanṣurūnahu` (18:43). The text is **silent** on whether he turned.

## 3. The garden-people 68:17-33

**Five fields.** (1) Brothers/owners, L. (2) `yā waylanā innā kunnā ṭāghīn` (68:31); hope `ʿasā rabbunā an yubdilanā khayran minhā` (68:32). (3) Cause: the middle one's diagnosis `law-lā tusabbiḥūn`; mutual blame 68:30. (4) Oath `wa-lā yastathnūn` (68:18); the blow came while they slept (68:19, before any act of theirs); plan: `lā yadkhulannahā al-yawma ʿalaykum miskīn` (68:24); `ʿalā ḥardin qādirīn` (68:25). (5) Named: the self-description `qādirīn`; the oath's omitted exception (`istithnāʾ`); later `ẓālimīn` / `ṭāghīn` as confessions. **[inferred]:** greed, self-reliance. First reading of the sight: `ḍāllūn` ("astray", 68:26), `maḥrūmūn` (68:27).

**Panel — 68:28-29:** قَالَ أَوۡسَطُهُمۡ أَلَمۡ أَقُل لَّكُمۡ لَوۡلَا تُسَبِّحُونَ ۝ قَالُواْ سُبۡحَٰنَ رَبِّنَآ إِنَّا كُنَّا ظَٰلِمِينَ

**Decisive observation (data-grounded).** The first thing they say after the loss is the very act the middle one had asked for (`tusabbiḥūn` → `subḥāna rabbinā`). They perform the omitted act; then confess; then blame one another (68:30); then confess again, with a stronger word (`ṭāghīn`, 68:31); then hope. The garden-owner never performs his omitted phrase. The poor are never mentioned again after 68:24, and no restitution is named. The conversion is a hope (`ʿasā`), not a reported outcome.

## 4. The calf 7:148-153; 20:85-97

**Five fields.** (1) The people of Moses, L, in the interval before Moses's return. (2) `la-in lam yarḥamnā rabbunā wa-yaghfir lanā la-nakūnanna mina al-khāsirīn`. (3) Stated cause: they took the calf although "did they not see that it could neither speak to them nor guide?" (7:148); in 20:96 the Sāmirī says `kadhālika sawwalat lī nafsī`. (4) Ornaments → image → worship; Aaron's warning refused: `lan nabraḥa ʿalayhi ʿākifīn` (20:91). (5) Named: `nafs` that embellished (20:96, the Sāmirī); `wa-kānū ẓālimīn` (7:148). **[inferred]:** craving for something tangible.

**Panel — 7:149:** وَلَمَّا سُقِطَ فِيٓ أَيۡدِيهِمۡ وَرَأَوۡاْ أَنَّهُمۡ قَدۡ ضَلُّواْ قَالُواْ لَئِن لَّمۡ يَرۡحَمۡنَا رَبُّنَا وَيَغۡفِرۡ لَنَا لَنَكُونَنَّ مِنَ ٱلۡخَٰسِرِينَ

**Observations.** The regret is two-step: the hand idiom, then `raʾaw annahum qad ḍallū` (seeing), then a **conditional supplication** which is itself a petition (mercy, forgiveness). No `nādim`. 7:152 and 7:153 hold both outcomes in one pair: this-life anger and humiliation for the calf-takers, and forgiveness for those who *did evil, then repented and believed* (`tābū… wa-āmanū`). **Conversion did not cancel the this-life consequence stated at 7:152; the text does not say which group the 7:149 speakers belong to.** Tension: 7:149 (regret) vs 20:91 (refusal until Moses returns); the surahs sequence it differently. Third party: Aaron before; Moses prays `ighfir lī wa-li-akhī` (7:151).

## 5. Thamūd 26:155-158 (with 23:37-41)

**Five fields.** (1) Thamūd, L→D. (2) **No speech at all.** Only `fa-aṣbaḥū nādimīn` (26:157), narrated. (3) No cause stated for regret. (4) Warned 26:156 `lā tamassūhā bi-sūʾ fa-yakhudhakum ʿadhāb`; `fa-ʿaqarūhā`; regret; seized. (5) Named: none. **[inferred]:** hardness.

**Why did regret not save them? (answer limited to what the text shows).** (i) The regret is **narrated, never voiced**: no `laytanā`, no confession, no supplication, no mention of God. (ii) It follows the irreversible act and precedes the seizure by a `fa-` with nothing between (26:157 → 26:158). (iii) 23:40-41 repeats it as prediction: `nādimīn` → `akhadhathumu al-ṣayḥa`. The text does not say regret *could not* have saved them; it shows regret without a turn, and the seizure unopposed. Whether a turn would have changed it is **unstated**.

## 6. Joseph's brothers 12:77-98

(1) Sons of Jacob, L. (2) 12:80 the eldest: `mā farraṭtum fī Yūsuf`; 12:91 `in kunnā la-khāṭiʾīn`; 12:97 `istaghfir lanā dhunūbanā`. (3) Cause stated: `farraṭtum fī Yūsuf` (the eldest names it; cause beneath it: none). (4) Past act → second test → despair `istayʾasū minhu` (12:80) → recall. (5) Named: nothing in this pack for the original motive; **[inferred]** envy, recalled from outside the pack. The eldest's speech is the third-party intervention *inside* the group, the structure of the garden-people's middle one.
Joseph's reply comes the same scene: `lā tathrība ʿalaykumu al-yawm yaghfiru Allāh` (12:92). Their father: `sawfa astaghfiru lakum rabbī` (12:98), a deferred promise, not an immediate grant. No `nādim`: the text never names their feeling.

## 7. The three left behind 9:117-118 (and 9:102)

**Panel — 9:118:** وَعَلَى ٱلثَّلَٰثَةِ ٱلَّذِينَ خُلِّفُواْ حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَا ضَاقَتۡ عَلَيۡهِمُ ٱلۡأَرۡضُ بِمَا رَحُبَتۡ وَضَاقَتۡ عَلَيۡهِمۡ أَنفُسُهُمۡ وَظَنُّوٓاْ أَن لَّا مَلۡجَأَ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ إِلَّآ إِلَيۡهِ ثُمَّ تَابَ عَلَيۡهِمۡ لِيَتُوبُوٓاْ

(1) Three men, L. (2) Not a spoken wish; a **state**: `ḍāqat ʿalayhimu al-arḍ`, `ḍāqat anfusuhum`. (3) Cause: `khullifū` only. (4) They were left behind; the text gives no further act here. (5) **Named:** `ẓannū an lā malja'a mina Allāhi illā ilayhi`. **[inferred]:** shame, isolation. No `nādim`/`ḥasra`; the vocabulary is constriction.

**Data-grounded findings.** (a) Sequence: constriction → realisation → `thumma tāba ʿalayhim li-yatūbū`. God's turning comes **first**, and is the stated *purpose* of their own: `li-yatūbū`. (b) The refuge is **the one they were away from**: `min Allāh illā ilayhi`. That is the textual marker of a regret that works: the flight points to God, not away. (c) 9:102: `iʿtarafū bi-dhunūbihim`, mixed deeds, outcome hope only (`ʿasā`). Like 68:32, `ʿasā`: hope, not a stated grant. No third party appears for the three.

## 8. The anticipated ones: 49:6, 5:51-53

**49:6.** `fa-tabayyanū an tuṣībū qawman bi-jahālatin fa-tuṣbiḥū ʿalā mā faʿaltum nādimīn`. Cause stated: **`jahāla`** (acting without knowing). The regret is future and conditional, and the text instructs a pre-emptive act (`tabayyanū`).
**5:51-53.** Named: `fī qulūbihim maraḍ`, fear `nakhshā an tuṣībanā dāʾira` (5:52), hurrying `yusāriʿūna fīhim`. The future regret is `ʿalā mā asarrū fī anfusihim`: over **what they hid inside themselves**. The same verb `asarra` in the hereafter regret formula (10:54, 34:33). **[inferred]:** choosing safety over a stance.
**Count:** of the five `nādim` ayahs, only 5:31 and 26:157 are narrated past; 5:52, 23:40 and 49:6 are forward-looking.

## 9. Wrong object and praised sorrow

**4:72-73.** The hanger-back (`la-man layubaṭṭiʾanna`) says one thing when disaster strikes (`qad anʿama Allāhu ʿalayya idh lam akun maʿahum`, 72) and the opposite when bounty comes (`yā laytanī kuntu maʿahum fa-afūza fawzan ʿaẓīmā`, 73). The text itself says his tie to them was `ka-an lam takun baynakum… mawadda`. Object of regret: **a missed gain**; same man, two readings of one absence.
**28:79-82.** The desirers of `al-ḥayāt al-dunyā` say `yā layta lanā mithla mā ūtiya Qārūn`. The knowers reply `waylakum thawāb Allāh khayr` (28:80). After the earth swallows him: `wa-aṣbaḥa alladhīna tamannaw makānahu bi-l-ams`, and the relief `law-lā an manna Allāhu ʿalaynā la-khasafa bina` (28:82). A regret **avoided** by watching the other man fall (another `aṣbaḥa`).
**9:92.** `tawallaw wa-aʿyunuhum tafīḍu mina al-damʿi ḥazanan allā yajidū mā yunfiqūn`. **Contrast with 18:42:** the garden-owner turns his hands over what he **spent** (`anfaqa`); these men weep because they cannot **spend** (`yunfiqūn`). Same root `nfq`, opposite objects. The text counts the second sorrow as blameless.

## 10. The key question: what separates regret that led somewhere?

| Marker | Converted | Did not / not shown |
|---|---|---|
| Direct address to God, with petition | calf 7:149; garden-people 68:29, 32; the three 9:118 (`illā ilayhi`) | Cain, Thamūd: none. Garden-owner: mention without petition |
| Words of confession (`ẓālimīn`, `khāṭiʾīn`, `iʿtarafū`) | 68:29, 12:91, 9:102 | Cain, Thamūd: none |
| Third party spoke before the loss | 68:28, 12:80, 20:90 | Cain (5:28), Thamūd (26:156), garden-owner (18:37-41), all unheeded; so a warning alone is **not** the marker |
| Timing relative to consequence | brothers, three, calf: before final sanction | Thamūd: regret then immediate seizure |
| Word used | none of them uses `nādim` | all narrated `nādim`: Cain, Thamūd |
| Outcome reported | 12:92, 9:118, 7:153 | 68:32 and 9:102 are hopes (`ʿasā`) |

**Strength of claim.** Strong: the two narrated `nādim` cases have no address to God; no converting case uses `nādim`; three of five `nādim` ayahs are warnings. Moderate: the converting cases all turn toward God. Weak: that regret *without* a turn "cannot" work; Thamūd's text shows the absence, not an impossibility. **[interpretive]:** `nādim` marks the felt state; what the converting cases add is a *direction*.
Morning (`aṣbaḥa`) recurs in Cain, 18:42, 68:17-22, 26:157, 5:52, 23:40, 49:6, 28:82; a sibling file tests it.

## For introspection

1. When something I built or owned fails, what is my first spoken sentence, and is it addressed to anyone? (The garden-people said *subḥāna rabbinā* first; Cain and Thamūd said nothing to God.)
2. Is there a phrase someone has asked me to say at the moment of success (`mā shāʾa Allāh`, `tusabbiḥūn`) that I skip?
3. What did someone tell me before, and did I dismiss them? (The warner is present in nearly every case here.)
4. Do I write exceptions into my promises (`istithnāʾ`), or am I swearing as if the morning were mine?
5. Where am I about to act on a report I haven't checked (49:6), or hedging with a side I fear (5:52)?
6. Is my sorrow about what I **spent** or about what I could not spend (18:42 vs 9:92)? And am I envying a fortune (28:79) whose cost I haven't seen?
