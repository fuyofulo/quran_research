# Narrative: blame and companions — regret over whom one followed

## Headline finding

Across the 14 hereafter disputes, **no blamed party ever claims to have compelled anyone.** Of the 9 whose answer is recorded, 5 explicitly deny coercion and hand responsibility back (7:39, 14:22, 34:32, 37:29-30, 50:27), 1 disowns the tie (2:166), and 3 do not argue the point at all (14:21, 38:59-60, 40:48). Two phrases show how the text weighs the excuse: `baʿda idh jāʾakum` (34:32) and `baʿda idh jāʾanī` (25:29), *after it had come*. God's verdicts (7:38, 34:33, 37:33, 40:48, 43:39) leave blame relieving no one. Only 43:36 and 25:29 tie the companion to `*kr` (remembrance), and they point in opposite directions: companion as consequence, companion as cause. 37:50-57 gives one cause for the saved man: `niʿmatu rabbī`.

Three roles recur: **leader/elite** (`istakbarū`, `sādat`, `kubarāʾ`), **follower** (`ḍuʿafāʾ`, `tabaʿ`), **intimate** (`khalīl`, `qarīn`). In 2:166-167 the chain goes up to the andād. In 14:22 it goes to Satan.

A detail on the passive: `istuḍʿifū` (34:31-33) is form X passive, *deemed/made weak*. The text names the follower's status as something done to him, yet in 34:32 the answer is `kuntum mujrimīn`. **[interpretive]** The weak are not exonerated by being weak.

## 2. What the blamed parties answer

| Ref | Answer | Denies coercion? |
|---|---|---|
| 2:166 | `tabarraʾa` — they disown the followers | disowns (does not argue) |
| 7:39 | `mā kāna lakum ʿalaynā min faḍl … bi-mā kuntum taksibūn` | yes, returns it to *their* earning |
| 14:21 | `law hadānā Allāh la-hadaynākum … mā lanā min maḥīṣ` | no — blames absence of guidance; no claim of force |
| 14:22 | `mā kāna lī ʿalaykum min sulṭān illā an daʿawtukum … fa-lā talūmūnī` | yes, fullest form |
| 34:32 | `a-naḥnu ṣadadnākum ʿan al-hudā baʿda idh jāʾakum? bal kuntum mujrimīn` | yes |
| 37:29-30 | `lam takūnū muʾminīn; wa-mā kāna lanā ʿalaykum min sulṭān` | yes (`sulṭān`, as 14:22) |
| 37:32 | `fa-aghwaynākum innā kunnā ghāwīn` | **admits** leading astray, after denying authority |
| 38:59 | `lā marḥaban bihim` — hostile, no argument | no |
| 40:48 | `innā kullun fīhā; inna Allāha qad ḥakama` | no — refuses relief, concedes shared fate |
| 50:27 | `rabbanā mā aṭghaytuhu wa-lākin kāna fī ḍalālin baʿīd` | yes |

**Count: 5 clear denials of coercion (7:39, 14:22, 34:32, 37:30, 50:27) of 9 recorded answers; 1 disowning; 3 non-denials (14:21, 38:59, 40:48).** Phase 0's fixed shape holds for a majority, not all. The verifiable stronger statement: **none of the 9 claims compulsion.** Exceptions that complicate it: 37:32 (`aghwaynākum`, an admission beside the denial) and 14:22 (`akhlaftukum`: Satan admits he broke his promise and still insists he had only invited).

Silent when blamed: 33:67 (`sādat`), 41:29 (the two), 25:28 (`fulān`), 43:38 (the `qarīn`), 37:56 (the friend). The text records the regretter's accusation without the accused's reply.

The recurring formula in the denials is `sulṭān` (14:22, 37:30): *authority, power to compel*. **[interpretive]** The Quran presents influence as invitation (`daʿā`), not force.

## 3. God's verdict: does blame relieve anyone?

- 7:38: `li-kullin ḍiʿf` — double for each, `wa-lākin lā taʿlamūn`. The followers ask double for the leaders; God gives double to all.
- 34:33: both parties `asarrū al-nadāmata` (the same clause as 10:54). The followers' counter-charge (`makr al-layl wa-l-nahār`) is not adjudicated; the verse ends `hal yujzawna illā mā kānū yaʿmalūn`.
- 37:33 `mushtarikūn`; 40:48 `kullun fīhā`; 43:39 `lan yanfaʿakumu al-yawma … annakum fī l-ʿadhābi mushtarikūn`: shared fate is stated as no benefit.
- 2:166-167: disowning does not rescue the followed, and the followers' wish to disown them back (`fa-natabarraʾa minhum`) is answered with `yurīhim Allāh aʿmālahum ḥasarāt`.
- 50:28 `lā takhtaṣimū ladayya`; 33:68 asks double, with no stated reply.

**Answer (data-grounded):** no verdict reduces anyone's share because of someone else's role. **Interpretive:** the text treats the excuse "I only followed" as non-exculpatory, but it never says the leaders are unburdened (7:38 gives them double).

Why the excuse fails is stated twice. 34:32 `baʿda idh jāʾakum` and 25:29 `baʿda idh jāʾanī` are the only two such phrases in my set. 40:50 adds `awalam taku taʾtīkum rusulukum bi-l-bayyināt? qālū balā`. The followers concede it themselves.

## 4. The remembrance thread

Panels:

**43:36** `وَمَن يَعۡشُ عَن ذِكۡرِ ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ نُقَيِّضۡ لَهُۥ شَيۡطَٰنࣰا فَهُوَ لَهُۥ قَرِينࣱ` — *whoever turns blind from the remembrance of the Most Merciful, We assign him a devil, and he is his companion.*

**25:29** `لَّقَدۡ أَضَلَّنِي عَنِ ٱلذِّكۡرِ بَعۡدَ إِذۡ جَآءَنِيۗ وَكَانَ ٱلشَّيۡطَٰنُ لِلۡإِنسَٰنِ خَذُولࣰا` — *He led me away from the remembrance after it had come to me; Satan is ever a deserter of man.*

**Morphology check (script over `words.jsonl`):** `*kr` (`*ikor`) occurs in 43:36 and 25:29 and in no other verse of my 14 passages. `ʿashā` is root `E$w` (`yaʿshu`). `qarīn` is lemma `qariyn` in 37:51, 43:36, 43:38, 50:23, 50:27 (also 4:38, 41:25 elsewhere). The verb `qyD` (`qayyaḍ`) occurs only in 41:25 and 43:36 in the whole corpus. In 41:25 it also takes a `qarīn` object. *[Orchestrator addendum, verified]:* 41:25 reads `wa-qayyaḍnā lahum quranāʾa fa-zayyanū lahum mā bayna aydīhim wa-mā khalfahum` — *We assigned them companions who made attractive to them what was before them and behind them*. The second `qyD` verse names the companion's mechanism: **`tazyīn`, making things look good** — not coercion, which matches the blamed parties' denials of `sulṭān`.

**What the text supports:**
1. 43:36 is a conditional: turning from remembrance (**named**: `ʿashā`) comes first, then God assigns (`nuqayyiḍ`, divine agent) the devil-companion. 43:37 adds `yaḥsabūn annahum muhtadūn` (named: `ḥasiba`, a mistaken assumption). The companion is a **consequence**, and God is its grammatical subject.
2. 25:29 reverses the direction: the man **chose** the friend (`ittakhadhtu`), and the friend led him from `al-dhikr`. The companion is **cause** here, and the verse names the object lost.
3. Together: a loop. Both can hold, one verse each. 25:29 goes first in the surah order, but the text makes no claim about priority.

**Strength: moderate on 43:36 (explicit syntax), moderate on 25:29, weak as a general law.** 50:23-29 and 37:50-57 have no `*kr` word in them. In 50:25 the vocabulary is `manna'ʿ li-l-khayr … muʿtadin murīb`, not remembrance. The Saheeh renders the `qarīn` of 50:23 as angel and of 50:27 as devil; the Arabic uses the same lemma, so who the companion is differs by translator choice. Do not claim that every `qarīn` follows turning from remembrance.

## 5. The near-miss, 37:50-57

**37:56-57** `قَالَ تَٱللَّهِ إِن كِدتَّ لَتُرۡدِينِ ۝ وَلَوۡلَا نِعۡمَةُ رَبِّي لَكُنتُ مِنَ ٱلۡمُحۡضَرِينَ` — *"By God, you nearly ruined me. Were it not for my Lord's favor, I would have been among those brought forward."*

- Text-stated cause of his escape: `niʿmatu rabbī`. He names no deed of his own. The verse before is `qarīn` quoting: `a-innaka la-mina l-muṣaddiqīn?` (37:52-53): the companion mocked belief in resurrection.
- The scene opens with the same sentence as 37:27 (`wa-aqbala baʿḍuhum ʿalā baʿḍin yatasāʾalūn`, `fa-` in 37:50). **Data-grounded.** In 37:27-33 they blame; in 37:50-57 one recalls. Same posture, different side.
- He does not regret. There is no `layta`. He sees the companion `fī sawāʾ al-jaḥīm` (37:55) and speaks only of near-ruin (`kidta … turdīn`).
- **Interpretive:** the one saved credits grace, not insight. The companion's pressure was real (`kidta`) and he does not minimise it. The text does not say what he did when pressed.

## 7. Negative space

- No follower says "I was forced." No leader says "I forced them."
- No dispute ends in agreement or reconciliation. The only non-quarrel (37:50-57) belongs to the saved.
- The `qarīn` in 43:38 and the `khalīl` in 25:28 are never quoted in reply.
- The saved man's own deed is not given.

## For introspection

1. Name the three people whose judgment you rely on most. For each, ask what 34:32 asks: *has guidance already reached me, apart from them?* If yes, "I followed" will not be an excuse.
2. 25:28 names a specific `fulān`. Is there someone whose company has pushed you from what you already know (`baʿda idh jāʾanī`)? Name them concretely.
3. 43:36-37: is there an area where you feel guided (`yaḥsabūn annahum muhtadūn`) but have stopped remembering? Test the feeling against practice.
4. Do you ever excuse yourself as the weak one (`istuḍʿifū`) while others make the decisions? 34:32 and 40:50 answer that the message had come.
5. 37:56-57: when did a friend nearly ruin you, and what did not break? Can you name it as favor, or only as your own strength?
6. Are you, to anyone, the one who invites (`daʿawtukum`)? 14:22 says the inviter denies authority and the invited are still answerable.
