> Deterministic layer. Counted, not interpreted, unless flagged **[interpretive]**.
> Generated by `scripts/build_regret_census.py` → `data/structural/regret-census.json`, and
> `scripts/build_regret_versepack.py` → `data/structural/regret-versepack.json` (332 verses, 15 groups).
> The `core` table in the census (setting / person / kind per ayah) is an **orchestrator reading**,
> marked as such in the JSON. Everything else is counted.

## Headline finding

The Quran has **two words for regret, and they never share a world.** `nādim` (regretful, 5×) occurs
only in this life, and **all five occurrences take the verb `aṣbaḥa`** — *he became* regretful, a state
passed into after the act (never `kāna`, *was*). *(CORRECTED after Phase 1: an earlier version read
`aṣbaḥa` as "woke up to find himself". The root agent tested all 28 `aṣbaḥa` ayahs: only 2 are literal
morning, and none of the 5 regret uses. See `1-roots/concept-ndm-hsr-the-two-words.md`.)* `nadāma` (regret, 2×) occurs only in the hereafter, and **both occurrences are the identical
clause** `wa-asarrū al-nadāmata lammā raʾawu al-ʿadhāb` — *they concealed regret when they saw the
punishment* (10:54, 34:33). This-world regret is a state one passes into after the act. Hereafter
regret is hidden, and is triggered by sight. Of 56 ayahs where people regret their own conduct, **34
are in the hereafter, 10 at death or as punishment arrives, 12 in this life.**

## 2. The core set — where regret happens

65 core ayahs. **56 are regret over one's own conduct**; 3 are regret aimed at the wrong object
(4:73, 28:79, 3:156); 2 are lament over others (35:8, 36:30); 2 are non-culpable wishes (19:23
Maryam, 36:26 the martyr); 2 controls (11:72; 17:29, excluded after Phase 1 — `maḥsūr` = depleted).

| Setting (of the 56) | Ayahs | Speaker singular / plural / narrated |
|---|---|---|
| **H** — hereafter | **34** | 9 / 16 / 9 |
| **D** — at death, or as punishment arrives | 10 | 3 / 5 / 2 |
| **L** — this life | 12 | 2 / 4 / 6 |

Meccan 46 / Medinan 10. **[interpretive]** Regret is overwhelmingly an eschatological genre, and its
dominant voice there is the plural — *we*. The singular `yā laytanī` voices (25:27-28, 39:56,
43:38, 69:25-27, 78:40, 89:24) are a minority inside it.

## 3. The two words — a clean split

| | `nādim` (5) | `nadāma` (2) |
|---|---|---|
| World | this life, every time | hereafter, every time |
| Verb | `aṣbaḥa` 5/5 — *became* (never `kāna`) | `asarrū` 2/2 — *concealed* |
| Trigger | the consequence of one's own act | `lammā raʾawu al-ʿadhāb` 2/2 — *seeing* |

`aṣbaḥa` occurs in 28 ayahs in the whole Quran; 6 of them are regret ayahs (the five `nādim` plus
18:42, the garden-owner turning his palms). In the root-lift table `SbH` is over-represented in
self-regret ayahs by ×15 — **but that lift double-counts**: 5 of its 6 hits are the `nādim` ayahs
themselves. Only 2 of the 28 `aṣbaḥa` ayahs are literal morning (30:17, 68:20).

**CORRECTED [Phase-1 verdict].** The original reading here — "this-life regret is what you wake up
to" — does not survive. `aṣbaḥa` is *became*. What does survive: regret is a state one **passes into
after the act** (4 of 5 after `fa-`, *and so*), never a standing condition (`kāna`); and 3 of the 5
are in the imperfect — **God announcing the regret before the act** (5:52, 23:40, 49:6).

## 4. Regret is triggered by seeing

`rAy` (see) occurs in 12 of the 56 self-regret ayahs — lift ×4.5. "Seeing the punishment"
(`raʾawu / yarawna / tarā al-ʿadhāb`) closes 8 ayahs: 2:165, 2:166, 10:54, 25:42, 28:64, 34:33,
39:58, 42:44. `lammā raʾaw` (when they saw) recurs at 10:54, 34:33, 40:84, 40:85, 42:44; and
7:149 is `raʾaw annahum qad ḍallū` — *they saw that they had strayed*. 2:167 makes God the one who
shows: `yurīhimu Allāhu aʿmālahum ḥasarātin ʿalayhim` — *God will show them their deeds as regrets*.

**[interpretive]** The Quran does not depict regret as arising from reflection. It depicts regret as
arising from **seeing what was already true**. 50:22 states it directly: *you were in heedlessness
of this, so We removed your covering, and your sight today is sharp.* The question for
introspection is what one could see now, before the covering is removed.

## 5. The wish has four shapes [interpretive classification of the 14 `layta` + 4 `wadda law`]

| Shape | What is wished | Refs |
|---|---|---|
| **Undo** | to have acted otherwise | 18:42, 25:27, 25:28, 33:66, 89:24, 6:27, 15:2 |
| **Return** | to be sent back and act again | 26:102, 39:58, 2:167, 23:99, 32:12, 35:37, 63:10 |
| **Separate** | distance from one's deeds or companion | 3:30, 43:38, 70:11 (ransom), 2:167 (disown) |
| **Unexist** | not to be, not to have been given the record | 78:40, 69:27, 4:42, 69:25 |

Note that **undo** wishes name a specific thing. They are the reverse-engineering material.

## 6. The hands

All three bodily gestures of regret are gestures of the **hands**: biting them (25:27), turning the
palms over (18:42), *it fell into their hands* (7:149, an idiom for realisation). The formula *what
your hands sent forward* (`qaddamat aydīkum`) occurs 12×; two regret utterances embed it — 78:40
(*the day a man sees what his hands sent forward and the denier says: if only I were dust*) and
89:24 (*if only I had sent ahead for my life*). 59:18 is the prescriptive form: *let every soul look
to what it has sent forward for tomorrow.* **[interpretive]** The hand is the Quran's instrument of
deeds, and regret is staged on it.

## 7. The same confession, two outcomes

`innā kunnā ẓālimīn` — *we were wrongdoers* — is said by humans four times. At 7:5, 21:14 and 21:46
it is said as punishment lands, and 7:5/21:15 say it was **all they could say** until they were
mown down. At 68:29 the garden-people say the identical words after losing a crop, in this life —
and three verses later they are hoping for a better garden (68:32). **Same words. The outcome is set
by when they are said.** The window is the investigation's second axis.

## 8. The prophylactic refrain

`min qabli an yaʾtiya` — *before there comes* — 8×: 2:254, 14:31, 30:43, 39:54, 39:55, 42:47, 63:10,
71:1. Each names what comes: a day with no trade or friendship (2:254, 14:31), a day with no
turning back (30:43, 42:47), the punishment (39:54, 39:55, 71:1), death (63:10). 39:53-58 places this refrain
immediately before the three hereafter excuses (`yā ḥasratā` / *if God had guided me* / *if I had a
return*). The text pre-empts regret by name.

## 9. Regret the Quran forbids, and sorrow it does not blame

- **Forbidden**: `li-kay-lā taʾsaw ʿalā mā fātakum` — *so you do not grieve over what escaped you*
  (57:23); `li-kay-lā taḥzanū ʿalā mā fātakum` (3:153); `fa-lā taʾsa` over a defiant people (5:26,
  5:68); *do not let your soul go out in regrets over them* (35:8, also 18:6, 26:3).
- **Converted into punishment**: the hypocrites' *if only they had stayed with us they would not have
  died* — God makes **that** a `ḥasra` in their hearts (3:156). The "if only" of fate is itself the
  regret.
- **Not blamed** (CORRECTED — an earlier version said "praised"): 9:92 — men who turned away *with
  their eyes overflowing with tears, from sorrow that they could not find the means to spend*. The
  text's verdict is `wa-lā ʿalā` — *no blame upon* them — and 9:91 counts such people among the
  `muḥsinīn`. Exempted and approved, not praised for the tears.

**[interpretive]** Three registers: the regret that converts (repentance), the regret that cannot
(too late), and the regret that should not be felt at all (over what was decreed or what others
chose). An introspection tool has to separate all three.

## For introspection

- This-life regret is a state you pass into *after* the act, and three of its five uses are God
  announcing it *in advance*. The question it hands you: *what state will this act leave me in?*
- Hereafter regret is triggered by seeing what was already true. The useful practice is to look now.
- Most `if only` wishes in the Quran name a specific thing — a friend, an obedience, a neglect, a
  charity left undone. A vague unease is not the Quranic shape. A named item is.
- Some regret the Quran forbids. Grief over what escaped you, or over what others chose, is not the
  regret it asks you to feel.
