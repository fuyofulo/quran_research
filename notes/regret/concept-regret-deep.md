# Regret in the Quran: synthesis

> Phase 4. Integrates all nine investigation files; where a later file corrected an earlier one, the later reading is used (§7). **[D]** = data-grounded (counted, scripted, or quoted). **[I]** = interpretive; **[i]** = an inferred link. Hadith and tafsīr are outside the evidence base.

## Headline finding

The Quran shows people regretting their own conduct in **56 ayahs (49 scenes): 34 in the hereafter, 10 at death or seizure, 12 in this life.** Their objects fall into ten families, led by *the warning denied* (12) and *the whole conduct, unnamed* (10). None is a bodily appetite, poverty, illness or work [D]. The deeds regretted are debts owed to a party [I]. Two junctions carry the causal map: **lost remembrance** and **denial**. The regretful name only absence or defeat in themselves (*heedless, overcome*), never wanting. The Quran never commands regret. It commands return, and it dates the window: *before it comes*.

## 1. What counts, and how it behaves [D]

**Scope.** An exhaustive marker inventory gives 65 core ayahs: 56 self-regret, 3 misdirected, 2 lament over others (35:8, 36:30), 2 non-culpable wishes (19:23, 36:26), 2 controls (11:72; 17:29 `maḥsūr`, *depleted*). Meccan 46, Medinan 10. In the hereafter the dominant voice is *we*.

**Two words, two worlds.** `nādim` (5) occurs only in this life. It always takes `aṣbaḥa`, *became*, never `kāna`. Four of the five follow `fa-` (*and so*). Three of the five are imperfect: God forecasting the regret before the act (5:52, 23:40, 49:6). `nadāma` (2) occurs only in the hereafter, both times in the same clause: *they concealed regret when they saw the punishment* (10:54, 34:33). `ḥasra` (9 nouns) spans both worlds and is feeling, object and outcome at once: God makes it (3:156), shows deeds *as* `ḥasarāt` (2:167); spent wealth becomes it (8:36); a Day is named for it (19:39). 7 of 9 take `ʿalā`, *upon*. **[I]** It falls on a person more than it rises in one.

**Seeing.** `rAy` occurs in 12 of the 56 (lift ×4.4), and *seeing the punishment* closes 8. 50:22: *We removed your covering, and your sight today is sharp.* **[I]** The Quran does not show regret arising from reflection. It shows regret arriving when what was already true becomes visible.

**Speech.** Soliloquy (`yā laytanī`) names a specific past item: a friend, an obedience, shirk, what was sent ahead. Petition (`rabbanā` + imperative) promises generic goodness: of 13 return-requests only 2 name an act (63:10 charity, 2:167 disowning). God's replies re-narrate the past: time given (35:37), the speakers' own words or conduct (14:44, 23:110, 32:14), or flat closure (23:100, 63:11). Only 6:28 answers the hypothetical: *even if they were returned, they would return to what they were forbidden.*

**Two patterns [D split; I reading].** No this-life regret falls in C, A, D, I or J; all 12 are in E, B, F, G, plus 9:102. **[I]** In this life regret attaches to a concrete thing lost or done: a garden, a calf, a brother, a report. The families with no this-life case concern what one faced. And H, I and J (18 ayahs) name no object; 17 of them are at death or after. **[I]** The vaguest regrets are the latest.

**Causes by scene (49; catalog coding) [D].** Denial of warner, signs or Day 20 · lost remembrance (`dhikr`, `nisyān`, `ghafla`, `farraṭa`) 13 · world-orientation 9 · **no cause stated 9** (5:31, 26:157, 7:5, 9:102, 23:99, 42:44, 3:30, 18:49, 10:54) · shirk 7 · following 6 · the poor or orphan 4 · arrogance 3 · mockery 2 · kin-preference, unchecked report, fear 1 each. Where God *itemises* causes (19 passages, chosen for that), world-orientation leads (11), then denial 8, poor and orphan 4, prayer 1 (74:43). Both counts hold for their sets. **[I]** God names the swap, the immediate loved and the next life left (75:20-21, 87:16-17); the regret scenes attach to what it produced, denial and forgetting. Twice the poor-clause faults *not urging* others (69:34, 89:18).

## 3. The reverse-engineering map

The layers come from the catalog. Every inner state at L1 is a word in the text; all but the fear of 5:52 are spoken by God, the narrator or believers.

```
L0 conditions given     wealth, children 63:9 · luxury 23:33, 21:13 · long life 21:44, 35:37 · companions 41:25
L1 named inner states   wanting:    ḥubb al-ʿājila 75:20 · ḥubb al-māl 89:20 · ḥubb of rivals 2:165 · āthara 79:38 · hawā 18:28
                        deception:  ghurūr 6:130, 7:51, 45:35 · amānī 57:14 · amal 15:3 · tazyīn 41:25
                        assumption: ẓann 18:35-36 · ḥisbān 18:104, 43:37 · "no end" 14:44 · no reckoning expected 78:27
                        elevation:  istikbār 39:59 · ṭughyān 79:37 · qādirīn 68:25      fear: nakhshā, maraḍ 5:52
L2 junctions            LOST REMEMBRANCE (13/49) ──── DENIAL (20/49)
L3 behaviours           follow · associate · mock · withhold from poor and orphan · hoard / spend to obstruct · hedge · act unchecked · harm kin
L4 regretted object     A–J
```

**Edges** (S = stated, with ref; [i] = inferred):
- wealth and children → diverted from `dhikr` → the charity wish (S 63:9-10); competing for more diverts until the graves (S 102:1-2)
- a chosen friend → *led me away from the remembrance* (S 25:29); turning blind to remembrance → a devil assigned as companion → *they think they are guided* → the distance wish (S 43:36-38). Together, a loop [i]
- companions → *made attractive* (S 41:25) → following [i]
- mocking those who prayed → *until they made you forget My remembrance* (S 23:110)
- heedless heart → follows desire → affair *furuṭ* (S 18:28) → *ʿalā mā farraṭ-* in the cries (6:31, 39:56) [i: same root, no textual link]
- forgot the meeting → forgotten (S 7:51, 32:14); forgot God → made to forget themselves (S 59:19); earning → stain on hearts (S 83:14)
- comparing wealth + *I do not think this will perish* → shirk named in the regret (S 18:34-42); luxury → denial (juxtaposed S 23:33; causation [i])
- love of rivals → following → the disowning wish (S 2:165-167); love of wealth + orphan and poor neglected → *if only I had sent ahead* (S 89:17-24)
- fear + diseased heart → alliance → `nādim` (S 5:52); ignorance + unchecked report → harm → `nādim` (S 49:6)
- father's preference resented → plan → *farraṭtum fī Yūsuf* (S 12:8-9, 12:80); offering rejected → *his soul permitted it* → killing → `nādim` (S 5:27-31)
- thinking one does well → effort lost, unfelt (S 18:104) → so no prompt to regret [i]
- self-temptation, waiting, doubt, wishful thinking *until God's command came* (S 57:14; a `wa-` sequence, not causation)

> **18:28** وَلَا تُطِعۡ مَنۡ أَغۡفَلۡنَا قَلۡبَهُۥ عَن ذِكۡرِنَا وَٱتَّبَعَ هَوَىٰهُ وَكَانَ أَمۡرُهُۥ فُرُطࣰا
> *Do not obey one whose heart We have made heedless of Our remembrance and who follows his desire and whose affair is ever [in] neglect.* (Saheeh) State → act → outcome in one sentence; the first verb is God's.

**[I] The map has a waist.** Wealth, friends, looking away, mockery and a heedless heart reach the object through lost remembrance. Assumption and luxury reach it through denial. On the text's model, regret comes less from one bad desire than from a narrowing of attention that desires produce.

**Who names the inner state [D, corrected].** A morphology scan of 81 regret-speech ayahs finds the regretful naming states in themselves: `ghafla` (21:97), `shiqwa` and `ḍāllīn` (23:106), `ḍalāl mubīn` (26:97), `ḍāllūn` (68:26), `ṭāghīn` (68:31), `sākhirīn` (39:56), and fear as a motive (5:52). It finds **zero** self-applied `ḥubb`, `hawā`, `āthara`, `ghurūr`, `amānī`, `rayb`, `rān`, `ẓann` or `istikbār`. The wanting and assuming layer always comes from outside. And both self-diagnoses below are overruled. 21:97 corrects itself with `bal`, *rather*. 23:106 is answered *remain despised therein and do not speak to Me* (23:108) and *you took them in mockery until they made you forget My remembrance* (23:110).

> **21:97 · 23:106** يَٰوَيۡلَنَا قَدۡ كُنَّا فِي غَفۡلَةࣲ مِّنۡ هَٰذَا بَلۡ كُنَّا ظَٰلِمِينَ · رَبَّنَا غَلَبَتۡ عَلَيۡنَا شِقۡوَتُنَا وَكُنَّا قَوۡمࣰا ضَآلِّينَ
> *O woe to us; we had been unmindful of this; rather, we were wrongdoers. · Our Lord, our wretchedness overcame us, and we were a people astray.*

**[I]** Self-report will say *I didn't notice*. The text asks *what did you want, prefer, assume?* So an instrument should use the named states as a checklist rather than wait to feel them, and admit a peer's view: the escape from loss includes mutual counsel (103:3).

**Following [D].** In 14 hereafter disputes no blamed party claims to have compelled anyone; 5 of 9 recorded answers deny it outright (7:39, 14:22, 34:32, 37:30, 50:27). The mechanism named is invitation (*I invited you, and you responded to me*, 14:22) and making-attractive (41:25). No verdict reduces anyone's share (7:38, 43:39). The excuse fails *after it had come* (34:32, 25:29).

## 4. What is commanded: return, not regret

**[D]** `ndm` and regret-`ḥasra` have 16 nouns and no verb. No grief verb is ever commanded, no prophet is called `nādim`, and none of the 12 converting cases uses `nādim`, `ḥasra` or `layta`. What is commanded: `tūbū` 7×, `istaghfir-` for one's own sins 13×, `anībū` 1× (39:54). The praised agent-nouns are all from the return family: `tawwābīn` (2:222), `tāʾibūn` (9:112), `awwāb` (38:17, 30, 44), `munīb` (11:75). Of four approved tears, three are praised (5:83, 17:109, 19:58) and one is not blamed (9:92); none is over the weeper's own sin. The only imperative of blame is Satan's *blame yourselves*, after the matter is decided (14:22). **[I]** Regret is something that happens (`aṣbaḥa`). Return is something done.

### Register A: regret that converts

**[D]** Twelve cases: Adam 7:23, Moses 28:16, Yūnus 21:87, David 38:24, Noah 11:47, Bilqīs 27:44, the calf 7:149, Joseph's brothers 12:91/97, the three left behind 9:118, the garden-people 68:29-32, 3:135, 9:102. All 12 orient to God by name or as *rabb*. Five, all individuals or the first couple, speak to Him in the second person; the brothers go through their father, and the three have an unspoken realisation. Eight name their own wrong. Seven ask, and every request is for forgiveness or mercy, never for the loss reversed. God moves first: *He turned to them so they could repent* (9:118). What lasts has a second verb: in 17 of 32 ayahs where a human `tāba`, another act follows in the verse (`aṣlaḥa` 8, belief or good deeds 6, prayer and zakāt 2, following 1). 3:135 adds *not persisting*, 4:17 *soon after*. Moses names the ground of his resolve: *for the favor You bestowed upon me, I will never be an assistant to the criminals* (28:17).

> **21:87 · 10:90** فَنَادَىٰ فِي ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ أَن لَّآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّآ أَنتَ سُبۡحَٰنَكَ إِنِّي كُنتُ مِنَ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَآ أَدۡرَكَهُ ٱلۡغَرَقُ قَالَ ءَامَنتُ أَنَّهُۥ لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا ٱلَّذِيٓ ءَامَنَتۡ بِهِۦ بَنُوٓاْ إِسۡرَٰٓءِيلَ
> *He called out within the darknesses, "There is no deity except You; exalted are You. Indeed, I have been of the wrongdoers." · When drowning overtook him, he said, "I believe that there is no deity except that in whom the Children of Israel believe."*

**[D]** `mulīm` (*blameworthy*) is used of these two alone (37:142, 51:40). Both are cast out, in water, saying *no deity except*. Yūnus speaks *to* God and names his wrong. Pharaoh speaks *about* God through other people's faith. Both deciders look backward: *had he not been of those who exalt* (37:143); *now? and you had disobeyed before?* (10:91).

**Admit or defend [D].** Iblīs: *because You put me in error* (`aghwaytanī`, God the cause, 7:16, 15:39). Adam: *Adam disobeyed his Lord and erred* (20:121), Adam the subject. The garden-people say *we were transgressors* (68:31); the hereafter leaders say *you were* (37:30). Naming an influence is not defending: Moses says *this is from the work of Satan*, then *I have wronged myself* (28:15-16). **[I]** The grammatical subject of the wrong is the most portable test in the material.

### Register B: too late

**[D]** Failure is `lā yanfaʿu`, *it does not benefit* (6:158, 40:85, 30:57, 40:52, 32:29). It is affirmed once, for Yūnus's people (10:98), framed as a rarity: a this-life disgrace lifted *for a time*. The window closes at seizure, not at a clock time: death (4:18, 63:10), drowning (10:90), the punishment seen (40:84-85), the signs (6:158), the Day (30:57). The late turn is judged against *before* (`qablu`, 10:91, 6:158). Confession goes on past the window (67:11, 40:11), so admission is necessary inside it and insufficient outside it. *We were wrongdoers* is said too late at 7:5, 21:14, 21:46, and in time at 68:29, followed by hope (68:32).

Too late also has a this-life form **[D]**: crisis-contrition that lapses on relief, in all six candidate passages (10:12, 10:22-23, 29:65, 30:33, 31:32, 7:134-135; and 44:15). The vow `la-in… la-nakūnanna` occurs 6×. The 2 conditioned on forgiveness convert (7:23, 7:149). The 4 conditioned on rescue or a gift are all broken in the next verse (6:63-64, 10:22-23, 7:189-190, 9:75-76). **[I]** A regret that ends when the pressure lifts was a response to pressure.

### Register C: grief forbidden, grief permitted

**[D]** Grief is forbidden 16× (`Hzn`, `Asw`): over others' unbelief or speech 10, over danger or loss 6, **over one's own sin 0**. Five purpose clauses have God acting *so that* no one grieves (3:153, 20:40, 28:13, 33:51, 57:23). Freedom from grief is promised 17×. Grief is Satan's aim (58:10).

> **57:23** لِّكَيۡلَا تَأۡسَوۡاْ عَلَىٰ مَا فَاتَكُمۡ وَلَا تَفۡرَحُواْ بِمَآ ءَاتَىٰكُمۡۗ وَٱللَّهُ لَا يُحِبُّ كُلَّ مُخۡتَالࣲ فَخُورٍ
> *In order that you not despair over what has eluded you and not exult over what He has given you. And Allah does not like everyone self-deluded and boastful.*

Two reasons are given: the outcome was written before it came to be (57:22; 3:156 *it is Allah who gives life and causes death*), and others' choices are not one's to carry (35:8, 26:3, 18:6). The "if only" of fate is itself made a `ḥasra` by God (3:156). **[D→I]** The line runs through the grammatical subject. The forbidden "if only" has circumstances or other people as subject (3:156, 3:168); converting regret has the speaker's own act (`ẓalamtu nafsī`). Regret over acts can work. Grief over outcomes is what is forbidden. Jacob shows permitted grief (12:84-87): addressed only to God, held with hope, ending in an act (*go and find out*), over a person lost, not a decree contested. 9:92 puts *no blame* on those who wept over what they could not spend.

**Despair [D].** `yAs`/`qnT` occur in 16 ayahs: fault-coded in 10, unblamed in 6, including the messengers' despair of their people (12:110). Despair of people is human; despair of God is named as the disbelievers' and the astray's (12:87, 15:56). It is paired with exultation (57:23, 11:9-10, 30:36). **[I]** Despair is not too much regret. It is the boaster's self-measure by outcome, turned over.

> **39:53** قُلۡ يَٰعِبَادِيَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَسۡرَفُواْ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمۡ لَا تَقۡنَطُواْ مِن رَّحۡمَةِ ٱللَّهِۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَغۡفِرُ ٱلذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًا
> *Say, "O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of the mercy of Allah. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins."*

**[D]** The sequence runs: no despair (39:53) → turn back *before the punishment comes* (39:54) → follow the best revealed (39:55) → three regret speeches as what to avoid (39:56-58) → the reply (39:59). **[I]** Hope is placed first because despair would block the return.

## 5. The window

**[D]** *Before there comes* (`min qabli an yaʾtiya`) occurs 8×, each naming what comes: a day with no trade or friendship (2:254, 14:31), a day with no turning back (30:43, 42:47), the punishment (39:54, 39:55, 71:1), death (63:10). The commands attached are to spend, pray, set the face, turn back, respond.

> **63:9-10** لَا تُلۡهِكُمۡ أَمۡوَٰلُكُمۡ وَلَآ أَوۡلَٰدُكُمۡ عَن ذِكۡرِ ٱللَّهِ … وَأَنفِقُواْ مِن مَّا رَزَقۡنَٰكُم مِّن قَبۡلِ أَن يَأۡتِيَ أَحَدَكُمُ ٱلۡمَوۡتُ فَيَقُولَ رَبِّ لَوۡلَآ أَخَّرۡتَنِيٓ إِلَىٰٓ أَجَلࣲ قَرِيبࣲ فَأَصَّدَّقَ وَأَكُن مِّنَ ٱلصَّٰلِحِينَ
> *Let not your wealth and your children divert you from remembrance of Allah… And spend from what We have provided you before death approaches one of you and he says, "My Lord, if only You would delay me for a brief term so I would give charity and be among the righteous."*

**[D]** The regret wish is the verse's own command, read backwards, and its cause is named one verse earlier. 9:75 has the same promise made in life, *we will surely spend in charity, and we will surely be among the righteous*, conditioned on a gift and broken (9:76-77): living evidence for 6:28. 59:18 commands the look now: *let every soul look to what it has put forth for tomorrow*. **[I]** It is the look that 50:22 forces later. All three regret gestures are of the hands (25:27, 18:42, 7:149), and *what his hands have put forth* sits inside the regret speech of 78:40.

**The blind spot [D].** 18:104 gives the only named mechanism by which loss builds up unfelt: *their effort is lost… while they think that they are doing well* (`yaḥsabūn`). **[I]** If regret depends on seeing, the person sure he is doing well has no prompt to look.

## 6. Negative space [D, script-checked]

- No regret within three ayahs of the roots for fornication, wine, usury, poverty, trade, hunger or appetite. Cain's regret clause names the burial, not the killing. No regret is over a missed pleasure or gain, except where the text corrects it (K). None is over poverty, illness, work, or what others did to one.
- Family appears only as distraction (63:9), as ransom (70:11-14) and as victims (F).
- Appetite-sins appear in **repentance**, outside the core: Adam's eating (7:22-23), Moses's killing (28:15-16), and 25:68-70 *except for those who repent*.
- No tears of guilt are praised. Only 9:118 describes what a converting person felt (*their souls confined them*), and never how much.
- No `nādim` is followed by repentance in its verse (23:41, 26:158: seizure next). No hereafter dispute ends in reconciliation.
- Saheeh adds regret the Arabic lacks at 7:149 (*when regret overcame them* renders *it fell into their hands*) and 9:118 (a bracket).

**[I]** The Quran places regret in orientation and in debts owed. `farraṭa fī` always takes a party to whom something was due: the Hour (6:31), the side of God (39:56), Joseph (12:80). Appetite-sins sit with repentance.

## Open questions (carried, not chased)

1. **Blank or avoidance?** 50:19 *that is what you were trying to avoid* sits beside 50:22 *you were in unmindfulness*, with no causal particle. Does heedlessness begin as absence or as flight?
2. **The silent nine.** Nine scenes state no cause (Thamūd 26:157, Cain 5:31, 7:5 …). The text leaves them open.
3. **Moving a resentment.** The brothers turned *more beloved to our father* (12:8) into *Allah has preferred you over us* (12:91). Is moving a grievance from a person to God's decree the repair?
4. **Denial for a believer.** For one who does not deny, is the 20/49 better read as `ḥisbān` (18:104, 43:37)? What would count as evidence against one's own?
5. **One state under three?** `qādirīn` (68:25), *I do not think this will perish* (18:35) and *no cessation for you* (14:44) all estimate one's own capacity or permanence. The text names it only by its effects.
6. **Companion first or forgetting first?** 25:29 and 43:36 point opposite ways.
7. **Unsettled readings:** who laments at 36:30; whether `kāna` at 37:143 means a habit; which group the 7:149 speakers join (7:152-153).

## For introspection

1. **Name it.** Write one regret in the Quranic shape, *if only I had [not]…*, naming a person, an act or a duty. Which of A–J is it? If it is K, the text calls it the wrong object.
2. **Find the junction.** This week, where did remembrance drop out first: after wealth or children (63:9), a friend (25:29), mockery (23:110), or simply looking away (43:36)?
3. **Translate the alibi.** Take one *I didn't notice* and rewrite it as what you wanted, preferred or assumed, in the text's words: love, preference, desire, wishful thinking, assumption.
4. **Check the subject.** When you recall a wrong, is the subject *I*, or a circumstance or a person?
5. **The second verb.** After *I turned*, what did you set right, and on what ground (28:17)?
6. **Read the wish backwards.** Complete *My Lord, delay me so I could ___* (63:10). Is your version conditional, like 9:75?
