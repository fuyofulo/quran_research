# Regret that works: what the Quran commands, permits and forbids

## Headline finding

**The Quran never asks anyone to feel regret.** `ndm` and regret-`ḥasra` have no verb (16 nouns, 0 verbs), no prophet is called `nādim`, and 0 of 12 converting cases uses `nādim`, `ḥasra` or `layta`. What it commands is **return**: `tūbū` 7×, `istaghfir-` for one's own sins 13×, `anībū` 1×. It forbids grief 16× (never over one's own sin) and calls despair of God's mercy a fault. That prohibition (39:53) is the gate before the regret sequence 39:54-58. Regret that works is told apart by its grammar: who is the subject of the wrong, and what is asked for.

## 1. Is regret ever commanded or praised? (data-grounded; QAC morphology, scripted)

| Test | Count | Refs |
|---|---|---|
| Verbs of `ndm` | **0** | nouns only: `nādimīn` 5, `nadāma` 2 |
| Verbs of `Hsr` meaning regret | **0** | 9 nouns; the one verb, `yastaḥsirūn` 21:19, means *weary* |
| Positive imperative of a grief verb (`Hzn`, `Asw`, `Asf`) | **0** | — |
| `nādim`/`ḥasra` as a prophet's virtue | **0** | the only prophetic `ḥasarāt` is forbidden (35:8) |
| `tūbū` to humans | **7** | 2:54, 11:3, 11:52, 11:61, 11:90, 24:31, 66:8 (+ `tub ʿalaynā` 2:128, a petition to God) |
| `istaghfir-` for one's own sins | **13** | 2:199, 11:3, 11:52, 11:61, 11:90, 41:6, 71:10, 73:20; to the Prophet `li-dhanbika` 4:106, 40:55, 47:19, 110:3; 12:29 (excluded, for others: 3:159, 9:80, 12:97, 24:62, 48:11, 60:12) |
| `anībū` | **1** | 39:54 |
| Imperative of blame | **1** | Satan, 14:22 `lūmū anfusakum` |
| Imperative of weeping | **1** | 9:82, imposed on hypocrites `jazāʾan` (as recompense) |

The praised agent-nouns all come from the return family: `al-tawwābīn` whom God loves (2:222), `al-tāʾibūn` (9:112), `awwāb` for David, Solomon and Job (38:17, 38:30, 38:44), `munīb` for Abraham (11:75). There are **4 praised tears** (5:83, 9:92, 17:109, 19:58), shed over recognised truth, over a deed one could not do, and over hearing the verses. **None is over the weeper's own sin.** The one rebuke for *not* weeping (53:60) is followed by a command to prostrate (53:62). It does not command tears.

The one affective virtue-word, `awwāh` (Abraham, 9:114, 11:75; translators: *grieving / imploring / tender-hearted*), comes each time with pleading for the condemned that is then ended (9:114; 11:76 `aʿriḍ ʿan hādhā`).

**Finding.** Regret is never commanded, never praised, and never forbidden over one's own sin. The text treats it as something that *happens* (`aṣbaḥa nādiman`, *became*). What it commands is an act with a direction.

## 2. Register A: regret that converts

| Case | Speaks to God (2nd person)? | Names own wrong | Asks for | God's response in text |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adam 7:23 | yes | `ẓalamnā anfusanā` | forgiveness, mercy | 2:37, 20:122 |
| Moses 28:16 | yes | `ẓalamtu nafsī` | forgiveness | `fa-ghafara lahu` |
| Yūnus 21:87 | yes, + `subḥānaka` | `min al-ẓālimīn` | nothing | `fa-stajabnā` |
| David 38:24 | narrated | — | `istaghfara` | `fa-ghafarnā` |
| Noah 11:47 | yes, + `aʿūdhu bika` | asking without knowledge | forgiveness, mercy | blessing 11:48 |
| Bilqīs 27:44 | yes | `ẓalamtu nafsī` | nothing (`aslamtu`) | — |
| Calf 7:149 | 3rd person | — | mercy, forgiveness | 7:153, 2:54 |
| Brothers 12:91, 97 | to Joseph and father | `khāṭiʾīn` ×2 | via father | human only, 12:92, 12:98 |
| The three 9:118 | unspoken thought | — | nothing; refuge `illā ilayhi` | God turns first |
| Garden-people 68:29-32 | 3rd person, + `subḥāna` | `ẓālimīn`, `ṭāghīn` | a better garden | `ʿasā` |

| 9:102 | narrated | `iʿtarafū` | — | `ʿasā` |

**Counts (scripted for lexicon).** **0/12** use `ndm`, `ḥasra` or `layta`, and 1/12 has a woe-cry (68:31). **12/12 orient to God** by name or as `rabb`. **5/12** speak *to* Him in the second person, all of them individuals or the first couple. Own wrong is named in 8/12, with the `ẓlm` root in 5. In 7/12 something is asked, and **every request is for forgiveness or mercy. None asks to have the loss reversed** (68:32 only hopes). "If You do not forgive us, we will be among the losers" appears 3× (7:23, 7:149, 11:47). God moves first: `tāba ʿalayhim li-yatūbū` (9:118), and `talaqqā Ādamu min rabbihi kalimātin` (2:37), so even the words of repentance are received. `ijtabāhu` (*He chose him*) follows a fault only for Adam (20:122) and Yūnus (68:50).

**Correction to Phase 1.** `narrative-regret-in-this-life.md` places the calf, the garden-people and the three under "direct address to God, with petition", and its headline includes the brothers. None of these four group cases addresses God in the second person. 7:149 and 68:32 say `rabbunā`. 12:91/97 speak to Joseph and to Jacob. 9:118 is an unspoken `ẓann`. The three ask for nothing, and the brothers ask through their father. What survives is orientation to God by name.

**The decisive pair.**

> **21:87** …فَنَادَىٰ فِي ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ أَن لَّآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّآ أَنتَ سُبۡحَٰنَكَ إِنِّي كُنتُ مِنَ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ
> *Saheeh:* "…he called out within the darknesses, 'There is no deity except You; exalted are You. Indeed, I have been of the wrongdoers.'"

> **10:90** …حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَآ أَدۡرَكَهُ ٱلۡغَرَقُ قَالَ ءَامَنتُ أَنَّهُۥ لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا ٱلَّذِيٓ ءَامَنَتۡ بِهِۦ بَنُوٓاْ إِسۡرَٰٓءِيلَ وَأَنَا۠ مِنَ ٱلۡمُسۡلِمِينَ
> *Saheeh:* "…when drowning overtook him, he said, 'I believe that there is no deity except that in whom the Children of Israel believe, and I am of the Muslims.'"

*Data-grounded.* `wa-huwa mulīm` (*blameworthy*) occurs only twice: Yūnus (37:142) and Pharaoh (51:40). Both are cast out (`nabadha`, 37:145 and 51:40), both are in water, and both say `lā ilāha illā`. Yūnus speaks *to* God and confesses. Pharaoh speaks *about* God, through other people's faith, and claims a status. Both stated deciders look backward: `law-lā annahu kāna mina al-musabbiḥīn` (37:143) and `āl-āna wa-qad ʿaṣayta qablu` (10:91). **[interpretive]** Whether `kāna` means a prior habit is not settled, and 68:49 credits `niʿma` from his Lord.

**Same words, different time.** `tubtu` (*I have repented*) is said 3×. Moses (7:143) and the man at forty (46:15, accepted at 46:16) are heard. The dying man's `innī tubtu al-āna` (4:18) is rejected.

## 3. Register B: too late (summary of the window file)

Failure is `lā yanfaʿu`, *it does not benefit* (6:158, 40:85, 30:57, 40:52, 32:29). The window closes at seizure or death, and the late turn is judged against `qablu`. **One addition:** confession also happens beyond it (`fa-ʿtarafū bi-dhanbihim fa-suḥqan` 67:11, 40:11, 7:5). Admission is necessary inside the window and insufficient outside it.

## 4. Register C: grief forbidden, grief permitted

**Data (`Hzn`, `Asw`, scripted).** There are **16 prohibitions.** Nine are `lā taḥzan`-type (3:139, 9:40, 15:88, 16:127, 19:24, 27:70, 28:7, 29:33, 41:30), five are `fa-lā yaḥzunka` (3:176, 5:41, 10:65, 31:23, 36:76), and two are `fa-lā taʾsa` (5:26, 5:68). Another 5 purpose clauses have God acting *so that* no one grieves (3:153, 20:40, 28:13, 33:51, 57:23). By object: **others' unbelief or speech 10, danger or loss 6, one's own sin 0.** Freedom from grief is promised as reward 17×. Grief is Satan's aim: `li-yaḥzuna alladhīna āmanū` (58:10).

> **57:23** لِّكَيۡلَا تَأۡسَوۡاْ عَلَىٰ مَا فَاتَكُمۡ وَلَا تَفۡرَحُواْ بِمَآ ءَاتَىٰكُمۡۗ وَٱللَّهُ لَا يُحِبُّ كُلَّ مُخۡتَالࣲ فَخُورٍ
> *Saheeh:* "In order that you not despair over what has eluded you and not exult over what He has given you. And Allah does not like everyone self-deluded and boastful."

**57:22's reason** is that every calamity "on earth or in yourselves" is `fī kitābin min qabli an nabraʾahā`, *in a record before We bring it into being*. The parallels make the same point: 3:154 `kutiba`, 3:156 `Allāhu yuḥyī wa-yumīt`, 3:168 *then avert death from yourselves*. For grief over others: 35:8 (God guides and leads astray), 26:4, and Shuʿayb's `fa-kayfa āsā` (7:93). The reasons come in two kinds: **the outcome was written, and others' choices are not yours to carry.**

**The object is the line [grounded, then interpretive].** The forbidden "if only" has circumstances or other people as subject: `law kānū ʿindanā mā mātū` (3:156, made a `ḥasra` by God), `law aṭāʿūnā` (3:168). Converting regret has the speaker's own act as subject (`ẓalamtu nafsī`), and so do the hereafter wishes (`ittakhadhtu` 25:27, `lam ushrik` 18:42). **Regret over acts can work. Grief over outcomes is forbidden.**

**Despair and exultation are a pair.** 57:23 forbids both and ends on `fakhūr`. So does 11:9-10 (`yaʾūs kafūr` / `fariḥ fakhūr`), and 30:36 pairs them too (`fariḥū` / `yaqnaṭūn`). The exception given is `alladhīna ṣabarū wa-ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt` (11:11). **[interpretive]** Despair is not too much regret. It is the boaster's self-measurement by outcome, turned the other way.

**Permitted grief.** At 9:92 Phase 0 says "praised". The text says *no blame* (`wa-lā ʿalā`), and 9:91 counts the sincere-but-unable among the `muḥsinīn`. Jacob is the full case:

> **12:86** قَالَ إِنَّمَآ أَشۡكُواْ بَثِّي وَحُزۡنِيٓ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ وَأَعۡلَمُ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ مَا لَا تَعۡلَمُونَ
> *Saheeh:* "I only complain of my suffering and my grief to Allah, and I know from Allah that which you do not know."

His grief whitens his eyes (12:84), and it is not rebuked. What separates it from the forbidden kind is in the text. (1) It is addressed **only to God** (`innamā… ilā Allāh`). (2) It is held together with hope from God. (3) It ends in action: `idhhabū fa-taḥassasū` (12:87). (4) Its object is a person lost, not a decree contested. Forbidden grief faces the outcome, and the text shows it consuming the self (`bākhiʿun nafsaka` 18:6, 26:3; `lā tadhhab nafsuka` 35:8). God still acknowledges it (6:33).

## 5. Despair, the failure mode of regret

> **39:53** قُلۡ يَٰعِبَادِيَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَسۡرَفُواْ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمۡ لَا تَقۡنَطُواْ مِن رَّحۡمَةِ ٱللَّهِۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَغۡفِرُ ٱلذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًا…
> *Saheeh:* "O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of the mercy of Allah. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins…"

**Data.** `yAs` and `qnT` occur 19× in 16 ayahs. Despair is never commanded or praised. It is **fault-coded in 10**. Its object there is God's mercy or relief (12:87, 15:55, 15:56, 29:23, 39:53), the hereafter (60:13), or the human reflex under harm (11:9, 17:83, 30:36, 41:49). It is **unblamed in 6** (5:3, 12:80, 12:110, 13:31, 42:28, 65:4), including *the messengers'* despair of their people (12:110). The text names who despairs of God's mercy: the disbelievers (12:87), the astray (15:56), and the deniers of the meeting (29:23). **Despair of people is human. Despair of God is the fault.** 30:36 shows it at the regret moment: harm comes `bi-mā qaddamat aydīhim` (for what their own hands sent ahead), and they despair.

**Placement.** 39:53 is addressed to those with most to regret (`asrafū ʿalā anfusihim`). The sequence runs: no despair, because God forgives *all* sins; then `anībū` before the punishment (39:54); then follow (39:55); then the three regret speeches as what to avoid (39:56-58); then the reply (39:59). **[interpretive]** The order is hope, then command, then regret as warning. Despair would block the return, so it is removed first. Jacob (12:87) and Abraham (15:56) refuse despair where they had most cause for it.

## 6. Feeling vs change

**The petition's shape (data).** `la-in… la-nakūnanna` (*if… we will surely be*) occurs **6×**. **2 are conditioned on forgiveness** (7:23, 7:149), and both convert. **4 are bargains for rescue or a gift**: 6:63, 10:22 (deliverance), 7:189 (a child), 9:75 (wealth). **All four are broken in the next verse** (6:64, 10:23, 7:190, 9:76). The other crisis prayers ask for removal (7:134, 44:12), Iblīs asks for respite (15:36), and the dead ask for delay (63:10). **Only converting petitions ask for forgiveness.**

**The death-bed promise is already broken in this life.** 9:75 vows `la-naṣṣaddaqanna wa-la-nakūnanna mina al-ṣāliḥīn`. 63:10's dying man wishes `fa-aṣṣaddaqa wa-akun mina al-ṣāliḥīn`. Same promise, made and broken (9:76-77): in-life evidence for 6:28 `wa-law ruddū la-ʿādū`. Even `munīb`, Abraham's praise (11:75), describes crisis-prayer that relapses (30:33, 39:8).

**What lasts (data-grounded).** In **17 of 32** ayahs where a human subject `tāba` (non-imperative), a second act follows in the same verse. It is `aṣlaḥa` in 8 (2:160, 3:89, 4:16, 4:146, 5:39, 6:54, 16:119, 24:5), `āmana`/`ʿamila ṣāliḥan` in 6 (7:153, 19:60, 20:82, 25:70, 25:71, 28:67), prayer and zakāt in 2 (9:5, 9:11), and *followed Your way* in 1 (40:7). 3:135 defines the turn as `lam yuṣirrū` (*did not persist*); 4:17 adds `min qarīb` (*soon*). Moses, once forgiven, commits on the ground of his fault: `fa-lan akūna ẓahīran li-l-mujrimīn` (28:17). **[interpretive]** The turn that lasts asks for forgiveness rather than relief, has a second verb, and names its ground.

## 7. Admit vs defend

**The Iblīs wording is verified.** It is `fa-bi-mā aghwaytanī` (7:16) and `rabbi bi-mā aghwaytanī` (15:39): Form IV, with God as the one who *caused* the error. Of Adam the text says `ʿaṣā Ādamu rabbahu fa-ghawā` (20:121): Form I, with Adam as the subject. All 11 `ghwy` verbs were checked. In the hereafter the leaders take up Iblīs's verb (28:63 `aghwaynāhum kamā ghawaynā… tabarraʾnā`; 37:32), fusing half-admission with disowning. `mā kāna lanā ʿalaykum min sulṭān` (37:30) is Satan's defence at 14:22, and only these two verses contain it. One word shows the flip: the garden-people say `innā kunnā ṭāghīn` (68:31), *we*, and the leaders say `bal kuntum qawman ṭāghīn` (37:30), *you*. Blaming God reproduces Iblīs most closely: `law anna Allāha hadānī` (39:57, answered 39:59 `balā qad jāʾatka āyātī`) and `law shāʾa Allāhu` (6:148).

**14:22.** The Quran's only imperative of blame, `lūmū anfusakum`, is Satan's, spoken *after the matter is decided*: Adam's self-blame, prescribed when it no longer helps.

**How far the parallel holds.** (a) It holds for the direction of blame. (b) Naming an influence is not defending: Moses says `hādhā min ʿamal al-shayṭān` (28:15) and then `ẓalamtu nafsī` (28:16). (c) Direct address is not the marker. Iblīs says `rabbi` and petitions, but for time, not forgiveness. (d) **It breaks at the window.** The hereafter speakers also admit (67:11, 40:11) and are still lost.

## Negative space

There are no praised tears of guilt. Only 9:118 (`ḍāqat ʿalayhim anfusuhum`) describes what a converting person felt, and the text never measures how much.

## For introspection

1. When I recall a wrong, who is the subject of my sentence: *I* (`ẓalamtu nafsī`), or a circumstance or person (`aghwaytanī`, `law-lā antum`)?
2. Am I asking forgiveness for the act, or removal of its consequence? If removal, would my resolve survive relief (10:22-23, 9:75-76)?
3. What is my second verb? After "I turned", what did I set right, and is there a specific *never again* (28:17)?
4. Is my "if only" about my own act, or about an outcome I never controlled (3:156, 57:22)?
5. Does my grief go to God and move me to act (12:86-87), or is it consuming me (35:8)?
6. When the consequence of my own deeds lands (30:36), do I conclude it is over? 39:53 is addressed to exactly that person.
