# The Quranic Signs of the Hour (Ashrāṭ al-Sāʿa)

## Headline finding

The Quran names 3-4 specific pre-Hour signs — Yaʾjūj wa Maʾjūj, al-Dābba, al-Dukhān, and (ambiguously) the Prophet's own coming — and describes each in 1-3 verses with conspicuous minimalism. The signs are identified by nature, not by timing. What they do, when they arrive, who or what they are: the Quran withholds. The only verse that uses the word *ashrāṭ* (signs/preconditions) for the Hour [47:18] says those signs have already come — past tense. This architecture is theologically deliberate: the Quran forecloses sign-reading as a substitute for readiness.

---

## 1. Yaʾjūj wa Maʾjūj — The Most-Detailed Sign

The Yaʾjūj wa Maʾjūj (Gog and Magog) narrative spans [18:83–99] within the Dhū al-Qarnayn travelogue, then resurfaces in a single eschatological flash at [21:96].

**The barrier narrative [18:93–98]:** At the third journey's end, Dhū al-Qarnayn reaches a people between two mountains who have almost no language in common with him. They ask him to build a barrier against Yaʾjūj wa Maʾjūj, describing them with a single epithet: *mufsidūna fī al-arḍ* — corrupters in the land [18:94]. Dhū al-Qarnayn builds an iron-and-copper barrier leveling the two mountain flanks. Then [18:98]:

> **قَالَ هَٰذَا رَحۡمَةٌ مِّن رَّبِّي ۖ فَإِذَا جَآءَ وَعۡدُ رَبِّي جَعَلَهُۥ دَكَّآءَ ۖ وَكَانَ وَعۡدُ رَبِّي حَقًّا**
> "This is a mercy from my Lord; but when the promise of my Lord comes, He will make it level — and ever is the promise of my Lord true." (Saheeh)

The collapse is guaranteed but undated. The phrase *waʿdu rabbī* (my Lord's promise) is Quranic code for the Hour without naming it directly. [18:99] then immediately cuts to the Trumpet-blowing and the gathering — the Hour-event itself — without narrating what Gog and Magog do in the interim. The Quran fast-forwards from "barrier collapses" to "Trumpet blows."

**The eschatological release [21:96]:** The clearest single verse on the sign itself:

> **حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَا فُتِحَتۡ يَأۡجُوجُ وَمَأۡجُوجُ وَهُم مِّن كُلِّ حَدَبٍ يَنسِلُونَ**
> "Until when Gog and Magog have been opened and they, from every elevation, descend." (Saheeh)

The verb *futiḥat* (opened/released) is passive: they are released by divine act, not by their own escape. *Yansilūn* (hasten/swarm/descend rapidly) from every *ḥadab* (elevated/raised ground). That is everything the Quran says about what they do. No armies, no conquests, no territorial details.

**What the Quran does not say:**
- Who Gog and Magog are (ethnically, geographically, or ontologically — human, jinn, or other)
- Where the barrier stands
- When it collapses relative to other signs
- What they do after swarming from the heights
- Whether they are defeated or simply present

The Quran's textual minimalism — *mufsidūna fī al-arḍ* plus *yansilūna* — is the entire description. Everything in tradition (the drinking of seas, the arrow-shooting at heaven, the death by worms in the neck) comes from hadith, not from the Quran's text.

## 2. al-Dābba — Single Verse, Single Beast

The entire Quranic account of the Beast of the End is one ayah:

> **وَإِذَا وَقَعَ ٱلۡقَوۡلُ عَلَيۡهِمۡ أَخۡرَجۡنَا لَهُمۡ دَآبَّةً مِّنَ ٱلۡأَرۡضِ تُكَلِّمُهُمۡ أَنَّ ٱلنَّاسَ كَانُواْ بِـَٔايَٰتِنَا لَا يُوقِنُونَ**
> "And when the word befalls them, We will bring forth for them a creature from the earth speaking to them, [saying] that the people were, of Our verses, not certain [in faith]." [27:82] (Saheeh)

Three features the Quran specifies:
1. **Trigger:** *waqaʿa al-qawl ʿalayhim* — "the word falls upon them." This is the divine sentence of judgment being rendered. The Beast arrives at the moment of verdict, not before it.
2. **Origin:** *mina al-arḍ* — from the earth. Not from the sea, not from the sky.
3. **Function:** *tukallimuhum* — it speaks to them. Its speech declares that people had no certain faith in God's signs.

The Beast is a witness and announcer, not an executor. It speaks the verdict; it does not carry it out.

**What the Quran does not say:**
- The Beast's appearance, size, species, or composition
- Where it emerges from
- Whether it marks, brands, or injures people (the hadith tradition's "it marks believers and disbelievers on their faces")
- How long it speaks or to whom specifically
- Any chronological relationship to Gog and Magog or to al-Dukhān

One verse. One function. Tradition has generated extensive elaboration; the Quran offers a speaking animal from the earth at the moment of judgment. That is the entirety of the textual base.

## 3. al-Dukhān — Past Event or Eschatological Sign?

Surah al-Dukhān (44) opens a seven-verse sequence [44:10–16] built around a day of visible smoke:

> **فَٱرۡتَقِبۡ يَوۡمَ تَأۡتِي ٱلسَّمَآءُ بِدُخَانٍ مُّبِينٍ يَغۡشَى ٱلنَّاسَ هَٰذَا عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ**
> "Then watch for the Day when the sky will bring a visible smoke, covering the people — this is a painful torment." [44:10–11]

The sequence's logic: smoke envelops the people → they cry *rabbanā kshif ʿannā al-ʿadhāb innā muʾminūn* ("Our Lord, remove the torment — we are believers") → God notes the messenger they rejected → God lifts the punishment *qalīlan* (a little) → they return to disbelief → [44:16]: *yawma nabṭishu al-baṭshata al-kubrā innā muntaqimūn* ("The Day We strike the greatest strike, We will be avengers").

The structure follows a pattern seen elsewhere in Meccan revelation: sign → false repentance → reprieve → relapse → final punishment. The "greatest strike" (*al-baṭsha al-kubrā*) follows the smoke as a second, terminal event.

**The exegetical dispute:** Classical commentators divide sharply. Ibn Masʿūd's reading (followed by many Meccan-context interpreters): the smoke was the famine-induced haze the Meccans saw during their persecution before Badr — a historical, already-past event. Ibn ʿAbbas's reading: al-Dukhān is a future eschatological sign that will genuinely envelop the earth before the Hour. The Quran's text does not resolve this. The imperative *fa-rtaqib* (then watch/wait) can introduce both a near-future historical event and a distant eschatological one. The phrase *yawma taʾtī al-samāʾu bi-dukhānin mubīn* uses a future verb (*taʾtī*), favoring a future reading, but the subsequent narrative of the prophet being rejected ([44:13–14]) uses past tense, grounding the passage in the Meccan situation.

**Data-grounded observation:** The word *dukhān* (smoke) appears only once in the Quran — here. There is no cross-Quranic pattern to resolve the temporal location. The ambiguity is in the text.

## 4. The Hour's Timing — Knowledge Withholding

Three ayahs use an identical formula:

- [7:187]: *yasʾalūnaka ʿani al-sāʿati ayyāna mursāhā* — "they ask you about the Hour: when is its arrival?"
- [33:63]: *yasʾaluka al-nāsu ʿani al-sāʿa* — "people ask you about the Hour"
- [79:42]: same formula as 7:187 verbatim

The response in every case: *ʿilmuhā ʿinda rabbī / ʿinda Allāh* — knowledge of it is with my Lord / with God. The Prophet is given an epistemology to repeat, not a date. The formula is theologically stable across early Meccan ([79]), late Meccan ([7]), and Medinan ([33]) revelation alike — the withholding is not a stage of revelation but a permanent structural feature.

The critical distinction: the Quran says the signs are *knowable* (they are described), but the *timing* is unknowable. The signs inform about nature; they cannot be used to calculate the when. This is consistent with the broader doctrine at [31:34] where knowledge of the Hour heads a five-item list of things kept exclusively with God. See `notes/time/1-roots/concept-swE.md` for the full knowledge-withholding architecture.

## 5. Cosmic-Collapse Signs vs Pre-Hour Signs

A structural distinction runs through the Quran's eschatological material: some signs *precede* the Hour as this-worldly events; others are *the Hour-event itself* beginning to unfold.

**Pre-Hour (this-worldly heralds):** Yaʾjūj/Maʾjūj release, al-Dābba speaking, al-Dukhān enveloping. These occur while the present world-order still runs — people react to them, repent falsely, return to disbelief. They are events within history that signal history's terminus.

**Hour-event signs (cosmic collapse):** Surah 81's 12 cascading images (*al-shams kuwwirat, al-nujūm inkadarat, al-jibāl suyyirat…*) — these are not heralds; they are the Hour itself beginning. Similarly [54:1]: *iqtarabati al-sāʿatu wa-nshaqqa al-qamar* — the moon-splitting is presented as evidence of the Hour's nearness, already occurring at the time of revelation.

The distinction matters: tradition often lists cosmic-collapse events (sun rising in the west, stars falling) alongside the human-scale pre-Hour signs (Mahdī, Dajjāl, Jesus). The Quran keeps them separate by structure: one set has human agents reacting to them; the other set dissolves the conditions for human reaction.

## 6. The 47:18 *Ashrāṭ* Verse — "They Have Already Come"

The only Quranic use of the word *ashrāṭ* (signs/preconditions) for the Hour:

> **فَهَلۡ يَنظُرُونَ إِلَّا ٱلسَّاعَةَ أَن تَأۡتِيَهُم بَغۡتَةً ۖ فَقَدۡ جَآءَ أَشۡرَاطُهَا**
> "Are they only waiting for the Hour to come upon them unexpectedly? Its signs have already come." [47:18] (Saheeh)

Two features of this verse bear on the entire investigation:

1. **The word *ashrāṭ* is not *āyāt*.** *Ashrāṭ* (singular *sharṭ*) means conditions, preconditions, or the advance marks that signal what is coming — closer to "its preconditions are in place" than "its miraculous signs have appeared." The Hour's conditions have been met; what remains is the arrival itself.

2. **Past tense — *fa-qad jāʾa*.** The conditions have already come. By the time of the verse's revelation, the preconditions are already present. Tradition's standard reading: the Prophet's own coming is the first and decisive sign, fulfilling the *ashrāṭ*. This is internally consistent: the Quran repeatedly describes the Prophet's sending as an eschatological event, not merely a historical one.

The verse does not enumerate *which* ashrāṭ have come. The Quran names the category and asserts their arrival but does not provide a list. This is the inverse of what tradition does: tradition lists exhaustively; the Quran asserts without enumerating.

## 7. Minimalism as Theological Choice

The Quran's pre-Hour sign vocabulary is deliberately sparse. Compare:

- **Pre-Hour signs:** 3-4 signs, ~1-3 verses each, no chronological order given, no causal mechanism described, no geographic specificity
- **The Hour-event itself:** rich imagery across dozens of ayahs — [81:1–14], [82:1–5], [69:13–16], [77:8–13], [84:1–5] — each with vivid kinesthetic detail
- **Post-Hour eschatology:** massively elaborated — the weighing, the scroll, the bridge, the intercession, the gardens, the fire

The asymmetry is structural. The Quran gives cinematic detail to the Hour-event and the afterlife; it gives minimal detail to the pre-Hour signs. This is consistent with the knowledge-withholding theology: dwelling on signs would feed the calculation it forbids. [7:187] explicitly frames sign-curiosity as a posture incompatible with the correct response — the questioners want a calendar; the Quran gives them an epistemology.

The minimalism also creates a specific kind of interpretive space. Each sign is described just enough to be recognizable in principle but not enough to be identified in any historical occurrence. Gog and Magog are *mufsidūna* — corrupters — but what kind? The Dābba speaks, but what does it sound like? The smoke covers the people, but which people? The Quran's silence is not accidental; it is the silence that makes every generation feel the signs could be imminent.

## 8. The Contrast with Hadith Elaboration

The hadith tradition's major pre-Hour signs (al-ashrāṭ al-kubrā — the ten great signs): the Dajjāl (Antichrist), descent of ʿĪsā (Jesus), al-Mahdī, Gog and Magog, the Beast, the Smoke, rising of the sun in the west, a great fire, the sinking of the earth in three places, the destruction of the Kaʿba. Of these ten, the Quran directly mentions only three (Gog and Magog, the Beast, possibly the Smoke). The other seven are entirely hadith-based.

Furthermore, even for the three the Quran does mention, the hadith tradition adds:
- **Gog and Magog:** drinking the Sea of Tiberias, shooting arrows at heaven, divine destruction by worms
- **the Dābba:** specific physical description (head of bull, body of lion, hooves of camel), marking of foreheads
- **al-Dukhān:** duration (forty days), geographic spread, symptoms (people's heads swim)

The Quran's textual base for all of this is three named entities and approximately seven combined ayahs. The elaboration is of a different order of magnitude from the textual warrant.

## 9. Negative Space — What the Quran Does Not Say

- **No al-Mahdī.** The word *mahdī* does not appear in the Quran. The eschatological figure of the Guided One who emerges before the Hour is entirely hadith-based.
- **No al-Dajjāl.** The word *dajjāl* (the Deceiver/Antichrist) does not appear anywhere in the Quran. The tradition's most detailed pre-Hour figure is Qurbanically absent.
- **No descent of ʿĪsā.** [43:61] is the contested verse — *wa-innahū la-ʿilmun li-al-sāʿa* — where two of four major translators insert Jesus into the pronoun *innahū*. The bare Arabic leaves the referent ambiguous. There is no verse that unambiguously describes Jesus descending before the Hour.
- **No rising sun in the west.** No Quranic basis.
- **No chronological ordering of signs.** The Quran gives no sequence among Gog and Magog, the Beast, and the Smoke. Tradition constructs elaborate sequences; the text does not.
- **No mechanism for the barrier's collapse.** [18:98] says God will make it level (*jaʿalahu dakkāʾ*). No earthquake, no internal pressure, no human action — just the divine promise activating.
- **No description of what happens after the signs.** The Quran moves from the pre-Hour signs directly to the Hour-event (as in [18:99]'s immediate transition to the Trumpet). The period *between* signs and the Hour is narrative blank.

## 10. Verses for Direct Study

1. **[18:98]** — "when the promise of my Lord comes, He will make it level." The eschatological turn inside the Dhū al-Qarnayn narrative. The collision of a historical-hero narrative with the terminal promise.
2. **[21:96]** — *futiḥat Yaʾjūju wa-Maʾjūj / yansilūn.* The clearest pre-Hour sign verse. Passive release + motion verb is the entire content.
3. **[27:82]** — *akhrajnā lahum dābbatan mina al-arḍ tukallimuhum.* The one Beast verse. Note the divine "We" bringing it forth — the Beast is a divine instrument, not a spontaneous creature.
4. **[47:18]** — *fa-qad jāʾa ashrāṭuhā.* The only *ashrāṭ* verse. Its past-tense structure argues the prophetic mission itself fulfills the precondition category.
5. **[44:10–16]** — The Smoke sequence. The false-repentance loop and "greatest strike" frame. Best read as a unit; the debate over past-vs-eschatological is the whole interpretive crux.

## 11. Rabbit Holes

- **The *futiḥat* passive in [21:96].** Who or what opens Gog and Magog? The passive construction is theologically loaded — it is God who releases them, not natural forces or their own agency. A full pass on the passive-divine-act construction across eschatological verses would surface a pattern.
- **[43:61] and the Jesus question.** The single verse most likely to bear on the "descent of Jesus" sign — and the one where two of four major English translators supply a proper noun not in the Arabic. A morphological-and-exegetical pass specifically on *innahū* in [43:61] would test whether the Arabic commits.
- **al-Dukhān's *kāshifū qalīlan*.** God lifts the punishment "a little" (*qalīlan*) and the people return to disbelief. This reprieve-and-relapse structure is also present in the Moses-Pharaoh narrative. Is this a Quranic type-scene? Comparing the Dukhān sequence with [43:48–50] and the plagues narrative would test whether al-Dukhān is structurally parallel to a this-worldly punishment, not an eschatological one.
- **The *ashrāṭ* word itself.** Root *$rT* (shin-ra-ta). The Quran uses *ashrāṭ* once; the cognate *sharṭ* (condition) elsewhere. A concept pass on this root would clarify whether "signs" or "preconditions" is the better translation — and what is at stake in the difference.
- **[18:99]'s transition.** The verse immediately following the barrier-collapse promise: *wa-taraknā baʿḍahum yawmaʾidhin yamūju fī baʿḍ / wa-nufikha fī al-ṣūr.* "We left them that day surging over each other / and the Trumpet was blown." The "them" is grammatically ambiguous — Gog and Magog, or all humanity? And the Trumpet follows with no intervening narrative. The structural compression here — barrier collapses → Trumpet immediately — is the closest the Quran comes to placing Gog and Magog immediately before the Hour-event itself.
