# Narrative: Surah 75 al-Qiyāma + 39:67-75
## The Two Most Direct Judgment-Scene Passages

---

## Headline Finding

Surah 75 builds the resurrection argument from the body *outward* — from fingertip-precision reconstruction through deathbed physiology to a closing creation-proof — while 39:67-75 frames the same judgment from cosmic *inward*: earth in God's grasp, heavens folded, then the two blasts, then procedural verdict, then the two processions. Together they establish the Quran's core claim: resurrection is not asserted dogmatically but *argued*, from the evidence of the body (75) and from the scale of divine power (39:67). Both passages converge on the same structural end: everything is driven to its destination, and praise closes the scene.

---

## PART 1 — Surah 75 al-Qiyāma

### 1. The Double Oath — Day + Lawwāma Soul (75:1-2)

> **لَآ أُقۡسِمُ بِيَوۡمِ ٱلۡقِيَٰمَةِ / وَلَآ أُقۡسِمُ بِٱلنَّفۡسِ ٱللَّوَّامَةِ**
> "I swear by the Day of Resurrection / and I swear by the self-reproaching soul"

The opening formula pairs two oaths: the eschatological *event* (the Day) with an internal *faculty* (the self-blaming conscience). The *nafs al-lawwāma* is the soul caught mid-fall — not the *nafs al-ammāra* that drives toward sin blindly, not the *nafs al-muṭmaʾinna* that rests in God, but the conscience that looks back at its own actions and accuses itself. The oath-pairing is architecturally significant: the external Day of Judgment and the internal judgment-of-the-self are yoked in the very first breath of the surah. The Quran announces *al-qiyāma* by invoking the small daily resurrection already happening inside the person — conscience as a preview of the cosmic event.

The *lā uqsimu* formula is traditionally read as intensive — "I truly swear." The content of the oath is not stated as a proposition; the swearing-by *is* the argument.

### 2. Bone Reassembly + Fingertip Claim (75:3-4)

> **أَيَحۡسَبُ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنُ أَلَّن نَّجۡمَعَ عِظَامَهُۥ / بَلَىٰ قَٰدِرِينَ عَلَىٰٓ أَن نُّسَوِّيَ بَنَانَهُۥ**
> "Does the human think We will not gather his bones? / Yes — capable of even making smooth his fingertips"

The *balā* ("yes, certainly") correction is unexpected. The skeptic's objection is about large-scale disintegration; the Quran leaps past the obvious (bones) to the finest anatomical extreme: *banān* (fingertips — or fingerprint ridges specifically). The argument is a fortiori: if God is capable of the finest detail, the gross fact follows trivially. This is not prediction of fingerprint science but a claim about *personhood-preservation* — God reconstructs the most individuating marks of the human body.

### 3. Procrastination by Sin — Fujūr (75:5-6)

The surah identifies why the human asks "when is the Day?" not as genuine skepticism but as strategic delay: *Bal yurīdu al-insānu li-yafjura amāmahu* — the human *wants* to commit *fujūr* in his future, so he deflects with the question. This is psychologically more precise than "they don't believe." The question is a stall. Procrastination does not postpone the recording — [18:49] says the book leaves nothing unenumerated.

### 4. The Cosmic Signs (75:7-12)

Sight dazzled [75:7] → moon eclipsed [75:8] → sun and moon joined [75:9] → "where to flee?" [75:10] → *lā wazara* (no refuge) [75:11] → *ilā rabbika yawmaʾiḏin al-mustaqarr* (unto your Lord that day is the settling-place) [75:12]. The sequence moves from individual perception (sight dazzled) to cosmic reversal (sun-moon joined). The joining of sun and moon inverts [55:5]'s image of their ordered calculation — the cosmic order that makes calendrical time possible is collapsed. The answer to "where to flee?" is a double move: negation (*lā wazara* — no mountain refuge) then redirection (*al-mustaqarr* — settling in God). There is no escape *from* God; there is only arrival *at* God.

### 5. "Qaddama wa-Akhkhara" + Self-Witness (75:13-15)

Two phrases that concentrate the judgment mechanism:

**"mā qaddama wa-akhkhara"** — what the human *sent ahead* (commissions) and *held back* (omissions). The judgment counts both. [18:49] says the book "leaves nothing small or great but enumerated it" — the formula here makes that explicit: the accounting runs in both directions of time.

**"al-insānu ʿalā nafsihi baṣīra"** — the human is a *baṣīra* (penetrating witness) upon his own self [75:14]. Cross-reference [41:20-23] (skins testifying) and [17:14] (self-as-reckoner): three separate passages establish the person as their own primary witness. At [41:23], the eschatological disaster is traced to a wrong assumption about divine observation; at [75:14], the person's own internal sight is invoked as the testimony-ground. [75:15] closes: even if he throws his excuses, the self-witness stands.

### 6. The Quran-Recitation Interruption (75:16-19)

The surah pivots to address the Prophet: do not rush your tongue with the revelation [75:16]; God guarantees its collection, recitation, and clarification [75:17-19]. Why here? The parallel is structural: the human at [75:5] hastens into sin (*li-tafjura amāmahu* — rushes his future); the Prophet at [75:16] hastens reception (*li-taʿjala* — same urgency verb). Both are corrected. The human races *away* from the Day; the Prophet races *toward* the words that prepare for it. [75:20-21] makes this explicit: "You love the immediate (*al-ʿājila*) and leave the afterlife (*al-ākhira*)." The interruption is not a digression — it is the structural pivot of the surah.

### 7. Two-Faces Typology (75:22-25)

> **وُجُوهٌ يَوۡمَئِذٍ نَّاضِرَةٌ / إِلَىٰ رَبِّهَا نَاظِرَةٌ / وَوُجُوهٌ يَوۡمَئِذٍ بَاسِرَةٌ / تَظُنُّ أَن يُفۡعَلَ بِهَا فَاقِرَةٌ**
> "Faces that Day — bright (*nāḍira*), looking at their Lord; / and faces that Day — frowning (*bāsira*), expecting a back-breaking calamity (*fāqira*)"

*Nāḍira* (bright) is paired with *nāẓira* (gazing) — the faces are simultaneously *luminous* and *directed*. They shine *because* they face God. [75:23] — *ilā rabbihā nāẓira* — "looking at their Lord" — is the Quran's strongest attestation for the beatific vision and the basis of the classical *ruʾyat Allāh* debate. The opposite faces are *bāsira* (grim) and certain of *fāqira* (a word from the root meaning to break the spine). The facing-toward/facing-away axis governs the two-faces typologies across surahs 75, 80, and 88 — Surah 88 uses *khāshiʿa* vs. *nāʿima*, Surah 80 uses growling vs. laughing — confirming this as a fixed Quranic formula for binary eschatological outcome stated in terms of physical affect.

### 8. The Deathbed Scene (75:26-30)

> **كَلَّآ إِذَا بَلَغَتِ ٱلتَّرَاقِيَ / وَقِيلَ مَنۡ رَاقٍ / وَظَنَّ أَنَّهُ ٱلۡفِرَاقُ / وَٱلۡتَفَّتِ ٱلسَّاقُ بِٱلسَّاقِ / إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ يَوۡمَئِذٍ ٱلۡمَسَاقُ**
> "When [the soul] reaches the collar-bones / and it is said: who will heal? / and he knows it is the parting / and the leg twists with the leg / unto your Lord that day is the driving"

Five images in succession:

1. *Tarāqī* (collar-bones) — the soul reaching the throat-junction, the point of no-return.
2. *"Man rāqin?"* — "who will heal/raise?" The bedside question, asked while the answer is already foreclosed.
3. *Ẓanna annahu al-firāq* — he becomes *certain* (ẓanna here = certainty, not doubt) it is the parting.
4. *Wa-ltaffati al-sāqu bi-l-sāq* — "the leg twists with the leg." Two classical readings: the physical legs' spasm, or the "leg of this life" and "leg of the next" meeting at the threshold. Both hold.
5. *Al-masāq* — the *driving* (toward God). Not a journey; not a return. *Sāqa* = to herd. The soul at death is herded.

This passage is the micro-version of the 39:71-73 procession: individual *masāq* (death) anticipates the collective *sīqa* (eschatological herding). Death is each person's individual trumpet-blast.

### 9. The Closing Creation → Resurrection Argument (75:31-40)

The disbeliever: did not confirm (*lā ṣaddaqa*), did not pray (*lā ṣallā*), denied, turned away, then went to his family swaggering (*yatamaṭṭā*) [75:31-33]. The quadruple *awlā laka fa-awlā* [75:34-35] is pronouncement, not description — woe as escalating verdict.

Then [75:36]: *a-yaḥsabu al-insānu an yutraka sudā* — "Does the human think he will be left *aimlessly* (*sudā*)?" — without purpose, like unherded livestock. The cosmological claim: a universe that creates a human and leaves him without accountability would itself be purposeless. The anti-absurdity argument grounds resurrection.

The proof: sperm-drop [75:37] → clinging clot [75:38] → God created and proportioned → made from him the two sexes [75:39]. The creation-sequence is not biology but argument: the same God who crafted fingertips from a sperm-drop can reconstruct the dead. The surah closes [75:40]:

> **أَلَيۡسَ ذَٰلِكَ بِقَٰدِرٍ عَلَىٰ أَن يُحۡـِۧيَ ٱلۡمَوۡتَىٰ**
> "Is not that [Creator] capable of giving life to the dead?"

*Dhālika* (that one) refers back across the whole creation sequence. The demonstrative pronoun is the closing argument: the One who did all of *that* — the sperm, the clot, the proportion, the sexes, the fingertip-ridges — is the same One making the resurrection claim. The proof is not in the afterlife; it is in the origin.

## PART 2 — 39:67-75 (The Cosmic Throne-Judgment Scene)

### 10. Cosmic Measure (39:67)

> **وَمَا قَدَرُواْ ٱللَّهَ حَقَّ قَدۡرِهِۦ**
> "They have not measured God His true measure"

*Qadr* (measure, magnitude) is the Quran's word for calibrated knowledge. The two images that follow make the failure concrete: the entire earth is in God's *qabḍa* (grasp, clenched fist), and the heavens are *maṭwiyyāt* (folded) in His right hand. These are images of *scale reduction* — the universe the human treats as unbounded fits in a divine grasp. The Quran answers underestimation with a perspective-reversal: from inside, the cosmos is vast; from God's position, it is portable.

### 11. The Two Trumpet Blasts (39:68)

The trumpet investigation confirms: [39:68] is **the only** Quranic verse that explicitly enumerates two blasts. First blast: *fa-ṣaʿiqa man fī al-samāwāti wa-man fī al-arḍ illā man shāʾa Allāh* — all swoon/die except whom God wills (the exempt are not named — a deliberate negative space). Second blast: *thumma nufixa fīhi ukhrā fa-iḏā hum qiyāmun yanẓurūn* — blown again, and suddenly they are *standing, looking on* — stunned wakefulness before any orientation. First blast = ending the living world; second blast = initiating the resurrected one. The interval between the two is unstated.

### 12. The 4-Step Procedural Judgment (39:69)

The verse unfolds in four sequential clauses:

| Step | Arabic | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 1. *wa-ashraqati al-arḍu bi-nūri rabbihā* | Earth illumined by Lord's light | Environmental condition — visibility established |
| 2. *wa-wuḍiʿa al-kitāb* | The Book placed/laid open | Evidence set — the master record deployed |
| 3. *wa-jīʾa bi-l-nabiyyīna wa-l-shuhadāʾ* | Prophets and witnesses brought | Testimony assembled — human and prophetic witnesses present |
| 4. *wa-quḍiya baynahum bi-l-ḥaqq* | Judged between them with truth | Verdict executed — *qDy* closes the procedure |

Step 1 is not decorative — the earth shining by God's light is the condition for the visibility of the judgment. The *nūr* (light) is not sun or moon; those have been abolished or altered (75:7-9). This is a different light — a theophanic light that makes the scene legible. Step 4 uses *qDy* in the passive past tense (*quḍiya*) — the decree executed, the closure sealed. The judgment-process investigation's table confirms: qDy is the verb of finality, enacted *after* witnesses and records.

[39:70] adds: *wuwufiyyat kullu nafs mā ʿamilat* — "every soul is paid in full (*wufiyyat*) for what it did." The root *wfy* (completeness, fulfillment) — no underpayment, no overpayment. This is the Quran's precision guarantee for the recompense system.

### 13. The Two Processions — Both Driven, Different Destinations (39:71-74)

This is the structural heart of the passage. The *sīqa* (driving) verb appears for BOTH groups:

**Disbelievers** [39:71]: *sīqa* — driven to Hell in groups (*zumarā*). At arrival, gates open; keepers ask: did messengers not come? They confess yes — "but the word of punishment was justified." Enter eternally — "evil is the home of the arrogant."

**Believers** [39:73]: *sīqa* — driven to Paradise in groups. But their gates are found *already open* — a grammatical mark of pre-prepared welcome. Keepers say: *salāmun ʿalaykum ṭibtum fa-dkhulūhā khālidīn* (peace, you are pure, enter eternal).

The verb is *identical* for both: *sīqa* (herded/driven). Both groups are involuntary in their movement. The difference is not freedom vs. coercion — both are driven — but *destination and greeting*. This is the cosmic-scale version of [75:30]'s individual *masāq*: death herds one person; the Day herds everyone. [39:74] gives the believers' response: praise to God who fulfilled His promise. Human praise echoes the angelic praise closing the surah.

### 14. The Angelic Throne-Frame (39:75)

> **وَتَرَى ٱلۡمَلَٰٓئِكَةَ حَآفِّينَ مِنۡ حَوۡلِ ٱلۡعَرۡشِ يُسَبِّحُونَ بِحَمۡدِ رَبِّهِمۡ / وَقُضِيَ بَيۡنَهُم بِٱلۡحَقِّ / وَقِيلَ ٱلۡحَمۡدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلۡعَٰلَمِينَ**

Three simultaneous moves: (1) angels *ḥāffīna* (encircling) the Throne, glorifying throughout — the judgment is performed *within* ongoing praise, not between interruptions of it; (2) *wa-quḍiya baynahum bi-l-ḥaqq* repeated from [39:69] as retrospective confirmation — the decree-formula echoes; (3) *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi al-ʿālamīn* — the Fātiḥa's opening formula, the Quran's first words, now closing the judgment of history.

## PART 3 — Cross-Cutting: The Deathbed as Recurring Motif

The deathbed scene at [75:26-30] has a twin at [56:83-87]: soul reaching the throat (*ḥulqūm*), family watching helplessly, "We are nearer to him than you but you do not see" [56:85], then the challenge: "if you are not accountable (*ghayr madīnīn*), bring it back — if you are truthful" [56:86-87].

Both passages locate the scene at the throat-threshold. Both assert invisible divine nearness. Both issue an impossibility-challenge: return the departing soul, if you can. The challenge is itself the proof: the impossibility of returning the soul demonstrates divine sovereignty, which grounds the resurrection claim.

The two accounts emphasize complementary aspects: [75] → *al-masāq* (destiny — the soul is driven); [56] → the challenge (proof — the soul cannot be recalled). Note [56:86]'s *madīnīn* (accountable) comes from *dīn* — the same root as "religion" and "recompense." The deathbed challenge embeds the theology: you cannot separate the claim of religion from the claim of accountability.

---

## Negative Space

Surah 75 does not describe what happens *after* the soul arrives at its Lord following death — the deathbed scene ends with *al-masāq* (the driving) but says nothing of reception. The afterlife is positioned as consequence [75:22-25], not as the next narrated moment. Similarly, 39:67-75 never describes individual reckoning in detail — it gives the cosmic frame and the two group-processions but omits the intermediate judgment of individual souls (ḥisāb). The Quran's two most direct judgment scenes both *frame* the event without *narrating its interior mechanics*.

## Verses for Direct Study

1. **[75:14]** — *al-insānu ʿalā nafsihi baṣīra wa-law alqā maʿādhīrahu* — the self-witness formula. Cross-reference with [41:20-22] body-testimony and [17:14] self-as-reckoner. Three separate passages establish the person as their own primary witness; the structural rhyme across the three passages is load-bearing.
2. **[75:26-30]** — the dying sequence in full: collar-bones, *man rāqin*, the knowing of parting, leg-twisting, *al-masāq*. Five images as argument for divine control at the individual's eschatological threshold.
3. **[39:68]** — the two-blast verse (the only Quranic two-blast attestation). Cross-reference with [69:13] (*nafkhatun wāḥida*) for the one-blast tradition. The tension is not resolved in the text.
4. **[39:69]** — the four-step procedural judgment: light, book, witnesses, decree. The tightest compression of the judgment-process vocabulary in the corpus.
5. **[39:75]** — the angelic Throne-frame closing: praise as the environment *within* which judgment occurs. The Fātiḥa formula (*al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi al-ʿālamīn*) as the final word of history.

## Rabbit Holes

- **The *lawwāma* taxonomy across Quranic nafs-states.** The Quran names three: *ammāra* [12:53], *lawwāma* [75:2], *muṭmaʾinna* [89:27]. They appear in different surahs with no connecting passage that orders them. Classical psychology arranges them as a hierarchy; the Quran distributes them. A cross-reference of all three passages may reveal whether the Quran intends a progression or three independent characterizations.
- **The *balā* correction formula.** [75:3-4] uses *balā* (yes, certainly) to correct the human's incredulity. This formula appears elsewhere in the Quran — most notably in [7:172] (the primordial covenant: *balā shahidnā*, "yes, we testify"). Is *balā* at [75:4] a lexical echo of the covenant affirmation? The same word that answered the first divine question ("Am I not your Lord?") now answers the resurrection question.
- **The *zumar* (groups/processions) word.** The surah is named *al-Zumar* (the Groups) after [39:71, 39:73]. Why *zumar*? The word implies organized flocks or processions — voluntary groupings in the world (tribal, clan-based) become involuntary eschatological processions. Tracking *zumra* and related collective-movement terms across the corpus could map the Quran's sociology of eschatological assembly.
- **The *ghayr madīnīn* challenge at [56:86].** *Madīnīn* is from the root *dyn* (religion/debt/recompense) — literally "those who are not subjected to recompense/debt." The challenge is: if there is no *dīn* (accountability), return the soul. The word for religion and the word for accountability are the same root. The deathbed challenge embeds an etymology: you cannot separate the religious claim from the accountability claim.
- **The angelic *ḥāffīna* (surrounding) at [39:75] and at [69:17].** At [69:17] the angels surround the Throne *ḥāffīna*, and at [69:17] eight of them bear the Throne above themselves on the Day. These two images together establish the Throne-judgment scene's spatial architecture. A study pairing [39:75] with [69:17] could clarify whether the *ḥāffīna* image is a fixed formula for the Throne-encirclement or varies in different judgment scenes.
