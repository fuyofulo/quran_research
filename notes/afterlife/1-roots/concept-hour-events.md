# HOUR-EVENT Vocabulary: Seven Roots of Resurrection, Gathering, and Cosmic Collapse

## Headline Finding

Seven roots form the Quranic vocabulary of the Day's events. They describe a **staged sequence**, not a single moment: $qq cracks sky and earth open; bEv raises the dead from suspension; n$r spreads/unfurls them into readability; H$r gathers humanity before God; jmE assembles them collectively; qwm names the final standing for judgment. rkm (piling) marks the disbelievers' refusal to read the cosmic signals. The Quran encodes an **ordered protocol**: collapse → raise → spread → gather → assemble → stand. The Day of Qiyāma is named after its final verb — humanity upright before the divine tribunal.

---

## 1. qwm — Qiyāma as Standing-and-Rising

**Stats:** 660 occurrences, 22 lemmas, 79 surahs. Top lemma: *qawm* (people/tribe, 383 occ). Second lemma: *qiyāma* (70 occ). Third: *aqāma* (establish, 54 occ). The root is overwhelmingly nominal (81% N, 15% V) — but its eschatological payload sits in that second lemma.

*Qiyāma* — the verbal noun meaning "the act of standing/rising" — is the Quran's **most-used name** for the Day of Judgment. It appears in the formula *yawm al-qiyāma* approximately 70 times across 70+ ayahs. This is more than any other single eschatological day-name. The frequency is structural: the Day is not named after judgment (*ḥisāb*) or punishment (*ʿadhāb*) or even resurrection (*baʿth*) — it is named after **the posture of the resurrected**, the act of standing.

Etymology: qwm = "to stand, rise, get up." *Qiyāma* is the masdar of the Form II intransitive: the universal rising-up. The dead RISE from graves; humanity then STANDS upright before God. [78:38] captures the Day's geometry: *yawma yaqūmu al-rūḥ wa-l-malāʾika ṣaffā* — "the Day the Spirit and the angels will stand in rows." [40:51] — *yawma yaqūmu al-ashhād* — "the Day the witnesses will stand." [39:68] — *thumma nufikha fīhi ukhrā fa-idhā hum qiyāmun yanẓurūn* — "then [the horn] is blown again, and at once they are standing, watching." The qiyām posture (standing, upright, alert) is the body's condition at judgment. **The Day is named after what the resurrected do with their bodies after being raised.**

Co-occurrence with *ywm* (104 shared ayahs) confirms the formula's dominance. The cluster placement (Allegiance & the Object of Worship) reflects that qwm's everyday senses (a people/tribe; to establish prayer) pull the root toward communal-religious life, not eschatology — yet its eschatological lemma is the second-most-frequent shape. Both usages sit under the same root because establishing prayer (iqāma) and standing for judgment (qiyāma) are both acts of the upright human before God.

## 2. H$r — Gathering (Ḥashr)

**Correction first:** the task brief listed "HSr (حصر)" with 6 occurrences for ḥashara. That root is *constrain/besiege*. The gathering root is **H$r (حشر)** with 43 occurrences, 4 lemmas, 28 surahs. The Arabic is حَشَرَ.

**Stats:** 43 occ, 37 verbal. Top lemma: *ḥashara* (V, 37 — active and passive verb forms). Secondary lemma: *ḥāshirīn* (gatherers, 3 — describing Moses' summoning magicians). Nominal: *ḥashr* (the act of gathering, 2). 34 Meccan occurrences; strongly eschatological.

The root carries the sense of **driving a crowd together** — gathering livestock, assembling an army, pressing people toward a destination. In the Quran, the eschatological use dominates: God *yajmaʿu*, *yaḥshuru*, *naḥshuru* all humanity to the judgment. The direction is always toward God or toward Hell.

**[50:44]** — the keystone:

> **يَوۡمَ تَشَقَّقُ ٱلۡأَرۡضُ عَنۡهُمۡ سِرَاعًا ۚ ذَٰلِكَ حَشۡرٌ عَلَيۡنَا يَسِيرٌ**
>
> *Yawma tashaqqaqu al-arḍu ʿanhum sirāʿan. Dhālika ḥashrun ʿalaynā yasīr.*
>
> Saheeh: "On the Day the earth breaks away from them [and they emerge] rapidly; that is a gathering easy for Us."
> Khattab: "˹Beware of˺ the Day the earth will split open, letting them rush forth. That will be an easy gathering for Us."

This single ayah links $qq (earth splitting) directly to ḥashr (the gathering), making the sequence explicit: the earth splits, the dead rush out, and that is the ḥashr. "Easy for Us" (*ʿalaynā yasīr*) is a divine assertion that the logistics of gathering all humanity are trivial for God.

Non-eschatological uses confirm the core sense: [27:17] Solomon's *jund* (army) of jinn, humans, and birds *ḥushira* (were gathered) before him. [7:111] — Pharaoh's advisors call for *ḥāshirīn* (gatherers) to summon magicians. The eschatological ḥashr is the cosmic version of what a general does with troops.

## 3. jmE — Assembly (*Yawm al-Jamʿ* + *Faṣl* Pairing)

**Stats:** 129 occ, 11 lemmas, 45 surahs. Top lemma: *jamīʿ* (all together, 53 — collective); *ajmaʿīn* (all of them, 26); *jamaʿa* (verb, 22). The root covers both the act of assembling and the completed state of being assembled.

**[64:9]** names the Day twice:

> **يَوۡمَ يَجۡمَعُكُمۡ لِيَوۡمِ ٱلۡجَمۡعِ**
>
> *Yawma yajmaʿukum li-yawm al-jamʿ.*
>
> "The Day He assembles you for the Day of Assembly."

The verb *yajmaʿ* and the nominal *al-jamʿ* appear in the same phrase — the Day of assembling *for* the Day of Assembly. The tautology is emphatic: the Day IS the assembly. [42:7] — *wa-tundhira yawm al-jamʿ lā rayba fīh* — "and to warn of the Day of Assembly, about which there is no doubt." Two of the Quran's four "Day-of-X" names for the eschaton use jmE.

**The faṣl pairing:** *yawm al-faṣl* (Day of Separation/Decision) appears in [77:13, 38], [78:17], [37:21], [44:40]. Crucially, [77:38] places them together: *hādhā yawm al-faṣl. jamaʿnākum wa-l-awwalīn* — "This is the Day of Decision. We assembled you and the former peoples." **Assembly (jmE) precedes separation (faṣl).** The structural logic: you cannot separate until you have first assembled. The jamʿ is the gathering of all souls into one arena; the faṣl is the subsequent sorting of righteous from wicked. The sequence is grammatically encoded in the cited ayah — perfect-tense *jamaʿnākum* (we have assembled) followed by the designation *yawm al-faṣl* (day of the separation now underway).

[45:26] adds the full life-sequence: God gives life → causes death → *yajmaʿukum* (assembles you) → *yawm al-qiyāma*. The jmE event is sandwiched between death and the Day of Standing — it is the transition from raised-dead to standing-for-judgment.

## 4. bEv — Recap and Eschatological Extension

From concept-bEv (teleportation investigation): bEv is the **release-from-suspension** verb — unifying messenger-dispatch, grave-raising, and sleep-waking under one verb because all three are the same divine operation. Key baseline:

- 67 occ, 5 lemmas. Dominant verb (52 occ). Passive participle *mabʿūthūn* (9 occ) is exclusively a disbelievers' incredulity formula.
- *Yawm al-baʿth* at [30:56 ×2]: the Day of Raising named in its nominal form.
- [22:7] — *inna Allāha yabʿathu man fī al-qubūr* — "God raises those in the graves" — the keystone.

**Eschatological extension:** [36:51] links bEv directly to the trumpet-blast sequence:

> **وَنُفِخَ فِي ٱلصُّورِ فَإِذَا هُم مِّنَ ٱلۡأَجۡدَاثِ إِلَىٰ رَبِّهِمۡ يَنسِلُونَ**
>
> *wa-nufikha fī al-ṣūr fa-idhā hum min al-ajdāth ilā rabbihim yansilūn.*
>
> Saheeh: "And the Horn will be blown; and at once from the graves to their Lord they will hasten."

[39:68] extends the sequence: first trumpet → all die; second trumpet → *fa-idhā hum qiyāmun yanẓurūn* (they are upright, watching). The bEv-event happens between the two blasts — the raising happens in that instant. The speed of the raising (*sirāʿan*, "rapidly" at [50:44]) mirrors the speed of the *baʿth*: no interval, no transition period. The suspension ends instantaneously.

The *mabʿūthūn* participle — the disbelievers' objection — marks a conceptual wall: they cannot imagine the promised-but-not-yet-executed raising. [56:47], [83:4] repeat the formula. The disbelief is grammatically locked into the passive-participle tense: suspended-promise. **The Quran's answer is structural: they are already being raised every morning.**

## 5. n$r — Recap and Nushūr as Eschatology

From concept-n$r (teleportation investigation): n$r names the spreading-out/unfurling operation — making-what-was-compact-open. 21 occ, 10 lemmas. The root covers scroll-unfurling, mercy-spreading, cloud-spreading, and corpse-raising under one semantic frame.

**Eschatological extension:** The noun *nushūr* (5 occ) is the Quran's eschatological "resurrection-as-unfurling." [25:3] places it in a triad of absolute divine powers:

> **وَلَا يَمۡلِكُونَ مَوۡتًا وَلَا حَيَوٰةً وَلَا نُشُورًا**
>
> *wa-lā yamlikūna mawtan wa-lā ḥayātan wa-lā nushūrā.*
>
> "They have no power over death or life or *nushūr*."

Death — life — nushūr: the three fundamental ontological operations. *Nushūr* is not synonymous with life (*ḥayāt*) — it is the **act of spreading-back-into-readability** after death. A corpse is compacted, folded, unavailable. *Nushūr* makes it extended again, spread out, alive for encounter.

[35:9] closes with *kadhālika al-nushūr* — "thus is the nushūr" — after the rain-revives-dead-land argument. The agricultural parable is not metaphor but analogy: the mechanism (compact → extended by divine action) is identical for land and corpse.

The n$r contribution to the staged sequence: after bEv raises the dead from suspension, n$r describes the mode of their appearing — spread out, unfurled, like scrolls being opened for reading. The eschatological n$r is the moment of becoming-available-for-encounter with the divine.

## 6. $qq — Cosmic-Splitting Vocabulary

**Stats:** 28 occ, 9 lemmas, 21 surahs. Split between Meccan and Medinan (14/14). **The most morphologically diverse root in this cluster.** Top lemmas: *shiqāq* (dissension/opposition, 7 — the everyday interpersonal sense), *shāqqa* (to oppose, 7), *inshaqqat* (Form VII infix-n "was split," 5 — ALL eschatological). Form VII is the morphological marker for the cosmic-collapse sense.

The root has two distinct semantic lives: (a) *shiqāq*/opposition/breach between persons (22/28 uses); (b) physical splitting of sky/earth (6/28 uses — all Form VII *inshaqqat* or *tashaqqa*). These are not unrelated: a *shaqq* is a crack, a rift. The sky that rifts and the people who split apart from God share one verbal image.

**Eschatological-split instances:**

- [84:1] — *idhā al-samāʾu inshaqqat* — "when the sky splits" — opening verse of Surah al-Inshiqāq, the paradigmatic sky-split formula.
- [55:37] — *fa-idhā inshaqqati al-samāʾu fa-kānat wardatan ka-l-dihān* — "when the sky splits, becoming rose-red like [melted] oil."
- [69:16] — *wa-inshaqqati al-samāʾu fa-hiya yawmaʾidhin wāhiya* — "the sky splits, for that Day it is frail."
- [25:25] — *wa-yawma tashaqqqaqu al-samāʾu bi-l-ghamām* — "the Day the sky splits with clouds, and the angels are sent down."
- [50:44] — *yawma tashaqqaqu al-arḍu ʿanhum sirāʿan* — the earth splits *from them*, disgorging the dead.

Note [19:90] — the cosmic near-split: *takādu al-samāwātu yatafaṭṭarna minhu wa-tanshaqqu al-arḍu wa-takhirru al-jibālu haddā* — the heavens nearly burst, the earth *tanshaqqat* (Form VII passive), mountains crash. This is a conditional cosmic response to blasphemy, not the eschaton itself — but uses identical vocabulary.

**Structural finding:** The Form VII *inshaqqat* marks a process-happening-to-a-subject: the sky is not cut by an external agent — it splits as an intransitive event. The cosmic collapse is **self-executing**, a consequence of the Day's arrival, not a prior divine action. The sky ruptures because the Day requires it.

[54:1] — *inshaqqa al-qamar* — the moon's splitting is pre-eschatological (a sign/miracle), using the same Form VII. This verse is important: the same verb used for the eschatological sky-split also names a historical miracle. **The cosmic-collapse and the prophetic-sign share one vocabulary.** The moon-split is the eschaton's prefiguration in compressed form.

## 7. rkm — Piled Clouds and the Denial Pattern

**Stats:** 3 occ, 3 lemmas, 3 surahs. All three uses are distinct in morphological shape — a tiny root with zero repetition.

- [8:37] — *fa-yarkumahu jamīʿan* — "He piles them all together [and puts them in Hell]." The verb names God's piling-up of the wicked into Hell. Co-occurrence with *jmE* (jamīʿan) is precise: gathering (*jmE*) + piling (*rkm*) describe the condemned's fate.
- [24:43] — *thumma yajʿaluhu rukāman* — "then He makes [the clouds] into a *rukām* (piled mass)." Pure meteorological description. God drives clouds, joins them, piles them into a mass. The *rukām*-cloud is a natural phenomenon, a *sign* (*āya*) of divine power. The image: layered, heaped, architecturally dense.
- [52:44] — the denial pattern:

> **وَإِن يَرَوۡاْ كِسۡفًا مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ سَاقِطًا يَقُولُواْ سَحَابٌ مَّرۡكُومٌ**
>
> *wa-in yarawū kisf'an min al-samāʾ sāqiṭan yaqūlū saḥābun markūm.*
>
> Saheeh: "And if they were to see a fragment from the sky falling, they would say, '[It is merely] clouds heaped up.'"

This is the **rkm denial pattern**: the disbelievers see a *kisfan min al-samāʾ* (a fragment/piece falling from the sky) — which in context is the eschatological collapse of the sky — and re-read it as *saḥābun markūm* (merely piled-up clouds). The natural phenomenon from [24:43] becomes their interpretive escape from the cosmic sign. **They possess the vocabulary of the real event (*markūm* = piled) but apply it as a naturalistic explanation, refusing the eschatological reading.** The root's theological function is to mark the disbelievers' cognitive refusal: they see the sky falling and call it weather.

Note: 77:33 (in the task brief) is NOT an rkm verse — it uses *jimalāt* (camels) for sparks. The three rkm verses are [8:37], [24:43], [52:44].

## 8. The Staged Chronology of the Day

The seven roots encode a **sequential protocol**, not simultaneous events. Reading across the key verses:

**Stage 1 — Cosmic collapse ($qq):** The sky *inshaqqat*, cracks open, becomes frail [84:1, 69:16, 55:37]. The earth *tashaqqaqu* from below the dead [50:44]. The physical structure of the world tears.

**Stage 2 — The Trumpet (context for bEv):** The horn is blown [36:51, 39:68, 27:87]. At the first blast, all die; at the second, all rise. The *baʿth* event is instantaneous — [39:68]: *fa-idhā hum qiyāmun yanẓurūn* (they are standing, watching).

**Stage 3 — The dead are raised (bEv) and spread out (n$r):** bEv: the dead rush from the graves (*ajdāth*) [36:51]. n$r: they emerge spread out, like *locusts spreading* [54:7], like scrolls unfurled. The *nushūr* is the state of the newly-raised — extended, available, present.

**Stage 4 — Gathering (H$r):** All humanity is *yuhshar* — gathered, driven together. [50:44] names this the *ḥashr* and asserts its ease for God. The gathering is directional: toward God, toward the assembly-ground.

**Stage 5 — Assembly (jmE):** The *jamʿ* is the completed state: all present together. [77:38] — *jamaʿnākum wa-l-awwalīn* (We assembled you and the former peoples). All generations, simultaneously present.

**Stage 6 — Standing for judgment (qwm):** *Qiyāma* — the posture of the assembled. [78:38]: *yawma yaqūmu al-rūḥ wa-l-malāʾika ṣaffā* (the Spirit and angels stand in rows). Humans stand upright for the *ḥisāb*. The Day is named for this final posture.

**rkm as counter-sequence:** The disbelievers, seeing Stage 1 beginning (sky-collapse), deploy rkm-vocabulary to deny it. Their misreading interrupts the sequence for them alone — they refuse to enter the protocol.

## 9. Negative Space

The Quran's HOUR-vocabulary is rich in verbs of *entering* the Day's events (split, raise, gather, assemble, stand) but has **no vocabulary for the subjective duration** of the Day itself. The 50,000-year figure [70:4] applies to divine timing; the assembled have no experience of that span. The gap between stages is structurally unaddressed — the sequence is given, but no inter-stage transition-time is named.

## 10. Verses for Direct Study

1. **[50:44]** — *tashaqqaqu al-arḍ + ḥashr yasīr*. Earth-split + gathering in one ayah. The clearest sequential pairing in the corpus.
2. **[36:51–52]** — *nufikha fī al-ṣūr + yansilūn + man baʿathanā min marqadinā*. Trumpet → rushing from graves → resurrection-cry. The bEv event in narrative.
3. **[77:38]** — *yawm al-faṣl. jamaʿnākum wa-l-awwalīn.* jmE + faṣl in one verse.
4. **[52:44]** — *kisf'an sāqiṭan + saḥābun markūm.* The rkm denial pattern at its sharpest.
5. **[84:1]** — *idhā al-samāʾu inshaqqat.* The $qq formula at its most compressed — five words opening a surah with the Day's first event.

## 11. Rabbit Holes

- **The trumpet (*ṣūr*) as inter-stage trigger.** [36:51], [39:68], [27:87], [20:102] all use the same formula: *nufikha fī al-ṣūr*. The trumpet links Stages 2–4. What root does *ṣūr* come from, and does its semantics bear on the transition-event concept?
- **$qq opposition-sense vs. cosmic-sense: are they theologically linked?** The Quran uses *shāqqa* (to oppose God/Messenger) 7 times alongside cosmic *inshaqqat*. Hypothesis: the people who "split" from God in opposition are eschatologically mirrored by the sky that splits — same root, inverted subject.
- **H$r + jmE co-occurrence map.** They appear together in [18:99] (*fa-jamaʿnāhum jamʿan*) after the trumpet, and jmE co-occurs with H$r in 5 ayahs by root-reference. Map all co-occurrences: do the verbs always appear in the same order (H$r then jmE, or synonymous)?
- **The *kisf* vocabulary in [52:44].** *Kisf* = a fragment/piece of the sky. This term appears also at [17:92] and [30:48]. Is it an eschatological technical term, or general "piece of something"? The 52:44 usage implies the sky literally breaking apart in fragments — which maps precisely onto $qq.
- **Qiyāma vs. Baʿth as Day-names: distribution by surah type.** *Yawm al-qiyāma* (70 occ) appears in both Meccan and Medinan surahs. *Yawm al-baʿth* (2 occ) appears only at [30:56]. Does *qiyāma* concentrate in certain rhetorical contexts (warning, legal), while *baʿth* concentrates in cosmological arguments? The name chosen may signal the rhetorical register.
