# The Judgment-Process Vocabulary — Six Roots

> Six roots that map the stages of divine judgment on the Day: **Hsb** (reckoning, 109 occ), **jzy** (recompense, 118 occ), **Hkm** (judgment/wisdom, 210 occ), **qDy** (decree-execution, 63 occ), **gfr** (forgiveness, 234 occ), **fSl** (separation/decision, 43 occ). Together they constitute the Quran's procedural grammar of the eschaton.

---

## 1. Headline Finding

The Quran's Day of Judgment is not a single event but a **staged, dual-track process**. The reckoning-track (Hsb → Hkm → jzy) runs alongside the mercy-track (gfr), and both conclude in fSl — the final separation of humanity into camps. The decisive structural finding: **gfr (234 occ) is the largest root of the six**, outnumbering even Hkm (210). Forgiveness is not the exception to judgment; it is its dominant possibility. qDy seals everything: when the decree is executed, it is described in the past tense even for future events — the Quran treats the final decree as already accomplished.

## 2. Hsb — Eschatological Reckoning (109 occ)

**Lemma table:** *Hasiba* (حَسِبَ, "to think/reckon," verb) 44; *HisaAb* (حِسَاب, "reckoning/account," noun) 39; *Hasob* (حَسْب, "sufficient/enough") 11; *Hasiyb* (حَسِيب, "reckoner," divine attribute) 4; *HusobaAn* (حُسْبَان, "reckoning/calculation") 3; *yaHotasibu* (يَحْتَسِبُ, "to expect/count upon") 3.

The noun *ḥisāb* (39 occ) is the eschatological workhorse. "Yawm al-ḥisāb" appears at [38:16, 38:26, 40:27] and *sarīʿ al-ḥisāb* ("swift in reckoning") appears at [2:202, 3:19, 13:41, 14:51, 24:39, 40:17] — this swiftness formula is the single most-repeated predicate attached to the reckoning.

The reckoning is **radically individual**. At [17:14]: *iqraʾ kitābaka kafā bi-nafsika al-yawma ʿalayka ḥasībā* — "Read your record; sufficient is yourself this Day as your own reckoner." Each soul accounts for itself, from its own book. This collapses the distance between judge and judged: you are both. The *Hasiyb* (divine reckoner) attribute at [4:6, 4:86, 17:14, 33:39] reinforces that God is the ultimate reckoner, but [17:14] places the person as *ḥasīban* — their own reckoning witness.

Cross-reference to `notes/time/3-rabbit-holes/research-year-and-scale.md`: *ḥisāb* functions in [10:5, 17:12] as the bridge between cycle-counting (months) and linear time, the only Quranic concept that legitimately spans counting-modes. In the eschaton, that accounting function becomes personal audit: [78:27] "they did not expect a *ḥisāb*" — those who failed are precisely those who forgot the accounting was coming.

At [84:7–8] the right-hand book-recipients are promised *ḥisāban yasīran* — an "easy reckoning." This implies that ḥisāb is not uniformly severe; its weight varies. The easy-reckoning promise is a mercy-inflection within the reckoning-track.

## 3. jzy — Recompense as Reciprocity (118 occ)

**Lemma table:** *jazaY* (verb "to recompense") 73; *jazāʾ* (noun "recompense") 42; *jizyah* (tribute, distinct legal sense) 1; *jāz* (pass-participle) 1; *nujāzī* (causative, "We recompense") 1.

**jzy is the reciprocity root, not the punishment root.** The structural formula is that what you did comes back to you in kind — for good and for ill. The clearest text is [36:54]: *lā tujzawna illā mā kuntum taʿmalūn* — "you are recompensed only for what you used to do." Zero more, zero less. At [40:17]: *al-yawma tujzā kullu nafsin bi-mā kasabat* — "Today every soul is recompensed for what it earned."

[42:40] gives the **evil-side reciprocity**: "The recompense of an evil act is an evil act like it (sīʾah mithluhā)." But the verse immediately pivots — whoever pardons and reconciles, their reward (*ajr*) is with God. The word shifts from *jazāʾ* to *ajr* when mercy enters. This is not accidental: *jazāʾ* is strict reciprocity; *ajr* is grace-reward that may exceed it.

[78:26] delivers the verdict formula with the highest divergence score of any verse in this set (combined 0.865): **جَزَآءً وِفَاقًا** — *jazāʾan wifāqan*. "A fitting / corresponding recompense." Translators diverge maximally on *wifāq* (fitting? matching? commensurate? appropriate?). The Arabic is precise: *wifāq* from the root wfq (harmony, accord) — the recompense *harmonizes* with the deed. It is not mechanical equivalence but proportional accord.

Co-root data: jzy co-occurs most with *Eml* (action/work) — the action-recompense pair is the structural spine of jzy. Significant co-occurrence with *Hsn* (good) and *kfr* (disbelief) — the reciprocity moves in both directions.

## 4. Hkm — Judgment, Disambiguating from Wisdom (210 occ)

**Lemma table:** *Ḥakīm* (حَكِيم, "Wise" — divine attribute) **97**; *Ḥakama* (verb "to judge/rule") 45; *Ḥukm* (noun "judgment/ruling") 30; *Ḥikma* (noun "wisdom") 20; *Ḥākimīn* (plural "judges") 5; *Ḥakam* (noun "judge/arbiter") 3.

The disambiguation is data-grounded: **Ḥakīm (97) and Ḥikma (20) together (117) outnumber the judgment-sense lemmas Ḥakama + Ḥukm + Ḥākimīn + Ḥakam (83)**. The root leans toward wisdom in raw frequency. But in eschatological contexts the judgment-sense dominates.

The divine title cluster: *al-Ḥakam* (the Arbiter/Judge, [6:114, 13:41]), *Aḥkam al-ḥākimīn* (the most just of judges, [7:87, 10:109, 11:45, 12:80, 95:8]), and — critically — *ḥukmuhu* (His judgment) at [6:62] paired with *asraʿ al-ḥāsibīn*: "His is the ḥukm and He is the swiftest of reckoners." This collapses Hkm (judgment) and Hsb (reckoning) into a single divine act.

The eschatological formula: *Allāhu yaḥkumu baynahum* ("God will judge between them") at [2:113, 22:17, 32:25, 60:3] — always with *bayna* (between). Hkm in the Day context is specifically **arbitration between communities**, not individual audit. The individual audit is Hsb; Hkm is the inter-community verdict.

[38:26] links Hkm to the prophetic commission: David is told to judge between people with truth (*iḥkum bayna al-nāsi bi-l-ḥaqq*) and warned not to follow desire, lest he stray from God's path — on pain of yawm al-ḥisāb (Day of Reckoning). Earthly judgment-by-truth is a preview of divine judgment.

The *Ḥakīm* (wise) sense and the *Ḥukm* (judgment) sense are **theologically unified**: God's wisdom is precisely what makes His judgment reliable. Translators flag this — 14:22 has combined divergence 0.619 but the Ḥkm intersection at [6:62] scores 0.623.

## 5. qDy — Decree-Execution at the Eschaton (63 occ)

**Lemma table:** *qaDā* (verb "to decree/execute") 59 — overwhelmingly dominant; *maqḍīy* (adjective "decreed/accomplished") 2; *qāḍ* (active participle "judge") 1; *qāḍiya* (noun "doom") 1.

The eschatological sense of qDy is **finality, not predetermination**. The same root covers divine predetermination (God's pre-decree of creation) AND the final execution of judgment — but in eschatological contexts qDy consistently marks the moment of irrevocable closure.

The critical formula is **وَقُضِيَ الأَمْرُ** — *wa-quḍiya al-amr* ("and the matter was decreed/executed"). It appears in narrative past-tense in [11:44] (after Noah's flood: "the earth swallowed its water, and the matter was decreed") and in [14:22] (Satan's speech after the judgment is concluded: "the matter has been decreed, and I had no authority over you"). These narrative uses prefigure the eschatological moment. The passive voice (*quḍiya* — it was decreed) removes the agent: the decree enacts itself.

At [39:69]: *wa-quḍiya baynahum bi-l-ḥaqq* — "and it was judged between them with truth." The full judgment scene: earth lit by God's light, the Book placed, prophets and witnesses brought, and *then* qDy executes. This verse maps qDy as the **final step after witnesses and records**, not the first step.

[40:20]: *wa-Allāhu yaqḍī bi-l-ḥaqq* — "God decrees/judges with truth." The idols decreed by polytheists judge nothing; God alone decrees with reality. *Bi-l-ḥaqq* (with truth/reality) is qDy's constant eschatological modifier — the decree is not arbitrary but truth-grounded.

The strong co-occurrence of qDy with *Amr* (command/matter) confirms: qDy executes *the matter*, the thing that has been building. It is the verb of closure.

## 6. gfr — Forgiveness as the Mercy-Track (234 occ)

**Lemma table:** *Ghafūr* (غَفُور, "Most Forgiving" — divine attribute) **91**; *Ghafara* (verb "to forgive") 65; *Istaghfara* (verb "to seek forgiveness") 40; *Maghfira* (noun "forgiveness") 28; *Ghaffār* (intensified "Ever-Forgiving") 5; *Ghāfir* (active participle "Forgiver") 2.

gfr is the largest root of the six (234 occ). The divine name *Ghafūr* alone (91) exceeds the total occurrences of fSl (43). The **mercy-axis** structurally outweighs any single component of the justice-axis.

**The shirk exception** [4:48]: "God does not forgive *an yushrika bihi* (that He be given partners) but forgives what is less than that for whom He wills." This is the single hard limit on gfr — the only thing that cannot be forgiven. Everything else falls under *li-man yashāʾ* (for whom He wills). The exception clause's phrasing is precise: it is associating partners with God that cannot be forgiven, not any particular sin-category.

**[7:156]** — the mercy-scope verse: *raḥmatī wasiʿat kulla shayʾ* — "My mercy has encompassed everything." This is Moses' prayer, answered by God directly. *Kulla shayʾ* (every thing) is the broadest scope predicate in the Quran. gfr's co-root with *rHm* (mercy) confirms these always operate together.

The **Istaghfara cluster** (40 occ): seeking forgiveness is a pre-eschatological act — humans ask for forgiveness in this life, and God grants it. At the eschaton, the seeking is over; only the granting or withholding remains. This temporal structure means gfr operates on two timescales: active seeking (this life) and final granting (Day of Judgment).

The co-root *twb* (repentance) alongside gfr confirms the sequence: tawba (turning back) enables istighfār (seeking forgiveness), which yields maghfira (forgiveness). This is the mercy-track in three stages.

## 7. fSl — Separation into Camps (43 occ)

**Lemma table:** *Faṣṣala* (verb "to clarify/detail" — Quran describing itself) 17; *Faṣl* (noun "decision/separation") 9; *Faṣala* (simple verb "to separate/depart") 5; *Tafṣīl* (noun "detailed explanation") 5; *Fiṣāl* (noun "weaning/separation") 3.

**The critical disambiguation**: *Faṣṣala* (17 occ, intensive Form II) means "to detail/expound" — this is the Quran describing its own clarification of signs. This sense has nothing to do with eschatology. The eschatological *faṣl* is from *Faṣl* (noun, 9 occ) and *Faṣala* (simple verb, 5 occ).

"**Yawm al-faṣl**" — the Day of Decision/Separation — appears at [37:21, 44:40, 77:13–14, 77:38, 78:17]. At [37:21]: *hādhā yawm al-faṣl alladhī kuntum bihi tukadhibūn* — "This is the Day of Decision that you used to deny." At [77:13]: *li-yawmi al-faṣl* — "for the Day of Decision." At [78:17]: *inna yawm al-faṣl kāna mīqātan* — "Indeed the Day of Decision is an appointed time."

The name "Day of Decision" points to **resolution of all ambiguity**. During worldly life, communities disputed, individuals hedged, believers and disbelievers coexisted mixed. The faṣl cuts through: [22:17] *yaḥkumu baynahum yawm al-qiyāma* (God judges between them), and fSl names the result — separated camps.

At [86:13]: *innahu la-qawlun faṣl* — "it [the Quran] is a decisive/separating word." fSl's meaning extends to the Quran as separator of truth from falsehood. The Day of Faṣl is the eschatological completion of what the Quran began — separating those who received the decisive word and those who rejected it.

Co-root with *qwm* (standing up, resurrection) and *ywm* (day) confirms fSl's structural role: it names the Day, and the name implies its function.

## 8. The Judgment Machinery — Staged Dual-Track Process

The six roots fit into a sequence. Gathering roots (Hsr/jmE, from prior investigations) bring all souls; then:

| Stage | Root | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Gathering | (Hsr/jmE) | Everyone assembled |
| 2. Standing | (qwm) | Each stands before God |
| 3. Record | (ktb) | Books produced per soul |
| 4. **Individual audit** | **Hsb** | Each soul recounts its account |
| 5. **Weighing** | (wzn/mīzān) | Scales balance deeds |
| 6. **Verdict** | **Hkm** | Judgment rendered between communities |
| 7a. **Recompense** | **jzy** | Reciprocal return — good for good, bad for bad |
| 7b. **Forgiveness** | **gfr** | Mercy overrides recompense for whom God wills |
| 8. **Decree executed** | **qDy** | *Wa-quḍiya al-amr* — closure sealed |
| 9. **Separation** | **fSl** | Groups divided — the camps fixed |

Tracks 7a and 7b run **in parallel, not sequence**. [40:17] places *tujzā* (recompensed) and *sarīʿ al-ḥisāb* in the same verse without mentioning forgiveness; [4:48] places gfr alongside the shirk-exception — both are active on the Day simultaneously. The grace-vs-justice axis is not sequential but **dual**: both operate, and gfr's larger frequency (234 vs jzy's 118) suggests forgiveness is statistically the more-often-invoked outcome.

The data-grounded claim: **the Quran encodes the Day as a process with distinguishable steps, not a single instantaneous verdict**. The *sarīʿ al-ḥisāb* (swift reckoning) formula does not compress the stages but emphasizes that God's processing speed is unlimited — swiftness is a divine attribute, not a collapse of procedure into instant.

---

## 9. Negative Space

The judgment-process vocabulary has **no "acquittal" root** — there is forgiveness (gfr) and recompense (jzy), but no root meaning "the charges were mistaken" or "the record was wrong." The audit is always accurate; the only question is whether mercy overrides the accurate verdict, not whether the verdict itself is in error. The Quran treats perfect divine knowledge as the presupposition of all judgment.

## 10. Verses for Direct Study

1. **[17:14]** — *iqraʾ kitābaka kafā bi-nafsika al-yawma ʿalayka ḥasībā* — the individual-as-own-reckoner formula. Arabic + translations essential; divergence score 0.697.
2. **[40:17]** — *al-yawma tujzā kullu nafsin bi-mā kasabat, lā ẓulm al-yawm, inna Allāha sarīʿ al-ḥisāb* — recompense + no injustice + swift reckoning in one verse. Load-bearing nexus of jzy and Hsb.
3. **[4:48]** — *inna Allāha lā yaghfiru an yushraka bihi wa-yaghfiru mā dūna dhālika li-man yashāʾ* — the single gfr-limit (shirk); divergence 0.703. The *li-man yashāʾ* caveat is load-bearing — forgiveness of everything else is conditional on divine will.
4. **[78:26]** — *jazāʾan wifāqan* — highest divergence score of the set (0.865). The *wifāq* concept (harmonic fitting) is the Quran's precision word for proportional recompense. Requires philological study.
5. **[39:69]** — the full judgment scene: earth illumined, book placed, prophets and witnesses assembled, *quḍiya baynahum bi-l-ḥaqq* — all six root-functions are visible in surrounding verses. Map the sequence here.

## 11. Rabbit Holes

- **The "easy reckoning" threshold** [84:7–8]: *ḥisāban yasīran* is promised to right-hand-book recipients. What determines easy vs. hard reckoning? Cross-reference to the book-giving gesture (right/left/behind-back) and its structural logic.
- **fSl's two registers**: The Quran describing itself as *qawlun faṣl* [86:13] and the Day as *yawm al-faṣl* [78:17] — is there a deliberate mirror? The Quran separates truth from falsehood now; the Day executes that separation eschatologically.
- **gfr + *li-man yashāʾ*** — the "for whom He wills" caveat appears repeatedly in gfr contexts. What conditions or signals divine will toward forgiveness? The co-root *SlH* (righteousness/rectification) and *twb* (repentance) alongside gfr suggest a non-arbitrary pattern worth mapping.
- **jzy vs. ajr disambiguation**: When does the Quran shift from *jazāʾ* (reciprocal recompense) to *ajr* (reward)? [42:40] makes the shift explicit — mercy moves the vocabulary from *jazāʾ* to *ajr*. Map all verses where both appear in proximity.
- **qDy's narrative past-tense for future events**: *wa-quḍiya al-amr* at [11:44, 14:22] uses completed past for a still-future final decree. This is the Quran's prophetic perfect — the decree is treated as accomplished because divine certainty makes its future execution epistemically equivalent to past fact. Cross-reference to *kāna* (was) used of future events in eschatological contexts.
