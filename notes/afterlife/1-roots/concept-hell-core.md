# Hell Core Vocabulary — 7 Roots + 2 Proper Nouns

## Headline Finding

The Quran fields **eight distinct hell-names** (jahannam, al-nār, al-jaḥīm, al-saʿīr, laẓā, al-hāwiya, al-ḥuṭama, saqar) but does not enumerate hell's levels — that is Islamic tradition's inference, not Quranic text. Each name is rhetorically selected: short surahs use the visceral, hapax-dense names (al-ḥuṭama, laẓā, al-hāwiya, saqar); longer credal passages use jahannam or al-nār. The same root (nwr) produces nār (fire) and nūr (light) — but in Quranic cosmology they are not opposites by accident: God is described as the nūr of the heavens; the damned enter nār. Root and anti-root from the same three consonants.

## 1. nwr — the Fire vs the Light (Disambiguation)

**Root:** نور — **occurrences:** 194 — **lemmas:** 3

| Lemma | Arabic | Count | Sense |
|-------|--------|-------|-------|
| naAr | نَار | 145 | Fire (hell, physical fire) |
| nuwr | نُور | 43 | Light (divine, guidance) |
| m~uniyr | مُنِير | 6 | Illuminating (adj., positive) |

**The same three consonants, two opposite registers:**

- **al-nār** (the Fire) is the most-used hell-term after jahannam. It is introduced as eschatological at [2:24]: *"Fear the Fire whose fuel is people and stones, prepared for the disbelievers."* It appears 145 times; the majority are eschatological, though a minority describe ordinary fire (Moses's bush, the parable of a man who kindles fire at [2:17]).

- **al-nūr** (the Light) names a divine attribute and a guidance-metaphor. The paradigm verse:

> **[24:35]** *اللَّهُ نُورُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ* — "Allah is the Light of the heavens and the earth." The verse describes His light as a lamp in a niche, glass like a pearly star, oil that almost glows without touching fire — and then "nūr ʿalā nūr" (light upon light). The word **nār** appears in the verse as contrast: *"even if untouched by fire (nār) — it would still illuminate."*

The collision is not accidental. Both nūr and nār appear in 24:35 — the divine light needs no nār (fire). The damned who reject divine nūr receive nār.

**Co-occurrence note:** nwr's top co-occurring roots are kfr (disbelief, 44 ayahs), Zlm (wrongdoing, 28 ayahs), and xld (eternal abiding, 23 ayahs) — confirming al-nār is predominantly eschatological-punitive.

**Key eschatological nār instances:** [2:24], [2:201], [3:103], [9:35], [66:6].

## 2. E*b — Three Registers of ʿaḏāb

**Root:** عذب — **occurrences:** 373 — **lemmas:** 5

| Lemma | Arabic | Count | POS |
|-------|--------|-------|-----|
| ʿadhāb | عَذَاب | 322 | N |
| ʿadhdhaba | عَذَّبَ | 41 | V |
| muʿadhdhibūn | مُعَذِّب | 4 | N |
| muʿadhdhabīn | مُعَذَّبِين | 4 | N |
| ʿadhb | عَذْب | 2 | N (fresh/sweet water — unrelated sense) |

ʿadhāb operates in three registers. The third (fresh water) belongs to ʿadhb as adjective/noun for sweet water — 2 occurrences only. The 322-count main noun divides as:

**Register 1 — Worldly destruction (ʿadhāb al-dunyā pattern).** Nations of ʿĀd, Thamūd, Lūṭ, Pharaoh receive ʿadhāb in this life — storm, earthquake, drowning. These appear mostly in the historical narrative surahs (Hūd, al-Aʿrāf, al-Qamar). Cross-reference: *notes/failure/1-narratives/narrative-destroyed-peoples.md*.

**Register 2 — Eschatological punishment.** Overwhelming majority. Key qualifiers:
- **ʿadhāb ʿaẓīm** (great punishment): 15+ instances, first at [2:7] for those with sealed hearts
- **ʿadhāb al-ḥarīq** (punishment of the Burning Fire): 4 instances [3:181, 8:50, 22:9, 22:22] — specifically paired with *dhūqū* ("taste!")
- **ʿadhāb muqīm** (abiding/lasting punishment): [39:40] "a humiliating punishment, and abiding punishment settles upon him"
- **ʿadhāb shadīd** (severe): 21+ instances across credal passages
- **al-ʿadhāb al-akbar** (the greatest punishment): [88:24] — the supreme eschatological register

**Register 3 — Intermediate (ʿadhāb al-qabr).** Punishment of the grave is largely a hadith concept; Quranic support is indirect. [40:46] — "The Fire, they are exposed to it morning and evening" — is read by some exegetes as grave-punishment (barzakh stage), but the text does not specify the grave explicitly.

**Distribution:** 232 Meccan / 141 Medinan — ʿadhāb language is more concentrated in early Meccan warning rhetoric.

## 3. jahannam — the Most-Used Hell-Name

**Type:** Proper noun — **occurrences in quran.json:** 77 — **surah distribution:** 38 surahs

jahannam is the **generic, credal hell-name** — the word a Muslim uses when referring to hell in abstract discourse. Etymology debated: probable derivation from Hebrew *gehinnom* (the Valley of Hinnom, a trash-burning site outside Jerusalem); the Quran uses it as a fully naturalized proper noun. It takes no definite article (it is already definite as a proper name).

**Key structural facts:**
- 77 occurrences across 38 surahs — most widely distributed hell-name
- Surah 9 (al-Tawba) has the highest single-surah density (8 occurrences)
- jahannam has **seven gates** [15:44]: *"It has seven gates; for every gate is of them a portion designated"* — the only verse specifying hell's gate count
- The disbelievers are **driven to it in groups** (*zumaran*) [39:71], its gates open on arrival, its keepers interrogate them

**The wārid verse — universal passing:**

> **[19:71]** *وَإِن مِّنكُمۡ إِلَّا وَارِدُهَا* — "And there is none of you except he will come to it." Both Saheeh and Khattab confirm: "There is none of you who will not pass over it." 19:72 immediately follows: "Then We will save those who feared Allah and leave the wrongdoers therein, on their knees." The passage implies all humans cross/approach the Fire; the righteous are saved, the wrongdoers remain.

**The fuel verse:**

> **[21:98]** *إِنَّكُمۡ وَمَا تَعۡبُدُونَ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ حَصَبُ جَهَنَّمَ* — "Indeed, you and what you worship other than Allah are the firewood of Hell." The idols themselves are fuel — a rhetorical collapse of the idol and the idolater into one combustible unit.

**Total named uses:** ~272 across all names (nār dominates in raw count).

**Critical clarification on lZy root:** The concept file for lZy records only the verb *talaẓẓā* at [92:14] ("a Fire which is blazing"). The actual hell-name **laẓā** at [70:15] is classified in QAC morphology as a **proper noun (PN) with root=null** — a name, not a derived form. The root *lZy* gives the verb "to blaze"; the name *laẓā* is fixed.

Islamic tradition assigns **seven levels** to jahannam (one per gate), mapping each name to a level. This is not Quranic; the Quran uses different names in different surahs but does not arrange them hierarchically in the text.

## 5. Hell-Name to Surah-Context: Rhetorical Selection

The hypothesis holds with strong data support:

**jahannam** = the GENERIC/CREDAL hell-name. Used in long credal surahs (al-Baqara, Āl ʿImrān, al-Tawba, al-Isrāʾ, al-Kahf) and in juridical contexts (threats against specific groups — hypocrites, 8 uses in surah 9). It names the institution.

**al-nār** = the SHORT-FORM WARNING name. Appears in formulas like *"fear the Fire"*, *"the people of the Fire"*, *"entering the Fire"*. Works across all lengths of surah because it is a common noun, not a proper name.

**al-jaḥīm** = the ESCHATOLOGICAL VISION name. Dense in surah 37 (al-Ṣāffāt, 6 uses) — which is saturated with vivid eschatological scenes of the guilty confronting their fate. *"They will be guided to the path of al-jaḥīm"* [37:23]; *"he saw him in the midst of al-jaḥīm"* [37:55]; *"the tree issues from the root of al-jaḥīm"* [37:64]. Jaḥīm conveys intensity of heat — the root means to kindle intensely.

**al-saʿīr** = the BLAZING-VISUAL name. Dense in surah 67 (al-Mulk, 3 uses), which is a short eschatological surah. Also appears at [81:12] as *suʿʿirat* (the verb form: *"When Hellfire is set ablaze"*) — eschatological Day-scene. al-saʿīr carries the image of a flame fanned to full blaze.

**laẓā** = the SCORCHING HAPAX at [70:15-16]. Surah 70 (al-Maʿārij) describes the miser hoarding wealth, and laẓā is presented with visceral physical detail: *"nazzāʿatan li-l-shawā"* (70:16 — "a remover/stripper of exteriors" — i.e., it scorches off the skin). One name, two verses of physical description.

**al-hāwiya** = the FALLING/ABYSS name at [101:9-11]. Surah 101 (al-Qāriʿa — the Striking Hour) describes cosmic calamity and the weighing of deeds. Those whose scales are light: *"His refuge will be an abyss"* (101:9). The surah immediately glosses: [101:11] — *"It is a Fire, intensely hot."* The hāwiya name images the abyss into which light-scaled souls fall.

**al-ḥuṭama** = the CRUSHING name at [104:4-9]. Surah 104 (al-Humazah — the Backbiter) targets the wealth-hoarder and slanderer. The name ḥuṭama derives from the root HTm meaning to smash/crush — also used of crops reduced to chaff [39:21, 56:65, 57:20]. The name fits: the slanderer who crushes people's reputations is himself crushed.

**saqar** = the PARCHING/THIRSTING name at [74:26-27, 42]. Surah 74 (al-Muddaththir) contextualizes it with: *"It lets nothing remain and leaves nothing"* [74:28], *"blackening the skins"* [74:29], and four reasons the damned give for being there (no prayer, no feeding the poor, idle discourse, denial of Judgment Day) [74:43-46]. Etymology debated; root unclear; QAC treats it as a proper noun.

## 6. The ḥuṭama Passage — Surah 104 Deep Read

| Verse | Arabic | Translation |
|-------|--------|-------------|
| 104:4 | كَلَّاۖ لَيُنۢبَذَنَّ فِي ٱلْحُطَمَةِ | No! He will surely be thrown into the Crusher. |
| 104:5 | وَمَآ أَدۡرَىٰكَ مَا ٱلۡحُطَمَةُ | And what can make you know what is the Crusher? |
| 104:6 | نَارُ ٱللَّهِ ٱلۡمُوقَدَةُ | It is the fire of Allah, [eternally] fueled, |
| 104:7 | ٱلَّتِي تَطَّلِعُ عَلَى ٱلۡأَفۡـِٔدَةِ | Which mounts directed at the hearts. |
| 104:8-9 | إِنَّهَا عَلَيۡهِم مُّؤۡصَدَةٌ فِي عَمَدٍ مُّمَدَّدَةٍ | Indeed it will be closed down upon them in extended columns. |

**The fire reaches the hearts** — this is the structurally decisive detail. Quranic theology consistently locates the locus of sin in the *qalb* (heart) and the *fuʾād* (deeper heart/mind — the word used here). Wealth-hoarding and slander are sins of the heart (arrogance, contempt, love of accumulation). The ḥuṭama fire does not merely burn the body — it penetrates to the **organ of sin**. This is a precise theological claim, not decoration.

The rhetorical structure of surah 104 is: sin (slander, accumulation, arrogance about wealth) → anti-sin (the Crusher smashes what you accumulated) → targeting the sin's organ (the fire mounts to the hearts). Each element of the punishment answers an element of the crime.

**Parallel with HTm in non-eschatological use:** the same root describes dried crops reduced to **ḥuṭām** (chaff) in [39:21], [56:65], [57:20] — worldly illustration of reduction to nothing. The ḥuṭama of hell reduces persons as rain reduces crops.

## 7. Hell's Personnel — 19 Angels and Mālik

**The 19 keepers** [74:30]: *"ʿalayhā tisʿata ʿashar"* — "Over it are nineteen." Surah 74 (al-Muddaththir) gives this number without naming the nineteen. The following verse [74:31] is remarkable: it explains that the number was made *fitna* (trial/test) for disbelievers, so that People of the Book would be confirmed and believers would increase in faith — and then asks "what does Allah intend by this as a parable?" The number is deliberately withheld as a theological trial.

**Mālik** [43:77]: the named keeper of hell. The damned call out: *"O Mālik, let your Lord finish us off!"* His answer: *"Indeed, you will remain."* Three words — *innakum mākhithūn* — in Saheeh. The finality is in the brevity. There is no intercession, no appeal, no timeline. Mālik is addressed as intermediary, but he does not intercede; he confirms the verdict.

**The Zabāniya** [96:18]: *"We will call the Zabāniya"* — angels of coercive enforcement. The verse occurs in the context of Abū Jahl threatening the Prophet; God's response is to invoke the Zabāniya, who are hell's enforcers. Only one occurrence of the word; plural form of a root meaning to push/thrust violently.

**The keepers (khazana)** [39:71, 40:49]: at 39:71 the keepers interrogate arriving disbelievers; at 40:49 the damned beg the keepers to petition God for respite — the keepers presumably deliver the message but cannot change the outcome.

**Hell has angels, not demons.** The keepers are all classified as angels (*malāʾika*) — 66:6 makes this explicit: *"over which are angels, harsh and severe, who do not disobey Allah in what He commands them."* Hell's administration is angelic — the obedient, not the rebellious.

## 8. Hell as a Named, Architectured Place

The Quran's hell is not metaphor or vague dread — it is a **cosmological structure with named components**:

- **Eight names** (jahannam, al-nār, al-jaḥīm, al-saʿīr, laẓā, al-hāwiya, al-ḥuṭama, saqar) functioning as different aspects or registers
- **Seven gates** [15:44] — each gate has a designated portion of the damned
- **Keepers** (khazana) — angelic personnel administering the structure [39:71, 40:49]
- **Named chief keeper** (Mālik) [43:77]
- **Coercive enforcers** (Zabāniya) [96:18]
- **Fuel** — people and stones [2:24, 66:6]
- **Food** — the tree of zaqqūm rooted in the bottom of al-jaḥīm [37:64]
- **Enclosed structure** — *muʾṣada* (sealed/closed down) in columns [104:8-9]
- **Exposure cycle** — "morning and evening" for Pharaoh's people [40:46], suggesting temporal experience within hell

This mirrors the structural description of paradise (also containing — gates, rivers, named subzones, servants, food, named trees). Both are **physical-cosmological spaces** that receive the dead and have internal architecture. The parallelism is not coincidental: the Quran's eschatological cosmology is symmetric — what is denied by the damned is mirrored as given to the saved.

---

## 9. Negative Space

The Quran **never assigns a specific person to a specific hell-name** in the text (Islamic tradition does this via exegesis). It also never enumerates hell's levels — the seven-level structure is inferred from the seven gates of [15:44] and the eight names, not stated.

## 11. Rabbit Holes

- **The wārid problem:** "None of you except he will come to it" [19:71] — does this mean all humans pass over the bridge (*ṣirāṭ*) above the Fire, or enter it? Translators diverge (Saheeh: "come to it"; Khattab: "pass over it"). High-divergence exegetical question; would require full morphological study of *wurūd*.

- **Saqar and the four causes:** [74:43-47] lists four reasons the damned give for being in saqar — no prayer, no feeding the poor, idle discourse, denial of Judgment Day. This is one of the Quran's most explicit moral taxonomies for damnation; it maps onto four dimensions of failure (ritual, economic, social, epistemic).

- **The nūr/nār anti-pair in eschatological cosmology:** If God is nūr, and the damned receive nār, is there a systematic cosmic inversion? The 57:12-15 passage describes the light (*nūr*) of believers running before them on Judgment Day and the hypocrites seeking to borrow that light but being refused — the nūr is denied and they enter darkness. Links nūr-denial → nār-entry.

- **ʿadhāb al-ḥarīq vs al-saʿīr vs al-nār:** Three fire-punishment phrases that partially overlap. ʿadhāb al-ḥarīq (the burning) appears 4 times as a distinct formula; al-saʿīr as a proper name 16 times; al-nār 145 times. What distinguishes the register of ḥarīq from saʿīr? Worth a translation-divergence pass.

- **The timing of hell:** [40:46] implies Pharaoh's people are exposed to the Fire morning and evening before the Hour — a barzakh (intermediate) punishment. If this is confirmed, the Quran acknowledges a pre-resurrection hell-exposure, not just a post-resurrection one. This would split ʿadhāb into three temporal registers, not two.
