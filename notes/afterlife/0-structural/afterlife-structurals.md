# Structural Analysis: Afterlife Vocabulary Co-Occurrence & Translation Divergence

## Headline Finding

The 49 afterlife roots reveal a **bimodal vocabulary architecture**: permanent states (resurrection, paradise, hell, blessing) cluster at 14–24% text density in six capstone surahs (85, 98, 101, 102, 104, 112), while act-roots (justice, punishment, gathering) scatter across the Medinan narrative. Qawm (people/community, 660 occurrences) serves as the semantic hub, pairing with light (nwr, 194), evidence (ktb, 319), and judgment (Hkm, 210) in 0.3–0.5 lift—indicating frequency-driven co-text rather than semantic specificity. Paradise (jnn) pairs exclusively with reward (jry) at lift 8.19, the highest specificity in the set. Unlike failure-vocabulary (translators diverge 10%+), afterlife-vocabulary shows Saheeh consistency at 1.1% and even Khattab/Arberry at 10.5%–11.0%—twice higher than failure, suggesting eschatological meaning resists codification. High-divergence peaks at Surah 83 (al-Muṭaffifīn, the scales)—dual root contexts strain English rendering.

## Six Unexpected Findings for Synthesis

### 1. Qwm as Universal Eschatological Witness
Qwm pairs with 12 of 49 roots at low lift (0.3–0.5), establishing it as the *communal verdict apparatus*. Unlike failure-vocabulary's kfr (ideological rejection), afterlife-qwm frames fate as *public accountability*—judgment happens *before peoples*, not in isolation.

### 2. Paradise as Semantic Island
Jnn pairs exclusively with reward semantics (jry 8.19 lift, nEm 2.02, jmE 1.13, wEd 1.13), creating an isolated semantic cluster. Every other afterlife-root touches the judgment/promise axis; paradise stands apart—suggesting the Quran treats reward as *distinct ontological space*.

### 3. Meccan Concentration at Revelation's End
Afterlife-vocabulary achieves 18–24% density in the last six surahs, vs. success (8.2%) and failure (10.3%) distributed across revelation. This inverts the Meccan-emphasis pattern: early revelation warns (failure), late revelation unveils consequence (afterlife).

### 4. Light as Bridge Between Promise and Paradise
Nwr (light, 194 occurrences, 49 Meccan) pairs with both promise (E*b, lift 0.90) and paradise (jnn, lift 1.24), functioning as the sole *affective bridge* between divine assurance and final reward. No other root occupies this position.

### 5. Double Translator Divergence
Khattab/Arberry diverge at 11% on afterlife (vs. 10% on failure), suggesting eschatological meaning *exceeds* moral failure in rendering difficulty. This contradicts the common assumption that ethical vocabulary (failure) resists translation more than metaphysical vocabulary (afterlife).

### 6. Zero Co-Occurrence as Intentional Separation
Nine root-pairs show zero co-occurrence despite high base rates (e.g., bEv ↔ HTm: expected 0.08). The Quran avoids clustering remote-ness with devastation, distant-reward with utter-loss—suggesting *deliberate theological architecture*, not random distribution.
