# The Grace–Justice Axis and the Mediation Economy

> Phase-2 cross-cutting synthesis. Maps the Quran's dual-track eschatology (justice + mercy operating in parallel) and the heavily-gated mediation economy (intercession, pardon, mercy as procedural mechanisms). Builds on `concept-judgment-process.md`, `concept-records-scales.md`, `concept-paradise-core.md`, `concept-hell-core.md`, `notes/failure/2-cross-cutting/research-iblis-adam-binary.md`, `notes/time/3-rabbit-holes/research-exception-respite.md`, and `notes/success/2-cross-cutting/research-success-as-escape.md`.

---

## 1. Headline finding

The Quran's eschatology runs **two parallel tracks**: a JUSTICE track (Hsb-audit → jzy-recompense → Hkm-verdict → fSl-separation) and a MERCY track (gfr-forgiveness → riḍwān-pleasure → admission). They do not contradict — they operate together on the same Day, on the same souls. The mercy-vocabulary is **structurally dominant**: gfr (234 occ) is the largest of the six judgment-process roots, mercy "embraces all things" [7:156], and the eternity of hell is bracketed with an exception clause that paradise's eternity, while sharing the bracket, closes with a positive uninterrupted-gift guarantee. The mediation economy (intercession) **exists** but is gated by a triple lock: divine permission, recipient God-approves, intercessor-with-covenant. The architecture is mercy-weighted, justice-real, and pastorally calibrated to refuse both despair and presumption.

## 2. The dual-track structure — justice and mercy in parallel

The Phase-1 judgment-process file maps a staged sequence (gathering → standing → record → audit → weighing → verdict → recompense/forgiveness → decree → separation), but the load-bearing structural finding is that **stages 7a and 7b run in parallel**:

| Track | Roots | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **JUSTICE** | Hsb (109) → jzy (118) → Hkm (210) → fSl (43) | Audit, recompense, verdict, separation |
| **MERCY** | gfr (234) → rḥm (mercy, ubiquitous) → riḍwān (13) → adkhalahu (admission) | Forgiveness, mercy, divine pleasure, entry |

Both are divine acts. Both occur on the Day. The believer can be subject to BOTH: judged accurately by justice AND forgiven by mercy. [40:17] places *tujzā kullu nafsin bi-mā kasabat* (every soul recompensed for what it earned) and *sarīʿ al-ḥisāb* (swift in reckoning) in one verse without contradicting [4:48]'s *yaghfiru mā dūna dhālika li-man yashāʾ* (He forgives what is less than that for whom He wills). The two operate on the same agent through different channels.

The structural marker: when mercy enters, the Quran's vocabulary shifts from **jazāʾ** (strict reciprocity) to **ajr** (grace-reward that may exceed the deed). [42:40]: the recompense of an evil deed is a like evil, **but** whoever pardons and reconciles, *fa-ajruhu ʿalā Allāh* — "his ajr is upon God." The lexical pivot from jazāʾ → ajr is the linguistic signature of the mercy-track activating within the justice-frame.

[6:160] formalizes the asymmetry: good deeds multiplied tenfold, bad deeds matched 1:1. The reciprocity-baseline (jzy) is preserved on the negative side; on the positive side, mercy multiplies. The Quran's accounting is structurally biased toward the upside.

## 3. gfr as the dominant root (234 occ) + the 39:53 statement

Forgiveness is, by raw count, the **largest single root in the judgment-process cluster**. The divine name *Ghafūr* (91 occurrences) alone exceeds the total of fSl (43). The verbal *Ghafara* (65), *Istaghfara* (40 — seeking), *Maghfira* (28 — the noun), *Ghaffār* (5 — intensified), *Ghāfir* (2 — active participle) round out a thoroughly-saturated lexical field.

The Quran's most frequent two-word divine formula is *Ghafūr Raḥīm* (Forgiving, Merciful) — repeated 70+ times across the corpus. The pairing is structural: every time God is named in a context of human failure, the named attribute is forgiveness-mercy, not wrath. This is the leading note of the eschatological frequency, not an afterthought.

The most expansive statement of forgiveness scope:

> **[39:53]** قُلۡ يَٰعِبَادِيَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَسۡرَفُواْ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمۡ لَا تَقۡنَطُواْ مِن رَّحۡمَةِ ٱللَّهِۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَغۡفِرُ ٱلذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًاۚ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلۡغَفُورُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ
> "Say: O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of Allah's mercy. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins together. Indeed, He is the Forgiving, the Merciful." (Saheeh)

Three remarkable features: (a) the address is *yā ʿibādī* — "O My servants" — God claims even the transgressors as *ʿibād* (servants); (b) the prohibition is *lā taqnaṭū* — do not despair; (c) the scope is *al-dhunūba jamīʿan* — ALL sins, taken together. The qualifier *jamīʿan* (collectively/all) is the broadest forgiveness-claim in the corpus.

This appears to contradict [4:48] — *inna Allāha lā yaghfiru an yushraka bihi* (God does not forgive that partners be associated with Him). The reconciliation is supplied by the failure-investigation's Iblīs–Adam binary: 39:53 covers the agent who **TURNS** (the verse continues into 39:54: *anībū ilā rabbikum*, "turn to your Lord"); 4:48 covers PERSISTENT shirk. The mercy-track is unlimited for those who turn; the limit is on persistence. The category is set by the LAST RESPONSE, not by the act.

The Iblīs–Adam binary's response-not-content rule resolves the apparent paradox: forgiveness is structurally available for any content, conditional on the Adam-type response (admission + turning + asking). Permanent refusal of the response — the Iblīs-stance — forecloses the channel through which mercy could arrive. Shirk is named as the cardinal unforgivable because shirk IS the cardinal Iblīs-type refusal: it denies the only Being who could forgive.

## 4. "raḥmatī wasiʿat kulla shayʾ" — mercy as background condition

The cosmic-mercy statement:

> **[7:156]** ... وَرَحۡمَتِي وَسِعَتۡ كُلَّ شَيۡءٍ
> "...and My mercy embraces every thing." (the closing of Moses' prayer at Sinai)

*Wasiʿat* (from wsʿ, to extend, to encompass) + *kulla shayʾ* (every thing) is the broadest scope-predicate the Quran applies to any divine attribute. No other attribute receives this universal-scope claim — not justice, not knowledge in this exact construction, not power. **Mercy alone is given the cosmic envelope.**

This is the architectural foundation that contextualizes everything else. The justice-track operates **WITHIN** the mercy-frame, not alongside it on equal footing. Hell exists, recompense is real, the scales weigh — but the entire system is enclosed in a mercy that "embraces every thing." The judgment-machinery is the local detail; mercy is the background condition.

The classical reading (Ibn ʿArabī radically, but also more mainstream commentators in muted form) develops 7:156 as the foundational principle: justice is a mode of mercy, not its opposite. The hadith-saying *"sabaqat raḥmatī ghaḍabī"* ("My mercy precedes My wrath") encodes the same theology in oral form. The Quran's own ratio is consistent: the divine attributes paired with divine action skew heavily toward mercy-formulas (Raḥmān 57 occ as proper name, Raḥīm in the basmala that opens every surah but one, Ghafūr Raḥīm formula 70+ times).

## 5. Intercession's constraint architecture — the triple lock

The Phase-1 records-scales file documents intercession ($fE, 31 occurrences) as a **gated** mechanism with three nested conditions. Intercession is neither unlimited (it cannot be exercised freely) nor forbidden (it does occur). The Quran's lexical architecture preserves the category and tightens the constraints:

> **[2:255 — Throne Verse]** مَن ذَا ٱلَّذِي يَشۡفَعُ عِندَهُۥٓ إِلَّا بِإِذۡنِهِۦ
> "Who is it that can intercede with Him except by His permission?"

> **[20:109]** يَوۡمَئِذٍ لَّا تَنفَعُ ٱلشَّفَٰعَةُ إِلَّا مَنۡ أَذِنَ لَهُ ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنُ وَرَضِيَ لَهُۥ قَوۡلًا
> "On that Day, no intercession will benefit except [that of] one to whom the Most Merciful has given permission and approved his speech."

> **[21:28]** وَلَا يَشۡفَعُونَ إِلَّا لِمَنِ ٱرۡتَضَىٰ
> "And they do not intercede except for one whom He approves."

> **[19:87]** لَّا يَمۡلِكُونَ ٱلشَّفَٰعَةَ إِلَّا مَنِ ٱتَّخَذَ عِندَ ٱلرَّحۡمَٰنِ عَهۡدًا
> "They do not possess intercession, except he who has taken with the Most Merciful a covenant."

The triple lock:

| Gate | Verse | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Permission | [2:255] | God must permit the intercession |
| 2. Approval of recipient | [20:109], [21:28] | God must approve the one being interceded for |
| 3. Covenant of intercessor | [19:87] | The intercessor must hold a divine covenant |

[39:44] adds the ownership clause: *qul lillāhi al-shafāʿatu jamīʿan* — "Say: To Allah belongs intercession entirely." Intercession is God's property, not a transferable endowment. He may permit its exercise; He does not delegate it.

The architecture is precise: intercession EXISTS as a mechanism, but every gate is held by God. **It is not a humans-can't-be-saved-without-permission negation, nor a Christians-have-it-Muslims-don't displacement.** It is a real procedural channel through which divine mercy operates, with ownership and gate-control retained by the Owner.

## 6. The historical polemic — preserving the category, rejecting the corruption

The constraint architecture exists because the Quran is in active polemical engagement with two contemporary systems: pre-Islamic Arab idolatry (where idols at the Kaʿba were called *shufaʿāʾ* and approached as unconditional brokers with the divine) and Christian saintly intercession (where canonized figures could be petitioned without divine permission as a structural prerequisite).

> **[10:18]** وَيَعۡبُدُونَ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ مَا لَا يَضُرُّهُمۡ وَلَا يَنفَعُهُمۡ وَيَقُولُونَ هَٰٓؤُلَآءِ شُفَعَٰٓؤُنَا عِندَ ٱللَّهِ
> "And they worship besides Allah that which neither harms them nor benefits them, and they say: 'These are our intercessors with Allah.'"

> **[39:43]** أَمِ ٱتَّخَذُواْ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ شُفَعَآءَۚ قُلۡ أَوَلَوۡ كَانُواْ لَا يَمۡلِكُونَ شَيۡـًٔا وَلَا يَعۡقِلُونَ
> "Or have they taken besides Allah intercessors? Say: Even though they own nothing and do not reason?"

The polemical structure is precise: the Quran does NOT abolish intercession; it dismantles the **unconditional** version. The idols cannot intercede because they own nothing (cannot meet gate 1 ownership-condition for a covenant) and do not reason (cannot meet gate 3's intentional-covenant-holder requirement). The category survives; the corrupted instances are voided.

This explains why $fE-vocabulary is paradoxically dense in negation contexts AND preserved in affirmation contexts. The Quran needs the word to say what cannot happen (idol-mediation) AND what can happen under conditions (permitted intercession on the Day). Both deployments belong to the same polemical project: **rectifying** the mediation economy, not eliminating it.

The early Meccan formula cluster — [2:48], [2:123], [2:254] — denies intercession in unqualified form precisely because the audience held the unqualified version. Once the audience has internalized the negation, the later verses (20:109, 21:28, 19:87) reintroduce intercession under the gates. The negation-then-restoration pattern is the rhetorical sequence by which the Quran replaces one mediation theology with another.

## 7. The 11:107-108 closure asymmetry — paradise more lexically locked

Cross-reference to the time-investigation rabbit hole (`research-exception-respite.md`). The two verses:

> **[11:107]** خَٰلِدِينَ فِيهَا مَا دَامَتِ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتُ وَٱلۡأَرۡضُ إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ رَبُّكَۚ إِنَّ رَبَّكَ فَعَّالࣱ لِّمَا يُرِيدُ
> "Abiding therein as long as the heavens and the earth endure, except what your Lord wills. Indeed, your Lord is doer of what He wills."

> **[11:108]** وَأَمَّا ٱلَّذِينَ سُعِدُواْ فَفِي ٱلۡجَنَّةِ خَٰلِدِينَ فِيهَا مَا دَامَتِ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتُ وَٱلۡأَرۡضُ إِلَّا مَا شَآءَ رَبُّكَۖ عَطَآءً غَيۡرَ مَجۡذُوذࣲ
> "And as for those who were felicitated, in the Garden — abiding therein as long as the heavens and the earth endure, except what your Lord wills — a gift uninterrupted."

The first 11 morphemes are identical. Both bracket eternity with *illā mā shāʾa rabbuk*. The divergence is in the closing affirmation:

- 11:107 (hell): *faʿʿālun li-mā yurīd* — bare divine-will assertion
- 11:108 (paradise): *ʿaṭāʾan ghayra majdhūdh* — positive uninterrupted-gift guarantee

The paradise-closure tells you what the gift IS (*ʿaṭāʾan*, a gift) and adds a positive guarantee (*ghayra majdhūdh*, "not amputated/cut off"). The hell-closure tells you what God CAN do. **Paradise is positively sealed; hell is left open within divine discretion.** This is the load-bearing seam Ibn Taymiyya and Ibn Qayyim used for the *fanāʾ al-nār* argument.

The grace-bracket on hell is part of the larger grace-architecture. The mercy-track does not just operate during life and at the Day — it remains structurally available **even within the post-judgment frame**. The Quran refuses to lock God OUT of any future state He has set up.

## 8. The 14-verse "illā mā shāʾa" exception network

The exception clause is not eschatological-specific. The time investigation cataloged the full network:

**Six core *illā mā shāʾa* verses:** [6:128], [7:188], [10:49], [11:107], [11:108], [87:7] — covering hell-residency of jinn-and-humans, Prophet's agency, paradise-eternity, and Prophet's memory.

**Eight *illā an yashāʾa* verses:** [6:80], [6:111], [7:89], [12:76], [18:24], [74:56], [76:30], [81:29] — covering guidance, future-promise, and creaturely-belief.

The eschatological subset (3 verses):
- [6:128] — jinn-and-humans say "we abide therein, except what God wills" — eschatological residency
- [11:107] — hell-eternity exception
- [11:108] — paradise-eternity exception

The Quran applies the SAME syntactic safeguard across heterogeneous domains: anywhere the text might generate a creature-prediction of certainty, the exception clause re-opens it. **Eschatological exceptions are preserved in the text deliberately.** The Quran refuses to lock in any final outcome — God's sovereignty stays grammatically active.

This is the textual basis Ibn Taymiyya and Ibn Qayyim built *fanāʾ al-nār* on, combined with the [78:23] *aḥqāb* (countable ages) on the hell-side and the absence of comparable finite-suggestive vocabulary on the paradise-side. The doctrine remains a classical exegetical debate rather than a settled position; the Sunni mainstream rejects it. But the textual warrants are real: paradise is more lexically committed than hell, by exactly one closing clause + several reinforcement-frequency asymmetries.

The methodological point: the [11:107] exception should NOT be read as a unique seam. It is one instance of a generic Quranic posture. Doctrinal weight built on it must reckon with the parallel exception on paradise-eternity at [11:108] — the same clause, with the inverse closure tilt.

## 9. The riḍwān vs ʿaḏāb akbar parallel

The two poles of the dual-track:

> **[9:72]** ... وَرِضۡوَٰنࣱ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ أَكۡبَرُۚ ذَٰلِكَ هُوَ ٱلۡفَوۡزُ ٱلۡعَظِيمُ
> "...and **riḍwān from Allah is greater**. That is the great attainment."

> **[88:24]** فَيُعَذِّبُهُ ٱللَّهُ ٱلۡعَذَابَ ٱلۡأَكۡبَرَ
> "Then Allah will punish him with the greatest punishment."

The two "greatests" are placed in structural parallel — riḍwān akbar vs ʿaḏāb akbar. But the parallelism is **theologically asymmetric**:

| Greatest reward | Greatest punishment |
|---|---|
| **Riḍwān** — God's pleasure | **ʿAḏāb akbar** — the greatest punishment |
| RELATIONAL POSITIVE | CONSEQUENTIAL (not relational) |
| State of mutual recognition (raḍiya... wa-raḍū) | State of suffering (consequence of action) |
| Above the gardens (akbar than the rivers + dwellings) | Above the Fire (akbar than ʿaḏāb shadīd) |

The greatest reward is **relational** — God is pleased with them, they are pleased with Him. The greatest punishment is **consequential** — pain, suffering, exposure to fire — but the Quran does NOT give it a relational-negative inverse. There is no "God's *displeasure* is the greatest" formula symmetric to *riḍwān akbar*. The Quran has limited divine-wrath vocabulary (ghaḍab is used, but sparingly compared to riḍwān; saḫiṭa appears occasionally) and does NOT make divine-anger the structural pole opposite riḍwān.

This is a deliberate asymmetry. The Quran's framework: positive ultimate reality is RELATIONAL (mutual pleasure), negative ultimate reality is CONSEQUENTIAL (the predictable result of choosing the wrong orientation, not God's personal vengeance). God's relation to the saved is mutual love-pleasure; God's relation to the damned is more aptly described as **disengagement** ("they are forgotten by Him as they forgot the meeting of this Day" [7:51, 32:14], "We will not look at them" [3:77]) than as active hatred. Hell is what disengagement-from-God looks like; paradise is what engagement-with-God looks like.

The asymmetry feeds the mercy-weighting: the apex of the system is positive-relational (riḍwān), and the apex of the negative is consequence (ʿaḏāb), not personal wrath. The system's gravitational pull is toward relation, not retribution.

## 10. The mercy-weighted overall architecture

Combining the threads, the Quran's eschatological architecture is **structurally weighted toward mercy**:

| Feature | Weighting toward mercy |
|---|---|
| gfr is the largest root in the judgment-cluster (234 occ) | Lexical |
| Mercy "embraces all things" [7:156] — universal-scope predicate | Cosmological |
| All sins forgivable for those who turn [39:53] | Doctrinal |
| Hell-eternity bracketed with exception [11:107], [6:128] | Modal |
| Paradise-eternity closes with positive guarantee [11:108] | Lexical |
| Good deeds multiplied 10×, bad deeds matched 1:1 [6:160] | Accounting |
| Intercession is gated but EXISTS | Procedural |
| "save what your Lord wills" preserves mercy-discretion | Architectural |
| Riḍwān is GREATER than the gardens [9:72] | Hierarchical |
| Greatest punishment is consequential, not relational-negative | Asymmetric |
| *xālidīna fīhā abadan* for paradise: 8 verses; for hell: 3 | Frequency |

The justice-track is REAL — Hsb audits, jzy recompenses, Hkm renders verdict, fSl separates camps. But it operates WITHIN the mercy-frame. The Quran's apophatic protocol withholds certain knowledge while emphasizing certain certainties; what it emphasizes most consistently is mercy's scope, dominance, and structural priority.

This is why the Quran's eschatological rhetoric **cannot be reduced** to either threat-only (the early-Meccan warning surahs) or comfort-only (the Medinan promise sequences). Both registers are genuine, and they interlock through the dual-track architecture. The threat is real because justice is real; the comfort is real because mercy is dominant.

## 11. Cross-investigation integration

The dual-track architecture is the eschatological INSTANTIATION of patterns that recur across all five prior investigations.

**Time investigation's exception-respite work** = the textual basis for the mercy-bracket. The 14-verse *illā mā shāʾa* network is the grammatical-architectural mechanism by which mercy retains discretion over every apparently-fixed eschatological state. Without the exception clause, mercy would be ON the system; with it, mercy stays sovereign OVER the system.

**Failure investigation's Iblīs-Adam binary** = response-not-content rule = matches the mercy-track requirement. The mercy-track is **available** for any content (39:53's *jamīʿan*), but **conditioned** on response (39:54's *anībū*). Forgivability is response-determined: those who turn are forgiven, regardless of what they have done; those who refuse the turning-response are excluded, regardless of how minor the failure-content. The Iblīs-Adam binary's mechanism is precisely the mechanism of the mercy-track's gate.

**Success investigation's "success-is-given" thesis** = success comes from divine action; the mercy-track is the OPERATIONAL channel for that giving. The muflihūn at [7:43] attribute their guidance to God's giving (*alladhī hadānā li-hādhā*); they do not credit themselves. The mercy-track is what the giving-of-success looks like at the eschatological terminus. Salvation is structurally the same shape as success: divine action arriving on the agent who has maintained the Adam-type orientation.

**Teleportation investigation's apophatic-causation** = mechanism of mercy is silenced. The Quran tells us THAT God forgives (with maximum frequency); it does NOT tell us HOW the forgiveness-mechanism operates internal to the divine. Like the mechanism of the throne-bearer's transport in [27:40], the mechanism of forgiveness is sequestered. We see the result, not the process. This is mercy-as-divine-act, not mercy-as-procedure-we-could-audit.

The dual-track architecture is therefore not a stand-alone eschatological feature. It is the eschatological terminus of **the same axis the Quran runs across all its investigations**: divine action on a responsive (or unresponsive) human, with the mechanism withheld and the orientation determining the outcome.

## 12. The pastoral theology — the hope-fear balance

What does the dual-track architecture mean for how the Quran addresses its audience? It refuses BOTH:

**The despair-position**: "I have sinned too much; I am definitely damned." The Quran's response is 39:53 — *lā taqnaṭū min raḥmati Allāh* (do not despair of Allah's mercy). The mercy-track is structurally available; despair is precluded by the architecture itself. This is the pastoral counter to the over-internalized weight of sin.

**The presumption-position**: "I am a believer; mercy is automatic." The Quran's response is 7:99 — *fa-lā yaʾmanu makra Allāhi illā al-qawmu al-khāsirūn* ("None feels safe from Allah's plan except the losing people"). Feeling SECURE in divine mercy is itself a marker of loss. The justice-track is real and active; presumption is precluded by the justice-machinery.

The believer is therefore supposed to live in a STATE OF HOPE-FEAR — *rajāʾ + khawf*. Both tracks are simultaneously active in the believer's psychology. The classical framing: rajāʾ alone produces presumption; khawf alone produces despair; the believer holds both. The Quran's pastoral architecture — addressed to the audience IN-LIFE, not just to the dead at the eschaton — is precisely calibrated to maintain the dual-track tension as the believer's affective stance.

The dual-track architecture is therefore not just an eschatological description; it is a **pastoral instrument**. The architecture exists so that the human can locate themselves within it: subject to justice (which means take it seriously, the audit is coming) and subject to mercy (which means there is always a turning-channel open, before the terminal sealing). Living between the two is the structural Quranic disposition.

This is why the eschatological rhetoric oscillates throughout the corpus. It is not stylistic variation; it is the calibration of the pastoral instrument. Threat-passages activate khawf; promise-passages activate rajāʾ; the believer is held in the productive tension between them. Removing either track would collapse the pastoral architecture into either complacency or terror, both of which the Quran explicitly diagnoses as losing-postures.

## 13. Negative space

What the Quran does NOT do, given the architecture:

1. **No rate of forgiveness given.** "For whom He wills" — never a percentage, quota, or estimate of saved vs lost. Architecture described; population statistics withheld.
2. **No felt-experience of riḍwān.** Asserted as greatest reward; no sensory-experiential content. Riḍwān is structurally un-narratable — preserved as relation-not-content.
3. **No ʿaḏāban ghayra majdhūdh on the hell side.** Paradise positively sealed with "uninterrupted gift"; hell never receives the parallel formula. Asymmetry is total.
4. **No mechanism description for forgiveness.** Forgiveness happens; how the *imām mubīn* record-removal occurs is undescribed. Apophatic-causation applies to mercy.
5. **No roster for permitted intercessions.** Conditions specified; recipient-categories and frequencies withheld.
6. **No final answer on hell-finality.** Both possibilities preserved via exception clause without actualization-statement. Text refuses to close the question.
7. **No "default outcome" stated.** Neither paradise-default nor hell-default is named. Mercy-weighting tilts but never becomes doctrinal default.

- **The riḍwān-ʿaḏāb akbar parallel — corpus mapping.** Are other "akbar" pairings relational-vs-consequential? Test whether the asymmetry is structural or local.
- **The shafāʿa-permitted-recipients roster.** Quran specifies gates, withholds recipients. What does the bare text license vs hadith extension?
- **The 39:53 vs 4:48 reconciliation in tafsīr history.** When did the persistence-resolution become standard? The *anībū ilā rabbikum* turning-condition in 39:54 is the in-text signal.
- **Rajāʾ+khawf as Quranic vs hadith pair.** Heavily Sufi-developed. Does the Quran itself coordinate the pair, or is the balance tafsīr-extracted?
- **The wārid problem at [19:71-72].** Everyone passes the Fire; mercy extracts the saved. Does wārid imply a structural mercy-rescue moment for ALL believers?
- **Closure-vocabulary typology.** Which divine-attribute closures (Ḥakīm-ʿAlīm vs Ghafūr-Raḥīm vs faʿʿālun li-mā yurīd) attach to which exception-clauses?
