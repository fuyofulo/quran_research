# The fast planner

> A form to fill in **before** a fast, keep **during** it, and answer **after**.
>
> Why a form at all: the investigation's central finding is that the Quran specifies the emptying of
> a fast in detail and leaves the filling almost entirely unspecified — which makes the filling the
> one part that can actually fail. A fast that goes nowhere usually isn't a fast done badly. It's a
> fast that was never furnished. This form is the furnishing, done in advance.
>
> Every question traces to something in this folder. See [`practice-guide.md`](practice-guide.md) for
> the reasoning, [`concept-fasting-deep.md`](concept-fasting-deep.md) for the full research.
>
> **Copy this file per fast.** Suggested: `fasts/2026-08-12-three-days.md`.

---

## The rule this form is built on

**Set input goals, not outcome goals.**

2:183 says fasting is prescribed *"so that **perhaps** you may build taqwā."* The Quran frames the
outcome as a hope — four "perhaps" clauses in five verses. So the outcome is not yours to commit to.
The inputs are. Every question below asks about something you control and can answer yes or no.

If you can't check it off with one word, it isn't a goal yet — it's a wish.

# PART 1 — Before

## A. The gate

**A1. Am I medically clear for this?**
> (Diabetes, kidney disease, pregnancy, a history of disordered eating, or relevant medication all
> mean talk to a doctor first. This is the one question with a hard stop.)

`____________________________________________`

**A2. Is there something ranked higher that I'm substituting a fast for?**
> Uncomfortable but honest. In the Quran's own expiation ladders, fasting is repeatedly what you do
> *when you can't* free someone or feed people. If there's a person you could actually help this
> week, the text ranks that above the fast. Do both, or do that first.

`____________________________________________`

## B. The why

**B1. What is this fast for?** One sentence.
> "To get closer to Allah" is the category, not the answer. Push one level down: closer *how*? What
> would be different about you on the other side? Maryam's fast in 19:26 had a concrete target — an
> accusation she couldn't answer. Hunger without a target is just hunger.

`____________________________________________`

**B2. What am I guarding against?** One specific thing.
> **The most important question on this form.** Taqwā comes from a root meaning *to shield, to guard
> from*. In the Quran the verb almost always names what it's guarding against. A shield with nothing
> named in front of it isn't doing anything. Name one habit, one drift, one direction you're going
> that you don't want to be going.

`____________________________________________`

**B3. How long, and why that length?**
> The Quran gives no number for a voluntary fast. That's freedom, not an invitation to add days.
> Whatever you pick, be able to say why in one line. "Because more is better" isn't a reason —
> consecutive fasting is penalty-language in the text, and sleep debt compounds across days.

`Length: ________`  ·  `Because: ______________________________`

**B4. What would make this a success?** Write it now, before you're hungry.
> Set it low and concrete. "I did the four things I wrote down on most days." Deciding in advance
> that *unremarkable counts as success* is what stops one flat day becoming proof the whole thing
> failed. Do not write a feeling here.

`____________________________________________`

## C. The contents — furnishing the room

**C1. Which passage will I hold?** Surah and ayat. Short.
> The only method the Quran ever attaches to building taqwā is 2:63's two commands: *take what We
> have given you, and remember what is in it.* Take it, then hold it. One short passage held all day
> beats an ambitious reading plan dropped on day two.

`____________________________________________`

**C2. What will I say to Him, and at what time?**
> 2:185 names the fast's own outputs as declaring God's greatness and thanking Him — both things you
> **say**. 35:10 says the word ascends and the deed only lifts it. A fast with nothing said in it is
> a crane with an empty hook. Pick a fixed time so it doesn't depend on remembering.

`What: _______________________`  ·  `When: ____________`

**C3. When will I do the answering, not just the asking?**
> 2:186 sits inside the fasting rules: *"I am near. I answer the one who calls Me. **So let them
> answer Me.**"* His side is stated as already happening. Yours is a command still outstanding. Most
> of us treat a fast as asking. Book the responding.

`____________________________________________`

**C4. What am I giving — amount, person, day?**
> 3:92 is the only place the Quran states a precondition for reaching goodness: *"you will never
> attain it until you spend from what you love."* From what you love, so it should cost. Not "some
> charity" — a number, a name, a date.

`Amount: ________`  ·  `To: ____________`  ·  `On: ________`

**C5. What am I removing besides food?**
> The hunger is the physical part — it stays here. What actually changes the hours is what stops
> competing for your attention. Across the first-person accounts, cutting phone and social media was
> the single most-repeated concrete practice. Also consider Maryam's version: a fast of *speech*
> — she was told to eat and drink in the same verse.

`____________________________________________`

## D. The if-thens — the proven lever

> Write these as **"When X happens, I will do Y."** Not resolutions. Pre-committed if-then plans are
> one of the most reliably replicated findings in behaviour change; "I'll be more intentional" is a
> restatement of the willpower model that 23 laboratories failed to reproduce. Be embarrassingly
> specific.

**D1.** When `_________________________________`, I will `_________________________________`
> (Use this one for the hardest hour — usually 10–16 hours in.)

**D2.** When `_________________________________`, I will `_________________________________`
> (Use this one for the phone.)

**D3.** When `_________________________________`, I will `_________________________________`
> (Use this one for the moment you want to complain about it, or tell someone you're fasting.)

## E. The people

**E1. Who knows I'm doing this, or who is doing it with me?**
> The research on ritual finds that doing something like this alongside others — or where someone
> knows — changes how much it lands. Fasting alone and out of season is a structural disadvantage,
> not a flaw in you. One check-in partner is enough.

`____________________________________________`

## F. The body — so you can do any of the above

**F1. Caffeine taper — start date:** `________`
> The single most preventable source of fasting misery. Taper over several days; don't quit on day one.

**F2. Suhoor plan:** `____________________________________________`
> Protein and slow carbohydrates, not sugar and pastries. **Add salt** — sodium loss drives the
> headaches and cramps people blame on the fast.

**F3. Which sleep block am I protecting?** `________ to ________`
> Sleep debt compounds across consecutive days and isn't repaid overnight. This is the most
> underrated variable in the entire body of research, and the one most often mistaken for spiritual
> dryness. Decide the hours now.

**F4. Hydration across the night:** `____________________________________________`
> Spread it. Don't drink the day's water in the last thirty minutes before dawn.

## G. The exits — pre-authorised

**G1. Under what conditions will I break or stop?** Decide now, while you're clear-headed.
> This is not weakness, it's design. The fasting passage carries nine clauses giving relief against
> six placing restrictions, and states the illness and travel exemptions **twice, word for word.**
> 2:185 says outright: *God intends ease for you and does not intend hardship.* Writing the exit in
> advance also stops the spiral where one broken day gets read as total failure and the whole thing
> gets abandoned.

`____________________________________________`

| 7 | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | |

> **That last column is the one that matters.** In the first-person accounts, the fasts that failed
> almost never say "I didn't try hard enough." They say *something else filled the space I meant to
> fill* — chores, scrolling, illness, work. Write what actually occupied the hours, honestly, even
> when it's "nothing" or "YouTube." That's the data.

**P2. Which if-then plans fired? Which didn't, and why not?**
> A plan that never fired is usually too vague or attached to a trigger that doesn't happen. Fix the

**P3. What did the hunger produce or test in me?**
> Deliberately not "was I hungry." 22:37 says the flesh and blood of a sacrifice never reach God —
> only the taqwā does. The hunger is the fast's flesh and blood; it stays here. So: what did it
> surface? What did it show you about what you reach for when you're uncomfortable?

**P4. Did I meet the success condition I wrote in B4?** Yes / No — and it's the one I wrote, not a
new one invented afterwards.

`____________________________________________`

**P5. What one thing changes next time?**
> One. Not five. The accounts converge on moderation over intensity — a few things done consistently
> beat total transformation attempted at once and abandoned.

## A note on what this form can't do

It can't make a fast work, because the Quran doesn't promise that a fast works. It says *perhaps.*
Every experiential state the Quran describes — the shivering skin, the trembling heart, the settled
heart — attaches to hearing the Book, to remembrance, or to praying at night. **None attaches to
fasting.** So if you fill this in completely and still feel ordinary, the form didn't fail and
neither did you. You did the inputs. The outcome was never on the form because it was never yours.

What the form does do is make sure that if nothing arrives, it isn't because the room was empty.
