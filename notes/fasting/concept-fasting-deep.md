# Fasting — synthesis

> Phase-4 capstone. Integrates the structural pre-cut, five root files, two narrative files, two
> cross-cutting files and three external-evidence files. Every claim is marked **[data]** (counted,
> verified in the morphology or the published literature) or **[interp]** (a reading built on it).
> Hadith and classical tafsir are **outside this evidence base**.

---

## 1. Headline finding

The Quran legislates a fast with unusual precision and specifies almost nothing to put inside it.
Fourteen occurrences of `Swm`; five concern Ramadan; six are restitution. The passage states its
purpose twice — *laʿallakum tattaqūn* — and never states its mechanism. That same purpose-clause
occurs twelve times in the corpus, and **nine of the twelve attach taqwā to received divine
speech**, not to any physical act. Hunger is never praised anywhere. No reward is promised for
fasting. No intention-formula exists. `Swm` and `Slw` never share an ayah. The text supplies a
vacancy and hands it over unfurnished — and all three independent evidence streams here, examined
by agents who did not read each other's work, located the active ingredient in **what occupies the
emptied hours**, not in the emptying. Seven days of hunger with nothing loaded into them is the
predicted outcome, not a failure of sincerity.

## 2. The corpus, in one paragraph

**[data]** `Swm` (ص و م) occurs **14 times, in 11 ayahs, in 6 surahs**. By category: Ramadan 5,
expiation-or-substitution 6, vow 1, identity 2. By morphology: 12 nominal, 2 verbal — both verbs
inside the founding passage and never again, after which the Quran only *reasons with* an
established noun. By revelation: **13 Medinan, 1 Meccan** — the Meccan one being 19:26, Maryam's
fast of speech, also the only unqualified `ṣawm`. The annual fast's entire textual basis is five
verses, one of which (2:186) is not about fasting. Full tables:
[0-structural/fasting-structurals.md](0-structural/fasting-structurals.md),
[1-roots/concept-Swm.md](1-roots/concept-Swm.md).

## 3. What the Quran actually says

**The decree and its purpose.** One unconditional imposition, with a stated aim and no stated
mechanism.

> **2:183** — كُتِبَ عَلَيۡكُمُ ٱلصِّيَامُ كَمَا كُتِبَ عَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبۡلِكُمۡ لَعَلَّكُمۡ تَتَّقُونَ
> "Decreed upon you is fasting, as it was decreed upon those before you — that you may guard
> yourselves." (Divergence 0.7581; translators split hard on `kutiba` and on `tattaqūn`.)

**The ring. [data]** `wqy` occurs exactly twice in the 172-word passage — closing 2:183 and closing
2:187 — and nothing else brackets both ends. But the two clauses name different instruments: 2:187
closes on *thus God makes clear His signs, that they may guard themselves*. The ring opens on
fasting-for-taqwā and closes on clarified-signs-for-taqwā
([2-cross-cutting/research-how-fasting-builds-taqwa.md](2-cross-cutting/research-how-fasting-builds-taqwa.md)).

**Taqwā is not a mood. [data]** `wqy` occurs 258×; 166 are the verb `ittaqā`, only 17 the noun
`taqwā`. The base form `waqā` means to *shield* — God shielding people from the Fire (66:6, 52:27,
40:9) — and the verb is transitive, taking a named threat. Taqwā is a repeated defensive act, not
an arriving state ([1-roots/concept-taqwa-wqy.md](1-roots/concept-taqwa-wqy.md)).

**Ease, not hardship. [data]** 2:185: `yurīdu Allāhu bikumu al-yusra wa-lā yurīdu bikumu al-ʿusr`.
Counted clause by clause, the five verses carry **nine relief clauses against six restrictive
ones**; illness and travel are stated twice, verbatim, in consecutive verses; 2:187 pardons the
community for breaking the rule *before* relaxing it. **[interp]** Difficulty is not the payload.

**The month is named for a descent, not a discipline. [data]** 2:185's naming clause is about
revelation — `shahru Ramaḍāna alladhī unzila fīhi al-Qurʾān` — so the chain runs revelation → month
→ fast. And *laylat al-qadr* (97:1–5, 44:1–6) contains **zero occurrences of `Swm`**: descent,
decree and peace only, on a *night*, the one time nobody is fasting
([1-roots/concept-ramadan-qadr-night.md](1-roots/concept-ramadan-qadr-night.md)).

**The interruption.** Between the command and the rules, one non-legislative verse:

> **2:186** — وَإِذَا سَأَلَكَ عِبَادِي عَنِّي فَإِنِّي قَرِيبٌۖ أُجِيبُ دَعۡوَةَ ٱلدَّاعِ إِذَا دَعَانِۖ فَلۡيَسۡتَجِيبُواْ لِي
> "And when My servants ask you about Me — indeed I am near. I answer the call of the caller when
> he calls Me. So let them answer Me."

**[data]** `ujību` and `fal-yastajībū` are the same root `jwb`, form IV against form X, verified in
the morphology; `dEw` saturates the 17-word verse three times; `qrb` appears twice in the passage,
both after the intrusion — God is `qarīb` at 186, `fa-lā taqrabūhā` at 187. **The widely repeated
claim that 2:186 is the passage's exact centre is false**: it is the fourth of five verses and the
shortest, and the word-count midpoint falls inside 2:185
([1-narratives/narrative-2-183-187.md](1-narratives/narrative-2-183-187.md)).

**The kaffāra ladders. [data]** Five occurrences are expiation (4:92, 5:89, 5:95, 58:4, plus
2:196's substitution), built on `fa-man lam yajid fa-ṣiyāmu…` — *whoever cannot find, then fasting
of…* — the apodosis of another remedy's unavailability. But 5:95 prices it as `ʿadl`, the
**equivalent** of feeding the needy, and 58:3–4 puts fasting *above* feeding sixty, that last rung
using `lam yastaṭiʿ` (cannot) rather than `lam yajid` (cannot afford). **[interp]** Fasting is what
a person without money pays instead of money
([1-roots/concept-kaffara-ladder.md](1-roots/concept-kaffara-ladder.md)).

**Maryam.** The Quran's one unqualified `ṣawm`, and the faster is told to eat.

> **19:26** — فَكُلِي وَٱشۡرَبِي وَقَرِّي عَيۡنࣰ ا … إِنِّي نَذَرۡتُ لِلرَّحۡمَٰنِ صَوۡمࣰ ا فَلَنۡ أُكَلِّمَ ٱلۡيَوۡمَ إِنسِيًّا
> "So eat and drink and be comforted… I have vowed to the Most Merciful a *ṣawm*, so I will not
> speak today to any human."

**[data]** Self-imposed (`nadhartu`, no legislator), addressed to al-Raḥmān by name, functional —
the silence hands her defence to an infant who cannot lie (19:29–30) — and its negated speech takes
a *person* as object, `insiyyā`
([1-narratives/narrative-maryam-sawm-silence.md](1-narratives/narrative-maryam-sawm-silence.md)).

**33:35. [data]** `Ṣāʾimīn/ṣāʾimāt` sit eighth of ten paired participles, between the
charity-givers and the guarders-of-chastity; the reward attaches to the whole list, not to any
item. The only place in the corpus where a person is described *as* a faster.

## 4. What the Quran does not say

Each line was verified by root code against the 60-root inventory of 2:183–187 and the 133-root set
sharing any ayah with `Swm`. Source:
[research-fasting-negative-space.md](2-cross-cutting/research-fasting-negative-space.md).

- **`Swm` and `Slw` share zero ayahs in the entire Quran.** Fasting and prayer are never once in
  the same verse. (Verified independently by the commissioner.)
- **No hunger.** `jwE` occurs 5×, never in a fasting verse; all five are affliction, punishment,
  curse, or a blessing consisting of hunger's *removal*. `ZmA` (thirst), 3×, is the same shape.
  Hunger is never praised anywhere, under any root ([1-roots/concept-Sbr-and-hunger.md](1-roots/concept-Sbr-and-hunger.md)).
- **No reward.** `Ajr` and `gfr` each share exactly one ayah with `Swm` — 33:35, where both attach
  to the ten-item list. No verse promises anything for fasting specifically.
- **No intention-formula.** The root `nwy` does not occur in the Quran at all.
- **No prescribed programme.** `*kr`, `sbH`, `zkw`, `qlb`, `nwr`, `jnn` are absent from the passage;
  `qrA` appears once, as the noun *al-Qurʾān*, and there is **no command to recite**. The complete
  positive devotional content is three items — magnify God for guidance, respond to Him, believe in
  Him — and four of the five imperatives are permissions to eat, drink and have relations.
- **No experiential vocabulary.** No `q$Er` (shiver — a hapax, spent on the effect of *hearing the
  Book*, 39:23), no `wjl`, no `Tmn`, no `qlb`. Every felt state the Quran describes attaches to
  recitation, remembrance, or the night vigil. None to fasting.
- **No asceticism.** 5:87 files self-imposed prohibition of the lawful under `Edw` — *transgression* —
  and 57:27 disowns monasticism with **the same lemma** used to prescribe the fast:

> **57:27** — وَرَهۡبَانِيَّةً ٱبۡتَدَعُوهَا **مَا كَتَبۡنَٰهَا عَلَيۡهِمۡ**
> "And monasticism — they invented it; We did not *prescribe* it upon them." (`ktb`, lemma
> `kataba`, same preposition `ʿalā` — identical to 2:183's `kutiba ʿalaykum`.)

- **No number for a voluntary fast.** Every number attached to fasting is legislated; the corpus's
  only "seven" is 2:196's post-hajj substitute. **[interp]** 2:184's `taṭawwaʿa khayran` — the sole
  candidate — most likely means *extra good works*: it occurs twice, and its twin at 2:158 has no
  fasting in view.
- **No community requirement, and no solitude requirement.** `jmE` is absent from the passage and
  from all 133 `Swm`-adjacent roots; the command to fast is grammatically singular; `Ekf` is
  presupposed and regulated, never commanded — and the same lemma names devotion to idols in five
  of its other eight uses.
- **No word for breaking the fast.** `fTr` occurs 19× and touches no fasting verse. No `ʿīd` (1
  occurrence, 5:114). No `suḥūr` — 2:187's dawn word is `al-fajr`.
- **2:177 omits fasting.** The Quran's own extended definition of *al-birr*, closing on
  `wa-ulāʾika humu al-muttaqūn`, is built from belief, giving wealth, prayer, zakat,
  covenant-keeping and ṣabr. Fasting is not in it.
- **Surah 97 is silent about fasting.**

## 5. The mechanism — a reconstruction, with its strongest objection carried

**Mark this section clearly: it is [interp] built on [data]. The Quran does not state a mechanism
for fasting, and no reading below changes that.**

**The scoreboard. [data]** The clause `laʿalla…tattaqūn/yattaqūn` occurs twelve times. Six name a
revealed text or its contents (2:63, 7:171, 6:51, 20:113, 39:28, 2:187); three more name delivered
divine speech of another kind (6:69 *dhikrā*, 7:164 admonition, 6:153 *this He has enjoined*).
**Three name none: 2:21, 2:179, and 2:183 — fasting.** No rival class has more than one member, and
under the strictest classification six of twelve still name a text.

**The formula. [data, independently verified]** The clause `li-tukabbirū Allāha ʿalā mā hadākum`
occurs in exactly two places — 2:185 and 22:37 — in both cases in the same structural position,
after the ritual is described, as its stated output. In the second:

> **22:37** — لَن يَنَالَ ٱللَّهَ لُحُومُهَا وَلَا دِمَآؤُهَا وَلَٰكِن يَنَالُهُ ٱلتَّقۡوَىٰ مِنكُمۡ
> "Their flesh will never reach God, nor their blood — but the taqwā from you reaches Him."

**What travels. [data]** There are exactly two verses stating something moving from human to God.
22:37 above, and:

> **35:10** — إِلَيۡهِ يَصۡعَدُ ٱلۡكَلِمُ ٱلطَّيِّبُ وَٱلۡعَمَلُ ٱلصَّٰلِحُ يَرۡفَعُهُۥ
> "To Him ascends the good **word**, and the righteous **deed raises it**." (`ilayhi yaṣʿadu`
> occurs nowhere else.)

Neither names a substance; neither names a deprivation. **[interp]** The hunger is the fast's
`luḥūm wa-dimāʾ`, the part that does not travel; 35:10 gives the deed a subordinate job, the
lifting mechanism and not the cargo. Hence 2:185's own stated proximate outputs — `takbīr` and
`shukr`, speech-acts of recognition — and hence the only traffic described inside the passage being
a call and an answer.

**The invariant. [interp]** The Quran fasts from food (13×, Medinan, legislated) and from speech
(1×, Meccan, vowed). What covers both: **ṣawm is the time-bounded suspension of a horizontal
exchange, with the vertical channel left open.** The food-fast suspends intake from the world; the
speech-fast suspends output to it. Neither suspends anything directed at God — Maryam's vow *is
itself* vertical speech, breaking into testimony about a given Book (19:30), and into the middle of
the food-fast's legislation the Quran inserts 2:186, whose entire content is that the vertical
channel is open and answered. This also predicts why hunger is never named: hunger belongs to the
suspended channel, not the open one.

**The `qul`-omission. [data]** Sixteen verses put a question or petition to the Prophet; thirteen
are answered with `qul` — *Say:* — the messenger relaying. Three are not: 4:153 is a demand, not a
question; 79:42 is the Hour, deflected (and the same question at 33:63 *does* get `qul`, so the
omission is pointed); and **2:186, the only genuine question about God in the set, which God
answers Himself, in the first person, past the messenger it was addressed to.** It is also the only
one calling the askers `ʿibādī` and the only one using the perfect `saʾalaka` rather than the
habitual `yasʾalūnaka`. **[interp]** The verse's form enacts its content — and the reciprocity is
asymmetric in mood: `ujību` is imperfect indicative, a standing fact already true, while
`fal-yastajībū` is a jussive with lām al-amr, a command not yet done. The fast is the question;
`fal-yastajībū lī` is the part the faster supplies.

**The strongest objection, and it lands.** The container/content reading — fast is vessel,
revelation is content — is one inferential step past what 2:185's grammar licenses. The syntax says
the *month* is the one the Qur'an came down in, and *whoever witnesses the month, let him fast it*:
the fast is a **response to** the descent, not a vessel for it. So the join between "taqwā comes
from received speech" and "fasting is for taqwā" is **negative space, not a stated claim**. But
negative space constrains: the reader filling it is not free to fill it with hunger (never
praised), hardship (2:185 rules it out), or duration (a penal marker). Three candidates foreclosed;
one indicated nine times in twelve.

**The rival thread, kept.** Giving. `Swm`'s strongest content co-roots are `TEm` (feed, 4 ayahs),
`skn` (the needy, 4), `wjd` (afford, 4), `Sdq` (3). Feeding-praise outnumbers fasting-praise ~3:1
by verse, across five independent surahs (76, 90, 69, 107, 89) against fasting-praise's two verses
in one passage. And **3:92 is the corpus's only stated precondition for attaining *birr*** — `lan
tanālū al-birra ḥattā tunfiqū mimmā tuḥibbūn` — using the same verb `nāl` and emphatic `lan` as
22:37. Hold both threads; don't pick one.

## 6. Where the science lands

Sources: [3-external/science-fasting-body.md](3-external/science-fasting-body.md),
[3-external/science-fasting-mind.md](3-external/science-fasting-mind.md).

- **The confounds are most of the story.** Sleep drops ~7.2h → ~6.4h and **debt compounds across
  consecutive days**; caffeine withdrawal hits ~55% of habitual users on day one; dehydration is
  measurable by late afternoon. Studies controlling sleep, light and schedule **do not support
  fasting itself impairing cognition**.
- **Autophagy is the most overclaimed part of fasting culture:** every specific-hour claim comes
  from rodent and cell-culture kinetics, and **no human study has tracked autophagic flux against
  fasting duration**.
- **Ego depletion failed replication** — 23 labs, N=2,141, no effect. "I wasn't intentional enough"
  restates the discredited model.
- **What replicated instead:** implementation intentions (d≈0.65 across 94 studies, re-confirmed
  across 642 tests in 2024), habit formation in a fixed context, situation selection over
  in-the-moment resistance, and communal ritual (synchrony predicting cohesion four months out).
- **The mid-fast trough is a known phase** — a metabolic gap between glycogen depletion and ketone
  adaptation. And expectancy shapes how an experience is *judged*, not the experience itself:
  exactly the trap of reading unremarkable days as personal failure.

## 7. What people actually report

Sources: [3-external/stories-fasting-experiences.md](3-external/stories-fasting-experiences.md)
(blogs and Substack, four traditions) and
[3-external/stories-reddit-accounts.md](3-external/stories-reddit-accounts.md) (~60 threads,
snippet-only via Brave Search; direct Reddit fetches were blocked).

- **Almost nobody who calls a fast transformative credits the abstaining.** They credit what got
  added into the emptied time: night prayer kept small and irregular, a trackable Qur'an schedule,
  deleted social media (four independent accounts, the most-replicated practice found), a single
  repeated duʿāʾ, a reframe of *why*. Reddit independently credits the same top item — night prayer
  paired with recitation.
- **The failure accounts converge in reverse** — not "I didn't try hard enough" but "something else
  filled the space I meant to fill": chores, illness, exhaustion, scrolling. Or the addition arrived
  "too late and too thin" to register.
- **Burnout is a named failure mode**, distinct from laziness: one writer increased worship
  dramatically, collapsed in the last ten days, and diagnosed it as "mistaking Ramadan for a magic
  pill that instantly transforms character flaws."
- **The blog corpus skews redemptive; Reddit does not.** Its threads are posted mid-struggle — "is
  this normal?", "I feel lost", "I feel nothing at all" — with no arc imposed afterwards, and it is
  the only source containing fasting performed under **coercion** rather than choice.
- **The most-cited single variable is a held "why"** — one writer attributes her worst Ramadan to
  lacking an understood *why*, not to physical difficulty. **One account dissents outright** (§8).

## 8. The convergence

Three streams: a 7th-century Arabic text analysed by root morphology; contemporary physiology and
psychology; hundreds of first-person accounts. The agents producing them did not read each other's
files, so where they agree, the agreement was not arranged.

**They agree on three things.**

1. **The abstention is not the active ingredient.** The text never praises hunger anywhere, promises
   no reward for fasting, attaches every experiential state it describes to the Book and never to
   the fast, and states outright at 22:37 that a ritual's physical substance does not reach God.
   The psychology finds no confound-isolated cognitive or mood benefit from food restriction alone,
   and locates sustained-attention gains in deliberate attention training, not caloric restriction.
   Neither account corpus — blog or Reddit, and they were gathered separately — credits the
   abstaining. **Three methods, one conclusion, none needing the others to reach it.**

2. **Structure decided in advance beats resolve in the moment.** The Quran leaves the contents
   unspecified, making them the faster's job and the only part that can actually fail; the one
   method it ever attaches to the taqwā-formula is 2:63's *take it* and *remember what is in it*,
   both pre-committed. The replicated psychology says the same in its own vocabulary: if-then plans
   at d≈0.65, environment design over willpower, an obituary for the resource model of self-control.
   The accounts say it a third way — shared trackers, fixed schedules, a decluttered calendar
   *beforehand* — and the failures say "too late and too thin."

3. **Consecutiveness is not the lever.** The text attaches unbroken sequence only to the two
   heaviest kaffārāt — killing a believer and ẓihār — and declines to require it for the Ramadan
   make-up (`ʿiddatun min ayyāmin ukhar`). The physiology says consecutive days actively degrade
   the felt state, because sleep and hydration debt compound rather than resetting nightly. **Text
   and physiology reach the same verdict on seven straight days from opposite directions**, neither
   knowing about the other.

**They disagree on one thing, and it is the sharpest disagreement here: community.** The psychology
treats collective or witnessed practice as a **major, not incidental, moderator** — synchrony
predicting cohesion months later, costly-signalling theory holding that the display's psychological
work depends on an audience. **The Quran declines to require it.** `jmE` is absent from the passage
and from all 133 `Swm`-adjacent roots; the command to fast is grammatically singular; there is no
ʿīd, no `fTr`, no instruction to break the fast together — and equally, no instruction to withdraw.
The accounts split: converts describe Ramadan as "a traditionally lonely time," while one revert
fasting alone under lockdown found the stripped-down version *more* intimate — "just me, my hunger,
my thirst, and my Lord" — "precisely because there was no cultural performance to hide inside."
**Unresolved: the science prescribes something the text does not require and the accounts do not
agree about.** (Almost everything communal about Ramadan as practised comes from the later
tradition — see §9.)

**And one stream dissents from conclusion (1) — worth preserving rather than smoothing.** A secular
seven-day water-faster reports mental clarity arriving by day 3 **with no added practice at all**;
his only named method was sitting with the discomfort undistracted rather than filling the gap with
a substitute. That is a direct counter-case to the addition thesis and should not be filed as
noise. **[interp]** It cuts both ways: day-3 easing is exactly what the metabolic literature
predicts from ketone adaptation, so his timing has a physiological explanation requiring no
spiritual mechanism at all. Whether that vindicates the dissent or dissolves it, this investigation
cannot say.

## 9. Open questions

- **Is the container/content thread more than a pattern?** The Quran states fasting's purpose,
  states repeatedly what that purpose is normally produced by, grounds the month in exactly that
  thing — and never joins them. Nine of twelve is a strong pattern, not a stated mechanism, and
  should not be quoted as one.
- **Does `taṭawwaʿ` at 2:184 mean extra fasting or extra feeding?** Three of four translators refuse
  the referent; only Khattab commits, and he commits to giving. Position and the 2:158 twin push
  toward feeding. Genuinely open — and it determines whether the Quran licenses voluntary fasting
  from food at all.
- **What does the hadith corpus add?** Explicitly out of scope here. Everything the tradition places
  inside a fast — tarāwīḥ, khatm, dhikr, nightly duʿāʾ, niyya, ʿīd al-fiṭr, suḥūr, the "month of
  ṣabr" epithet, the six of Shawwāl — is built on ground this text left bare. That is a statement
  about *where the authority for a fast's contents comes from*, not a criticism of the tradition.
  This investigation cannot adjudicate it and did not try.
- **Why is 2:186 there?** The placement is measurable; the causal story is the most interpretive
  move in the whole file set, and 2:186 never mentions fasting.
- **Two weaker questions, flagged and left:** whether the horizontal/vertical invariant generalises
  beyond its two cases; whether fasting's absence from 2:177 is load-bearing.

## 10. What this changes

1. **The hunger is not the offering.** 22:37 says the substance does not travel; 35:10 says the word
   ascends and the deed only lifts it. A fast with nothing said in it is a crane with no cargo.
2. **Furnish the hours before entering them.** The part the text leaves to you is the only part that
   can fail — and its one attached method, *take it and remember what is in it*, is decided in
   advance.
3. **Name the threat.** `Ittaqā` is transitive 166 times; a shield with nothing to shield against is
   not doing what the root describes.
4. **Stop measuring in days.** Consecutiveness is penalty-language in the text and debt-accumulation
   in the physiology. One furnished day beats seven empty ones on every reading here.
5. **Feeling nothing was never evidence against the text.** Four `laʿalla` clauses in five verses,
   no reward promised, no experience described. The apparatus was built correctly; nothing was
   loaded onto it — the most fixable failure in this investigation.
