# Concept: wqy (و ق ي) — taqwā

## Headline finding

`wqy` occurs 258 times (166 as the verb `ittaqā`, only 17 as the noun `taqwā`). Its base form `waqā` (16x) means to GUARD/SHIELD — used physically for God shielding people from the Fire (66:6, 52:27, 3:16, 40:9). Taqwā is a protective posture, not a feeling; most English glosses ("piety," "righteousness," "God-consciousness") lose this. 2:177's own definition of the muttaqūn is belief + charity + prayer + zakat + covenant-keeping + patience — **fasting is not in it.** 22:37 states plainly that ritual flesh doesn't reach God, taqwā does. Taqwā is grammatically a repeated VERB, not a possessed state.

The Meccan/Medinan split is nearly even — 116 Meccan occurrences, 142 Medinan, across 63 surahs. This is a diffuse, pervasive concept, not a legislative one concentrated in Medina the way `Swm` is (13/14 Medinan). Taqwā is preached from the earliest Meccan material onward.

## 1. The literal sense: waqā = guard, shield, protect from

`waqā`'s 16 occurrences are overwhelmingly literal-protective, most with God as the shielding agent and *punishment* (the Fire, evil consequences, torment) as the thing shielded against:

- **66:6**: "guard yourselves and your families from a Fire whose fuel is people and stones" — an active, ongoing duty
- **52:27**: "Allah shielded us from the punishment of the Scorching Fire"
- **3:16**: "forgive us our sins and shield us from the punishment of the Fire"
- **40:9**: "shield them from evil deeds; whoever You shield from evil deeds that Day — You have given him mercy"
- **76:11, 44:56, 52:18, 40:7, 40:45, 59:9, 64:16, 16:81, 2:201, 3:191** — same pattern: a barrier interposed between a person and harm.

The four translations of this same root, in the same protective sense, converge cleanly: saheeh/khattab render "protect," arberry "guard," pickthall "guard"/"ward off." No translator reaches for "piety" here — the physical/legal sense is unambiguous. Divergence appears only once the root shifts into its abstract noun/verb-of-disposition uses (`taqwā`, `ittaqā`), where saheeh and khattab drift to "righteous(ness)"/"mindful," arberry to "godfearing"/"godliness," pickthall to "devotion"/"ward off (evil)" — four English strategies for one Arabic root, because English has no single word that keeps "shield" alive as a moral term. **Piety, righteousness, and God-consciousness all describe an internal quality. Waqā never does — it always describes an action of interposing protection between a person and a threat.** Taqwā is what you have when you have built that shield; it is a defensive stance, not a mood.

```
Ar:  يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ قُوٓاْ أَنفُسَكُمۡ وَأَهۡلِيكُمۡ نَارࣰا
     "O you who have believed, guard/protect yourselves and your families from a Fire" — 66:6
saheeh: protect | khattab: protect | arberry: guard | pickthall: ward off (from)
```

The imperative `qū` here is the bare root command — literally "shield" — addressed to the same believers who are commanded to fast in 2:183. The grammatical shape of taqwā-as-instruction is identical: an active thing you do to interpose a barrier, not a state you fall into.

## 2. What taqwā is measured by — and what it excludes

2:177 is the Quran's own extended definition of "al-birr" (true righteousness/piety), and it closes by naming the people it describes as `al-muttaqūn` — the ones with taqwā:

```
Ar (excerpt): لَّيۡسَ ٱلۡبِرَّ أَن تُوَلُّواْ وُجُوهَكُمۡ ... وَلَٰكِنَّ ٱلۡبِرَّ مَنۡ ءَامَنَ بِٱللَّهِ
              وَٱلۡيَوۡمِ ٱلۡأٓخِرِ ... وَءَاتَى ٱلۡمَالَ عَلَىٰ حُبِّهِۦ ذَوِي ٱلۡقُرۡبَىٰ وَٱلۡيَتَٰمَىٰ
              وَٱلۡمَسَٰكِينَ ... وَأَقَامَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَءَاتَى ٱلزَّكَوٰةَ وَٱلۡمُوفُونَ بِعَهۡدِهِمۡ ...
              وَٱلصَّٰبِرِينَ فِي ٱلۡبَأۡسَآءِ وَٱلضَّرَّآءِ ... وَأُوْلَٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلۡمُتَّقُونَ
saheeh:   "...righteousness is [in] one who believes... gives wealth... establishes prayer and
          gives zakah... fulfill their promise... patient in poverty and hardship... those
          are the righteous."
khattab:  "...the righteous are those who believe... give charity... establish prayer, pay
          alms-tax, keep pledges... patient in suffering... it is they who are mindful [of
          Allah]."
```

The list, itemized: (1) belief — in Allah, the Last Day, angels, the Book, the prophets; (2) giving wealth to relatives, orphans, the needy, travelers, beggars, and for freeing captives; (3) establishing prayer; (4) paying zakat; (5) keeping covenants; (6) patience (`Sabr`) in poverty, hardship, and battle. That is the entire inventory. **Fasting does not appear in 2:177 at all** — not `Swm`, not any synonym for hunger or food-restraint. The verse's divergence score is high (0.7503) because translators visibly strain to hold "birr" and "taqwā" apart in English while the Arabic uses them almost interchangeably here — a second signal this word resists a single gloss. This is data-grounded: the passage is explicitly built as an exhaustive definition ("al-birr is not X, but rather al-birr is [this list]"), and food-abstention is absent from a passage whose whole function is to be a checklist.

**Interpretive note:** this doesn't mean fasting is irrelevant to taqwā — 2:183 explicitly links them. It means the Quran's canonical definition of a person WITH taqwā is built almost entirely from other material: money, prayer, promise-keeping, endurance under hardship. If fasting isn't producing any of those, by this definition-passage it isn't producing taqwā yet, regardless of hunger endured.

## 3. 22:37 — the hinge verse

Said of sacrificial animals, but stated as a general principle about ritual and what actually reaches God:

```
Ar:  لَن يَنَالَ ٱللَّهَ لُحُومُهَا وَلَا دِمَآؤُهَا وَلَٰكِن يَنَالُهُ ٱلتَّقۡوَىٰ مِنكُمۡ
saheeh:   "Their meat will not reach Allah, nor will their blood, but what reaches Him is
          piety from you."
arberry:  "The flesh of them shall not reach God, neither their blood, but godliness from
          you shall reach Him."
pickthall: "Their flesh and their food reach not Allah, but the devotion from you reacheth
          Him."
```

`lan yanāl` — "will never reach/attain" — is emphatic negation, twice, before the pivot `wa-lākin` ("but rather"). The physical substance of the ritual (meat, blood) is explicitly and doubly denied any transit to God. What transits is `al-taqwā minkum` — "the taqwā from you" — grammatically the *only* subject of `yanāluhu` ("reaches Him") in the whole verse.

Applied to fasting by the same logic, since `Swm` is structurally the same kind of physical ritual act as sacrifice: the hunger itself does not reach God. What "reaches" from a fast — if anything does — is whatever guarding/shielding disposition (taqwā) the act of fasting produced or expressed in the person. Not the empty stomach; the shield it was meant to build. This is the single clearest verse in the corpus for stating the ritual/substance-vs-taqwā relationship directly, and it generalizes cleanly to 2:183's own logic: fasting is prescribed, `laʿallakum tattaqūn` — so that taqwā might result — not because the abstention itself is the payload.

## 4. The grammar of 2:183 — laʿalla + imperfect

`laʿallakum tattaqūn` ("so that you may attain taqwā" / "perhaps you will guard yourselves") occurs **exactly 6 times** in the Quran, verbatim:

| Ref | Attached to |
|---|---|
| 2:21 | worship of God as creator |
| 2:63 | holding firmly to the covenant/scripture given at Sinai |
| 2:179 | the law of retribution (`qiSaS`) — "in retribution there is life" |
| 2:183 | fasting |
| 6:153 | following God's straight path (the whole ethical code of 6:151–153: no shirk, kindness to parents, no killing children from poverty, no unjust killing, honest weights, just speech, fulfilling covenants) |
| 7:171 | holding firmly to the covenant given at Sinai (near-duplicate of 2:63) |

`laʿalla` is a particle of hope/expectation, not certainty — closer to "perhaps" than a guaranteed-outcome clause. It does not promise taqwā as fasting's automatic output; it frames fasting as aimed at a hoped-for result — a result also attached to five other, unrelated instruments: bare worship, gripping scripture, a criminal-law principle, and an entire ethical code. **Fasting is one of at least six things the Quran frames as possibly producing taqwā — not the unique or privileged route.** If a week of fasting alone didn't build it, the other five instruments (belief, covenant-fidelity, justice, the ethical code of 6:151-153) may be the missing pieces, not "fast harder."

```
Ar:  يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ كُتِبَ عَلَيۡكُمُ ٱلصِّيَامُ كَمَا كُتِبَ عَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ مِن
     قَبۡلِكُمۡ لَعَلَّكُمۡ تَتَّقُونَ
saheeh:  "...fasting as it was decreed upon those before you that you may become righteous"
khattab: "...as it was for those before you—so perhaps you will become mindful [of Allah]"
```

Khattab's "perhaps" is the more literal rendering of `laʿalla`; saheeh's "that...may" softens the hope into near-purpose. Neither implies guarantee.

## 5. State or practice?

`ittaqā` (166x, 100% verbal) dwarfs `taqwā` (17x, nominal). The verb almost always takes an object: `ittaqū Allāh` (guard yourselves against Allah/before Allah), `ittaqū al-nār` (guard yourselves against the Fire), `ittaqū yawman` (guard yourselves against a Day). It is repeated, imperative, transitive — a thing done again, toward something, not a badge acquired once. `muttaqīn` (49x) is a participle — "those who are [currently, ongoingly] guarding" — not a completed status. The noun `taqwā` shows up mostly as a *quality of provision* ("the best provision is taqwā," 2:197) or an *object one is clothed in/brings with*, but it is derivative of the verb, not prior to it. **Data-grounded conclusion: taqwā is overwhelmingly something you DO, repeatedly, against a named threat — not something you HAVE.** There is no verse in this dataset where taqwā is described as arriving passively or as a static possession independent of the guarding-action that produces it.

## For the fast

- Ask "what am I guarding against, right now, with this hunger?" not "do I feel pious yet?" — `waqā`'s object (Fire, evil deeds, punishment) is always named; a fast without a named threat is not doing what the root describes.
- By 2:177, check the other five: did today's fast come with charity, prayer, kept promises, or patience under real hardship? Hunger alone is not yet taqwā by the Quran's own definition-passage.
- Apply 22:37 at iftar: ask what "reached" God today — not that you were hungry, but what disposition the hunger produced or tested.
- Taqwā is not a mood to chase. It's a repeated act (`ittaqā`) — treat each fasting day as one guarding-act among many required, not a single feeling to arrive at by day seven.
- Fasting is one of six things scripture hopes builds this — not sufficient alone. If it isn't working, pair it with covenant-keeping and patience under hardship, not just longer hunger.
