# The container of the fast: month, night, retreat

## Headline finding (100 words)

Ramadan is defined by a revelation event, not a discipline: 2:185 names the month *because* the Quran was sent down in it — the fast is attached to the month, not the reverse. The night the month exists to mark, *laylat al-qadr* (97:1-5, 44:1-6), is described entirely in vocabulary of descent, decree, and peace — angels, the Rūḥ, "peace until the rising of dawn" — with zero occurrences of the fasting root. The summit of Ramadan is nocturnal and non-fasting. `qdr` splits between decree and power across translators; `Ekf` (retreat) is the same word used for clinging to idols — neutral, defined by its object.

---

## 1. Why Ramadan — the reason is a revelation, not a season

2:185 states the cause plainly, in the very first clause, before any instruction about fasting appears:

| Ref | Arabic | Saheeh | Khattab | Arberry | Pickthall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2:185 | شَهْرُ رَمَضَانَ ٱلَّذِىٓ أُنزِلَ فِيهِ ٱلْقُرْءَانُ | "The month of Ramadhan [is that] in which was revealed the Qur'an" | "Ramaḍân is the month in which the Quran was revealed" | "the month of Ramadan, wherein the Koran was sent down" | "The month of Ramadan in which was revealed the Qur'an" |

Grammatically, `$ahor Ramadan` is the subject, and the relative clause `alladhī unzila fīhi al-Qurʾān` is what identifies it — the naming clause of the whole passage is a revelation clause. Only *after* this does the verse move to the command (`fa-man shahida minkumu al-shahr fal-yaṣumh` — "whoever witnesses the month, let him fast it"). The fast is a response to the month's status, and the month's status is that the Quran happened in it. Nowhere in 2:183-187 does the text ground the fast in the season, in hunger as discipline, or in an independent virtue of restraint — the causal chain runs revelation → month → fast, not fast → nearness → revelation. This inverts the common framing (fast to get close to God) with the text's own framing (this month is marked because God already acted in it; the fast honors *that*).

Data-grounded: the syntax of 2:185 — subject-then-relative-clause naming pattern. Interpretive: that this "inverts how most people hold it" — that's a reading, stated here because the text supports it structurally, not because the text says so explicitly.

## 2. 97:1-5 and 44:1-6 — the night has no fasting vocabulary

Checked directly against both passages: **`Swm` (the fasting root) does not occur in 97:1-5 or 44:1-6.** Neither does any hunger/food/restraint vocabulary. What the text actually says happens on the night:

| Ref | Arabic | Saheeh |
|---|---|---|
| 97:1 | إِنَّآ أَنزَلْنَٰهُ فِى لَيْلَةِ ٱلْقَدْرِ | "Indeed, We sent the Qur'an down during the Night of Decree." |
| 97:4 | تَنَزَّلُ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ وَٱلرُّوحُ فِيهَا بِإِذْنِ رَبِّهِم مِّن كُلِّ أَمْرٍ | "The angels and the Spirit descend therein by permission of their Lord for every matter." |
| 97:5 | سَلَٰمٌ هِىَ حَتَّىٰ مَطْلَعِ ٱلْفَجْرِ | "Peace it is until the emergence of dawn." |

97:3 adds `khayrun min alfi shahr` — better than a thousand months — the night outranks the very unit (`$hr`) that structures the fast. 44:3-6 covers the same event from a different angle: `laylatin mubāraka` (a blessed night), `fīhā yufraqu kullu amrin ḥakīm` (every precise matter is made distinct), and closes on `raḥmatan min rabbik` — mercy. Every verb in both passages is either descent (`tanazzalu` / `unzila`) or a state (`salām`, `mubāraka`, `khayr`). None is abstention.

**This is the direct answer to the question this file was assigned to test: the night that gives Ramadan its meaning, per 97 and 44, is described in terms of descent and peace, and it is a night — the one time of day nobody in Ramadan is fasting.** The claim holds against the text as written; it is not an inference from silence elsewhere, it is the absence of the root in the two passages that are explicitly *about* that night.

## 3. `qdr` is polysemous — decree, measure, and power in one root

`qdr` occurs 132 times, 11 lemmas. The two dominant lemmas pull in different directions: `qadiyr` (all-powerful, 45x) and `qaAdir` (capable, 14x) anchor a **power/capacity** sense; `qadara`/`qad~ara` (to decree, apportion, measure out, 25+16x) and `qador` itself (measure/worth/decree, 7x — the lemma used at 97:1-3) anchor a **decree/measuring-out** sense. `laylat al-qadr` sits on the seam between them, and the translators split exactly there:

| Ref | Saheeh | Khattab | Arberry | Pickthall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 97:1 | "Night of **Decree**" | "Night of **Glory**" | "Night of **Power**" | "Night of **Predestination**" |

Four translations, four different English nouns for the same two Arabic words — none of them agreeing even loosely. This is not translator carelessness: 97:1 carries a divergence score of **0.7748**, the 4th-highest in the entire fasting verse set (behind only 2:197, 2:189, 5:95 — all legal/definitional verses). Saheeh and Pickthall lean toward apportionment/fate; Arberry and Khattab lean toward majesty/worth. The root genuinely supports both — `qador` as "measure" (a thing is measured out that night) and the same lemma's semantic neighborhood in "worth/rank" (a night of high standing). **This file does not resolve which reading is correct — the ambiguity is in the root itself, and the translators are showing you where the seam is, not failing to find one right answer.**

Data-grounded: lemma counts, divergence score. Interpretive: the "decree vs. power" framing of the split.

## 4. `Ekf` — the same word for the calf and for the retreat

`Ekf` occurs 9 times, 3 lemmas, and the concept file shows the pattern cleanly. Excluding 2:187 itself, the remaining 8 occurrences split: **5 are unambiguously about idol-devotion** — the Egyptians "devoted to idols" they encounter after the sea (`yaEkufuna Ealaa aSnaamin lahum`, 7:138), Abraham's father's community "cleaving" to statues (`Ealayha EaAkifuwn`, 21:52), and twice for the Israelites at the calf: "we will not cease being devoted to it" (20:91) and "your god to which you remained devoted" (20:97) — and 26:71 repeats the pattern with Abraham again. The other 2 non-Ramadan uses are positive or neutral: 2:125, purifying the Kaaba for "those who circle it and those who are devoted" (`al-Aaakifiyn`), and 22:25 on equal access for "the resident" (`al-Aaakif`) of the sacred mosque. (48:25 uses a different derived form, `maEokuwf`, meaning "detained/prevented" — a distinct sense, sacrificial animals blocked from reaching their place.)

So the same lemma, `EaAkif`, denotes both the worst thing in the Quran's polemic against idolatry (attachment to a golden calf) and the most commended form of ritual retreat (2:187's `wa-antum EaAkifuwna fiy al-masaAjid` — "while you are secluded in the mosques"). **The root does not encode virtue or vice. It encodes staying-put, clinging, attaching yourself to a thing** — and the moral content comes entirely from the object of attachment, not the act of attaching. I'tikaf in Ramadan is not virtuous because retreat is inherently good; it is the same psychological posture the Quran condemns when the object is a statue, redirected at the right object.

Data-grounded: the 9-occurrence breakdown by lemma and context. Interpretive: "the root encodes staying-put, not virtue" — a reading of the pattern, but a hard one to argue against given the identical lemma across both contexts.

## 5. Night-vigil vocabulary — sleep, not food

The Quran's most vivid pictures of devotional intensity are consistently nocturnal and about *waking*, never about eating: `qumi al-layla illā qalīlā` (73:2, "stand the night, except a little"); `nāshiʾata al-layl` (73:6, the night's "rising hour," described as heavier in impression); `fa-tahajjad bihi nāfilatan lak` (17:79, extra prayer at night); and most concretely, 51:17-18: `kaanuw qaliylan mina al-layli maa yahjaEuwn wa-bi-l-asHaari hum yastagfiruwn` — "they used to sleep but little of the night, and in the hours before dawn they would seek forgiveness." 32:16 adds the physical image: `tatajaafaa junuwbuhum Eani al-madaajiEi` — "their sides forsake their beds" — restlessness toward prayer, not toward food.

`al-asHaar` (51:18) is the plural of `saHar`, the pre-dawn hour — the exact window of *suhūr*, the meal fasters eat before the fast begins. The Quran's praised worshippers are already awake, already in that hour, seeking forgiveness — for reasons that have nothing to do with fasting. Someone observing Ramadan is structurally present at the hour these verses commend; whether they use it the way 51:18 describes (`yastaghfirūn`, seeking forgiveness) or simply eat and go back to sleep is the difference between coinciding with the vocabulary and enacting it.

## 6. 2:189 — the crescents and the formalism rebuke

`yasʾalūnaka ʿani al-ahillah` — they ask about the crescents. The answer, `hiya mawāqītu li-l-nāsi wa-l-ḥajj` ("they are time-markers for people and for pilgrimage"), is entirely functional — no cosmology, no sanctity claimed for the moon itself, just a timekeeping device. `hll` occurs only 5 times total; the crescent sense (`ahillah`) is unique to this verse — the root's other 4 occurrences (`>uhila`, 2:173, 5:3, 6:145, 16:115) are about meat consecrated aloud to something other than Allah, a different application of the same "audible proclamation" root.

The verse then pivots, without transition, to `wa-laysa al-birru bi-an taʾtuw al-buyuwta min DHuhuwrihaa wa-laakinna al-birra mani-ttaqaa` — "righteousness is not entering houses from the back, but righteousness is he who has taqwā." 2:189 carries a divergence score of **0.7937** — the second-highest in the entire fasting set, higher even than 97:1. A question about ritual timing gets an answer that immediately refuses to let timing become the point — the same move this whole file has been tracing: the container (month, night, crescent) is functional/derivative, and the substance (revelation, peace, taqwā) is what the text insists on. 2:189 makes that insistence explicit and confrontational: it names the wrong move (a technically-correct ritual gesture — entering from the back door — mistaken for righteousness) in the same breath it answers the calendar question.

Data-grounded: `hll` occurrence count and lemma split; divergence score. Interpretive: reading the pivot as thematically continuous with 2:185/97/2:187 — the text does juxtapose them, but "the point" is my synthesis.

## For the fast

- The night matters more than the month, and the night has nothing to do with hunger — if a Ramadan has no attention paid to its nights (esp. the last ten), the fast has skipped its own stated summit.
- `Ekf` says the retreat isn't inherently good — ask what you're actually attached to during it, not whether you retreated.
- Suhūr sits inside `al-asḥār`, the hour 51:18 praises for seeking forgiveness — that's a use for the hour beyond eating before the fast starts.
- 2:189's rebuke is a standing check: is the ritual gesture (correct hours, correct entry) substituting for the thing itself (taqwā)?
