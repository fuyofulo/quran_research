# What science says about fasting and the mind

**Headline (100 words):** The cognitive-science literature on fasting is weaker and more confounded than popular accounts suggest — most "fasting impairs/improves the brain" claims from Ramadan studies are actually sleep-deprivation, caffeine-withdrawal, or dehydration effects riding along with the fast, not the fast itself (established as a confound, contested as to fasting's independent effect). Mood flattening and irritability on a multi-day fast are metabolically and behaviourally plausible but not proven to be purely physiological. Meanwhile the two literatures that most directly explain why a solo, unstructured week "didn't work" — ego-depletion's replication collapse and the ritual/community research — are unambiguous, and both point the same direction: toward pre-committed structure and shared practice, not willpower.

---

## 1. Cognition during a fast: the confound is the whole story

The Ramadan cognitive-performance literature is large, inconsistent, and almost never isolates fasting from three co-travelling variables: **shortened/shifted sleep** (late-night meals, pre-dawn suhoor), **caffeine withdrawal**, and **dehydration** (not applicable to this water-permitted fast, but present in most of the source studies).

- A 2004 physiological review by Roky et al. found Ramadan fasting decreased nocturnal sleep, alertness, and psychomotor performance, but studies controlling for sleep/wake schedule, sleep duration, and light exposure **do not support** fasting itself impairing cognition — the effect tracks the sleep disruption, not the caloric restriction. (established confound; contested independent fasting effect) — [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15452402](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15452402/)
- Athlete studies designed to separate the two factors found sleep restriction, not fasting, drove reaction-time slowing and attentional lapses; fasting alone produced only mild effects. — [Ramadan sleep-loss study, athletes](https://avesis.atauni.edu.tr/yayin/28547fce-a24f-4f22-b913-54d4cea36375/ramadans-hidden-challenge-sleep-deprivation-outweighs-hunger-in-athletes-brief-running-title-sleep-loss-outweighs-fasting-in-athletes-during-ramadan/document.pdf)
- Caffeine-withdrawal headache alone affects ~55% of habitual consumers on the first fasting day and independently produces reduced concentration, anxiety, and sleepiness — fully separable from fasting physiology, resolving within days. (established) — [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36942412](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36942412/), [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11279933](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11279933/)
- Dehydration's own cognitive cost is real but small: a meta-analysis of 33 studies found overall impairment of only d = −0.21 (95% CI −0.31 to −0.11); a stricter follow-up meta-analysis of 10 crossover studies found no significant effect. (contested, small where found) — [dehydration meta-analysis](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5357466/), [dehydration/mood trial](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6603652/)
- Where studies try to disentangle the strands, results are genuinely mixed within a single week: some find memory/vigilance *improve* by Ramadan's end (adaptation), others find week-four declines, others acute within-day dips that recover. None of this is a stable, well-replicated single-direction effect. (weak, inconsistent evidence) — [PMC9682201](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9682201/), [Indonesia sustained-attention field study](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02673843.2025.2526461)

**Bottom line:** there is no well-replicated, confound-isolated finding that multi-day food fasting alone meaningfully impairs or enhances attention, working memory, or reaction time in a healthy adult with normal sleep. What the literature *does* establish is that sleep disruption and caffeine withdrawal — both likely present in an unplanned week-long fast — degrade cognition on their own and get misattributed to "the fast."

## 2. Mood, irritability, and the "spiritual dryness" confound

Findings on mood are mixed in a way that matters directly for the commissioner's experience. A 2025 comparative study of Saudi medical students found anxiety and mood disturbance elevated mid-Ramadan relative to before and after, with recovery post-fast — a real, measured, day-4-ish trough. — [frontiersin.org/1570557](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1570557/full)

At the same time, a systematic review of 20 Ramadan studies found a majority reported *reduced* depression, anxiety, and stress over the full fasting period — the trajectory across many studies is a mid-fast dip followed by improvement, not a monotonic decline. — [Springer systematic review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44202-025-00553-y)

On mechanism: fasting can raise cortisol, mechanistically linked to irritability and harder emotional regulation (probable, not established as *the* driver of irritability from short fasts specifically) — [German 17–18h Ramadan cortisol/BDNF/mood study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8387581/). Anger/confusion measures in some cohorts return to baseline as the fast progresses, consistent with an adaptation window rather than a fixed cost.

**The careful claim, not the overclaim:** a flat, distant, irritable day 4 is *consistent with* a known, physiologically plausible mid-fast trough (sleep pattern shift, cortisol dynamics, caffeine withdrawal tail, possible mild glucose-availability effects on self-regulation — see §3). It is not established that this feeling is *purely* physiological, and it would be an equal and opposite error to tell someone their felt spiritual dryness is "just" biochemistry — the research doesn't license that inference either way. What the evidence supports is: **the trough is a known, time-limited, mechanistically plausible phase, not necessarily evidence of personal or spiritual failure**, and several literatures show it tends to lift with time-on-practice rather than needing more willpower.

## 3. Self-control research: ego depletion collapsed, here's what replaced it

This is the most directly load-bearing science for the commissioner's stated failure mode ("I wasn't intentional enough").

**Ego depletion did not hold up.** The "resource model" of willpower — self-control as depletable fuel that resisting food would drain, weakening later resolve — was one of social psychology's most cited findings and one of its biggest replication failures. A 23-lab, N=2,141 Registered Replication Report found **no effect**. — [PMC4971805](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4971805/), [replicationindex.com critique](https://replicationindex.com/2016/04/18/rr1egodepletion/). Michael Inzlicht, a central figure and originally a defender of the effect, now writes that ego depletion "has become the textbook example of how seductive ideas and questionable research practices can lead an entire field astray." — [Inzlicht, "The Collapse of Ego Depletion"](https://www.speakandregret.michaelinzlicht.com/p/the-collapse-of-ego-depletion), [Friese, Loschelder, Gieseler, Frankenbach & Inzlicht 2019](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1088868318762183) (established: the original resource model failed to replicate)

**What replaced it:** motivation- and attention-allocation accounts (self-control failure reflects shifting motivation/attention priorities, not a used-up tank), and a broader move toward **structural** solutions over in-the-moment resistance:

- **Implementation intentions** (Gollwitzer): pre-specifying "if situation X, then I will do Y" produces a medium-to-large effect on goal attainment — d = 0.65 across 94 studies/8,000+ participants originally, replicated at similar magnitude in a 2024 update covering 642 tests. The mechanism is not "more willpower" — the if-then plan makes the cue automatically trigger the response, removing the need for in-the-moment deliberation. — [Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/37367696_Implementation_Intentions_and_Goal_Achievement_A_Meta-Analysis_of_Effects_and_Processes), [2024 update, PMC8149892](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8149892/) (established, large, well-replicated)
- **Habit formation** (Lally et al.): daily repetition in a fixed context builds automaticity over a median of 66 days (range 18–254, varying by complexity) — the point where conscious self-control is no longer needed. Simple behaviors automatize faster than complex ones; missing one day doesn't derail the process. — [Lally et al. 2010, EJSP](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ejsp.674) (established) — a single unplanned week is roughly the front 10% of that curve, not enough for the practice itself to become self-sustaining.
- **Situation selection / habit discontinuity** (Wood & Neal): behaviour change succeeds more reliably by changing the *environment/cues* than by resisting temptation in an unchanged environment; disruption points (new context, new role) are unusually good moments to install new routines. — [Wood & Neal 2016](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/237946151600200109) (established design principle, effect sizes vary by intervention)

**Direct read on the commissioner's diagnosis:** "I wasn't intentional enough" restates the discredited resource model — as if there were a willpower tank he failed to fill. The literature that replaced ego depletion says the lever isn't more resolve, it's a specific if-then plan made in advance ("when I feel the 4pm irritability, I will do X, not just 'try harder'") plus environmental structure. That is a testable, actionable difference, not a rhetorical one.

## 4. Ritual research: does communal practice change the outcome?

Yes — this is one of the more robust findings in the set. Multiple independent lines converge:

- **Synchrony and collective effervescence** (Xygalatas et al.): in fire-walking and other communal rituals, participants' heart rates synchronize with each other, synchrony extends to non-performing spectators close to participants, and the degree of synchronization predicts social cohesion and well-being **up to 4 months later**. — [Xygalatas, AJHB 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ajhb.24111), [fire-walking study](https://journal.equinoxpub.com/FIR/article/view/13040) (probable — well-replicated within this research program, less so outside it)
- **Costly signaling / credibility-enhancing displays** (Henrich): costly, effortful practices like fasting function partly as commitment signals whose value depends on an *audience* that can observe, share, and validate the sacrifice. Solitary costly practice removes the social-commitment and group-cohesion machinery this literature identifies as doing much of the psychological work. — [Henrich, "Evolution of costly displays"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228353018_The_Evolution_of_Costly_Displays_Cooperation_and_Religion_Credibility_Enhancing_Displays_and_Their_Implications_for_Cultural_Evolution), [signaling review, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1090513824000692) (probable — theory well-supported, direct fasting-specific tests thinner)
- **Religious priming's prosocial effects are real but conditional** — worth naming the replication scare honestly: a widely cited "God is watching" priming effect failed a Japanese replication; a large meta-analysis (93 studies) found effects reliable mainly for people who already hold strong prior religious belief, vanishing for non-believers. (contested/moderated) — [Shariff, Willard, Andersen & Norenzayan 2016](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1088868314568811), [Japanese replication failure](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/ajsp.12164)

**Direct answer to the key practical question:** the evidence — synchrony research, costly-signaling theory, and the anthropology of ritual — converges on collective participation being a **major, not incidental, moderator** of what costly practice delivers psychologically (bonding, meaning, sustained motivation). A solitary, out-of-season fast forgoes most of this machinery — not because solitary devotion is invalid theologically (outside this file's scope), but because the mechanisms the science identifies as producing the felt "it worked" experience are substantially social ones.

## 5. Attention, silence, and contemplative practice

Meditation research suggests attentional benefits appear with *surprisingly little* practice — measurable sustained-attention gains after 4 weeks of ~20-minute sessions 3x/week, even after a single 10-minute session or a 3-day brief training reducing mind-wandering — but the largest, most durable shifts (documented in practitioners with 10,000+ hours) track cumulative practice, not one retreat-like week. — [4-week RCT, Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/aging/articles/10.3389/fragi.2024.1322705/full), [PMC11925505](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11925505/) (probable short-term; established only at the expert-practitioner end)

None of this is about *food restriction* — it's about deliberate, structured attention *training*, which fasting doesn't automatically supply. The digital-load literature is unambiguous on the adjacent point: habitual smartphone/notification use produces measurable, replicated costs to sustained attention and working memory through interruption and media multitasking. — [digital technology cognitive-effects review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.10025), [mobile phone presence and lecture recall](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9497664/) (established)

**The bridge claim the evidence supports:** removing food while keeping constant phone/screen input does not, by the mechanisms known in the attention literature, address the load that most degrades sustained attention and the experience of stillness. Fasting from food is not a substitute for the deliberate attention-training (or reduced interruption load) that the contemplative and digital-cognition literatures identify as the actual active ingredients for a "quieter mind."

## 6. Expectation and framing effects

Placebo/expectancy research on cognition finds a consistent dissociation: expectation strongly shapes **subjective** perception of performance but has little to no effect on **objective** performance in most paradigms — people who believe they're doing better often are not, measurably. — [Frontiers, placebo/nocebo in cognitive neuroenhancement](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2019.00498/full), [PLOS ONE, subjective vs actual performance](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0130492) (established)

This matters for someone who went in expecting transcendence: if the week produces ordinary subjective experience, expectancy research predicts a specific trap — a felt gap between expected and actual, read as personal failure rather than ordinary variance. This is structurally the **abstinence-violation / "what-the-hell" effect** from self-regulation research: a perceived shortfall against a self-set standard triggers guilt and reduced self-efficacy, predicting disengagement rather than recommitment — while self-compassion framing measurably reduces subsequent lapse behavior in controlled studies. — [abstinence violation effect overview](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Motivation_and_emotion/Book/2021/What_the_hell_effect), [self-forgiveness and regulation, PMC2804396](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2804396) (established pattern in addiction/dietary-lapse literature; direct test in religious-fasting contexts is thin — treat the transfer as probable, not established)

## For the fast

1. **Write if-then plans before the fast starts, not resolutions.** "If I feel the urge to complain at hour X, then I will [specific act]" outperforms "I will be more intentional" by a well-replicated d≈0.65 margin — the single best-supported lever in this literature. — [Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/37367696_Implementation_Intentions_and_Goal_Achievement_A_Meta-Analysis_of_Effects_and_Processes)
2. **Redesign the environment, don't rely on resisting it.** Remove/hide food cues rather than out-willpowering them in an unchanged setting — situation selection reliably beats in-the-moment resistance.
3. **Expect the mid-fast trough as a known phase, not a verdict.** Mood/irritability dips mid-fast in several cohort studies, recovering as the fast progresses — treat day 3–4 flatness as cortisol/sleep/caffeine dynamics, not a referendum on sincerity.
4. **Separate caffeine withdrawal from the fast deliberately** — taper beforehand, or expect a few days of withdrawal fog that is not spiritual and will pass.
5. **Protect sleep on purpose.** Sleep disruption, not fasting, drives most of the measured attention/reaction-time costs in this literature.
6. **Do it with people, or make it visible to people, next time.** The single most actionable finding here: synchrony and costly-signaling research show collective/witnessed practice does social work solitary practice structurally cannot — a shared meal, a check-in partner, or a community fast changes the mechanism, not just the mood.
7. **Add an actual attention practice — the fast alone won't produce one.** Sustained-attention gains come from deliberate practice, not caloric restriction; pair the fast with brief daily silence/meditation if the goal is a quieter mind.
8. **Cut the input load competing with stillness.** Reducing phone/notification interruption addresses attentional fragmentation directly; fasting from food while keeping constant screen time leaves that load untouched.
9. **Set expectations for ordinary, not transcendent, experience.** Expectancy shapes how an ordinary experience gets *judged*, not the experience itself — deciding "unremarkable" is a success condition defuses the abstinence-violation spiral.
10. **Don't treat a missed day or an unmet goal as proof it failed.** A single lapse doesn't damage the underlying process, but interpreting it as failure (the "what-the-hell" effect) is what actually derails practice.
