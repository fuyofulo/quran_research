# Fasting (ṣawm) — investigation index

The first **applied** investigation in this project. Commissioned with a practical problem rather
than a scholarly question: *I fasted seven consecutive days intending to get closer to Allah, and it
did not work — what does the Quran actually say fasting is for, what does science say it does to a
body and a mind, and what have people actually experienced?*

Two things make it structurally new. It ran a **non-Quranic evidence track in parallel** with the
textual one — peer-reviewed physiology and psychology, plus several hundred sourced first-person
accounts — and it ends in a **practice guide** rather than a synthesis alone. The three streams were
researched by agents that never read each other's work, then compared in §8 of the synthesis. That
agreement was not arranged, which is what makes it worth anything.

**If you read one file, read [`practice-guide.md`](practice-guide.md).**

---

## The headline

The Quran legislates the **emptying** of a block of time with unusual precision, and specifies
almost nothing to put inside it.

`Swm` occurs **14 times in the entire Quran**, across 11 ayahs and 6 surahs. Only five concern
Ramadan; six sit inside expiation or substitution ladders where fasting is the fallback for someone
who cannot free a person or feed the poor. Thirteen are Medinan — and the single Meccan occurrence,
19:26, is Maryam's fast **of speech**, in a verse commanding her to *eat and drink*. Of the five
imperatives in the whole fasting legislation, **four are permissions to eat, drink, and be with your
spouse**; the fifth says finish the fast.

Hunger (`jwE`) never appears in a fasting verse and is uniformly negative across all five of its
occurrences. No reward is promised for fasting anywhere. The intention-root `nwy` is not in the
Quran at all. **`Swm` and `Slw` share zero ayahs in the entire corpus.** 2:177's own definition of
the *muttaqūn* omits fasting; surahs 97 and 44 describe the night the month exists to mark and never
mention it. Every experiential state the Quran describes — shivering skin (39:23), trembling heart
(8:2), sides forsaking beds (32:16) — attaches to the Book, to remembrance, or to the night vigil,
**never to fasting**. Meanwhile the purpose-clause `laʿalla…tattaqūn` occurs twelve times, and
**nine of the twelve attach taqwā to received divine speech**, not to any physical act.

The text supplies the vacancy; the person supplies the occupancy — which makes the contents the one
part of a fast that is genuinely yours, and therefore the only part that can actually fail.

## Where the three streams agree, and where they don't

**They agree** that the abstention is not the active ingredient; that structure decided in advance
beats resolve in the moment; and that consecutiveness is not the lever — the text attaching unbroken
sequence only to the two heaviest expiations, the physiology finding that sleep and hydration debt
compound across consecutive days rather than resetting.

**They disagree about community.** The psychology treats collective or witnessed practice as a major
moderator. The Quran declines to require it — `jmE` is absent from the passage and from all 133
`Swm`-adjacent roots, the command to fast is grammatically singular, and there is no ʿīd, no `fTr`,
no communal iftar anywhere in the text. The accounts split. Unresolved.

**And one account dissents outright** — a secular water-faster who credits *adding nothing at all*.
Preserved rather than smoothed, and it cuts both ways.

## Scope and limits

This project reads the Quranic text directly and treats **hadith and classical tafsir as outside its
evidence base**. That boundary is unusually visible here: nearly everything the tradition places
inside a fast — tarāwīḥ, khatm al-Qurʾān, dhikr, niyya, ʿīd al-fiṭr, suḥūr — sits on ground the
Quran left bare. That is a statement about where the authority for a fast's *contents* comes from,
not a criticism of the tradition, and this investigation makes no attempt to adjudicate it.

Nothing here is a ruling.
