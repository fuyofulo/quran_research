## Headline finding

Fasting is one of the smallest vocabularies in the Quran attached to one of its largest
institutions. The root `Swm` (ص و م) occurs **14 times, in 11 ayahs, in 6 surahs** — fewer
occurrences than `xyT` ("thread") has letters. Nine of those 14 sit in legal-substitution
constructions. The annual fast gets **five verses total**, and one of the five (2:186) is not
about fasting at all. Meanwhile the roots the fasting passage actually keeps company with are
not abstinence-words: they are *knowing*, *finding*, *feeding*, *easing*, *nearness*, and
*answering*.

## 2. Ring, not centre: the shape of 2:183–187

I tested the common claim that 2:186 ("I am near…") sits at the passage's centre. **It does not —
neither by verse count nor by word count.** 2:186 is the *fourth* of five verses; the verse-count
centre is 2:185. The 172 words of the passage (QAC tokenization from
`data/morphology/words.jsonl`; orthographic word-counts run 2–3 higher per verse, which is why
`1-narratives/narrative-2-183-187.md` reports 175) distribute:

| Ayah | Words | Cumulative |
|---|---|---|
| 2:183 | 14 | 8.1% |
| 2:184 | 32 | 26.7% |
| 2:185 | 44 | 52.3% |
| **2:186** | **17** | 62.2% |
| 2:187 | 65 | 100% |

The midpoint word falls inside 2:185, and 2:186 is the fourth of five verses and the **shortest**
in the block. So the "2:186 is the literal centre" reading, which circulates widely, is not
supported by the text's own metrics. Record it as false and move on.

What *is* structurally real, and stronger:

- **The passage is a ring closed on taqwā.** `laʿallakum tattaqūn` ends 2:183; `laʿallahum
  yattaqūn` ends 2:187. `wqy` occurs exactly twice in the passage — at both ends and nowhere in
  between. The block is bracketed by its own stated purpose.
- **2:186 is the only non-legislative verse in the block.** 183 decrees, 184 exempts and prices,
  185 names and eases, 187 permits and bounds. 186 does none of these. It is a thematic
  intrusion into a legal passage — and the Quran did not have to put it there.
- **`qrb` (nearness) occurs exactly twice in the passage, both after the intrusion**: God is
  `qarīb` (near) at 2:186, and `fa-lā taqrabūhā` — do not approach [the limits] — at 2:187. The
  same root carries the divine proximity and the forbidden proximity, seventeen words apart.
- **`jwb` (answering) appears only in 2:186**, twice over: `ujību` (I answer) and `fal-yastajībū
  lī` (so let them answer Me). A two-way verb, used in both directions, inside the fasting block.

**[interpretive]** The ring is the finding. The passage does not argue that fasting produces
taqwā; it *encloses* the mechanics of fasting inside two statements of the purpose, and lets one
verse about calling and being answered stand inside the enclosure without explanation.

## 3. What `Swm` keeps company with

Roots sharing an ayah with `Swm`, ranked (excluding grammatical high-frequency items `Alh`,
`kwn`, `nws`):

| Root | Ayahs | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| `Amn` | 6 | believe |
| `ywm` | 5 | day |
| `TEm` | 4 | **feeding** |
| `Elm` | 4 | **knowing** |
| `skn` | 4 | the needy (*miskīn*) |
| `byn` | 4 | making clear |
| `wjd` | 4 | **finding / being able to afford** |
| `wqy`, `mrD`, `Edd`, `hdy`, `$hr`, `Ahl`, `Sdq`, `kfr` | 3 each | taqwā, illness, counting, guidance, month, family, charity, covering |
| `fdy`, `sfr`, `ysr`, `Efw`, `twb`, `$kr`, `kml`, `TwE`, `Hdd` | 2 each | ransom, travel, ease, pardon, repentance, gratitude, completing, volunteering, limits |

Three of the top seven — `TEm` (feed), `skn` (the needy), `wjd` (find/afford) — are about
**other people's stomachs and your ability to pay for them**. `Sdq` (charity) is at three.
The vocabulary that clusters around Quranic fasting is redistributive, not ascetic.

**Absences, verified against the full 132-root co-occurrence list for `Swm`:**

- **`jwE` (hunger) — zero shared ayahs.** The Quran never mentions hunger in a fasting verse.
- **`nwy` (intention/niyya) — zero. The root does not occur in the Quran at all.** There is no
  Quranic command to form an intention before fasting.
- **`Ajr` (reward) and `gfr` (forgiveness) — one shared ayah each, both 33:35**, where they are
  promised to the *entire* ten-item list, not to fasting. **No Quranic verse promises a reward
  for fasting specifically.**

The only stated outcome anywhere in the corpus is 2:183 / 2:187's `laʿallakum tattaqūn` — and
`laʿalla` expresses hope, not guarantee.

| 2:186 | 0.728 | `qarīb`, `daʿwa`, `yastajībū` |

Two of the twelve highest-divergence verses in the entire fasting corpus are the two verses that
*name the thing*: 2:183 (what fasting is FOR) and 19:26 (what a fast IS). The English tradition
has no stable rendering for either `taqwā` or `ṣawm`.

Note also **2:197** at the very top: `wa-tazawwadū fa-inna khayra al-zādi al-taqwā` — "take
provision, for the best provision is taqwā." A verse about travel-rations, in the middle of the
pilgrimage rules, redefines provision as taqwā. The same word 2:183 gives as the purpose of
going without provisions.

## For the fast

- The Quran gives the annual fast five verses. You can hold the entire textual basis of the
  practice in your head. Read 2:183–187 in Arabic once before a fast; it takes four minutes.
- There is no Quranic reward promised for fasting alone, and no Quranic intention-formula. If
  a fast is going to be *about* something, the text leaves you to supply it.
- The words that surround fasting in the Quran are about feeding people, affording things, and
  the needy. If a fast contains no giving, it is missing the vocabulary the Quran built around it.
- 2:183 and 2:187 both end on taqwā. Whatever else a fast is, the text brackets it with a purpose
  twice — and states it as a hope, not a promise.
