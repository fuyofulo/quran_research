The Fast Planner

The Quran specifies the emptying of a fast in detail and leaves the filling almost entirely unspecified — which makes the filling the one part that can actually fail. A fast that goes nowhere usually isn't a fast done badly. It's a fast that was never furnished. This is the furnishing, done in advance.

Set input goals, not outcome goals.

2:183 says fasting is prescribed “so that perhaps you may build taqwā.” Four “perhaps” clauses across five verses. The Quran frames the outcome as a hope — so the outcome is not yours to commit to. The inputs are.

Every question below asks about something you control and can answer with one word. If you can't check it off, it isn't a goal yet — it's a wish.

Part one

Before

A · The gate

Diabetes, kidney disease, pregnancy, a history of disordered eating, or relevant medication all mean talk to a doctor first. This is the one question that can end the form.

Uncomfortable but honest. In the Quran's own expiation ladders, fasting is repeatedly what you do when you can't free someone or feed people. If there's a person you could actually help this week, the text ranks that above the fast. Do both, or do that first.

B · The why

“To get closer to Allah” is the category, not the answer. Push one level down: closer how? What would be different about you on the other side? Maryam's fast in 19:26 had a concrete target — an accusation she couldn't answer. Hunger without a target is just hunger.

The most important question on this form. Taqwā comes from a root meaning to shield, to guard from — and in the Quran the verb almost always names what it guards against. A shield with nothing in front of it isn't doing anything. Name one habit, one drift, one direction you're going that you don't want to be going.

How long, and why that length?

The Quran gives no number for a voluntary fast. That's freedom, not an invitation to add days. Consecutive fasting is penalty-language in the text, and sleep debt compounds across days — so “more is better” isn't a reason.

Set it low and concrete. “I did the four things I wrote down, on most days.” Deciding in advance that unremarkable counts as success is what stops one flat day becoming proof the whole thing failed. Do not write a feeling here.

C · The contents — furnishing the room

The only method the Quran ever attaches to building taqwā is 2:63's two commands: take what We have given you, and remember what is in it. Take it, then hold it. One short passage held all day beats an ambitious reading plan dropped on day two.

What will I say to Him, and at what time?

2:185 names the fast's own outputs as declaring God's greatness and thanking Him — both things you say. 35:10 says the word ascends and the deed only lifts it. A fast with nothing said in it is a crane with an empty hook. Fix the time so it doesn't depend on remembering.

2:186 sits inside the fasting rules: “I am near. I answer the one who calls Me. So let them answer Me.” His side is stated as already happening. Yours is a command still outstanding. Most of us treat a fast as asking. Book the responding.

What am I giving — amount, person, day?

3:92 is the only place the Quran states a precondition for reaching goodness: “you will never attain it until you spend from what you love.” From what you love, so it should cost. Not “some charity” — a number, a name, a date.

The hunger is the physical part — it stays here. What changes the hours is what stops competing for your attention. Across the first-person accounts, cutting phone and social media was the single most-repeated concrete practice. Also consider Maryam's version: a fast of speech — she was told to eat and drink in the same verse.

D · The if-thens — the proven lever

Write these as “When X happens, I will do Y.” Not resolutions. Pre-committed if-then plans are one of the most reliably replicated findings in behaviour change; “I'll be more intentional” is a restatement of the willpower model that 23 laboratories failed to reproduce. Be embarrassingly specific.

When
I will

For the hardest hour — usually 10–16 hours in. That dip is metabolic, not a verdict on you.

When
I will

For the phone. Better still: put it somewhere else before dawn so the trigger never fires.

When
I will

For the moment you want to complain about it, or be seen doing it.

E · The people

The research on ritual finds that doing this alongside others — or where someone simply knows — changes how much it lands. Fasting alone and out of season is a structural disadvantage, not a flaw in you. One check-in partner is enough. Note: this is the one point where the science and the Quran disagree — the text requires neither company nor solitude.

F · The body — so you can do any of the above

Caffeine withdrawal is the most preventable source of fasting misery — taper, don't quit on day one. And sleep debt compounds across consecutive days and isn't repaid overnight: it is the most underrated variable in this entire body of research, and the one most often mistaken for spiritual dryness.

Protein and slow carbohydrates, not sugar and pastries. Add salt — sodium loss drives the headaches and cramps people blame on the fast itself.

Spread it. Don't drink the day's water in the last thirty minutes before dawn.

G · The exits — pre-authorised

Not weakness — design. The fasting passage carries nine clauses giving relief against six placing restrictions, and states the illness and travel exemptions twice, word for word. 2:185 says outright: God intends ease for you and does not intend hardship. Writing the exit in advance also stops the spiral where one broken day gets read as total failure and the whole thing gets abandoned.

Part two

During

Keep this tiny. Heavy logging is the first thing to go — four marks a day, sixty seconds.

Day Passage Said Gave What actually filled the hours?

That last column is the one that matters. In the first-person accounts, the fasts that failed almost never say “I didn't try hard enough.” They say something else filled the space I meant to fill — chores, scrolling, illness, work. Write what actually occupied the hours, honestly, even when it's “nothing” or “YouTube.” That's the data.

Part three

After

A plan that never fired is usually too vague, or tied to a trigger that doesn't actually happen. Fix the trigger, not your resolve.

Deliberately not “was I hungry.” 22:37 says the flesh and blood of a sacrifice never reach God — only the taqwā does. The hunger is the fast's flesh and blood; it stays here. So: what did it surface? What did it show you about what you reach for when you're uncomfortable?

The one you wrote in advance — not a new one invented afterwards.

One. Not five. The accounts converge on moderation over intensity — a few things done consistently beat total transformation attempted at once and abandoned.

What this form can't do

It can't make a fast work, because the Quran doesn't promise that a fast works. It says perhaps. Every experiential state the Quran describes — the shivering skin, the trembling heart, the settled heart — attaches to hearing the Book, to remembrance, or to praying at night. None attaches to fasting. So if you fill this in completely and still feel ordinary, the form didn't fail and neither did you. You did the inputs. The outcome was never on the form, because it was never yours.

What the form does do is make sure that if nothing arrives, it isn't because the room was empty.

Not a fatwa and not a ruling. This reads the Quranic text directly and leaves hadith and fiqh out — so it is silent on much the tradition speaks about. For rulings, ask a scholar.
